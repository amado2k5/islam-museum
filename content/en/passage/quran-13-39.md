## Summary
Verse 39 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 39 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 39 (pp. 477-492)**

On "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said: God erases what He wills of the affairs of His servants and changes it, except wretchedness and happiness, which are not changed. Report 20461, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Bahr ibn Isa, Ibn Abi Layla, al-Minhal and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he said, God manages the affair of the servants and erases what He wills except wretchedness, happiness, life and death; in a note the editor discusses the chain of this report and thinks its correct form is "Abu Kurayb told us, Bakr told us, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 477}

Report 20462, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Bashshar, Ibn Abi Layla, al-Minhal ibn Amr and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he said, everything except happiness and wretchedness, for those two have been finished with; the editor notes that the chain has a gap, since Ibn Abi Layla died in 148 and Ibn Bashshar was born in 167. Report 20463, from Ibn Abbas, through Ali ibn Sahl, Yazid, Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan, Ibn Abi Layla, al-Minhal and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: except wretchedness, happiness, death and life. Report 20464, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym al-Fadl ibn Dukayn, Qabisa and Sufyan: the same. Report 20465, from Ibn Abbas, through Amr ibn Ali, Waki' and Ibn Abi Layla: except life and death, wretchedness and happiness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 478}

Report 20466, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym, Ibn Abi Layla, al-Minhal and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: God decrees the affair of the year in the Night of Power, except wretchedness, happiness, death and life. Report 20467, from Mujahid, through Amr ibn Ali, Abu Asim, Sufyan and Mansur: except life, death, happiness and wretchedness, for these two do not change. Reports 20468 and 20469, from Mujahid, through Umar, Abd al-Rahman, Mu'adh ibn Uqba and Mansur, and Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad and Sufyan: the same. Report 20470, from Mujahid, through Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Mansur: I asked Mujahid about the transmitted supplication in which a servant asks his Lord to confirm him if He has written him happy and to erase him if He has written him wretched; he said, wretchedness and happiness have been finished with; the editor refers to the report of Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud on the supplication. Report 20471, from Mujahid, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Mansur, and Sa'id ibn Sulayman and Sharik: "God erases what He wills and confirms", he said, God sends down everything of the year on the Night of Power and erases what He wills of terms, provisions and decrees, except wretchedness and happiness, which are fixed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 479}

Report 20472, from Mansur, through Ibn Humayd and Jarir: I asked Mujahid, what do you think of a supplication of one of us, saying, O God, if my name is among the happy, confirm it among them, and if it is among the wretched, erase it from them and put it among the happy? He said, good. Then I came to him after a year or more and asked him about it, and he recited "We sent it down on a blessed night, for We were warning; in it every wise matter is made distinct" [[passage/quran-44-3|44:3-4]], and said: what will be in the year of provision or misfortune is decreed on the Night of Power, then He advances what He wills and delays what He wills; but as for the book of wretchedness and happiness, it is fixed and does not change. Others said the meaning is that God erases what He wills and confirms from a book other than the Mother of the Book, of which nothing is changed. Report 20473, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Hammad, Sulayman al-Taymi and Ikrima: "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he said, two books: a book from which He erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book. Report 20474, from Ikrima, through Amr ibn Ali, Sahl ibn Yusuf and Sulayman al-Taymi: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 480}

he said, the Book is two books, a book from which God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book. Report 20475, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Amir, Hammad ibn Salama, Sulayman al-Taymi and Ikrima: the like. The repeated report from Ikrima, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman and his father: the Book is two books, "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book". Others said the meaning is rather that He erases all that He wills and confirms all that He wishes. Report 20476, from Shaqiq, through Abu Kurayb, Itham and al-A'mash: he used to say, O God, if You have written us wretched, erase us and write us happy, and if You have written us happy, confirm us, for You erase what You will and confirm, and with You is the Mother of the Book. Report 20477, from Abu Wa'il, through Amr, Waki' and al-A'mash: among the words he often used in supplication were these, O God, if You have written us wretched, erase us and write us happy...; the editor notes that Shaqiq is Abu Wa'il, that the pronoun refers to Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, and that Ibn Kathir presented it so as to suggest it was Shaqiq himself. Report 20478, from Abu Uthman al-Nahdi, through Mu'adh ibn Hisham, his father and Abu Hakima: Umar ibn al-Khattab said while circling the House, weeping, O God, if You have written on me wretchedness or a sin, erase it, for You erase what You will and confirm, and with You is the Mother of the Book, so make it happiness and forgiveness; the editor identifies the narrators of the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 481}

Report 20479, from Abu Uthman, through Mu'tamir, his father and Abu Hakima: and I think I heard it from Abu Uthman, the like. Report 20480, from Abu Uthman al-Nahdi, through Abu Amir, Qurra ibn Khalid and Isma Abu Hakima: from Umar, the like; the editor notes that al-Bukhari related it in the Kabir in similar wording. Report 20481, from Abu Uthman al-Nahdi, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Hammad and Abu Hakima: I heard Umar ibn al-Khattab say while circling the Ka'ba, O God, if You have written me among the people of happiness, confirm me in it, and if You have written against me sin and wretchedness, erase me and confirm me among the people of happiness, for You erase what You will and confirm, and with You is the Mother of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 482}

Report 20482, from Ibn Mas'ud, through al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, Hammad, Khalid al-Hadhdha' and Abu Qilaba: he used to say, O God, if You have written me among the people of wretchedness, erase me and confirm me among the people of happiness. Report 20483, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he says, it is the man who for a time acts in obedience to God then returns to disobedience to God and dies in his error; he is the one who is erased; and the one confirmed is the man who acts in disobedience to God while some earlier good was set aside for him, until he dies in obedience to God; he is the one confirmed. Report 20484, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sharik, Hilal ibn Humayd and Abd Allah ibn Ukaym: he used to say, O God, if You have written me among the happy, confirm me among the happy, for You erase what You will and confirm, and with You is the Mother of the Book; the editor identifies Hilal ibn Humayd and Abd Allah ibn Ukaym. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 483}

Report 20485, from Ibrahim, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Hammad and Abu Hamza: Ka'b said to Umar, Commander of the Faithful, were it not for a verse in God's Book I would tell you what will be until the Day of Resurrection. He said, which? He said, God's saying "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book"; the editor notes that Abu Hamza is Maymun al-A'war, very weak and with lost hadith, that the chain is very flimsy, and that he finds it strange that Rashid Rida attacked Ka'b al-Ahbar before checking the chain. Report 20486, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd: "for every term is a book", the verse; "God erases what He wills", He says, I abrogate what I will and do whatever acts I will; if I wish I add to them and if I wish I reduce. Report 20487, from al-Kalbi, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Affan and Hammam: "God erases what He wills and confirms", he said, it is erased from provision and added to it, and erased from the term and added to it; I said, who told you? He said, Abu Salih, from Jabir ibn Abd Allah al-Ansari, from the Prophet. Later al-Kalbi came and was asked about this verse "God erases what He wills and confirms", {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 484}

and said: all speech is written until Thursday, when everything is thrown out that has no reward in it and no punishment against it, such as your saying "I ate, I drank, I entered, I left", that and the like of speech, though it is true, and what has reward or punishment in it is confirmed. Report 20488, from al-Kalbi, through al-Hasan and Abd al-Wahhab: from Abu Salih, the like, going no further than Abu Salih; the editor notes that al-Kalbi has been spoken against to a degree that does not allow transmission from him and that al-Tabari said of him that he is not one whose transmission may be used as proof. Others said the meaning is rather that God abrogates what He wills of the rulings of His Book and confirms what He wills of them without abrogating. Report 20489, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "God erases what He wills", he said, of the Qur'an; he says, God replaces what He wills, abrogating it, and confirms what He wills without replacing it; "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", the whole of that is with Him in the Mother of the Book, the abrogating and the abrogated, what is replaced and what is confirmed, all of it in a book. Report 20490, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "God erases what He wills and confirms" is like His saying "whatever verse We abrogate or cause to be forgotten, We bring one better than it or like it" [[passage/quran-2-106|2:106]], and "with Him is the Mother of the Book" means the totality of the Book and its origin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 485}

Report 20491, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "God erases what He wills and confirms" what He wills, and He is the Wise, "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", and its origin. Report 20492, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "God erases what He wills" of what He sends down to the prophets, "and confirms" what He wills of what He sends down to the prophets; he said, "with Him is the Mother of the Book", which is not changed or replaced. Report 20493, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "God erases what He wills", he said, abrogates; "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he said, the Reminder. Others said the meaning is that He erases whoever's term has come and confirms whoever's term has not come, until his term. Report 20494, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Ibn Abi Adi and Awf: "God erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he says, He erases whoever's term has come and who has gone, and the one confirmed is the living who runs to his term. Report 20495, from al-Hasan, through Amr ibn Ali, Yahya and Awf: "God erases what He wills", he said, whoever's term has come, "and confirms", he said, whoever's term has not come, until his term. Report 20496, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Hawdha and Awf: like the report of Ibn Bashshar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 486}

Report 20497, from al-Hasan, through Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata', Sa'id and Qatada: "for every term is a book", he said, the terms of the children of Adam are in a book; God erases of a person's term what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book. Report 20498, from Mujahid, through Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "God erases what He wills and confirms", when "it was not for any messenger to bring a sign except by God's leave" [[passage/quran-13-38|13:38]] was revealed, Quraysh said, we do not see you, Muhammad, owning anything, and the matter has been settled; so this verse was revealed as a warning and threat to them: if We wish We bring about for him of Our command whatever We wish, and We bring about in every Ramadan, erasing and confirming what We wish of people's provisions, their misfortunes, what We give them and what We apportion to them. Reports 20499 and 20500, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa, and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: similarly. Others said the meaning is that He forgives what He wills of His servants' sins and leaves what He wills unforgiven. Report 20501, from Sa'id, through Ibn Humayd, Hakkam, Amr and Ata': "God erases what He wills and confirms", he said, in the womb He confirms wretchedness and happiness and everything, forgiving of it what He wills and delaying what He wills; the editor notes that al-Suyuti's wording is better than that of the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 487}

Al-Tabari says: the best of the views mentioned on this, by way of interpreting the verse and nearest to correct, is the view we reported from al-Hasan and Mujahid, because God, mighty be His praise, threatened with punishment the polytheists who asked the Messenger of God for signs, and said to them "it was not for any messenger to bring a sign except by God's leave; for every term is a book", letting them know thereby that His decree concerning them has a term fixed in a book, and they are delayed until the coming of that term. Then He said to them: when that term comes, God brings what He wills of those whose term has drawn near and whose provision has ended, or whose destruction or lowering from eminence or loss of wealth has come, deciding that among His creatures; that is the erasing; and He confirms what He wills of those whose term, provision and share remain, leaving them as they are and not erasing them. In this sense a report came from the Messenger of God, which Abu al-Darda' relates in report 20502, through Muhammad ibn Sahl ibn Askar, Ibn Abi Maryam, al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Ziyada ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Fadala ibn Ubayd: the Messenger of God said, God opens the Reminder in the last three hours of the night; in the first of them He looks into the Book which none but He looks into, erasing what He wills and confirming; then he mentioned what is in the other two hours; the editor notes that Ziyada ibn Muhammad al-Ansari is rejected in hadith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 488}

Report 20503, from Abu al-Darda', through Musa ibn Sahl al-Ramli, Adam, al-Layth, Ziyada ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Fadala ibn Ubayd: the Messenger of God said, God descends in the last three hours of the night, opening the Reminder in the first hour, which none but He has seen, erasing what He wills and confirming what He wills; the editor notes that this is another route of the preceding report, rejected like it. Report 20504, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sahl ibn Askar, Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Jurayj and Ata': God has a preserved tablet, a journey of five hundred years, of white pearl, with two covers of ruby, the two covers being two tablets of God; every day there are three hundred and sixty glances, and He erases what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book. Report 20505, from Qays ibn Abbad, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr, al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, his father and a man from his father: the tenth of Rajab is the day on which God erases what He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 489}

Then al-Tabari comments on "and with Him is the Mother of the Book": the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said its meaning is: with Him are the lawful and the unlawful. Report 20506, from Malik ibn Dinar, through al-Muthanna, Muslim ibn Ibrahim and Muhammad ibn Uqba: I asked al-Hasan, I said, "the Mother of the Book"? He said, the lawful and the unlawful. I said to him, then what is "praise be to God, Lord of the worlds"? He said, that is the mother of the Qur'an. Others said its meaning is: with Him is the whole of the Book and its origin. Report 20507, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he said, the whole of the Book and its origin. Report 20508, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the same. Report 20509, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd: "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he said, a book with the Lord of the worlds. Report 20510, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq ibn Yusuf and Juwaybir: the whole of the Book and its knowledge, meaning by that what is abrogated of it and what is confirmed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 490}

Report 20511, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he says, the whole of that is with Him in the Mother of the Book, the abrogating and the abrogated, what is replaced and what is confirmed, all of it in a book. Others said on this what is related in report 20512, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, his father and Sayyar: he asked Ka'b about "the Mother of the Book", and he said, God's knowledge of what He will create and what His creation will do; He said to His knowledge, be a book, and it was a book; the editor identifies Sayyar as a freedman of Khalid ibn Yazid ibn Mu'awiya. Others said it is the Reminder. Report 20513, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj, Abu Ja'far saying he does not know whether Ibn Jurayj is in it: "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", he said, the Reminder. Al-Tabari says: the best of the views on this is that of whoever said "with Him is the origin of the Book and its totality", since He, mighty be His praise, told that He erases {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 491}

what He wills and confirms what He wills, and then followed that with "and with Him is the Mother of the Book", so it is clear that its meaning is: with Him is the origin of what is confirmed of it and what is erased, and its totality, in a book with Him. The readers differed in reading "yuthbit". Most of the readers of Medina and Kufa read "yuthabbit" with a shadda on the ba', meaning that He leaves it and keeps it as it is without erasing; some of the Meccans, some of the Basrans and some of the Kufans read "yuthbit" lightly, meaning writes. We have shown before that the meaning of this, in our view, is keeping it written and leaving off erasing it, as we explained; that being so, the "tathbit" is more fitting and the shadda more correct than the light form, although the light form cannot be excluded, since the two meanings are close. As for "mahw", the Arabs have two dialects on it: Mudar says "mahawtu al-kitab amhuhu mahwan", and the revelation came so; and "mahawtuhu amhahu mahwan". It is related of some tribes of Rabi'a that they say "mahaytu amha"; the editor notes that this dialect is attributed in Lisan al-Arab and elsewhere also to Tayyi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 492}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
