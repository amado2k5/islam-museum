## Summary
Verse 5 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 5 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "their cry when Our might came to them was only that they said: we were wrongdoers", God says: the cry of the people of the town We destroyed, when Our might and onslaught came to them by night or while napping, was only their confession against themselves that they had done wrong to themselves, been sinful toward their Lord and gone against His command and prohibition. By "their cry" is meant here their supplication. "Da'wa" in Arab speech has two senses: supplication, and the claiming of a right. Among uses meaning supplication are His words in [[passage/quran-21-15|21:15]], "that continued to be their cry", and a line by the poet Kuthayyir Azza. The note refers to the earlier explanation of "sinful toward their Lord". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 303}

Al-Tabari gives, for the sense of "da'wa", a line of Kuthayyir Azza in which, when his leg went numb, he called his beloved and found relief from the numbness in calling her, so it eased; the note refers to his Diwan, Nihayat al-Arab and Lisan al-Arab. He says he explained earlier that "ba's" and "ba'sa'" mean hardship. This verse is a clear indication of the soundness of the report from the Messenger of God, "no people perish until they have made themselves excusable", and some interpreted it so: he gives (14323) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud this report from the Prophet, and that Abd al-Malik ibn Maysara al-Zarrad was asked how that could be and recited this verse. The note says the chain is broken because Abd al-Malik did not meet Ibn Mas'ud, that Ibn Kathir mentions it from al-Tabari and al-Suyuti ascribes it to Ibn Abi Hatim, and that "made himself excusable" means he gave the one who punishes him ground to punish him for his sin, that is, they are not destroyed until their sins multiply so that they deserve punishment and the one who punishes them has an excuse for inflicting it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 304}

If someone asks, how can it say "their cry when Our might came to them was only that they said: we were wrongdoers", and how could they make that claim when God's might had already come with destruction? Did they say it before the destruction? If before, then they said it before the might came, while God reports they said it when it came; or after it came, which is a state in which they had perished, so how can they be described as saying it when they saw God's might and the reality of what the messengers had promised of His onslaught? The answer is that not every nation perished in an instant with no interval between its beginning and end; some were drowned in the flood, and between the first appearance of the cause by which they knew they would perish and its end, which engulfed them all, lay a period no sane person could miss; and some were allowed to enjoy life for three days after the sign of destruction appeared before their eyes, like the people of Salih and their likes. Then, when they saw the first of God's might with which God's messengers had threatened them and were certain of the reality of His onslaught coming on them, they cried "woe to us, we were wrongdoers", and their faith did not benefit them once His threat had come and His vengeance had settled in their yard. So our Lord warned those to whom He sent His Prophet Muhammad of His onslaught and punishment for their disbelief in Him and denial of His messenger by what befell the nations before them when they disobeyed His messengers and followed the command of every stubborn tyrant. The note records that the manuscript's connection of this passage is disturbed and that the editor suspects something has dropped out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 305}
