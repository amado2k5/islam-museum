## Summary
Verse 93 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 93 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 93: "No blame on those who believe and do good for what they ate", pp. 576-579**

Al-Tabari says: God tells the people who asked, when the prohibition of wine came down, what of our brothers who died drinking it and of us who used to drink it: there is no blame on those of you who believed and did good for what they drank when God had not forbidden it, if they kept their duty, believed and did good; that is, if the living among them feared God and watched themselves in avoiding what He forbade, believed God and His messenger in what they commanded and forbade and obeyed them in all of it, and earned the works He is pleased with. "Then kept their duty and believed" means they stood firm in that duty and faith without change or alteration; "then kept their duty and did good" means their fear of God led them to do good, which is acting beyond what He made obligatory, in voluntary works by which they drew near to their Lord seeking His pleasure and fleeing His punishment; and "God loves the doers of good" means those who draw near to Him by voluntary works He is pleased with.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 576}

Al-Tabari explains that the first "keeping duty" is receiving God's command with acceptance, belief, devotion and action; the second is standing firm in belief, leaving alteration and change; the third is doing good by drawing near through voluntary works. The reason the third is voluntary rather than obligatory is that God reported that the blame is removed from those who drank before it was forbidden, if they feared God after the prohibition, believed and did obligatory good works, and there is no point in repeating that in one verse. The reports from Companions and Followers say it came down for this. Reports 12525 and 12526, from Ibn Abbas, say that when the prohibition of wine came down they asked: Messenger of God, what of our companions who died drinking it? and the verse came down. The editor traces it to Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 577}

Report 12527, from Anas ibn Malik, says that he was passing the cup round to Abu Talha, Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, Mu'adh ibn Jabal, Suhayl ibn Bayda and Abu Dujana, of a mixture of unripe and ripe dates, until their heads swayed, when they heard a crier call that wine was now forbidden; none of us went in or out until we poured out the drink and broke the jars, some of us performed ablution and some washed, we put on some of Umm Sulaym's perfume and went to the mosque, where the Prophet was reciting the verse to "so will you not abstain"; a man asked what is the standing of those of us who died drinking it, and "no blame on those who believe" came down. The editor notes what has been said of a narrator in the chain and traces the report to al-Bukhari, Muslim and al-Nasa'i.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 578}

Reports 12528 and 12529, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, say that when wine was forbidden they asked what of their companions who died drinking it and the verse came down; the editor traces it to al-Tirmidhi and others. Report 12530, from Mujahid, on the revelation of the verse, begins at the foot of the page.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 579}

**Commentary on verse 93, continued: reports on those who died before the prohibition, pp. 580-582**

Report 12530, from Mujahid, continues that the verse is about those killed at Badr and Uhud with the Prophet. Report 12531, from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, says that when the verse came down the Prophet said "I was told: you are one of them"; the editor traces the report to Muslim, al-Tirmidhi, al-Hakim and al-Haythami. Report 12532, from Qatada, says when the prohibition of wine came down in the surah of the table, some Companions said that so-and-so was killed at Badr and so-and-so at Uhud while they drank it, "and we testify they are people of Paradise", so the verse came down: the people drank it in God-fearing and good conduct when it was lawful for them, then it was forbidden after them, so no blame is on them. The editor notes a slip of the copyist in a word about the Confederates.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 581}

Report 12533, from Ibn Abbas, says they asked, Messenger of God, what do we say of our brothers who passed, who drank wine and engaged in gambling? and the verse came down, meaning before the prohibition, if they were doers of good and God-fearing, while "if they kept their duty and did good" means after it was forbidden, as in "whoever receives an admonition from his Lord and stops may keep what is past". Report 12534, from him, says men of the Companions died drinking wine before its prohibition and were not blamed, and when it was forbidden they said how can it be forbidden to us when our brothers died drinking it, and the verse came down.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 582}

Report 12535, from Mujahid, says it is for those who drank wine among the slain with Muhammad at Badr and Uhud, and report 12536, from al-Dahhak, that this concerns wine when it was forbidden, when they asked the Prophet about their brothers who died drinking it, and the verse came down.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 582}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
