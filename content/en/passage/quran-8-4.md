**Commentary on verse 4 (pp. 388-390)**

Report 15696, from Qatada: "those are the believers truly", they earned faith with right and God made it right for them. Then al-Tabari says on "they have ranks with their Lord, and forgiveness and a generous provision": by "they have ranks" he means for these believers whose description He gave there are ranks, which are high stations. The interpreters then differed over these ranks. Some said they are high deeds and virtues they put forward in their lifetimes; report 15697, from Mujahid: high deeds, the editor noting that Abu Yahya al-Qattat is weak. Others said they are rather stations in Paradise; report 15698, from Ibn Muhayriz: the ranks are seventy ranks, each the gallop of a swift trained horse for seventy years; the editor identifies Sufyan as al-Thawri, Hisham ibn Hassan al-Qardusi, Jabala ibn Uhayym al-Taymi and Ibn Muhayriz as Abd Allah ibn Muhayriz al-Jumahi, says he does not know Atiyya in the chain and is in doubt of him, and explains "hudr" as the horse's rising in its run and "mudammar" as the one made ready for the race. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 388} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 389} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 390}

As for "and forgiveness", it means pardon for their sins and covering them; "and a generous provision" is said to be Paradise, and to my mind it is what God has prepared for them in Paradise of increase in food and drink and pleasant living. Report 15699, from Qatada: "forgiveness" is for their sins, and "a generous provision" is Paradise. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "forgiveness" and "generous". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 390}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
