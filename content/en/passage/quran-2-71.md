## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari says Moses reported God as saying it was a cow "la dhalul", that is not made tame by work: not broken in by turning the soil with its hooves, nor used to draw water for the crop. He reports this from Qatada, al-Suddi, Abu al-'Aliya, al-Rabi' and Mujahid. "Turns the earth" means it overturns the ground for tilling, and it was so described because, it was said, it was wild, which is reported from al-Hasan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 212} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 213}
On "musallama" they differ: Mujahid says free of markings, with no white or black; Qatada, Abu al-'Aliya, al-Rabi' and Ibn Abbas say free of defects. Al-Tabari prefers Ibn Abbas's and Abu al-'Aliya's reading to Mujahid's, since if its soundness meant colour only, "no blemish in it" would have sufficed, which shows the two phrases carry different senses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 213} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 214}

Al-Tabari continues on "musallama": for him the verse says the cow was not tamed by turning the soil or drawing water for crops, and was moreover sound and free of defects. "La shiya fiha" means no colour in it differing from its hide, the word coming from "washy", the adorning of cloth; hence the "wāshi", the slanderer who dresses up his falsehood, for which he cites Ka'b ibn Zuhayr, and he does not accept those who make "washy" mean a mark. He reports from Qatada, al-Rabi', Abu al-'Aliya, 'Atiyya, Mujahid, al-Suddi and Ibn Zayd that it has no white, black or red, but one colour. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 214} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 215} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 216}
On "Now you have brought the truth" there are two views: Qatada's, that the sense is "now you have made it clear to us and we know which cow you mean"; and one reported from Ibn Zayd, that they were charging Moses with not having brought the truth before. In his own choice al-Tabari prefers Qatada's, since Moses had made things clear in every question, so it would be wrong to say to him "now you have brought the truth" as though he had not before. He notes that some earlier scholar held they apostatized and disbelieved by that saying; al-Tabari does not take that view, since they submitted by slaughtering it, though their words were ignorant and one of their slips. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 217} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 218}
On "and they almost did not", he says they came near abandoning God's command. The cause is disputed: Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Mujahid say the high price, since they bought it for its hide's worth in gold from the dead man's property; Ibn Abbas, through al-Dahhak, says they did not wish to slaughter it; and another view gives fear of exposure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 218} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 219}
