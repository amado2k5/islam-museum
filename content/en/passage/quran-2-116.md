## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "they said God has taken a son; glory be to Him! Rather to Him belongs what is in the heavens and the earth" al-Tabari says "they said" is coordinated with "and strove to ruin them", those meant being the ones who barred God's mosques, who are in his view the Christians who claimed Jesus is the son of God, and God said, denying what they said, "glory be to Him", meaning exalting and clearing Him of having a son. He then reported that what is in the heavens and the earth is His in dominion and creation, which in his reading means: how can the Messiah be God's son, when he must be in one of these places, in the heavens or the earth, and God owns what is in them; had he been a son as claimed, he would not be like all else in the heavens and the earth that He created, in the appearance in him of the signs of workmanship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 537} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 538}

On "all are devoutly obedient to Him" the interpreters differed. Some said "qanitun" means obedient, from Qatada, Mujahid (the unbeliever's obedience being in the prostration of his shadow), al-Suddi (obedient on the Day of Resurrection), Ikrima, and al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas. Others said each acknowledges servanthood to Him, from Ikrima. Others said each stands before Him on the Day of Resurrection, from al-Rabi'. Al-Tabari notes that "qunut" in Arabic has several senses: obedience, standing, and holding back from speech. He holds the first sense here is obedience and confession of servanthood to God by the witness of their bodies, through the marks of workmanship in them and their indication of God's oneness and that He is their maker and creator; and if some deny this, their tongues submit to Him in obedience by their witness to the marks of workmanship, and the Messiah is one of them, so how could he be God's son with such a description. He answers one who claimed that "all are devoutly obedient to Him" is for the obedient only and not general, that a restriction may not be claimed in a verse general in its plain sense without proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 538} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 539} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 540}
