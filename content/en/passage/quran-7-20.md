## Summary
Verse 20 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 20 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Then Satan whispered to them to disclose to them what had been hidden from them of their nakedness", he says: "whispered to them" means he whispered to them, and that whispering was his saying to them "your Lord forbade you this tree only lest you become angels or become immortal" and his swearing to them to it. "Whispered to them" is said with the sense I gave, as one says "I was drawn to him" meaning I longed for him, which really means I turned from these to him; the note says this is the wording of al-Akhfash in explaining a line by Ibn Harma, and corrects the printed text by the manuscript. So here, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 346}

Satan whispered from himself to them with lying speech to disclose to them what had been hidden from them of their nakedness, as Ru'ba said in a line describing a hidden hunter lying in wait for wild asses, which the note traces. The meaning of the speech is: Iblis drew Eve to Adam and put to them, your Lord forbade you to eat the fruit of this tree only lest you become angels or become immortal, so as to disclose what God had hidden from them of their private parts, covering it with His veil that He had drawn over them. Wahb ibn Munabbih used to speak of the covering with which God had covered them: he gives (14393) from him, on "their nakedness appeared to them", that there was a light on them, so their private parts were not seen. The note records the biography of Hawthara ibn Muhammad al-Minqari in the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 347}

Then, on "He said: your Lord forbade you this tree only lest you become angels or become immortal", God says that Satan said to Adam and his wife Eve, your Lord forbade you this tree, forbade you to eat its fruit, only lest you become angels; "not" is dropped from the speech because what is visible points to it, as it is dropped from His words in [[passage/quran-4-176|4:176]], "God makes clear to you lest you go astray", the sense being that you not go astray. Some Basran Arabists claimed the sense is that your Lord forbade you this tree only out of dislike that you become angels, as one says "beware doing it" meaning out of dislike of your doing it. "Or become immortal" means in Paradise, abiding in it forever, so as not to die. The reading is with fatha on the lam, meaning angels. It is reported of Ibn Abbas, (14394) from al-Suddi, that he read "malikayn" with kasra on the lam, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 348}

and of Yahya ibn Abi Kathir (14395) that he read it "malikayn" with kasra. It is as if Ibn Abbas and Yahya took the meaning to be that Satan said to them, your Lord forbade you this tree only lest you become kings, and they interpreted it by God's words elsewhere, in [[passage/quran-20-120|20:120]], "shall I lead you to the tree of immortality and a kingdom that does not decay?" Al-Tabari says the reading in which he allows no other is that of the readers of the lands, the fatha on the lam of "malakayn", meaning two angels, because of what he explained before, that whatever is widespread among the readers of Islam is the right reading and may not be opposed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 349}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
