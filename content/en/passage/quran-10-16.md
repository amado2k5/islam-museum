**Commentary on verse 16 (pp. 41-45)**

On "I follow only what is revealed to me": say to them, I follow, in everything I command you and forbid you, only what my Lord sends down to me and commands me; "I fear, if I disobey my Lord, the punishment of a great day" means I fear God, if I go against His command, alter the rulings of His book and change His revelation and so disobey Him, the punishment of a day whose terror is great, the day when every nursing woman will forget her nursling and every pregnant woman will lay down her burden and you will see people drunk though they are not drunk; the editor points out that this takes up wording from a verse of Surat al-Hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 41}

He begins "Say: had God willed I would not have recited it to you, nor would He have made you aware of it; I lived among you a lifetime before it; will you not understand?". God tells His prophet the argument to give the polytheists who said "bring a Qur'an other than this, or change it": say to them, Muhammad, if God had willed I would not have recited this Qur'an to you, people, had He not sent it down to me and commanded me to recite it to you, "nor would He have made you aware of it", that is I would not have made you know it; "I lived among you a lifetime before it" means I stayed among you forty years before I recited it to you and before my Lord revealed it to me; "will you not understand" that, if I were forging words that are not mine, I would have forged them in the days of my youth, before the time I recited it to you. The editor refers to the gloss on "recited". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 41}

Al-Tabari completes his remarks on "I lived among you a lifetime before it": today, had I not been inspired and commanded to recite it to you, I would have had room and ease in the position I held among you, short of your enmity. Then he gives the interpreters' reports: 17581, from Ibn Abbas, "nor would He have made you aware of it" means nor have I taught you; 17582, from him: had God willed, He would not have taught it to you; 17583, from him: what I warned you with; 17584, from Qatada, that "bring a Qur'an other than this, or change it" is what the polytheists of Mecca said to the Prophet and that God then told him to say "had God willed...", the lifetime being forty years; 17585, from Ibn Zayd: nor taught you of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 42}

Report 17586, from al-Hasan, is that he read "wa la adra'tukum bihi" with a hamza, saying it means: I did not make you aware of it; the editor notes that the manuscript has the usual form and the printed text a form without the hamza, and that the correct one is what he printed, as Ibn Khalawayh states in his book on irregular readings and al-Farra in his Ma'ani al-Qur'an. Report 17587, from al-Dahhak: nor would God make you aware of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 43}

Al-Tabari says this reading reported of al-Hasan is a mistake in the view of the people of Arabic. He quotes al-Farra as saying that al-Hasan is reported to have read it with a hamza; if there is a dialect form other than "darayt" and "adrayt", al-Hasan may have gone to it, but it cannot be formed from those two, because a ya or waw preceded by a vowel-marked letter and itself at rest stays sound and does not turn into an alif; perhaps al-Hasan followed his natural eloquence and added the hamza because it resembles "dara'tu al-hadd" and the like, and the Arabs sometimes err over a word when another with a hamza resembles it and put a hamza on one that has none, with examples from their speech, such as a woman of Tayy saying that she had lamented her husband in verses. The editor says this is al-Farra's text in full with slight differences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 43}

Some Basrans are quoted as saying that al-Hasan's reading has no ground because it would be from "adrayt", like "a'tayt", though the Banu Uqayl have a dialect form "a'tat" meaning "a'tayt", turning the ya into alif. He cites lines attributed to Hurayth ibn Unab al-Ta'i, Zayd al-Khayl, Labid in his lament for his brother Arbad, and a poet the editor could not identify, containing words of this kind (the verses described, not quoted), and says Tayy turn every ya preceded by a kasra into alif, saying "jarat" for "jariya", and that Yunus held that two such forms are a known dialect. The editor lists where the lines occur in Abu Zayd's Nawadir, the Lisan and Labid's diwan, restores the reading of Labid's line from the manuscript and explains two verbs in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 44}

Another reading is reported from Ibn Abbas: report 17588, through Shahr ibn Hawshab, that he read "nor have I warned you of it". Al-Tabari says the reading he does not allow himself to go beyond is that of the readers of the main cities, "wa la adrakum bihi", meaning nor would He have made you know or aware of it; the editor records a difference between the printed text and the manuscript in his wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 45}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
