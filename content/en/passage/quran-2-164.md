## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day..." al-Tabari says God, by this verse, showed the polytheists where people of understanding find proof of His oneness: if they are ignorant or doubtful, let them reflect on the creation of the heavens and earth, the alternation of night and day, the ships, the water sent down from the sky, the creatures and the clouds; if their gods could create the like of any of it they would have an excuse, otherwise they have none. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 267} He then sets out the interpreters' differing reports on the occasion. Some said it was sent down as an argument against the polytheists when "your God is one God" was revealed and they asked what proof there was; from 'Ata', the unbelievers of Quraysh in Mecca asked how one God could suffice for all people, and it was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 268} Others said it was revealed because the polytheists asked the Prophet for a sign; from Abu al-Duha they said, "if this is so, let him bring us a sign", and similarly from Ibn Jurayj from 'Ata' they said "show us a sign". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 268} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 269}

In Sa'id ibn Jubayr's report, Quraysh asked the Jews about the signs of Moses and were told of the staff and the white hand, and asked the Christians about the signs of Jesus and were told of healing the blind and the leper and raising the dead; they then asked the Prophet to make al-Safa gold, and it was revealed to him that He would give it, but if they denied it He would punish them as no one in the worlds had been punished; he said, leave me and my people, I will call them day by day, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 269} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 270} From al-Suddi, they said, turn al-Safa to gold for us if you are truthful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 270} Al-Tabari holds that God by this verse alerted His servants to the proof of His oneness; it may have been revealed on what 'Ata' said or on what Sa'id and Abu al-Duha said, he has no report that settles either side, and the meaning of the verse is the same whichever is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 270} He then explains "the creation of the heavens and the earth" as bringing them into being after they were not, and notes that people differed over whether "creation" is something other than the thing created: some made it other than the thing, citing this verse and one in al-Kahf, and arguing that God creates nothing without willing it and the will is a creation; some made it a quality of the thing, neither it nor other than it; others made it the thing itself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 271} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 272} "The alternation of night and day" is their succession, each following the other, for which he cites the verse of al-Furqan and a line of Zuhayr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 272} He adds notes on the plurals of "night" and "day". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 273} "The ships" are vessels, one form for singular and plural, masculine or feminine, and "what benefits people" means benefits them at sea. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 273} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 274} The water God sends down is rain; giving life to the earth is its being built up and bringing forth plants, and its death is its ruin and the end of its plants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 274}

On "and He scattered in it every kind of creature" he says "scattered" means dispersed, and "creature" (dabba) is the name of every living thing that does not fly with wings, from its creeping (dabib) on the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 274} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 275} "The turning about of the winds" is God's turning them, sending them at one time as fertilising winds, at another barren, and at another as punishment; from Qatada, God is able, if He wills, to make them a mercy fertilising the clouds and, if He wills, a punishment, a barren wind. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 275} He reports the view of some grammarians that the turning is their coming from south, north, east and west, and distinguishes "God's turning them" from "their own varying", allowing that the sense may be God's turning the blowing of the wind by the variety of its sources. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 276} "The subjugated cloud" is the plural of a cloud, named for one part drawing another, "signs" are indications that the creator of all this is one God, and "for a people who reason" means those who grasp where the proofs lie; the proofs were set for those with reason, who alone are charged with command and prohibition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 276} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 277} Asked how this argues against unbelievers when some deny these things are created, he answers that their denial does not remove their witness to a creator, and that God argued with a people who acknowledged Him as their creator yet associated idols in worship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 277} He then expounds the verse as a list of benefits, the earth and provision, the heavens with sun and moon, the winds driving ships, the rain, the livestock and the clouds, and asks whether any of their partners does any of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 277} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 278} He says those addressed are the people he described, not the deniers of a creator or the materialists, and that he left out further proofs to avoid lengthening the book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 278} Note on the text: in the file available to us the printed page 278 runs straight into a page printed 281, in the middle of the commentary on 2:165, so printed pages 279 and 280 have not been read here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 278}
