## Summary
Verse 183 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 183 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 448).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God heard the saying of those who said He had made a covenant with them not to believe a messenger, and that this relative clause stands in the genitive in apposition to the earlier one. By the covenant he understands that God charged them and told them beforehand in His books and by the tongues of His prophets not to affirm any messenger in what he says he brought from God, of command, prohibition and the like, until he brought an offering, which is what a servant draws near to his Lord with, such as charity; the word is a verbal noun like those of the pattern of 'udwan and khusran, from "I offered an offering". As to the fire that came down and consumed it, in that age the fire's consuming what one of them offered to God was a sign that God had accepted what was offered and that the offerer was truthful in what he claimed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 448}

**Reports 8310-8311 (pp. 448-449).** Ibn 'Abbas (8310), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father from his uncle from his father from his father from Ibn 'Abbas: a man would give charity, and when it was accepted a fire was sent down on it from heaven and consumed it. Al-Dahhak (8311), through al-Husayn from Abu Mu'adh from 'Ubayd: when a man gave charity and it was accepted, God sent a fire from heaven which came down on the offering and consumed it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 449}

**God's word to His Prophet and al-Tabari's explanation (pp. 449-450).** Al-Tabari says God told His Prophet to say to those who claimed that covenant: messengers came to you before me with proofs of the truth of their prophethood, and with what you claimed obliged you to believe a messenger who brought it, the fire's consuming the offering as a sign of truthfulness; so why did you kill them, while acknowledging that what they brought was a proof against you, if you are truthful that God covenanted with you to believe those of His messengers who came with an offering consumed by fire as proof of prophethood? He then says that God informed His servants by this verse that the Jews of the Prophet's time were no more, in lying about God, fabricating against their Lord, belying Muhammad while knowing him to be truthful, and denying his prophethood while finding it written with them in God's covenant that he is His messenger to creation whose obedience is obligatory, than like those of their forebears who killed God's prophets after God had cut off their excuse with proofs, fabricating about God and making light of His rights. The first note (p. 449): the manuscript words this passage differently, its copyist put a mark in the margin by the two lines meaning that the error was in the copy he took it from, and he transcribed it as he found it; the printed edition's publisher, or a copyist before him, tried to correct it by adding the opening of the verse, but the context stayed poor, so the editor added the bracketed words, guided by Abu Ja'far's method. The second: the manuscript and printed text read "when" in a form the context does not fit, which the editor corrects. The note on p. 450 says the manuscript and printed text give two forms that have no sense here, and the editor's reading is what he printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 450}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 450}
