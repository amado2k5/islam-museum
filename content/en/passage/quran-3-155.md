## Summary
Verse 155 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 155 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Those who turned away on the day the two hosts met, verse 155 (pp. 326-327).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning is those among the Companions of the Messenger of God who turned their backs on the idolaters at Uhud and fled from them; 'tawallaw' has the pattern 'tafa''alu' from the saying 'so-and-so turned his back' (the editor refers to earlier places in the commentary on 'tawalla'). The day the hosts met is the day the host of the idolaters and the host of the Muslims met, which is Uhud; it was Satan who called them to the slip, 'istazalla' having the pattern 'istaf'ala' from 'zalla', and 'zalla' is the fault (the editor refers to 4: 259-260 and 1: 524-525). 'Some of what they earned' means some of the sins they did (the editor refers to the commentary on 'kasaba'); God passed over punishment for their sins and forgave them; the 'ghafur' is the one who covers the sins of whoever believes in Him and follows His messenger by pardoning the punishment for them, and the 'halim' is the patient one who does not hasten retribution on one who disobeys and goes against His order (the editor refers to the language indexes).

**Disagreement over who exactly is meant (8098, p. 327).** Some said it means everyone who turned his back on the idolaters at Uhud. Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash from 'Asim ibn Kulayb from his father (8098): 'Umar gave the Friday sermon and recited Al 'Imran, which he liked to recite when he preached; when he reached this verse he said: at Uhud we defeated them, then I fled until I climbed the mountain, and I saw myself leaping like a mountain goat while people were saying that Muhammad was killed; I said: I will find no one saying Muhammad was killed but I will kill him; until we gathered on the mountain, and then it came down (the report continues on p. 328). The editor explains that 'anzu' means to bound, since 'nazw' is leaping, and that the 'arwa' are female ibex, strong at climbing in mountains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 326} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 327}

**The rest of 'Umar's report and its narrators (8098-8100, pp. 327-328).** The report ends with 'Umar saying they gathered on the mountain and the whole verse came down. The editor gives notes on the narrators of 8098: Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i is Muhammad ibn Yazid ibn Muhammad ibn Kathir (met earlier at 4888, 4557, 3286); Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash ibn Salim al-Asadi al-Kufi al-Hannat, whose name is said to be Muhammad or 'Abdallah, with Ibn Hajar's view that the sound opinion is that his name is his by-name, and that he was a retentive, exact memoriser whose memory worsened in old age so that he erred in reporting, error and misapprehension being two things humans cannot escape, as Ibn Hibban said (al-Tahdhib); 'Asim ibn Kulayb ibn Shihab ibn al-Majnun al-Jarmi, who narrated from his father, Abu Burda ibn Abi Musa, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and others, and from whom Ibn 'Awn, Shu'ba, Sharik and the two Sufyans narrated; Ahmad said there is no harm in his hadith, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Ma'in called him trustworthy, and he was one of the devout without much hadith; and his father Kulayb ibn Shihab, who narrated from his father, from his maternal uncle al-Fultan ibn 'Asim, from 'Umar, 'Ali, Sa'd, Abu Dharr, Abu Musa, Abu Hurayra and others, of whom Ibn Sa'd said he was trustworthy and that he saw people think his hadith good and use it as evidence. Qatada (8099): this was at Uhud, some Companions of the Messenger of God turned from the fighting and from the Prophet of God that day, and it was from Satan's doing and frightening; God sent down what you hear, that He passed over it for them and forgave them. Al-Rabi' (8100) said something like Qatada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 327} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 328}

**The second and third views on who is meant (8101-8104, pp. 328-330).** Others said that it means particular ones of those who turned away that day, namely those who reached Medina and no others. Al-Suddi (8101): when they were routed the Companions scattered from the Messenger of God; some entered Medina and some went up the mountain to the rock and stood on it, and God mentioned those who fled and entered Medina. Others said it was revealed about named men. 'Ikrima through Ibn Jurayj (8102): it was revealed about Rafi' ibn al-Mu'alla and others of the Ansar, Abu Hudhayfa ibn 'Utba and another man; Ibn Jurayj said of the pardon that He did not punish them. Ibn Ishaq (8103): 'Uthman ibn 'Affan, 'Uqba ibn 'Uthman and Sa'd ibn 'Uthman, two men of the Ansar, fled until they reached al-Jal'ab, a mountain on the edge of Medina near al-A'was, and stayed there three days, then returned to the Messenger of God, who said to them: you have gone far in it; the editor explains this as 'wide', the pronoun referring to the earth, the sense being that he marvels at what they did when the earth's expanses opened to them and they went far off. The editor gives the vocalisation of 'al-Jal'ab' from al-Bakri and Yaqut, notes that some made it a dual in verse and that the printed Mu'jam al-Buldan has many errors, and points to a line describing the hyenas of that place. Ibn Ishaq (8104): those whom Satan caused to slip were 'Uthman ibn 'Affan, Sa'd ibn 'Uthman and 'Uqba ibn 'Uthman, the two Ansaris of the Zurayq; the editor says he did not find 8103 and 8104 in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 328} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 329} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 330}

**The meaning of the pardon (8105-8106, p. 330).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning of pardon is that God passed over, for those who turned away on the day of the meeting of the two hosts, punishing them for retreating from their enemy. Ibn Jurayj (8105): He pardoned them, since He did not punish them. Ibn Zayd (8106), on their turning away at Uhud: I do not know whether that pardon was for that band or for all the Muslims. Abu Ja'far has already explained God's forgiveness and forbearance (the editor refers to 5: 117 and 5: 521 as we read them from the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 330}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
