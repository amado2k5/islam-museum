## Summary
Verse 155 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 155 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Those who turned away on the day the two hosts met, verse 155 (pp. 326-327).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning is those among the Companions of the Messenger of God who turned their backs on the idolaters at Uhud and fled from them; 'tawallaw' has the pattern 'tafa''alu' from the saying 'so-and-so turned his back' (the editor refers to earlier places in the commentary on 'tawalla'). The day the hosts met is the day the host of the idolaters and the host of the Muslims met, which is Uhud; it was Satan who called them to the slip, 'istazalla' having the pattern 'istaf'ala' from 'zalla', and 'zalla' is the fault (the editor refers to 4: 259-260 and 1: 524-525). 'Some of what they earned' means some of the sins they did (the editor refers to the commentary on 'kasaba'); God passed over punishment for their sins and forgave them; the 'ghafur' is the one who covers the sins of whoever believes in Him and follows His messenger by pardoning the punishment for them, and the 'halim' is the patient one who does not hasten retribution on one who disobeys and goes against His order (the editor refers to the language indexes).

**Disagreement over who exactly is meant (8098, p. 327).** Some said it means everyone who turned his back on the idolaters at Uhud. Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash from 'Asim ibn Kulayb from his father (8098): 'Umar gave the Friday sermon and recited Al 'Imran, which he liked to recite when he preached; when he reached this verse he said: at Uhud we defeated them, then I fled until I climbed the mountain, and I saw myself leaping like a mountain goat while people were saying that Muhammad was killed; I said: I will find no one saying Muhammad was killed but I will kill him; until we gathered on the mountain, and then it came down (the report continues on p. 328). The editor explains that 'anzu' means to bound, since 'nazw' is leaping, and that the 'arwa' are female ibex, strong at climbing in mountains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 326} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 327}
