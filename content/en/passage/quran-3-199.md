## Summary
Verse 199 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 199 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 199: who is meant? (pp. 496-497, reports 8376-8378).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over whom this verse refers to. Some said it refers to Ashama al-Najashi (the Negus), and that it came down about him.

**Report 8376.** 'Isam ibn Rawwad ibn al-Jarrah, from his father, from Abu Bakr al-Hudhali, from Qatada, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, that the Prophet said: go out and pray over a brother of yours; he led us in prayer with four takbirs and said: this is al-Najashi, Ashama; and the hypocrites said: look at this man, praying over a Christian foreigner he never saw! and God sent down this verse, [[passage/quran-3-199|3:199]]. In the editor's note, "'ilj" is a man from the unbelieving non-Arabs; 'Isam ibn Rawwad's biography and reliability came earlier; the printed edition adds the name "Ziyad" in his lineage with no basis and the manuscript, rightly, omits it; his father Rawwad ibn al-Jarrah appeared earlier with his weak grading; Abu Bakr al-Hudhali was shown to be very weak; al-Suyuti (2: 113) mentions the report and attributes it to no one but al-Tabari; Ibn Kathir (2: 330) gives it from al-Tabari with a different wording in places and without the start of the chain, so he may have taken it from another place in al-Tabari. The editor says this report is weak, as one sees, and that al-Tabari's remark on p. 499, "it was said: that is a report whose chain has something to consider", is to follow; the weakness is only in this chain for Jabir's report, whereas the basic matter, that the Prophet prayed the funeral prayer over the Negus in his absence, is in the editor's judgement established and sound beyond doubt, transmitted by the two Shaykhs and others from Jabir and from Abu Hurayra, and he refers to al-Muntaqa (nos. 1821-1824).

**Report 8377.** Ibn Bashshar, from Mu'adh ibn Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, that the Prophet said: your brother al-Najashi has died, so pray over him; they said: he prays over a man who is not a Muslim! and the verse came down. Qatada said: they also said that he did not pray towards the qibla, and God sent down the verse of al-Baqara, [[passage/quran-2-115|2:115]].

**Report 8378 (Qatada).** Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada on the verse; p. 497 breaks off as the report begins with the word "it was mentioned", and its text continues on p. 498.

Qatada (8378) said: it was mentioned to us that this verse came down about the Negus and some of his companions who believed in the Prophet of God and trusted him; and it was mentioned to us that the Prophet asked forgiveness for the Negus and prayed over him when news of his death reached him, saying to his companions: pray over a brother of yours who has died in a land other than yours; and some of the hypocrites said: he prays over a man who died and was not of his religion? so God sent down this verse. Qatada again (8379), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar: it came down about the Negus and his companions who believed in the Prophet, and the Negus's name was Ashama. Al-Muthanna (8380), from Ishaq from 'Abd al-Razzaq, said Ibn 'Uyayna said: the Negus's name in Arabic is 'Atiyya. Ibn Jurayj (8381), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: when the Prophet prayed over the Negus the hypocrites criticised it, and this verse came down.

**Second view: 'Abd Allah ibn Salam and those with him (pp. 498-499, reports 8382-8383).** Others said it refers to 'Abd Allah ibn Salam and those with him. Ibn Jurayj (8382), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: it came down about 'Abd Allah ibn Salam and those with him. Ibn Zayd (8383), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: the whole verse is about these, meaning the Jews.

**Third view: those of the People of the Book who became Muslims (p. 499, report 8384).** Others said it refers to those of the People of the Book who became Muslims. Mujahid (8384), through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih: they are of the Jews and the Christians, and they are the Muslims of the People of the Book.

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 499-500).** Al-Tabari says the soundest of these views for the meaning of the verse is what Mujahid said, because God spoke generally of the People of the Book as a whole, and did not single out the Christians without the Jews or the Jews without the Christians; He only told that among them are those who believe in God, and both groups belong to them. He puts the question: what do you say of the report you related from Jabir and others that it came down about the Negus and his companions? And answers that this is a report whose chain has something to consider, and that even if it were sound beyond doubt it would not conflict with what he said, because Jabir and those who spoke as he did only said it came down about the Negus, and a verse may come down about one thing and then apply to everyone of like description; so though the verse came down about the Negus, God made the ruling He gave for him a ruling for all His servants who share his description in following the Messenger of God and believing what came to them from God, after what they had been on before of following God's command in the two books, the Torah and the Gospel. In the editor's note the printed edition has a different word for "conflict" and the right one is in the manuscript; the note is then cut off at the end of p. 499 in the scan.

**The meaning of the verse (p. 500).** Al-Tabari takes the sense to be: among the people of the Torah and the Gospel there are those who affirm God's oneness and believe in what was sent down to the believers of His book and revelation on the tongue of His Messenger Muhammad, and in what was sent down to the People of the Book of books, namely the Torah, the Gospel and the Psalms, humble before God in obedience, submissive and abased. Ibn Zayd (8385), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: the humble is the one who abases himself to God, fearing. The word for "humble" is in the accusative as a state (hal) of the subject pronoun in the verb of believing; the note refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 251) and to the earlier explanation of "humility". He then says the sense is that they do not distort what was sent down to them in their books of the description of Muhammad, changing it, nor anything else of its rulings and proofs, for a base worldly gain given for such alteration, or to seek leadership over the ignorant; rather they submit to the truth, act on what God commanded in what was sent to them, desist from what He forbade and prefer God's command to their own desire. The note refers to the earlier explanation of "buying" and "price" shortly before.

**The reward and the swiftness of reckoning (p. 501).** Al-Tabari says these believers have the recompense for the deeds they did and the reward of their obedience to their Lord in what they obeyed Him in, stored for them with Him until they come to Him at the Rising and He pays them in full; and the swiftness of His reckoning is that nothing of their deeds is hidden from Him, before they do it or after, so He has no need to count their number, in which delay could occur. The notes say the printed edition has the phrase "he means by that, great is His praise" and the context requires what the editor set, and refer to earlier explanations of "reward" and of the swiftness of reckoning. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 501}
