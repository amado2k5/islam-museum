## Summary
Verse 117 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 117 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

This is God's report of Jesus' words: I said to them only what you ordered me to say, that is, I told them to serve God, my Lord and yours; "and I was a witness over them as long as I was among them" means I witnessed what they did, their deeds and words, while I was among them; "when you took me" means when you called me to you, "you were the watcher over them" means the guardian over them rather than I, since I witnessed only what they did while among them. This shows that God made known to Jesus the people's deeds and sayings only after taking him to himself, through his words "did you say to the people"; "and you are witness over all things" means you witness everything, for nothing is hidden from you, whereas I witnessed only some things, what I saw while living among the people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 239}

The interpreters said the like. Report 13032, from al-Suddi: the "raqib" is the guardian; 13033, from Ibn Jurayj: the guardian. A group of scholars used to say that Jesus' answer to his Lord was prompted by God. Reports 13034 and 13035, from Tawus: Jesus argued, and God prompted him. Report 13036, from Maysara: God said, Jesus, did you say that to the people? and his joints shook, fearing he had said it, and he said glory to you, if I said it you knew it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 240}

The editor refers for "witness", "as long as" , "took" and "watcher" to the indexes and earlier volumes, and corrects the printed and manuscript "tawfiq" (granting success) to "tawqif" (prompting), meaning it was taught him by God and not from himself, and likewise "waqqafahu".

Verse 117 closes here at p. 240, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-5-118|5:118]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
