## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 45 to 47 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "And remember Our servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, those of hands and sight": Reciters differed in the recitation of the phrase "Our servants," so the majority of the reciters of the lands read it as "and remember Our servants" in the plural manner, except for Ibn Kathir, who mentioned that he recited it as "And remember Our servant" in the sense of monotheism, as if he was directing the speech to Ishaq and Jacob, son of Abraham's descendants, and that they were mentioned after him. From Amr, from Ata, he heard Ibn Abbas recite: "And mention our servant Abraham," he said: He only mentioned Abraham, then mentioned his son after him. The correct reading for him in this regard is the reading of those who read it as intercourse, on the condition that Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob are a statement about the servants and a translation from him, due to the consensus of the proof among the reciters on him. And his saying: "Those of hands and eyes," meaning by hands: strength, he says: those who have strength in worshiping and obeying God. In the excerpt, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ata.
