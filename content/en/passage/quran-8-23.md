**Commentary on verse 23 (pp. 462-463)**

Al-Tabari says on "had God known any good in them He would have made them hear; and had He made them hear they would have turned away, swerving" [[passage/quran-8-23|8:23]]: the interpreters differed over whom this verse means and its sense. Some said it means the polytheists, and said its sense is that had God granted them understanding of what He sent down to His prophet they would not have believed in it, because God had ruled on them that they do not believe. Report 15863, from Ibn Jurayj: "had God known any good in them He would have made them hear, and had He made them hear" they would have said bring a Qur'an other than this, [[passage/quran-10-15|10:15]], and would have said why have you not chosen it, [[passage/quran-7-203|7:203]], and had he brought them another Qur'an "they would have turned away, swerving"; report 15864, from Ibn Zayd: had He made them hear after knowing there is no good in them, they would not have benefited from it and would have turned away, swerving; report 15865 from him another time: after He knows there is no good in them it would not have benefited them, His knowledge having gone through that they do not benefit from it. Others said it rather means the hypocrites, and report 15866, from Ibn Ishaq: "had God known any good in them He would have made them hear" He would have carried out for them their saying that they said with their tongues, but their hearts differed from it, and had they gone out with you they would have turned away, swerving, and would not have been true to you in anything they went out on. The editor gives the manuscript's reading in phrases of the report and says the printed edition has no sense, and that he follows Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 462} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 463}

Abu Ja'far said: the better of the two views on its interpretation, to my mind, is what Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Zayd said, for the reason mentioned before and because that is not a description of the hypocrites. So the sense of the verse is: had God known any good in these speakers He would have made them hear the admonitions and lessons of the Qur'an so that they would understand His proofs from Him, but He knew there is no good in them and that they are of those for whom wretchedness is written, so they do not believe; and had He made them understand it until they knew and understood, they would have turned from God and from His Messenger, swerving from believing in what God's admonitions, lessons and proofs showed to be sound, stubbornly opposing the truth after knowing it. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "turning" and "swerving". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 463}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
