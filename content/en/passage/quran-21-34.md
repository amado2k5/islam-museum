## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 34 to 35 in a single passage. He explains His saying, "And We did not grant immortality to any human before you. But if you die, then they are the eternal." He says: "And We did not immortalize any of the children of Adam, O Muhammad, before you in this world, so We will immortalize you therein. And you must die as our messengers died before you." "If you die, then they are the eternals." He means: So these polytheists with their Lord are the eternal ones in this world after you? No, that is not so; rather, they are dead in any case, whether I live or die. So I inserted the fa in 'inna', which is a consequence, and in its answer because the consequence is connected to the words before it. I also included in the phrase 'fahm' because it is an answer to the consequence. Even if the fa in his saying 'fahm' were not present, it would be permissible in two ways: one is that it is omitted, which is intended, and the other is intended to be preceded before the consequence, as if he said: 'The eternal will understand if it dies.' And His saying: 'Every soul tastes death,' Allah says: Every soul is breathed from His creation, processing the sting of death and drinking its cup. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, and Ibn Zayd.
