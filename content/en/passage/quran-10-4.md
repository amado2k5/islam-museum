**Commentary on verse 4 (pp. 20-22)**

He then explains "To Him is your return, all of you; the promise of God is true. He begins creation, then repeats it, that He may reward those who believe and do righteous deeds with justice; and for those who disbelieve there will be a drink of boiling water and a painful punishment for their disbelief". To your Lord, described in the previous verse, is your return, people, all of you on the Day of Resurrection; "the promise of God is true" is an accusative verbal noun because "your return" carries the sense of a promise, that is, God promises that He will bring you to life after your death, a true promise. "He begins creation, then repeats it" means He begins originating creation and then repeats it, bringing it back to life in the form it had when He began it, after its perishing and decay. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 20}

He gives report 17548, from Mujahid: He gives it life and then makes it die, and Abu Ja'far (al-Tabari) adds that he thinks he said "then He gives it life"; report 17549, from Mujahid: life, then death, then life; and reports 17550 and 17551 to the same effect: life, death, then He begins it again and gives it life. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 21}

The readers of the main cities read "innahu yabda'u" with a kasra on the particle, as a fresh sentence; it is reported of Abu Ja'far al-Razi that he read it with a fatha, as if meaning "truly that He begins creation, then repeats it", so that the particle takes a nominative role. He cites a line by a poet whom the editor could not identify (described, not quoted), and the editor sets out the manuscript reading against the printed distortion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 21}

On "that He may reward those who believe and do righteous deeds with justice": He repeats it after death in its form before death when it is raised from its grave, so that He rewards those who believed God and His messenger, did what God commanded and avoided what He forbade, for their good deeds; "with justice" means He rewards the good of what they did in the world with the good of reward and the righteous of recompense in the next, and that is justice, which is fairness and equity. The editor refers to the glosses on "recompense" and "justice". Report 17552, from Mujahid, then begins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 21}

Report 17552, from Mujahid: "with justice" means with equity. Then "for those who disbelieve there will be a drink of boiling water": God begins a fresh statement about what He has prepared for the unbelievers, though the sense joins it to what precedes, since He has stated that the return of all, unbelievers and believers alike, is to Him, and then that their return is so that each group is repaid for what it did, the doer of good with good and the doer of evil with evil; because what came earlier shows the hearer the intent, the statement is begun afresh while the sense is a joining. Those who denied God and His messenger and called His signs lies will have in hell a drink of boiling water, water heated and fiercely hot, so that, as is reported from the Prophet, the scalp falls off the head of one of them when it is brought near, as God also describes it, "like molten metal, it scalds the faces". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 22}

The word "hamim" derives from "mahmum", a form turned from one pattern to another, meaning heated, and the Arabs call anything heated "hamim"; he cites a line by al-Muraqqish that speaks of heated water and of incense wood made ready (described, not quoted), and the editor refers to the gloss on "hamim" and to the tracing of the line in the eleventh volume, where its wording differs. "A painful punishment" means that besides the drink of boiling water they have a punishing torment for their disbelief in God and His messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 22}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
