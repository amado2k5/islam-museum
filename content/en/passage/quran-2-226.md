## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari takes the word for swearing off in the verse ([[passage/quran-2-226|2:226]]) to mean that men swear an oath, the Arabic word for oath being the same root; from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: they swear (4478); one says a man swore off, and he cites a line of verse and a line of rajaz, which the editor could not find in any source nor identify the speaker. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 456} Waiting means watching and holding back; the sense for him is that those who swear off have a respite of four months to keep apart from their wives, the words "to keep apart" being left out as the sentence implies them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 456} The interpreters differed over the kind of oath that makes a man one who has sworn off from his wife. Some said it is that he swears in a state of anger, to harm her, that he will not have intercourse with her; if he swears with no intent to harm and not in anger, he has not sworn off. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457} From Umm 'Atiyya: Jubayr told her to nurse his nephew along with her son; she said she could not nurse two, so he swore not to approach her until she weaned him; when she weaned him, the people said this is swearing off, so he went to 'Ali and asked for a ruling, who said: if you did that in anger your wife is not lawful for you, and otherwise she is your wife (4479). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457} The editor sets out the differing reports on the narrator's name and refers to al-Bukhari, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457}

To finish the first view on [[passage/quran-2-226|2:226]], that swearing off arises only in anger and with intent to harm: Simak reported from 'Atiyya ibn Jubayr that a kinswoman of his was nursing a child, so he swore not to approach his wife until she weaned it; when four months passed, he was told she had become separate from him, and he went to 'Ali, who said: if you said that in anger you have no wife, and otherwise she is your wife (4480, 4481). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 458} In another report, Abu 'Atiyya's brother died leaving a small son and he told his wife to nurse him; she said she feared he would harm both by getting her with child, so he swore not to approach her until she weaned them, and 'Ali told him, you meant good, and swearing off is only in anger (4482, 4483, 4484, 4485), and in some versions 'Ali had him swear by God that he had not meant swearing off and then returned her to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 458} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 459} The editor explains the word for intercourse with a nursing wife and sets out the differences between the transmitters' names in al-Bukhari, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 458} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 459} From Ibn 'Abbas: there is no swearing off except in anger (4486, 4487, 4488), and from 'Ali the same (4489). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 459} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 460} From 'Ali, if a man says to his nursing wife, by God I shall not approach you until you wean my child, meaning his child's wellbeing, there is no swearing off on him (4490, 4491), and swearing off is what is sworn in anger, while what is for the good of nursing and the like is not swearing off and she does not become separate (4492). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 460} From al-Hasan (4493) and 'Ata': if he swears for the sake of nursing it is not swearing off (4494). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 460} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 461} From Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, who knew of swearing off only by an oath by God in what a man wants to harm his wife by keeping apart from her, and who did not think that one who swore on a matter in which he seeks the good bears what falls on one who swears off in anger (4495). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 461}

Others said it is the same whether the man swears in anger or not not to have intercourse with his wife; all of it is swearing off. From Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, about a man who told his wife "if I come to you before you wean your child you are divorced" and left her four months: it is swearing off (4496); and from him, anything that bars him from coming to her, if he leaves her until four months pass, applies to him (4497). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 461} From al-Hasan, asked about a man whose wife nursed a child and who swore not to have intercourse with her until she weaned it: I do not see this as anger, and swearing off is in anger; and Ibn Sirin said, I do not know what they are reporting, the rule being that when four months have passed he may propose to her again if he wishes (4498). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 461} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 462} From al-Nakha'i (4499, 4500) and al-Sha'bi (4501, 4502): every oath that bars intercourse until four months pass is swearing off. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 462}

Others said every oath a man swears to the harm of his wife is swearing off, whether on intercourse or something else, in contentment or anger. From al-Sha'bi: every oath that comes between a man and his wife is swearing off, such as "by God I shall anger you, by God I shall wrong you, by God I shall beat you" (4503). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 462} From al-Qasim and Salim, about a man who told his wife "if I speak to you for a year you are divorced": she is divorced once four months pass (4504). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 463} From Ibrahim (4505), and from al-Hakam: "by God I shall enrage you" and leaving her four months is swearing off (4506, 4507). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 463} From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, if a man swears not to speak to his wife for a day or a month, that is swearing off, unless he touches her; and returning means going back to her, speaking to her or touching her; whoever does so before four months pass has returned, and whoever returns after four months while she is in her waiting period has returned and keeps his wife, though one divorce has passed on her (4508). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 463}

The arguments for the views on which oath makes swearing off, as al-Tabari presents them: those who said swearing off is only in anger and harm argue that God set the term as a way out for the wife from her husband's obstruction and harming her, given what she is owed of good companionship; so when he is not harming her by his oath but seeking her contentment, he has not sworn off, as a term to free her from a hurt that did not touch her would be meaningless. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 464} Those who said they are the same argue from the generality of the verse and that God did not single out some who swear; so everyone who swears not to come to his wife for longer than the term is one who has sworn off according to some, and according to others he has, even if the oath lasts exactly the term. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 464} The view of al-Sha'bi, al-Qasim and Salim holds that the term frees the wife from her husband's bad treatment and harm, and an oath to forgo intercourse is no likelier to be that than an oath not to speak to her, to wrong her or to enrage her, since all of it harms her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 464} Al-Tabari's choice: the soundest view for him is that of those who said every oath that bars the swearer from intercourse for longer than the period God set for the one who swears off to wait, whether in anger or contentment, for the reason he gave for this view; he says he refuted the contrary view in his book "al-Latif" and disliked repeating it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 465} This is al-Tabari's own choice.

On the next clause of the verse ([[passage/quran-2-226|2:226]]), al-Tabari says it means: if they return to leaving what they swore, that is, to forgoing intercourse, and have intercourse and break their oaths, then God is forgiving of them for their oaths and what they did before to their wives, and merciful to them and other believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 465} The root sense of the word for returning is going back from one state to another; he cites the verse in al-Hujurat ([[passage/quran-49-9|49:9]]) and a line by Suhaym, slave of the Banu al-Hashas, whose reading and meaning the editor explains; one says a man returned, and the shade returned, because in all uses the word means going back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 465} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 466}

Al-Tabari says the interpreters said likewise but differed over what makes the one who swore off one who has returned. Some said he returns only by intercourse. From Ibn 'Abbas: returning is intercourse (4509 to 4512); from Masruq (4513, 4514), al-Sha'bi (4515, 4516) and Sa'id ibn Jubayr (4517, 4518), and from Sa'id: he has no excuse unless he has intercourse, even in prison or travelling (4519, 4520); and from al-Sha'bi and Masruq (4521). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 466} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 467} From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, on one who swore off and was then kept by illness: no excuse for him until he has intercourse (4522); from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, that when four months pass she is more entitled to herself (4523); and from al-Hakam and al-Sha'bi (4524). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 468}

Others said returning is going back by tongue or heart when there is an excuse, and by intercourse when there is none. From al-Hasan and 'Ikrima: if he has an excuse and calls witnesses, that is allowed (4525); from al-Nakha'i, if he has an excuse and calls witnesses, he has returned, while al-Hakam said no excuse until he has intercourse, so they went to Abu Wa'il, who said he hoped it valid if he had an excuse and called witnesses (4526). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 468} From al-Hasan and al-Zuhri, if one swore off then was ill, imprisoned or travelling and went back, he has an excuse not to have intercourse (4527); and from the companions of 'Abdallah on a woman in childbed: if he cannot, he expiates his oath and calls witnesses to the return (4528). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 469} From 'Alqama, to one who swore off and whose wife then gave birth so that he could not approach her: have you not returned in your heart and agreed? He said yes; then you have returned, she is your wife (4529, 4530). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 469} The editor gives biographies of narrators and explains the word for childbed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 466} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 467} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 468} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 469}
