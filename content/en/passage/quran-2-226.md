## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari takes the word for swearing off in the verse ([[passage/quran-2-226|2:226]]) to mean that men swear an oath, the Arabic word for oath being the same root; from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: they swear (4478); one says a man swore off, and he cites a line of verse and a line of rajaz, which the editor could not find in any source nor identify the speaker. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 456} Waiting means watching and holding back; the sense for him is that those who swear off have a respite of four months to keep apart from their wives, the words "to keep apart" being left out as the sentence implies them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 456} The interpreters differed over the kind of oath that makes a man one who has sworn off from his wife. Some said it is that he swears in a state of anger, to harm her, that he will not have intercourse with her; if he swears with no intent to harm and not in anger, he has not sworn off. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457} From Umm 'Atiyya: Jubayr told her to nurse his nephew along with her son; she said she could not nurse two, so he swore not to approach her until she weaned him; when she weaned him, the people said this is swearing off, so he went to 'Ali and asked for a ruling, who said: if you did that in anger your wife is not lawful for you, and otherwise she is your wife (4479). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457} The editor sets out the differing reports on the narrator's name and refers to al-Bukhari, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 457}
