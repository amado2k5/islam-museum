**Commentary on verse 97 (pp. 429-430)**

Al-Tabari says: the Bedouin are firmer in denial of God's oneness and firmer in hypocrisy than the townspeople in the villages and cities, and the Almighty described them so only because of their coarseness, the hardness of their hearts and their little witnessing of people of goodness, which is why they are harder of heart and less knowing of God's rights. As for "and more apt not to know the limits of what God has sent down on His Messenger", it means worthier not to know them, and by that, as Qatada said, the practices. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of God's limits. Report 17092, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they are less knowing of the practices. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 429}

Report 17093, from Ibrahim, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra' and al-A'mash: a Bedouin sat with Zayd ibn Sawhan while he was talking to his companions, his hand having been lost on the day of Nahawand, and said "by God, your talk pleases me, but your hand makes me doubt", and Zayd said "what doubt does my hand give you? It is the left", and the Bedouin said "by God, I do not know whether they cut off the right or the left", and Zayd ibn Sawhan said "God spoke the truth: the Bedouin are firmer in unbelief and hypocrisy and more apt not to know the limits of what God sent down on His Messenger". The editor notes that Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra' al-Dawsi is reliable though spoken against, that Zayd ibn Sawhan al-Abdi met the Prophet and is reliable with few reports, and that Ibn Sa'd relates the story in the Tabaqat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 429}

As for "and God is knowing, wise", it means God knows who knows the limits of what He sent down on His Messenger, and the hypocrite among His creatures and the unbeliever, none of them hidden from Him, and wise in His management of them and in His forbearance in not punishing them, though He knows their secrets and their deception of His friends. The editor refers to earlier explanations of knowing and wisdom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 430}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
