**Commentary on verse 35 (pp. 469-472)**

On "the likeness of the Garden promised to the God-fearing: beneath it rivers flow; its fruit is constant, and its shade. That is the outcome of those who were mindful, and the outcome of the disbelievers is the Fire", al-Tabari says: the scholars of Arab speech differed over what puts "mathal" in the nominative. One of the Kufan grammarians said: what governs "mathal" in meaning is "beneath it rivers flow", and said it is as you say "the description of so-and-so is tawny, such and such", where "tawny" is not governed by the description but is a beginning, meaning "he is tawny, he is such and such"; he said that were "anna" to enter in the like of this it would be right, and the like of it in speech is "your likeness, that you are so and that you are so", and also "let man look at his food: that We poured water" [[passage/quran-80-24|80:24-25]]; whoever took "the likeness of the Garden promised to the God-fearing" in the verse, and whoever said "anna sababna al-ma'" put the noun in the open, since it goes back to "food" in the genitive and is a new start, that is, his food is that We poured, then We did. He said the meaning of "the likeness of the Garden" is the attributes of the Garden. One of the Basran grammarians said its meaning is the description of the Garden, and said from it God's saying "and His is the highest likeness" [[passage/quran-30-27|30:27]], meaning to God belongs the highest description; so the sense of the speech is: the description of the Garden promised to the God-fearing is a description of a garden beneath which rivers flow or in which are rivers, God knows best. The editor notes that the wording is obscure and that he thinks the correct form is "the description of the Garden promised to the God-fearing is the description of a garden beneath which rivers flow or in which are rivers". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 469}

He said, and another view: it is as if when "the likeness of the Garden" is said it is said "the Garden promised to the God-fearing". He said so too His saying "and it is in the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate" [[passage/quran-27-30|27:30]], as if he said "by God, the Merciful, the Compassionate"; and His saying "for what I neglected in God's side" [[passage/quran-39-56|39:56]], meaning in God's being, as if it were said, in our view, "in God". He said so too His saying "nothing is like Him" [[passage/quran-42-11|42:11]], the meaning being "nothing is like anything" and nothing is His like, since He has no like; he said this is not like your saying to a man "none is like you", since he might have a like, whereas this cannot apply to God. He said the like of it is Labid's saying in a verse "until a year, then the name of peace upon you both", which, he said, was explained to us as meaning "peace upon you both". Aws ibn Hajar said in a verse describing noble slain men "like trunks, a pouring rain covered them"; he said the meaning to us is "as trunks", because he did not mean to make trunks a likeness and then liken the slain to it. He said the like of it is the saying of Umayya in a verse describing Saturn and a bull "under the foot of his right" with the eagle for the other and a lion lying in wait, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 470}

he saying "under the right foot", as if he said "under his foot" or "under his right foot". He said and Labid's saying in a verse about the wild bull that lost his herd and was visited by rainy nights, "their command is in the hand of the left", as if he said "their command is by the left and toward the left", and Labid's saying also "until when she cast a hand into the concealer", as if he said "until she fell into the concealer". Another of them said it is from the one withheld from his predicate, and he said the Arabs do that. He said it has another sense, "for those who responded to their Lord is the best", the likeness of the Garden being joined as a description of it to the first speech. The editor notes in the footnotes the sources of the verses, that this is the statement of Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an, and that "the concealer" here is the dark night, which covers what it contains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 471}

Al-Tabari says: the best of the views is that the "likeness" is mentioned, "the likeness of the Garden", and what is meant is the Garden, which is then described with its description, since its likeness is only its description, and its description is nothing other than it; that being so, then "the likeness" is mentioned, "the likeness of the Garden", its likeness being its description and the Garden's description, its description being like the description of the "likeness", and the speech is as if it ran with the mention of the Garden, so that it is said "the Garden, beneath which rivers flow", as Jarir said in a verse in which he sees the passing years having taken from him as the last night of the month takes from the crescent, mentioning the "passing" and then returning in the report to the "years". On "its fruit is constant, and its shade" he means what is eaten in it, saying that it is constant for its people, not cut off from them and not ceasing or perishing, but lasting without end; "and its shade", he says, its shade too is constant, since there is no sun in it. "That is the outcome of those who were mindful", he says, this Garden He, mighty be His praise, described is the outcome of those who were mindful of God, avoided His disobedience and performed His obligations. And "the outcome of the disbelievers is the Fire", he says, the outcome of those who disbelieve in God is the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 472}
