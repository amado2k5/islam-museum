**Commentary on verse 101 (pp. 7-10)**

Al-Tabari says that "those townships" are the ones whose story and people were told before this point: the people of Noah, Ad, Thamud, the people of Lot and Shu'ayb. "We relate some of their tidings" means that God informs His prophet about their people and the messengers sent to them, so that he knows that God gives victory to His messengers and the believers over their enemies in this world, and so that those who deny him among his people learn the end of those who denied the messengers, turn back, and return to God's oneness and obedience. The "clear proofs" are the plain arguments. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "narrative", "tiding" and "clear proofs". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 7}

He then says that the interpreters differed on "they could not believe because they had denied before". One group said: these idolaters were not going to believe, when the messengers were sent to them, in what they had disliked before, namely on the day God took their covenant when He brought them out of Adam's back. Report 14901 is from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: that was the day the covenant was taken from them, and they believed unwillingly. In a footnote the editor records that the printed text reads "in what they denied before that", and the manuscript reads differently; he judged the printed reading to be right because the report says "they believed unwillingly". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 7}

Others said the meaning is that they were not going to believe, when the messengers came, in what God's knowledge had already determined they would deny on the day He brought them out of Adam's loins. Report 14902 is from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Abu Ja'far, al-Rabi' and Abu al-Aliya: it was in His knowledge on the day they acknowledged the covenant. Report 14903 is from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far from his father: it is right for servants to take of knowledge what their Lord and the prophets have disclosed and to leave the knowledge God has hidden, since His knowledge extends to what was and what will be; His knowledge took effect in them when He created them in Adam's time, so that He knew the obedient from the disobedient. Al-Rabi' cites further verses: what was said to Noah ([[passage/quran-11-48|11:48]]), that if returned they would go back to what they were forbidden ([[passage/quran-6-28|6:28]]), that God does not punish until He sends a messenger ([[passage/quran-17-15|17:15]]), and that people should have no argument against God after the messengers ([[passage/quran-4-165|4:165]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 8}

Others said the meaning is that if they were brought back to life after their destruction, having seen God's punishment, they would still not believe in what they had denied before their destruction, as in "if they were sent back they would return to what they were forbidden". Report 14904 is from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 9}

Al-Tabari prefers the view reported from Ubayy ibn Ka'b and al-Rabi', because whoever God's knowledge has already marked as not believing will never believe, and God's prior knowledge was that the destroyed nations whose tidings are told in this sura would never believe; so He reports that they would not believe in what they were denying in His foreknowledge, before the messengers came and when they came. He adds that if one said the verse concerns the idolaters of the Prophet's people, who inherited the land after Ad and Thamud, that would be a possible direction, but he knows no reliable scholar of interpretation who said it. As for Mujahid's view, nothing in the apparent wording of the revelation or in a sound report from the Messenger points to it, so the better view is the one for which the wording supplies evidence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 9}

On "thus does God seal the hearts of the unbelievers" he says: as God sealed the hearts of those who disbelieved their Lord and disobeyed His messengers among the nations whose stories were told, until His punishment came and they perished, so He seals the hearts of the unbelievers among the Prophet's people who are decreed never to believe. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "sealing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 10}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
