## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "for each there is a direction which he faces" al-Tabari says "for each" means for each people of a creed, the "people of a creed" being omitted since the speech shows it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 192} He reports from Mujahid and 'Ata' that it means each holder of a creed, from al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd that the Jew has a direction and the Christian has a direction and God guided this community to the qibla that is the qibla, from Ibn 'Abbas that each people of a religion has a qibla they are content with, and God's face is wherever the believers turn, and from al-Suddi that each people has a qibla they have turned to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 192} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 193} Others, from Qatada, said it is their prayer toward Jerusalem and their prayer toward the Ka'ba, with the meaning that for each direction to which your Lord turned you, Muhammad, there is a qibla toward which God turns His servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 193} "Wijha" is a verbal noun like "qa'da" from "tawajjuh", meaning the point one faces in prayer; from Mujahid, a qibla. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 193}

He reports from al-Rabi' that "wijha" means a face, from Ibn Zayd a qibla, and from Mansur that they read it "for each We made a qibla they are content with". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 194} On "he who turns to it" he says it means he turns his face to it and faces it, from Mujahid, "turning" here meaning approaching; so the sense is for each people of a creed there is a direction, all of whom turn their faces to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 194} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 195} He notes that a reading "huwa muwallaha" is reported from Ibn 'Abbas and others, meaning he is directed toward it, and that some are reported to have read "wa li-kulli wijhatin" without nunation and with the genitive, which he calls a solecism not permitted since the statement would be incomplete. He chooses "wa li-kullin wijhatun huwa muwalliha" because the authoritative readers agree on it and those who differ are irregular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 195}

On "so race to the good" he says it means hasten and hurry, from al-Rabi'; the meaning is that the truth has been made clear to you, believers, and you were guided to the qibla from which the Jews, Christians and the other communities strayed, so hasten to good deeds in gratitude to your Lord and take provision for your Hereafter, and keep to your qibla so you do not lose it as the nations before you lost it. From Qatada, do not be overcome in your qibla, and from Ibn Zayd the good things are righteous deeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 196}

On "wherever you are, God will bring you all together; God has power over all things" he says: in whatever place you perish, God will bring you all together on the Day of Resurrection, from al-Rabi' and al-Suddi; God urges the believers by this verse to obey Him and take provision in this world for the next, and He brings them and those who differed from their qibla together on the Day of Resurrection so that the doer of good receives his repayment and the evildoer his punishment, or He is gracious and pardons. "Has power" means He can gather them from their graves and do whatever else He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 197}
