## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We said: enter this town" al-Tabari says the town is Bayt al-Maqdis, reporting this from Qatada, al-Suddi and al-Rabi', and from Ibn Zayd that it is Jericho, near Bayt al-Maqdis. "Eat from it wherever you wish, abundantly" means an easy, ample life without reckoning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 102} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 103}
On "enter the gate prostrating" it is said the gate is the Gate of Hitta of Bayt al-Maqdis, from Mujahid, al-Suddi and Ibn Abbas, and from Ibn Abbas that "sujjadan" means bowing, that they were told to enter bowing through a small gate. Al-Tabari explains that "sujud" originally means bending in reverence, so anyone who bends to something in veneration is "sajid", citing verses of Zayd al-Khayl and al-A'sha, and so Ibn Abbas's reading holds, since one who bows is bent, though the one who prostrates is bent more. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 103} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 104} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 105}
On "and say: hitta" he holds that "hitta" is a form from "God lowered (hatta) your sins from you", and then records the differences: al-Hasan, Qatada, Ibn Zayd, Ibn Abbas, al-Rabi' and Ata say it means that God remove their sins, and Ibn Abbas also says it means forgiveness; Ikrima that it means "say: there is no god but God"; and Ibn Abbas by another chain that they were told to ask forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 105} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 106}

Al-Tabari adds a further view from Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak that "say hitta" means say: this matter is true as it has been told to you. He then sets out the grammarians' differences over the nominative "hitta": the Basrans say it is nominative in the sense of "say: let there be from You a lowering of our sins", or that it is a word they were told to say in the nominative; the Kufans say there is an understood "this is hitta" or "what is hitta". His own choice is that it is nominative as the predicate of an omitted subject, meaning "our entering the gate prostrating is hitta", which is close to the interpretation of al-Rabi', Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Zayd. He holds that on the readings of Ikrima, al-Hasan and Qatada the accusative would have been required, and that the reciters' agreement on the nominative shows Ikrima's reading to be wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 107} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 108} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 109}
On "We shall forgive you" he says the meaning is We shall cover your sins with mercy and not expose you to punishment for them, the root "ghafr" meaning covering and concealing, whence "mighfar", the helmet that covers the head; he cites Aws ibn Hajar. "Khataya" is the plural of "khati'a", from "khati'a" when a man turns off the way of truth, and he cites a line of Umayya ibn al-Askar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 109} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 110}
On "and We shall increase the good-doers" he reports Ibn Abbas: whoever does good among you will be given more, and whoever erred will have his error forgiven. Al-Tabari sums up the verse, then says God reports their great ignorance and poor obedience to rebuke their descendants who are addressed by these verses and to warn that if they persist in denying Muhammad they will be like their forebears. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 111}
