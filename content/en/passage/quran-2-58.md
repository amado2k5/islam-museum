## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We said: enter this town" al-Tabari says the town is Bayt al-Maqdis, reporting this from Qatada, al-Suddi and al-Rabi', and from Ibn Zayd that it is Jericho, near Bayt al-Maqdis. "Eat from it wherever you wish, abundantly" means an easy, ample life without reckoning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 102} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 103}
On "enter the gate prostrating" it is said the gate is the Gate of Hitta of Bayt al-Maqdis, from Mujahid, al-Suddi and Ibn Abbas, and from Ibn Abbas that "sujjadan" means bowing, that they were told to enter bowing through a small gate. Al-Tabari explains that "sujud" originally means bending in reverence, so anyone who bends to something in veneration is "sajid", citing verses of Zayd al-Khayl and al-A'sha, and so Ibn Abbas's reading holds, since one who bows is bent, though the one who prostrates is bent more. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 103} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 104} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 105}
On "and say: hitta" he holds that "hitta" is a form from "God lowered (hatta) your sins from you", and then records the differences: al-Hasan, Qatada, Ibn Zayd, Ibn Abbas, al-Rabi' and Ata say it means that God remove their sins, and Ibn Abbas also says it means forgiveness; Ikrima that it means "say: there is no god but God"; and Ibn Abbas by another chain that they were told to ask forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 105} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 106}
