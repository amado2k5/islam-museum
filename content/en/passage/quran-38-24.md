## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "He said, 'He has wronged you by asking your ewe to his ewes. Indeed, many of the mixed people oppress one another, except those who have believed and done righteous deeds, but they are few.' And David thought that We had tried him, so he sought forgiveness from his Lord, and he fell down bowing and repenting." David said to the opponent who was grieving against his companion: Your companion wronged you by asking your ewe to his ewes, and this is among the things from which the 'h' was omitted, so the 'h' was added to the object by dropping the 'ha' from it. Similarly, His saying, the Almighty: "A person never tires of praying for good," meaning from his supplication for good. When the 'ha' is removed from supplication, it is added to goodness, and the 'b' is dropped from goodness. Here, the ewe is used as a metaphor for a woman. The Arabs do this, as al-A'sha said: 'I was her leader and a cautious sheep whose neglect is rarely noticed.' It means the sheep is a man who warns people about her. It meant: 'You wronged by asking your one wife to ninety-nine of his wives.' In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Al-Hasan, and Ibn Zayd.
