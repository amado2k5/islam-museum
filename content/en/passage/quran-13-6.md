**Commentary on verse 6 (pp. 350-352)**

On "they urge you to hasten the evil before the good, though exemplary punishments have passed before them; your Lord is full of forgiveness for people despite their wrongdoing, and your Lord is severe in punishment", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: "they urge you to hasten", Muhammad, the polytheists of your people, calamity and punishment before ease and well-being, saying, "O God, if this is the truth from You, rain stones on us from the sky or bring us a painful punishment" [[passage/quran-8-32|8:32]], although they know what befell those of the nations before them who disobeyed their Lord and denied their messengers, of God's punishments and great trial: one nation turned into apes, another into swine, one destroyed by the quake and another by the swallowing of the earth. That is "al-mathulat" of which God, mighty be His praise, said "exemplary punishments have passed before them". "Al-mathulat" are the deterrent punishments, the singular being "mathula" with a fatha on the mim and a damma on the tha', pluralised "mathulat", as the singular of "sadaqat" is "sadaqa" and it is pluralised "sadaqat". It is said that Tamim, among the Arabs, put a damma on both the mim and the tha' in "al-muthulat", {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 350}

so the singular in their dialect is "muthla" and the plural "muthulat", like "ghurfa" and "ghurufat". The verb is "mathaltu bihi amthulu mathlan", with a fatha on the mim and a sukun on the tha'. When you mean that you gave him retribution against another you say "amthaltuhu min sahibihi amthiluhu imthalan". The editor notes that "sadaqa" is a woman's dower and was dealt with in Sura al-Nisa'. The interpreters said the same: report 20130, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "exemplary punishments have passed before them", God's visitations on the nations among those before you; and "they urge you to hasten the evil before the good", being the polytheists of the Arabs who hastened evil before good and said "O God, if this is the truth from You", the verse. Report 20131, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "they urge you to hasten the evil before the good", he said, punishment before well-being, "exemplary punishments have passed before them", he said, the punishments. Reports 20132 to 20134, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; and Ishaq and Abd Allah: "al-mathulat", he said, the likenesses. Report 20135, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 351}

"exemplary punishments have passed before them", he said, "al-mathulat" are those with which God made an example of the nations, of the punishment He inflicted on them; the exemplary punishments came to pass before them, they knew it and what God made an example of in them when they disobeyed Him and His messengers reached them. The editor notes what he corrected in the printed wording. Report 20136, from al-Sha'bi, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Salim: the apes and the swine are "al-mathulat". On "your Lord is full of forgiveness for people despite their wrongdoing" he says, mighty be His praise: your Lord, Muhammad, veils the sins of whoever of the people repents of his sins, leaving off exposing him for them at the standing of the Resurrection and pardoning him the punishment for them in this world and the next, "despite their wrongdoing", that is, despite their doing what they did without my permission for them to do it, "and your Lord is severe in punishment" toward whoever perishes persisting in his disobedience on the Day of Resurrection, if that is not hastened for him in this world, or if He joins them for him in this world and the next. Al-Tabari says: though the apparent form of this speech is a report, it is a warning and threat from God to the polytheists of the Messenger's people, if they do not turn back and repent of their disbelief before God's vengeance comes upon them. Report 20137, from Ibn Abbas, through Ali ibn Dawud, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha: "your Lord is full of forgiveness for people", he says, but your Lord; the editor says the end of the report is missing from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 352}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
