**Commentary on verse 24: "they said: Moses, we shall never enter it", pp. 184-187**

Al-Tabari says this is a report of the assembly of Moses' people answering him when they were urged to fight their enemy and promised God's help if they rose against them and entered by their city gate: they said they would never enter their city.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 184}

He explains that "it" refers to the city, that "never" means all the days of their lives, and that "as long as they are in it" means as long as the giants remain in the city God had written for them; and that "go, you and your Lord, and fight" means they will not come with Moses if he goes to fight them, but leave him to go alone with his Lord. He mentions a view that the sense is "go, and may your Lord help you", since going cannot be said of God, which the editor traces to Abu 'Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an; al-Tabari holds that looking for a way out for their words would be needed only if they were believers, but for a people in opposition to God and His messenger there is no reason to seek one, save what fits their disbelief and error.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 185}

He then notes that al-Muqdad ibn al-Aswad told the Prophet the opposite of what Moses' people said. Report 11682, from Tariq, has al-Muqdad saying we will not say as the Children of Israel said, but go, you and your Lord, and fight, we are fighting with you. The editor gives notes on Mukhariq and Tariq ibn Shihab, traces the report to al-Bukhari's Sahih and Ahmad's Musnad, and says it belongs to the Prophet's consultation of his companions before Badr.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 185}

Report 11683, from Qatada, says it was told that on the day of al-Hudaybiya, when the polytheists blocked the sacrificial animals, the Prophet told his companions he would take the animals and slaughter them at the House; al-Muqdad said they would not be like the assembly of the Children of Israel, but would say go, you and your Lord, and fight, we are fighting with you, and when the companions heard it they followed. The editor notes the manuscript copied this report twice.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 186}

Ibn 'Abbas, al-Dahhak and others said the people said this to Moses once the giants and their might were clear to them. Report 11684, from al-Dahhak, says God told the Children of Israel to go to the Holy Land with Moses; near the city he told them to enter, they refused and lost heart and sent twelve chiefs to look, who came back with a single fruit from theirs, a man's load, saying: judge the strength of a people whose fruit this is; then they said to Moses, go, you and your Lord. Report 11685, from Ibn 'Abbas, is similar.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 187}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
