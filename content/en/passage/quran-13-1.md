**Commentary on verse 1 (pp. 319-322)**

On "Alif Lam Mim Ra. Those are the verses of the Book; and what has been sent down to you from your Lord is the truth, but most people do not believe", al-Tabari says: we have already explained, earlier and sufficiently, the sense of "Alif Lam Ra", "Alif Lam Mim Ra" and their like among the letters of the alphabet with which some Qur'anic suras open, and need not repeat it, but we mention the reports that came specifically for each sura that begins with some of them. What came in this sura from Ibn Abbas, by the transmission of Abu al-Duha Muslim ibn Subayh and Sa'id ibn Jubayr from him, distinguishes the sense of what this sura opens with, with the mim it adds over the other suras with "Alif Lam Ra", from the sense of what its sisters open with, lacking that. The editor notes what he corrected in the wording of the printed and manuscript text here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 319}

Report 20044, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-Rahman, Hushaym, Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "Alif Lam Mim Ra", he said, I am God, I see. Report 20045, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sharik, Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib and Abu al-Duha: "Alif Lam Mim Ra", he said, I am God, I see. Report 20046, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym al-Fadl ibn Dukayn and Sufyan: "Alif Lam Mim Ra" are openings with which He begins His speech. On "those are the verses of the Book" he says: those whose story I told you are the verses of the Book which I sent down before this Book that I sent down to you, to those of My messengers to whom I sent it before you. It is also said that the Torah and the Gospel are meant: report 20047, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "those are the verses of the Book", the books that were before the Qur'an. Report 20048, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym and Sufyan: "those are the verses of the Book", he said, the Torah and the Gospel. On "and what has been sent down to you from your Lord is the truth" it is the Qur'an, so act on what is in it and hold fast to it. The editor says that the added word "the Qur'an" in brackets is required, as the reports that follow show. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 320}

The interpreters said the same: report 20049, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym and Sufyan: "what has been sent down to you from your Lord is the truth", he said, the Qur'an. Report 20050, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: that is, this Qur'an. In "and what has been sent down to you" there are two grammatical readings: one is the nominative, as a fresh sentence, so that "the truth" is nominative; on this reading is the interpretation of Mujahid and Qatada we gave earlier. The other is the genitive, by conjunction with "the Book", so the meaning is: those are the verses of the Torah, the Gospel and the Qur'an, and then "the truth" begins a new sentence meaning "that is the truth", its nominative being due to an implied element for which the apparent text suffices. It would also be a possible interpretation to say the sense is: those are the verses of the Book which was sent down to you from your Lord, the truth, with the waw entered in "and what" although it is an attribute of the Book, as a poet entered it in a verse praising a king, the son of the lord, the lion of the battalion in the crush, joining with waw though all of it describes one person. The editor refers to an earlier place for the verse and notes that "lion" is accusative of praise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 321}

But if it is so interpreted, the correct reading of "the truth" is the genitive, as an attribute of "alladhi". On "but most people do not believe" he says: most of the polytheists of your people do not confirm the truth that has been sent down to you from your Lord, nor do they acknowledge this Qur'an and its firm verses. The editor notes that the wording of the verse dropped out of the printed text and the manuscript and that he supplied it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 322}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
