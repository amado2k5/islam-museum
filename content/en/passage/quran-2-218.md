## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "those who believed, those who emigrated and strove in God's way, those hope for God's mercy, and God is forgiving, merciful": al-Tabari means, those who believed in God and His Messenger and what he brought, and "those who emigrated" who left dwelling with the polytheists in their cities and living beside them in their lands, moving from them to others in hijra; the editor points to a gap in the original text here and suggests how the sentence ran. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 317} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 318} The root of "muhajara" is from a man's abandoning another out of enmity between them, then it came to be used of anyone who leaves something for a thing he dislikes in it; the Emigrants of the Messenger's Companions were so called for leaving their homes and dwellings out of dislike of living among the polytheists under their authority, where they were not safe from their persecution, for a place where they were. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 318} "And strove" means fought and made war; the root of "mujahada" is from "so-and-so exerted so-and-so", when he distressed and burdened him, and when the act is between two, each enduring hardship and strain from the other, it is said he "strives against" him; and "God's way" is His path and religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 318} So the meaning of the verse: those who left the authority of the people of polytheism in emigration and for fear of their persecution of their religion, and fought them in God's religion to bring them into it, those hope for God's mercy, that is they look to God to have mercy on them and admit them to His Garden by the grace of His mercy; "and God is forgiving" means covering His servants' sins by pardoning them, and gracious to them with mercy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 319} It is also reported that this verse was revealed about 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh and his companions; from Jundub ibn 'Abdallah: when what happened with 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh, his companions and Ibn al-Hadrami happened, some Muslims said, even if they incurred no sin on their journey, they have no reward in it; and God sent down the verse (4102), the editor noting it completes report 4084. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 319} From 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr: when what they had been in was cleared from 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh and his companions when the Qur'an came down, they hoped for reward and said, Messenger of God, may we hope this be a campaign for which we are given the reward of fighters? and God sent down about them the verse, which placed them on the greatest hope (4103). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 319} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 320} From Qatada: God praised the Companions of His Prophet most beautifully with this verse, these being the best of this community, then made them people of hope, as you hear, since one who hopes seeks and one who fears flees (4104); and from al-Rabi' the same (4105). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 320}
