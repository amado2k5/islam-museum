## Summary
Verse 15 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 15 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 15 (pp. 435-441)**

Al-Tabari says, on "you who believe, when you meet the unbelievers marching, do not turn your backs to them; whoever turns his back to them that day, except turning aside to fight or joining another company, has drawn on himself God's anger, and his refuge is Gehenna, a wretched end" [[passage/quran-8-15|8:15]] and [[passage/quran-8-16|8:16]]: you who trusted God and His Messenger, "when you meet the unbelievers" in fighting "marching" meaning advancing on one another, "tazahuf" being drawing near; "do not turn your backs to them" meaning do not give them your backs and be routed from them, but stand firm against them, for God is with you against them; "whoever turns his back to them that day" meaning whoever of you turns his back to them, "except turning aside to fight" meaning except drawing the enemy on to fight, seeking an opening he can strike and then charging back; "or joining another company" meaning except turning his back to join a company, going to the side of the believers who rally him with them to fight and return with him to the enemy. The editor notes that this explanation of "tazahuf" is not found in the dictionaries and should be recorded, and refers to the earlier explanations of "turning", "back" and "company". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 435}

The interpreters said the like, and report 15795, from al-Dahhak: "except turning aside to fight or joining another company", he said the one turning aside is one who goes ahead of his companions to see an opening in the enemy and strike it, and the one joining is one who flees to the Prophet and his companions, and so too one who flees today to his commander and his companions; al-Dahhak said this is a warning from God to the companions of Muhammad not to flee, the Prophet and his companions being their company. Report 15796, from al-Suddi: the one turning aside is one drawing the enemy on who means to return, and the one joining is one who joins the imam and his troops if he charges and has not the strength against them, and people are not excused, though many, for turning from the imam. The people of knowledge differed over the ruling of this verse, whether it is specific to the people of Badr or applies to the believers as a whole. Some said it is for the people of Badr specifically, since they were not to leave the Messenger of God with his enemy and flee from him, whereas today they may withdraw. Report 15797, from Abu Nadra: that was the day of Badr and they had nowhere to withdraw to, and had anyone withdrawn he would have withdrawn only to…, Abu Musa saying, meaning to the polytheists. Report 15798, from Dawud… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 435} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 436}

…from Dawud from Abu Nadra from Abu Sa'id on "whoever turns his back to them that day", then he gave the like, except that he said, had they withdrawn they would have withdrawn to the polytheists, there being that day no Muslim on earth but them. Report 15799, from Abu Sa'id: it was revealed on the day of Badr; report 15800, from Abu Sa'id: the day of Badr; report 15801, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri: that was only on the day of Badr, when the Muslims had no company but the Messenger of God; as for after that, the Muslims are a company one to another; report 15802, from Abu Nadra: this was revealed about the people of Badr; report 15803, from Ibn Awn: I wrote to Nafi asking whether that was that day or after, and he wrote to me, it was only the day of Badr. The editor identifies Abu Nadra, al-Mundhir ibn Malik al-Abdi, as trustworthy and says al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak, calling it sound on Muslim's condition, al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 436} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 437}
Report 15804, from al-Dahhak: the flight was only on the day of Badr, when they had no refuge to take refuge in, but today it is not flight; report 15805, from al-Hasan: this was for the day of Badr specifically, and fleeing from the advance is not among the great sins; reports 15806 and 15807, from al-Dahhak and al-Hasan: specific to the day of Badr and revealed about the people of Badr; report 15808, from Qatada: that was the day of Badr; report 15809, from al-Hasan: as for today, if he joins a company or a garrison town there is no harm; report 15810, from Nafi: this was only the day of Badr; report 15811, from Yazid ibn Abi Habib: God made the Fire due to whoever fled on the day of Badr, and when the day of Uhud came after that He said [[passage/quran-3-155|3:155]], and then at Hunayn seven years after that He said [[passage/quran-9-25|9:25]] and then [[passage/quran-9-27|9:27]]; report 15812, from Muhammad ibn Sirin: news of the killing of Abu Ubayd reached Umar and he said, had he joined me I would have been a company for him. The editor notes that Abu Ubayd ibn Mas'ud al-Thaqafi was the commander of the day of the bridge, whom Umar appointed over Iraq in the year 13, and he was killed; and that in some books the name is wrongly "Abu Ubayda". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 437} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 438} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 439}
Report 15813, from Ata ibn Abi Rabah: this is abrogated by the verse in al-Anfal [[passage/quran-8-66|8:66]], and a people are not to flee from twice their number, and that abrogated all but this number; report 15814, from Abu Uthman: when Abu Ubayd was killed the news came to Umar and he said, people, I am your company; report 15815, from Mujahid: Umar said, I am the company of every Muslim. Others said the verse rather has a general ruling for everyone who turns his back on the enemy in flight; report 15816, from Ibn Abbas: the greatest of the great sins is associating with God and fleeing from the advance, because God says "whoever turns his back to them that day has drawn on himself God's anger, and his refuge is Gehenna, a wretched end". The editor identifies Qays ibn Sa'd al-Makki as trustworthy and Abu Uthman as unknown, Ibn al-Madini saying only Sulayman al-Taymi narrated from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 439} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 440}
Al-Tabari chooses: the better of the two interpretations of this verse, in my view, is that of those who said its ruling is firm, that it was revealed about the people of Badr and that its ruling holds for all the believers, that God forbade the believers, when they meet the enemy, to turn their backs on them in flight except to turn aside to fight or to join a company of the believers wherever it is in the land of Islam, and that whoever turns his back on them after the advance in flight, with no intention of either of the two cases in which God allowed turning, has earned His threat, unless He favours him with His pardon. He says it is firm and not abrogated because he has shown in several places in his book and elsewhere that it is not permitted to judge that an Qur'anic ruling is abrogated when it has a sense other than abrogation, except by a proof one must submit to, whether a report that cuts off excuse or a proof of reason, and no proof of either kind indicates the abrogation of this verse. As for "has drawn on himself God's anger", it means he returned with God's anger, "and his refuge is Gehenna" meaning where he goes in his return on the Day of Resurrection, "a wretched end" meaning a wretched place to which that end leads. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "drawn on himself", "refuge" and "end". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 440} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 441}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
