## Summary
Verse 65 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/hypocrites-mock-the-army-and-excuse|Hypocrites Mock the Army and Excuse]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 65 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 65 (pp. 332-335)**

Al-Tabari says to His Prophet: if you ask these hypocrites, Muhammad, about the falsehood and lies they spoke, they will say to you that they said it only in play, that they were chatting idly in play and jest; God says to Muhammad, say, Muhammad: was it at God, the verses of His book and His Messenger that you were mocking? The editor refers to earlier explanations of chatting, play and mockery. Ibn Ishaq used to say that the one who spoke these words, as it reached him, was Wadi'a ibn Thabit, brother of Banu Umayya ibn Zayd of Banu Amr ibn Awf, which is report 16910, through Ibn Humayd and Salama, and follows report 16899 in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 332}

Report 16911, from Zayd ibn Aslam, through Ali ibn Dawud, Abdallah ibn Salih, al-Layth and Hisham ibn Sa'd: a man of the hypocrites said to Awf ibn Malik on the Tabuk campaign, "what is it with these reciters of ours, the greediest of us in the belly, the falsest of tongue and the most cowardly at the meeting of the enemy?", and Awf said to him "you lie, you are a hypocrite, I shall tell the Messenger of God"; Awf went to the Messenger of God to tell him and found that the Qur'an had come before him; Zayd said Abdallah ibn Umar said, I looked at him clinging to the girth-rope of the Messenger's camel, the stones bruising him, saying "we were only chatting and playing", and the Prophet saying to him "was it at God, His verses and His Messenger you were mocking?" and adding nothing to it. The editor notes that Hisham ibn Sa'd al-Madani is reliable though spoken against, that Zayd ibn Aslam the jurist related from Ibn Umar and that the chain is sound, and explains the girth-rope as the cord by which the saddle is bound under the camel's belly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 333}

Report 16912, from Abdallah ibn Umar, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Hisham ibn Sa'd and Zayd ibn Aslam, is similar: a man said on the Tabuk campaign in a gathering, "we have seen none like these reciters of ours, greedier in belly, falser in tongue or more cowardly at the meeting", and a man in the gathering said "you lie, you are a hypocrite, I shall tell the Messenger of God", and it reached the Prophet and the Qur'an came down; Abdallah ibn Umar said, I saw him clinging to the girth-rope of the Messenger's camel, the stones bruising him, saying "Messenger of God, we were only chatting and playing", and the Messenger of God saying "was it at God, His verses and His Messenger you were mocking? Make no excuses, you have disbelieved after your belief". The editor says the chain is sound and repeats the previous one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 334}

Report 16913, from Ikrima, through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Ibn Ulayya and Ayyub: there was a man, one of those whom, God willing, God pardoned, who used to say "O God, I hear a verse of which I am the one meant, which makes the skins shiver and the hearts flutter; O God, make my death by being killed in Your way, so that no one says I washed him, I shrouded him, I buried him"; he said, and he was killed on the day of al-Yamama and of all the Muslims none was found but him. The editor explains that the verb for the heart's fluttering means beating and trembling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 334}

Report 16914, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: while the Messenger of God was marching on his campaign to Tabuk, with some of the hypocrites ahead of him, they said "this man hopes to conquer the palaces and fortresses of al-Sham, far from it, far from it", and God informed His Prophet of that, and the Prophet of God said "hold the riders for me", and came to them and said "you said this, you said that", and they said "Prophet of God, we were only chatting and playing", and God sent down about them what you hear. Report 16915, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar, is similar, with the words "this man thinks he will conquer the palaces and fortresses of the Romans", and he called them and said "you said such and such", and they swore "we were only chatting and playing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 335}

Report 16916, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b and others, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Ma'shar: a man of the hypocrites said "I see these reciters of ours as nothing but the greediest in belly, falsest in tongue and most cowardly at the meeting", and it was reported to the Messenger of God, and the man came to him when he had set out and mounted his camel and said "Messenger of God, we were only chatting and playing", and he said "was it at God, His verses and His Messenger you were mocking" up to "criminals", while the man's feet kicked up the stones and the Messenger did not turn to him, he clinging to the Messenger's braided strap. The editor explains the verbs for kicking up and the strap. Report 16917, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: a man of the hypocrites said "Muhammad tells us that so-and-so's camel is in such a valley on such a day; what does he know of the unseen?"; report 16918, from Mujahid, is similar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 335}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
