**Commentary on verses 16-17 (pp. 546-550)**

On "behind him is Hell, and he is given to drink of pus-water, which he gulps but can hardly swallow; death comes to him from every place, yet he is not dying; and behind him is harsh punishment", al-Tabari says, mighty be His mention: "from behind him" means in front of every tyrant is Hell, which they will come to. "Wara'" in this place means "in front", as it is said "death is behind you", meaning in front of you, and as Jarir said in a verse in which he addresses one who threatens him from behind Banu Riyah, saying to him, you lie, your hands will fall short of me, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 546}

meaning by "behind Banu Riyah" in front of and ahead of Banu Riyah. One of the Basran grammarians used to say: by "from behind him" is only meant "from before him", because it is behind what he is in, as one says to you "all this is behind you", that is, it will come upon you and is behind what you are in, because what you are in came before that and this is behind it. He said "behind them was a king who seized every ship by force" [[passage/quran-18-79|18:79]] is of this sense, that is, behind what they were in, ahead of them. One of the Kufan grammarians used to say: this is most allowed with times, because time passes you and becomes behind you once you have passed it, and so "behind them was a king", because they pass it and it becomes behind them. Another used to say it is one of the words of opposites, meaning "wara'" can be in front and behind. On "and he is given to drink of pus-water" he says: he is given to drink of water, then He, mighty be His praise, made that water clear, and what it is, saying it is "sadid", and for that reason "sadid" follows "water" in its grammatical case, because it is an explanation of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 547}

"Sadid" is pus and blood. The interpreters interpreted it so: report 20626, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Harith, al-Hasan and Waraqa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "of pus-water", he said, pus and blood. Report 20627, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: the same. Report 20628, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and he is given to drink of pus-water", and the "sadid" is what flows from his flesh and skin. Report 20629, from Qatada, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: what flows from between his flesh and his skin. Report 20630, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Hisham: "and he is given to drink of pus-water", he said, by "sadid" he means what comes out of the disbeliever's belly, mixed with pus and blood. "He gulps it" means he sips it, "but can hardly swallow it", he says, he can hardly get it down for the extremity of his dislike, though he does swallow it for the extremity of his thirst. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 548}

The Arabs use "la yakadu" both for what was done and for what was not done. As for what was done, this is of it, for God, mighty be His praise, made that their drink; and as for what was not done and "kada" entered it, it is His saying "when he puts out his hand he can hardly see it" [[passage/quran-24-40|24:40]], and he does not see it. Like what we said, that the meaning of "and can hardly swallow it" is that he does swallow it, a report came from the Messenger of God. Report 20631, from Abu Umama, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Ibrahim Abu Ishaq al-Taliqani, Ibn al-Mubarak, Safwan ibn Amr and Ubayd Allah ibn Busr: from the Prophet on "and he is given to drink of pus-water, which he gulps", when he drinks it, it cuts his intestines until it comes out of his rear; God, mighty and exalted, says "they will be given boiling water that cuts their intestines" [[passage/quran-47-15|47:15]], and says "if they cry for help they will be helped with water like molten brass that scalds the faces; wretched the drink" [[passage/quran-18-29|18:29]]. Report 20632, from Abu Umama, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Ma'mar, Ibn al-Mubarak, Safwan ibn Amr and Ubayd Allah ibn Busr: from the Prophet on "and he is given to drink of pus-water", mentioning the like, except that he said "were given boiling water". Report 20633, from Abu Umama, through Muhammad ibn Khalaf al-Asqalani, Hayat ibn Shurayh al-Himsi, Baqiyya, Safwan ibn Amr and Ubayd Allah ibn Busr: the same exactly; in a long note the editor goes into Ubayd Allah ibn Busr and the transmission of the report in Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, al-Hakim and Abu Nu'aym. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 549}

On "death comes to him from every place, yet he is not dying", he says: death comes to him from before him and behind him, from his right and left, and from every part of his body's members, "yet he is not dying", because his soul does not leave so that he dies and is at rest, and he does not live, because his soul is caught in his throat and does not return to its place. Report 20634, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "which he gulps but can hardly swallow; and death comes to him from every place, yet he is not dying", he said, his soul is caught at his throat, so it neither leaves his mouth so that he dies nor returns to its place in his belly so that he finds relief in that and life benefits him. Report 20635, from Ibrahim al-Taymi, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Yazid ibn Harun and al-Awwam ibn Hawshab: "death comes to him from every place", he said, from under every hair on his body. On "and behind him is harsh punishment" he says: behind what he is in of punishment, meaning ahead of him and in front of him, is harsh punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 550}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
