## Summary
Verse 17 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 17 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the verse and the origin of the near (pp. 88-89).** Abu Jafar begins the interpretation of [[passage/quran-4-17|4:17]] by saying that God's acceptance of repentance belongs to no one among his creatures except those who commit the wrong through ignorance while believing in their Lord, then return to obedience and repent by regret, asking forgiveness and not going back to the like of it, before death arrives. He says this is the near that the verse names, and the editor refers forward to his treatment of it (p. 93). He notes that the interpreters agreed with this sense but differed over the ignorance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 88} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 89}

**First view: the evil deed itself is the ignorance (pp. 89-90, reports 8832-8839).** Bishr ibn Muadh (8832, from Yazid from Said) reports from Qatada from Abu al-Aliyah that the Companions used to say every sin a servant commits is done out of ignorance. Al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8833, from Abd al-Razzaq from Mamar) reports from Qatada that the Companions gathered and held that whatever God is disobeyed by is ignorance, deliberate or not. Muhammad ibn Amr (8834, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that whoever disobeys his Lord is ignorant until he desists; al-Muthanna (8835, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl) reports from Mujahid something close; Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8836, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the disobedient is ignorant as long as he disobeys; al-Qasim (8837, from al-Husayn from Muhammad ibn Fudayl from Abu al-Nadr from Abu Salih) reports from Ibn Abbas that whoever does evil is ignorant, and does it out of his ignorance; and al-Qasim again (8838, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj) reports Mujahid to the same effect, with Ibn Jurayj adding that Abd Allah ibn Kathir told him the same from Mujahid and that Ata ibn Abi Rabah said something like it. Yunus (8839, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that everyone who does any disobedience is ignorant until he desists, and that Ibn Zayd cited two verses from sura Yusuf ([[passage/quran-12-89|12:89]] and [[passage/quran-12-33|12:33]]) in which the word for the ignorant occurs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 89} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 90}

**Second view: ignorance means deliberateness (pp. 90-91, reports 8840-8842).** Al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8840, from Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri) reports from Mujahid that ignorance is deliberateness; Ibn Waki (8841, from his father from Sufyan from an unnamed man) reports the same from Mujahid; al-Muthanna (8842, from Ishaq from Abu Zuhayr from Juwaybir) reports it from al-Dahhak. The number of 8842 is garbled in the scan ("80142") and is read from the sequence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 90} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 91}

**Third view: those who commit wrong during worldly life (p. 91, report 8843).** Al-Qasim (from al-Husayn from Mutamir ibn Sulayman from al-Hakam ibn Aban) reports from Ikrima that the world as a whole is ignorance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 91}

**Al-Tabari's choice and argument (pp. 91-93).** Abu Jafar chooses the view that their doing of evil is the ignorance they were ignorant of, whether they were deliberate in the sin or unaware of what God has prepared for its people. His argument is that the Arabs do not call someone who does a thing deliberately ignorant of it, unless he is ignorant of the measure of its benefit and harm; one who knows the measure of its benefit and harm and aims at it is not called so because of his aiming. He then explains that the ignorant of a thing is one who does not know it when he proceeds, or one who knows it and is likened, since what he did was a mistake, to the ignorant person who misses the mark, so that he is called ignorant though he knows, because he did what only the ignorant of it would do. So the sense is that they acted as the ignorant act, not that they were ignorant, since their act is one only someone blind to the greatness of God's punishment for it, in this world and the next, would commit. He then records that some grammarians claimed they were ignorant of the true nature of the punishment and so did not know it as the knower does, though they knew it was a sin, and replies that if this were so, there could be no repentance for one who knew its true nature; that this contradicts what is established from the Messenger that God may turn toward every repentant person, and the saying that the door of repentance stays open until the sun rises from the west; and that it contradicts the statement in sura 25 ([[passage/quran-25-70|25:70]]) excepting the one who repents, believes and does righteous work. These views and the choice are al-Tabari's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 91} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 92} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 93}

**The editor's notes on these pages (pp. 91-93).** On p. 91 the editor refers to the commentary on the ignorant as the foolish in volume 2 (p. 183); he suggests that a phrase on ignorance of the benefit and harm should read "the measure of its benefit and harm" and was altered by the copyist; and on a clause about the doer's aim, which in both the printed text and the manuscript does not fit what precedes or follows, he judges it a copyist's slip, a word for "without" written in place of "because of", or a word the copyist could not read well and replaced, a very easy confusion, and he says he has met worse. On p. 92 he says the manuscript has "does it" where the first printer corrected to "knows it", that he added the relative word in brackets to balance the two sides of the sentence, that the grammarian's view is al-Farra' in Maani al-Quran (1:259), and that the word for repentance is the subject of "to be" in al-Tabari's reasoning. On p. 93 he says Abu Jafar gave the two reports without chains, apparently only the sense and not the wording. The first seems to be the report that God may forgive every sin except for one who dies a polytheist or deliberately kills a believer, which al-Suyuti, in al-Jami al-Saghir, traced to Abu Dawud from Abu al-Darda, and to Ahmad, al-Nasai and al-Hakim in al-Mustadrak from Muawiya. The second seems to be the report that God stretches out his hand at night so that the one who did wrong by day may repent, and by day for the one who did wrong at night, until the sun rises from the west, which Muslim (17:76) gave from Abu Musa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 91} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 92} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 93}

**The meaning of the near (pp. 93-94, reports 8844-8851).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed on the near in three ways. First, repenting while healthy, before illness and before death: Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8844, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that it is while he is healthy before death, and al-Qasim (8845, from al-Husayn from Muhammad ibn Fudayl from Abu al-Nadr from Abu Salih) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is in life and health. Second, before seeing the angel of death: al-Muthanna (8846, from Abu Salih from Muawiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is between a person and the moment he looks upon the angel of death; Muhammad ibn Abd al-Ala (8847, from al-Mutamir ibn Sulayman from Imran ibn Hudayr) reports from Abu Mijlaz that a man stays in repentance until he sees the angels; al-Qasim (8848, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Abu Mashar) reports from Muhammad ibn Qays that the near lasts as long as no sign from God has come down on him and death has not; and al-Muthanna (8849, from Ishaq from Abu Zuhayr from Juwaybir) reports from al-Dahhak that he has repentance until he sees the angel of death, and if he repents as he looks upon him, it is not for him. Third, before death: al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8850, from Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from an unnamed man) reports from al-Dahhak that everything before death is near, and al-Qasim (8851, from al-Husayn from Mutamir ibn Sulayman from al-Hakam ibn Aban) reports from Ikrima that the whole world is near. The number of 8849 is garbled in the scan ("4849") and is read from the sequence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 93} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 94}

**The editor's note on 8848 (p. 94).** Muhammad ibn Qays al-Madani was the judge of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz; Ibn Saad said he was a scholar with many hadith; Ibn Hibban lists him among the trustworthy; he has one hadith in Muslim, from Abu Surma from Abu Hurayra; he is the one Abu Mashar narrates from; and he has an entry in al-Tahdhib. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 94}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
