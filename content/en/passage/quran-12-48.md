**Commentary on verse 48 (pp. 126-128)**

On "then after that will come seven hard ones that devour what you have laid by for them, except a little of what you store" al-Tabari says: after the seven years in which you sow as usual will come seven hard years of drought and dearth, "devour what you have put forward", meaning the food and provisions that you prepared in the seven fertile years will be eaten in them. He said "devour" and so described the years as eating, though the sense is that the people of that land eat in them. He cited a verse of Abd Allah ibn Abd al-A'la ibn Abi Amra addressing a deluded man, whose day is heedless, his night sleep and death certain for him; the editor gives its sources in al-Akhbar al-Tiwal, Ibn al-Jawzi's Sirat Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and Ibn Kathir's History and sets out the whole poem. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 126}

So the day is described as heedless and negligent and the night as sleep, though it is in the one that one is heedless and in the other that one sleeps, since the listeners know the sense and what is meant. "Except a little of what you store" means except a little of what you keep safe, and "ihsan" is putting in a fortress, though what is meant is keeping safe. Report 19371, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they devour the provisions you laid by in them. Reports 19372 and 19373, from Qatada: the seven hard ones are the dry barren years, "what you store" meaning what you save. Report 19374, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: you hoard; report 19375 from him, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: you keep safe; report 19376, from al-Suddi: what you set aside. Al-Tabari says these sayings on "you store", though their wording differs, are close in meaning, and the root of the word and its interpretation are as he has set out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 127}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
