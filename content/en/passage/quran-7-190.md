## Place in the museum

**Commentary on verse 190 (pp. 308-317)**

Al-Tabari says: "when He gave them a sound one they made partners for Him in what He gave them; exalted is God above what they associate", meaning when God gave them a sound child as they asked, they made partners for Him in what He gave and provided them. The interpreters differed over the "partners" they made in what they were given of the child. Some said they made partners for Him in the name. Report 15513, from Samura ibn Jundab from the Prophet: Eve's children did not live, so she vowed that if a child of hers lived she would name him Abd al-Harith; a child of hers lived and she named him Abd al-Harith, and that was from the Satan's suggestion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 308} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 309}

The editor writes at length on the tracing of this report: Umar ibn Ibrahim al-Abdi was held trustworthy by Ahmad and others, but Ahmad said he narrates objectionable reports from Qatada; Abu Hatim said his hadith is written but not argued from; Ibn Adi said his hadith especially from Qatada is disordered; Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy and said he errs; and al-Daraqutni called him soft. Ahmad gave the report in the Musnad in other words, al-Hakim in these words calling the chain sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing, and al-Tirmidhi in the commentary on the verse, calling it good but strange, known only from Umar ibn Ibrahim from Qatada, and noting that some narrated it from Abd al-Samad without raising it. Ibn Kathir found it defective in three ways: that Umar ibn Ibrahim is not argued from; that it is narrated as the word of Samura himself, not raised; and that al-Hasan himself explained the verse otherwise, as in reports 15526 to 15528, which he calls sound chains from al-Hasan and one of the best explanations, adding that had al-Hasan held this hadith preserved he would not have turned from it, and that it may have been taken from People of the Book such as Wahb ibn Munabbih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 309}

The editor adds his own remarks on the matter: he holds that the agreement of the interpreters on accepting it does not stand, because the verse is difficult in that it ascribes association to Adam whom his Lord chose by the text of God's book; that al-Tabari wished to escape this by claiming, at p. 315, that the speech about Adam and Eve ended at "they made partners for Him in what He gave them" and that "exalted is God above what they associate" begins anew, meaning what the idol-worshipping polytheists of the Arabs associate, which he calls a very weak way out; that a difficulty like this, ascribing association to Adam and Eve, cannot be settled except by a proof one must submit to, from a text of the book or a report from the Messenger, and there is none except this weak report whose weakness he has shown; and that a group of commentators, among them Ibn Kathir and Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, rejected this view, al-Zamakhshari tried to answer and did not do well, and Ibn al-Munayyir followed him up in al-Insaf. After all that, he says, the explanation of the verse's wording and its fit with the sound meaning the scholars took, denying association to Adam and holding that the verse does not mean Adam and Eve, remains unclear, no one having treated it with sound explanation, and he found the matter too large to speak of in a note like this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 309} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 310}

Reports 15514 and 15515, from Samura ibn Jundab: Adam named his son Abd al-Harith; the editor identifies Abu al-Ala ibn al-Shikhkhir, Yazid ibn Abd Allah al-Amiri, as a devout and trustworthy Follower who read the Qur'an until he fainted. Report 15516, from Ibn Abbas: Eve used to bear for Adam and dedicate them to God, naming them Ubayd Allah, Abd Allah and the like, and death struck them; Iblis came to her and Adam and said if you named him other than what you name him he would live; she bore him a man and he named him Abd al-Harith, and about it God revealed "it is He who created you from one soul" up to "they made partners for Him in what He gave them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 310}

Report 15517, from Ibn Abbas on Adam: she doubted whether she had conceived; when she grew heavy they called on God, and Satan came to them and said, do you know what will be born to you, whether it will be a beast or not? and made falsehood seem fair to them; she had before that borne two children who died, and Satan said to them if you do not name him by me he will not come out sound and will die as the first two died, so they named their child Abd al-Harith. Report 15518, from Ibn Abbas: when his first child was born Iblis came and said I shall advise you about this child, name him Abd al-Harith; Adam said I take refuge in God from obeying you, his name in heaven being al-Harith, I obeyed you in eating from the tree and you drove me from Paradise, so I will not obey you. His child died; then another was born and he said obey me or he will die as the first did; he refused and the child died; he said I shall keep killing them until you name him Abd al-Harith, and he kept at him until he named him; that is "they made partners for Him in what He gave them", he made him a partner in obedience in other than worship, and he did not associate others with God but obeyed him. Report 15519, from Ikrima: neither Adam nor Eve associated anything; no child of theirs lived, and Satan came and said if it pleases you that a child of yours live, name him Abd al-Harith. The editor describes the disorder of the manuscript in the chain and identifies Harun as the one-eyed grammarian Harun ibn Musa al-Azdi, master of readings, trustworthy, and al-Zubayr ibn al-Khirrit as trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 310} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 311} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 312}

Report 15520, from Qatada: no child was born to Adam but died, and Satan came and said if it pleases you that this child live, name him Abd al-Harith, and he did; they associated in the name, not in worship. Report 15521, from Qatada: it was mentioned to us that no child of theirs lived, and Satan came to them and said name him Abd al-Harith; it was from Satan's suggestion and command and was association in obedience, not association in worship. Report 15522, from Mujahid: no child lived to Adam and his wife, and Satan said to them when a child is born to you name him Abd al-Harith; they did so and obeyed him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 312}

Report 15523, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: when Eve conceived her first child, as she grew heavy, Iblis came to her before she gave birth and said, Eve, what is this in your belly? She said I do not know. He said from where will it come out, from your nose or your eyes or your ears? She said I do not know. He said what if it comes out sound, will you obey me in what I command you? She said yes. He said name him Abd al-Harith, Iblis having been called al-Harith, and she said yes. Then she told Adam, someone came to me in sleep and said such and such; he said that is Satan, beware of him, he is our enemy who drove us from Paradise. Iblis came again and repeated it and she said yes; when she gave birth God brought it out sound and she named him Abd al-Harith. Report 15524, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, who was asked whether Adam associated: I take refuge in God from claiming that Adam associated, but when Eve grew heavy Iblis came to her and said from where will this come out, from your nose or eyes or mouth, and made her despair, then said what if it comes out sound, will you obey? She said yes. He said name him Abd al-Harith, and she did; Jarir added: his association was only in the name. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 313}

Report 15525, from al-Suddi: Eve bore a boy, and Iblis came and said name him my servant or I will kill him; Adam said I obeyed you and you drove me from Paradise, and he refused to obey him and named him Abd al-Rahman, so God let Iblis have power over him and he killed him. She conceived another, and when she bore him he said name him my servant or I will kill him; Adam said I obeyed you and you drove me from Paradise, he refused and named him Salih, and he killed him. When the third came he said to them, since you have overcome me name him Abd al-Harith, which was Iblis's name, he being called Iblis only when he despaired, and they did, which is when God says "they made partners for Him in what He gave them", meaning in the naming. The editor notes that the printed edition had "they did" in the wrong number. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 313} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 314}

Others said it means a man and woman of the unbelievers among the children of Adam who made partners of gods and idols for God when He gave them the child. They said the sense of the speech is: it is He who created you from one soul and made from it its mate that he might dwell with her; when he covered her, that is this unbelieving man, she bore a light burden; when she grew heavy, you two called on God your Lord. They said this is among speech begun in address and then turned to report about the absent, as in [[passage/quran-10-22|10:22]], and he has shown parallels with their evidence earlier. Report 15526, from al-Hasan: "they made partners for Him in what He gave them" was said of some of the followers of religions and was not of Adam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 314}

Report 15527, from al-Hasan: this meant the descendants of Adam who associated after him, in the words "when He gave them a sound one they made partners for Him in what He gave them"; report 15528, from Qatada: al-Hasan used to say they are the Jews and Christians, God having given them children whom they made Jews and Christians. Then al-Tabari says: the better of the two views is that of those who said that "when He gave them a sound one they made partners for Him" is in the name, not in worship, and that those meant are Adam and Eve, because of the agreement of the authoritative interpreters on that. If someone says: what do you say, if the matter is as you describe and those meant by this verse are Adam and Eve, of "exalted is God above what they associate"? Is it God's disdain at having a partner in names or in worship? If you say in names, its falsity is shown by "do they associate what creates nothing and is itself created?"; and if you say in worship, it is said to you: did Adam associate another with God in worship? He answers: the sense of "exalted is God above what they associate" is not what you supposed; the sense is rather exalted is God above what the idol-worshipping polytheists of the Arabs associate with Him; the report about Adam and Eve ended at "they made partners for Him in what He gave them", and "exalted is God above what they associate" begins anew. Report 15529, from al-Suddi: this is a break from the verse of Adam, and is specifically about the gods of the Arabs. The editor refers to his note on report 15513. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 314} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 315}
The reciters differed over the reading of "shuraka'". Most reciters of Medina and some Meccans and Kufans read "shirkan" with kasra on the first letter, meaning a share; some Meccans, most Kufan reciters and some Basrans read "shuraka'" with damma, the plural of "partner". Al-Tabari chooses the reading with damma as the better, because if the reading with kasra were sound the speech would have to be "they made for another a share in it", since Adam and Eve did not hold that their child was a gift of Iblis and then give God a share in it by naming him Abd Allah; they held that their child was God's provision and gift, then named him Abd al-Harith and so gave Iblis a share in it by name; and in the revelation's saying "they made for Him" is what shows that the sound reading is "shuraka'" with damma. If someone says they only named their son Abd al-Harith and al-Harith is one, while "partners" is a plural, he answers: it was shown earlier that the Arabs put a report about one in the form of a report about a group when they do not mean a particular one and do not name him, as in "those to whom people said, the people have gathered against you", [[passage/quran-3-173|3:173]], the speaker having been one man; and that is common in Arab speech and poetry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 316} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 317}
As for "exalted is God above what they associate", it is God's declaring Himself pure and magnifying Himself above what the falsifiers say of Him and the gods and idols they claim beside Him. Report 15530, from Ibn Jurayj: it is the "inkaf", He disdained for Himself, mighty and glorious, meaning He magnified Himself, and the angels disdained for Him and glorified Him; report 15531, from al-Suddi: this is of the joined and the separated, "they made partners for Him in what He gave them" being about Adam and Eve, then God said "exalted is God above what they associate", meaning above what the polytheists associate, and He did not mean those two. The editor notes that at this point the tenth part of their manuscript ends, with a colophon of the copyist recording the finishing of its copying in Jumada al-Ula of the year 715, and that the eleventh part begins with the commentary on "do they associate what creates nothing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 317}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
