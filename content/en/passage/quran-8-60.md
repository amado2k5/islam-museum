## Summary
Verse 60 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 60 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 60 (pp. 31-34)**

Al-Tabari says: "prepare" against these who disbelieved in their Lord, those with whom you have a covenant when you fear their treachery and perfidy, believers, "what you can of strength", meaning what you are able to prepare of the implements that give you strength over them, weapons and horses, "to frighten by it the enemy of God and your enemy", meaning that by preparing it you frighten God's enemy and yours among the idolaters. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "frustrate" and "capability". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 31}

Report 16224, from a man of Juhayna who traced the saying to the Messenger of God, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Idris, Usama ibn Zayd and Salih ibn Kaysan: "Truly, shooting is the strength; truly, shooting is the strength." The editor notes that "Ibn Idris" is the imam Abd Allah ibn Idris al-Awdi and that both manuscript and printed text erred over him. Report 16225, from Uqba ibn Amir, through Abu Kurayb, Sa'id ibn Shurahbil, Ibn Lahi'a, Yazid ibn Abi Habib, Abd al-Karim ibn al-Harith and Abu Ali al-Hamdani: I heard the Messenger of God say on the pulpit, "prepare for them what you can of strength", "truly the strength is shooting", three times. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 32}

The editor gives biographies for the men in the chain of 16225 and says it carries weakness for those who weaken Ibn Lahi'a, but that the report came by very sound routes: Muslim gave it in his Sahih through Harun ibn Ma'ruf, Ibn Wahb, Amr ibn al-Harith and Abu Ali Thumama ibn Shufayy; Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah gave it through Ibn Wahb; and al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak, saying it meets the conditions of the two shaykhs, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 32}

Report 16226, from Uqba ibn Amir al-Juhani, through Abu Kurayb, Mahbub, Ja'far ibn Awn, Waki, Abu Usama, Abu Nu'aym, Usama ibn Zayd and Salih ibn Kaysan from a man: the Messenger of God recited on the pulpit "prepare for them what you can of strength and of tethered horses", and said "truly the strength is shooting", three times; report 16227 is similar through Ibn Waki and his father, and report 16228 similar through Ahmad ibn Ishaq and Abu Ahmad. The editor notes that al-Tirmidhi gave it through Waki and said Waki's report is sounder, and that Salih ibn Kaysan did not meet Uqba ibn Amir but met Ibn Umar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 33}

Report 16229, from Uqba ibn Amir from the Prophet, through Ibn Humayd, Yahya ibn Wadih, Musa ibn Ubayda, his brother Muhammad ibn Ubayda and their brother Abd Allah ibn Ubayda: truly the strength is shooting. The editor says Musa ibn Ubayda al-Rabadhi is very weak and narrating from him is not permitted, gives biographies of his brothers and quotes Ahmad on them. Report 16230, from Ikrima, through Ibn Waki, his father, Sufyan and Shu'ba ibn Dinar: strength means fortresses, and "tethered horses" means mares. Report 16231, from Raja ibn Abi Salama, through Ali ibn Sahl and Damra ibn Rabi'a: a man met Mujahid in Mecca with a sack, and Mujahid said this is part of the strength; Mujahid was preparing for a campaign. Report 16232, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: "of strength" means weapons. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 34}

On "to frighten by it the enemy of God and your enemy": report 16233, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki, his father, Isra'il, Uthman ibn al-Mughira al-Thaqafi and Mujahid: you disgrace by it the enemy of God and yours; report 16234 is the same through Ahmad ibn Ishaq and Abu Ahmad; and report 16235, from Ibn Abbas, begins through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Isra'il, Khusayf, Ikrima and Sa'id ibn Jubayr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 34}

Report 16235, from Ibn Abbas: "you frighten by it" means you disgrace by it the enemy of God and yours, and he read it "you disgrace"; report 16236 is missing from the numbering; then report 16237, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Isra'il, Uthman ibn al-Mughira, Khusayf and Mujahid: "you frighten by it" means you disgrace by it; and report 16238 is the same through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Isra'il, Khusayf and Ikrima. The editor says the "frighten" in the manuscript and printed text is an error for "disgrace", that Ibn Khalawayh recorded this reading among the irregular readings, and that Abu Hayyan thinks al-Tabari gave it as explanation rather than as a reading because it departs from the written copy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 35}

One says "I frightened the enemy" in two forms, with the nouns "fear" in two forms, and al-Tabari cites a line of Tufayl al-Ghanawi about men of the Banu Kilab on a day of terror and fear; the editor gives its place in his Diwan and in Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 35}

On "and others besides them whom you do not know, God knows them", al-Tabari says the commentators differed over who these others are. Some said they are the Banu Qurayza; report 16239, from Mujahid, through Ammar ibn al-Hasan, Ibn Abi Ja'far, Warqa and Ibn Abi Najih: of the Banu Qurayza; report 16240, from Mujahid: the Qurayza. Others said they are from Persia; report 16241, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: these are the people of Persia. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 36}

Others said they are every enemy of the Muslims other than those the Prophet was commanded to scatter those behind them by, namely the hypocrites; report 16242, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: frighten them by what you do to these, and he recited "others besides them whom you do not know, God knows them"; report 16243 from him: these are the hypocrites, you do not know them because they are with you, saying there is no god but God and campaigning with you. The editor notes that 16242 repeats 16220 and that al-Tabari seems to have erred in transmitting it here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 36}

Others said they are a people of the jinn. Al-Tabari then says the right view is that God commanded the believers to prepare for jihad, the gear of war and what strengthens them against His enemy and theirs among the idolaters, weapons, shooting and others, and tethered horses; there is no ground to confine "strength" to one of its senses when God made the command general. If it is said the Prophet explained that the specific sense is meant by "truly the strength is shooting", he answers that nothing in the report shows that shooting alone is meant to the exclusion of other senses, for shooting is one sense of strength, and the sword, spear and lance and whatever helps fight the idolaters equally or better belong to it, quite apart from the weakness of the chain of that report from the Messenger of God. The editor refers to reports 16224 to 16229 and to the sound routes he gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 37}

As for "others besides them whom you do not know", al-Tabari holds that the view that they are the jinn is nearer to the truth, because God, by "and of tethered horses to frighten by it the enemy of God and your enemy", commanded keeping horses to frighten every enemy, and the believers surely knew of the enmity of the Qurayza and the Persians, knowing they were idolaters at war with them, so "whom you do not know" would make no sense of them; the meaning for him is that by keeping horses you frighten the enemy of God and your enemies among the sons of Adam whose enmity you know from their unbelief, and you also frighten another kind, not of the sons of Adam, whose places and states you do not know and God knows apart from you, because the sons of Adam cannot see them. It is said that the neighing of horses frightens the jinn and they do not approach a house with a horse in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 38}

If it is said that the believers did not know the hypocrites' state and so why not hold the hypocrites meant, his reply is that the Muslims' horses and weapons did not alarm the hypocrites; what alarmed them was the Muslims' coming to know the unbelief they hid, and the believers were commanded to prepare strength to frighten the enemy, so whoever is not frightened by it is not within the sense of those it is prepared against. "You do not know them" takes a single object because knowing here means being acquainted with, as a line of al-Namir ibn Tawlab shows, about God's knowing him and a man named Wahb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 38}

The editor gives two reports quoted by Ibn Kathir, one that the Prophet said they are the jinn, with an addition in al-Tabarani about horses, and Ibn Kathir judges it an objectionable report whose chain and text are not sound. He says the scholars rejected this view of al-Tabari, that Ibn Kathir and Abu Hayyan preferred the hypocrites, the view al-Tabari rejects, and that if he were to choose he would choose those of the idolaters whose affair was hidden from the believers, such as the Christians of Syria and others whose enmity was not yet shown, the verse being general. He then gives the occasion of al-Namir's line: his dispute with a man of his people called Wahb over a well called al-Duhul. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 39}

On "whatever you spend in God's way will be repaid to you, and you will not be wronged", al-Tabari says: whatever you spend, believers, in buying gear of war, weapons, lances, horses or other expenses in fighting God's idolatrous enemies, God will replace it for you in this world and lay up your reward with Him until He pays it in full on the day of resurrection; "you will not be wronged" means your Lord does this for you and does not waste your reward. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of spending and "repay". Report 16244, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: its reward will not be lost for you with God in the next life, with prompt replacement in this one; the editor notes it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 40}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
