## Summary
Verse 262 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 262 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

In ([[passage/quran-2-262|2:262]]) from p. 517, al-Tabari says the verse concerns one who gives his wealth to the fighters in God's way as support for their fighting God's enemies, that is, one who helps the fighters by spending on them and on their mounts and other provisions, and then does not follow what he spent with reproach or harm to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 517} Reproach is that he shows them he has done them a kindness by his act and gift by which he strengthened them, expressing it in speech or deed; harm is his complaining about them for what he gave and spent on them in God's way, that they did not do their duty in the fighting, and similar speech that hurts the one spent on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 517} He explains that this condition is set on the one who spends in God's way, and the reward is made due for the one who neither reproaches nor harms, because spending in God's way is what is done seeking God's face and what is with Him; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 517} The editor notes a correction to the singular verb in place of the plural in the printed text and the manuscript, and keeps the manuscript reading in the second note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 517} We stop here, before the rest of his argument. These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's choice and explanation are attributed to him, and the corrections are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-262|2:262]]) from p. 517. Al-Tabari says the one who spends has no ground for reproach toward the one spent on, because he has no claim on him and no favour for which he deserves reproach and harm, if he is not repaid for it, since his spending was done seeking reward and God's pleasure, and its recompense is with God, not with the one who received it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 517} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 518} A group of interpreters spoke in this sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 518} Qatada (6034): God knew that some people reproach with what they give, disliked it and put it first, and what follows is in ([[passage/quran-2-263|2:263]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 518} Ibn Zayd (6035): God said it to the others, those who do not go out to fight His enemy, and so set a condition on them, while the one who goes out has no condition on him, little or much, meaning the one going out in the fighting mentioned in the previous verse; and Ibn Zayd said his father used to say that if one who gives something to such a one, or supports him in God's way, annoys you, and you think your greeting weighs on him, withhold your greeting from him; Ibn Zayd said he forbade the best of Islam; and a woman said to his father, O Abu Usama, show me a man who truly goes out in God's way, for they go out only to eat fruit, and I have a quiver and arrows in it; and he told her not to be blessed in her quiver and arrows, for she had harmed them before giving; and a man used to tell them to go out and eat fruit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 518} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 519} The editor's notes correct the readings of the manuscript and printed text in several places, say that the copyist often slipped here, and explain that Ibn Zayd's words that he forbade the best of Islam refer to what al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah relate from Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As, that a man asked the Prophet which Islam is best and he said feeding food and greeting those you know and those you do not, and he reads the greeting as what Ibn Zayd forbade to the one harmed; these remarks and references are the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 518} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 519} Al-Dahhak (6036): that a man not spend his wealth is better than that he spend it and then follow it with reproach and harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 519}

Al-Tabari then explains the clause on their reward: it is for those who spend their wealth in God's way as described, the pronoun returning to the relative, and means they have their recompense and requital for their spending in God's way, which they did not follow with reproach or harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 519} He explains the denial of fear and grief: along with the recompense they have, no fear is on them on coming before God and leaving the world, nor in the terrors of the resurrection, nor that they be touched by its hardships or by God's punishment in it, and they do not grieve over what they left behind in the world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 519} The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the reward and to earlier passages, and notes that the fourth volume of his manuscript ends here, quoting the copyist's closing words of that volume, the date of its completion in Dhu al-Hijja of the year 714, and the opening of the fifth volume; this is the editor's description of the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 519}
