**Commentary on verse 24 (pp. 291-292)**

On [[passage/quran-11-24|11:24]] al-Tabari says: the likeness of the two parties, of disbelief and belief, is as the blind who sees nothing with his eyes and the deaf who hears nothing: so the party of disbelief does not see the truth so as to follow it and act on it, occupied with its disbelief in God and overcome by God's abandoning, not hearing God's caller to right guidance so as to answer and be guided, remaining in its error and wavering in its bewilderment; and the hearing and the seeing are the party of belief, who saw God's proofs and acknowledged what they point to of God's oneness, the renouncing of gods and rivals and the prophethood of the prophets, and heard God's caller and answered him and worked in obedience to God. Report 18102, from Ibn Jurayj from Ibn Abbas, begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 291}

Report 18102, from Ibn Abbas: the blind and deaf is the disbeliever, the seeing and hearing the believer; report 18103, from Mujahid: the two parties are the two disbelievers and two believers, the blind and deaf being the disbelievers and the seeing and hearing the believers; report 18104, from Qatada: this is a likeness God struck for the disbeliever and the believer; the disbeliever is deaf to the truth and does not hear it and blind to it and does not see it, while the believer heard the truth and benefited from it, and saw it, took it in, kept it and acted on it. His words 'are the two alike in likeness?' mean are these two parties alike, with their different states in themselves, in your view, people? They are not alike in your view, and so the disbeliever and the believer are not alike with God; 'will you not reflect?' means will you not take a lesson and think, people, and know the truth of the difference between their two affairs, and turn back from the error you are in to guidance and from disbelief to belief? The blind and deaf and the seeing and hearing are four in word and two in meaning, which is why it says 'are the two alike?'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 292}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
