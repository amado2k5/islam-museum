## Summary
Verse 75 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 75 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verses 75-77 (pp. 369-380)**

Al-Tabari treats these three verses together in a single place in his commentary, so his explanations are placed here and the following two verse pages refer back to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 369}

Al-Tabari says: among these hypocrites whose description I gave you, Muhammad, are those who made a covenant with God, meaning gave God a pledge, "if He gives us of His bounty", if God gives us of His bounty and provides us with wealth and widens us from Himself, "we shall give alms", that is, we shall bring out the alms from that wealth our Lord has provided us, "and be among the righteous", that is, we shall do with it the work of the righteous with their wealth, from joining kinship with it and spending it in God's way. God, blessed and exalted, says: so God provided them and gave them of His bounty, "and when He gave them of His bounty they were miserly with it", with God's bounty which He gave them, so they gave no alms from it, joined no kin with it and spent nothing from it for God's right, "and turned away", that is, turned back from the covenant they made with God, "and were disregarding" it; "so He made hypocrisy follow in their hearts" by their miserliness over God's right which He made obligatory on them of what He gave them of His bounty, their breaking the promise they made to God and their violating His covenant in their hearts, "until the day they meet Him", for they broke what they promised God of alms and spending in His way, "and because they used to lie" in their words, and He denied them repentance from it, because the Almighty stipulated that He made hypocrisy follow in them until the day they meet Him, which is the day of their death and their departure from this world. The editor refers to earlier explanations of covenant, giving, bounty, alms, the righteous, turning away, disregard and hypocrisy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 369}

The interpreters differed over who is meant by this verse. Some said a man called Tha'laba ibn Hatib of the Helpers is meant. Report 16986, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: "among them are those who made a covenant with God that if He gave them of His bounty", and that is that a man called Tha'laba ibn Hatib, of the Helpers, came to a gathering, called them to witness, and said "if God gives me of His bounty I shall give everyone with a right his right, give alms from it and join kinship with it", and God tried him and gave him of His bounty, and he broke what he promised God and angered God by breaking what he promised, and God told his story in the Qur'an, "among them are those who made a covenant with God", to the words "they used to lie". The editor notes that the manuscript stops at the words "called" and does not give the man's name, which the first editor supplied from the reports. Report 16987, from Abu Umama al-Bahili, from Tha'laba ibn Hatib al-Ansari, through al-Muthanna, Hisham ibn Ammar, Muhammad ibn Shu'ayb, Mu'an ibn Rifa'a, Ali ibn Yazid al-Alhani and al-Qasim ibn Abd al-Rahman: he said to the Messenger of God, "ask God to provide me with wealth", and he said "woe to you, Tha'laba, a little for which you give thanks is better than much you cannot bear"; then he said it again, and he said "are you not content to be like the Prophet of God? By Him in whose hand is my soul, had I wished the mountains to travel with me as gold and silver they would have", and he said "by Him who sent you with the truth, if you ask God and He gives me wealth I shall give everyone with a right his right", and the Messenger of God said "O God, give Tha'laba wealth". He took up sheep, which multiplied like worms, so that Medina became too narrow for him and he moved away from it and settled in one of its valleys, until he was praying noon and afternoon in congregation and leaving the rest; then they multiplied and grew, and he moved further until he left the prayers except Friday, and they kept multiplying like worms, until he left Friday too; he began to meet the riders on Friday and ask them for news, and the Messenger of God said "what has Tha'laba done?", and they said "Messenger of God, he took up sheep and Medina became too narrow for him", and they told him of his state and he said "woe to Tha'laba, woe to Tha'laba, woe to Tha'laba". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 371}

He said, and God sent down the verse that orders alms to be taken from their wealth, which is [[passage/quran-9-103|9:103]], and the obligations of alms came down on him, and the Messenger of God sent two men to collect the alms, a man of Juhayna and a man of Sulaym, and wrote for them how they should take the alms from the Muslims, and told them "pass by Tha'laba and by so-and-so, a man of Banu Sulaym, and take their alms". They went out until they came to Tha'laba and asked him for the alms and read him the letter of the Messenger of God, and he said "this is nothing but a tax, nothing but the sister of the tax, I do not know what this is; go until you are finished, then come back to me". They went off, and the man of Sulaym heard of them and looked to the best-aged of his camels and set them apart for the alms, then met them with them; when they saw them they said "this is not due from you and we do not want to take it from you", and he said "rather take it, for my soul is glad of it and they are my own", and they took them from him. When they had finished with their alms they returned and passed by Tha'laba, and he said "show me your letter", and looked at it and said "this is nothing but the sister of the tax; go until I see what I think". They went until they came to the Prophet, and when he saw them he said "woe to Tha'laba" before they had spoken to him, and prayed for blessing on the man of Sulaym, and they told him what Tha'laba had done and what the man of Sulaym had done. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 371}

God, blessed and exalted, sent down about him "among them are those who made a covenant with God that if He gave them of His bounty they would give alms and be of the righteous", to the words "they used to lie". A kinsman of Tha'laba was with the Messenger of God, heard it, went out to him and said "woe to you, Tha'laba, God has sent down about you such and such", and Tha'laba went to the Prophet and asked him to accept his alms, and he said "God has forbidden me to accept your alms", and Tha'laba began to scatter dust on his head, and the Messenger of God said to him "this is your own doing; I commanded you and you did not obey me". When the Messenger of God refused to take it he went back to his house, and the Messenger of God died without accepting anything from him. Then he went to Abu Bakr when he became caliph and said "you know my standing with the Messenger of God and my place among the Helpers, so accept my alms", and Abu Bakr said "the Messenger of God did not accept them, so shall I?", and Abu Bakr died without taking them. When Umar took charge he went to him and said "Commander of the Faithful, accept my alms", and he said "the Messenger of God did not accept them, nor Abu Bakr, so shall I accept them from you?", and he died without accepting them; then Uthman took charge and he went to him and asked him to accept his alms, and he said "the Messenger of God did not accept them, nor Abu Bakr, nor Umar, so shall I accept them from you?", and he did not accept them from him. Tha'laba died in the caliphate of Uthman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 372}

The editor notes that Hisham ibn Ammar is reliable but changed in old age, that Mu'an ibn Rifa'a is lax in hadith, that Ali ibn Yazid al-Alhani is very weak and all his reports from al-Qasim are weak, that there is great confusion in the entry on Tha'laba ibn Hatib al-Ansari as to whether he is one man or two, one who was at Badr and Uhud and was killed at Uhud and another who is the subject of this story, that al-Haythami says of the report that it contains Ali ibn Yazid al-Alhani, who is abandoned, that the report is utterly weak with no corroboration, and that al-Suyuti cites it in al-Durr al-Manthur and attributes it to a number of compilers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 373}

Report 16988, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that a man of the Helpers came upon a gathering of the Helpers and said that if God gave him wealth he would pay everyone with a right his right, and God gave him wealth and he did with it what you hear. It was mentioned to us that the Prophet of God related that when Moses came to Banu Israel with the Torah, they said "the Torah is long and we have no leisure for it; ask your Lord for a summary of the matter we can keep and still be free for our livelihood", and he said "wait, wait, this is the book of God, the light of God and the protection of God", and they repeated it and he repeated his answer, three times; God revealed to Moses "what are My servants saying?", and he said "Lord, they say such and such", and He said "I command them three things which, if they keep them, will bring them into Paradise: that when they come to the division of an inheritance they do not wrong in it, that they do not turn their eyes into houses until given leave, and that they do not eat food until they have washed as for prayer". He took them back to his people and they rejoiced and thought they would keep them, and it was not long before they turned aside and were cut off. When the Prophet of God related this about Banu Israel, he said "stand surety for six, and I shall stand surety for Paradise for you"; they said "what are they, Messenger of God?", and he said "when you speak do not lie, when you promise do not break, when you are trusted do not betray, and lower your eyes, your hands and your private parts: your eyes from betrayal, your hands from theft and your private parts from adultery". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 374}

Report 16989, from al-Hasan, through Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id and Qatada: the Prophet used to say "three things, whoever has them becomes a hypocrite even if he fasts, prays and claims to be a Muslim: when he speaks he lies, when he is trusted he betrays and when he promises he breaks it". Others said rather that two men are meant, one Tha'laba and the other Mu'attib ibn Qushayr. Report 16990, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq and Amr ibn Ubayd: the ones among them who made the covenant with God were Tha'laba ibn Hatib and Mu'attib ibn Qushayr, both of Banu Amr ibn Awf, which follows report 16969 in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 374}

Reports 16991, 16992 and 16993, from Mujahid, by the routes of Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ishaq, Abdallah and Warqa': two men went out upon a seated company and said "by God, if God provides for us we shall give alms", and when God provided for them they were miserly with it; in report 16992, "so He made hypocrisy follow in their hearts because they broke what they promised God", when they said "we shall give alms" and did not. Report 16994, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: these are a kind of hypocrites, and when He gave them that they were miserly with it, and since they were miserly with it He made hypocrisy follow in them until the day they meet Him, with no repentance, forgiveness or pardon for them from it, as happened to Iblis when he was barred from repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 375}

Al-Tabari says: in this verse God, glorious is His praise, makes clear the sign of the people of hypocrisy, in His words "so He made hypocrisy follow in their hearts until the day they meet Him because they broke what they promised God and because they used to lie". A number of the Companions and Followers said the like, and reports to this effect have come from the Messenger of God. Report 16995, from Abdallah ibn Mas'ud, through Abu al-Sa'ib, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash, Umara and Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid: know the hypocrite by three things: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when he makes a pact he is false; and God sent down the confirmation of that in His book, "among them are those who made a covenant with God that if He gave them of His bounty", to the words "they used to lie". The editor says the chain is sound and stops at Ibn Mas'ud, and that al-Haythami cites it and says its men are the men of the Sahih. Report 16996, from Abdallah ibn Amr, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba, Simak and Subayh ibn Abdallah: three things, whoever has them is a hypocrite: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it and when he is trusted he betrays; and he recited this verse, "among them are those who made a covenant with God that if He gave them of His bounty they would give alms and be of the righteous", to the end of the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 376}

Report 16997, from Subayh ibn Abdallah al-Absi, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abu Dawud, Shu'ba and Simak: I asked Abdallah ibn Amr about the hypocrite, and he gave something like it. The editor notes that al-Firyabi relates it in his book on the description of hypocrisy with a sound chain stopping at a Companion, and that the hadith of the sign of the hypocrite is related by al-Bukhari and Muslim from Abu Hurayra and Abdallah ibn Amr. Report 16998, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through Muhammad ibn Ma'mar, Abu Hisham al-Makhzumi, Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad and Uthman ibn Hakim: I used to hear that the hypocrite is known by three things, lying, breaking a promise and betrayal, and I looked for them in God's book for a time and did not find them, then I found them in two places of God's book, His words "among them are those who made a covenant with God" up to "they used to lie", and His words "We offered the trust to the heavens and the earth", which is [[passage/quran-33-72|33:72]]. The editor says the manuscript has "two" and he kept it, because the passages named after it are three verses of al-Tawba and a verse of al-Ahzab, so he must mean two surahs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 377}

Report 16999, from al-Hasan, through al-Qasim ibn Bishr ibn Ma'ruf, Shababa and Muhammad al-Muharram: the Messenger of God said "three things, whoever has them is a hypocrite even if he prays and fasts and claims to be a Muslim: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it and when he is trusted he betrays"; I said to al-Hasan, "Abu Sa'id, if a man has a debt from me and meets me and presses me for it and I have nothing, and I fear he will imprison and ruin me, and I promise him I will pay at the new moon and do not, am I a hypocrite?", and he said "that is how the hadith came"; then he related from Abdallah ibn Amr that when death came to his father he said "marry off so-and-so, for I promised him I would marry him, so that I do not meet God with a third of hypocrisy", and I said "Abu Sa'id, can a third of a man be a hypocrite and two thirds a believer?", and he said "that is how the hadith came". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 378}

He said, I went on pilgrimage and met Ata ibn Abi Rabah and told him the hadith I had heard from al-Hasan and what I said to him and what he said to me, and he said "could you not say to him, tell me of the brothers of Joseph, did they not promise their father and break it, tell him and lie to him, was he not trusting them and they betrayed him, were they hypocrites? Were they not prophets, their father a prophet and their grandfather a prophet?". I said to Ata, "Abu Muhammad, tell me the origin of hypocrisy and of this hadith", and he said, Jabir ibn Abdallah told me that the Messenger of God said this hadith only about the hypocrites in particular, who told the Prophet and lied to him, whom he entrusted with his secret and who betrayed him, and who promised to go out with him on a raid and broke it. He said Abu Sufyan left Mecca and Gabriel came to the Prophet and said "Abu Sufyan is in such and such a place", and the Prophet said to his Companions "Abu Sufyan is in such and such a place, so go out to him, and keep it secret", and a man of the hypocrites wrote to him "Muhammad wants you, so take care", and God sent down the verse "do not betray God and the Messenger and betray your trusts while you know", [[passage/quran-8-27|8:27]], and sent down about the hypocrites "among them are those who made a covenant with God that if He gave them of His bounty" up to "so He made hypocrisy follow in their hearts until the day they meet Him because they broke what they promised God and because they used to lie"; "so when you meet al-Hasan, give him my greeting and tell him the origin of this hadith and what I said to you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 378}

He said, I came to al-Hasan and said "Abu Sa'id, your brother Ata sends you greetings", and told him the hadith he had related and what he said to me, and al-Hasan took my hand and raised it and said "people of Iraq, can you not be like this man? He heard a hadith from me and did not accept it until he worked out its origin; Ata is right, that is how the hadith is, and it is about the hypocrites in particular". The editor notes that Muhammad al-Muharram is rejected in hadith, that the report is thoroughly objectionable and al-Bukhari alludes to it in his larger History, and explains the verb for raising the hand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 379}

Report 17000, from al-Hasan, through Ya'qub and Ibn Ulayya: the Messenger of God said "three things, whoever has them, even if he prays and fasts and claims to be a Muslim, is a hypocrite"; he was asked "what are they, Messenger of God?", and he said "when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it and when he is trusted he betrays". Report 17001, from Abdallah ibn Amr, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Mubashshir, al-Awza'i and Harun ibn Riyab: when death came to him he said "so-and-so asked for my son's hand and I said something to him like a promise; by God I shall not meet God with a third of hypocrisy, and I call you to witness that I have married her to him". The editor vouches for Mubashshir and Harun ibn Riyab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 379}

Some said the covenant these hypocrites made with God was something they intended within themselves and did not speak. Report 17002, from Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, through al-Qasim and al-Husayn: I went to sea and a strong wind struck us and some of us made vows, and I intended one and did not speak it; when I came to Basra I asked my father Sulayman, and he said to me "my son, fulfil it"; Mu'tamir said, Kahmas related to us from Sa'id ibn Thabit that about the words "among them are those who made a covenant with God" he said it was only something they intended within themselves and did not speak, "have you not heard His words, did they not know that God knows their secret and their private talk and that God is the knower of the unseen?" The editor says Kahmas is reliable, that he found no mention of Sa'id ibn Thabit and fears a corruption. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 380}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
