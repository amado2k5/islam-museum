**Commentary on verse 22 (pp. 21-23)**

Al-Tabari says: when Joseph reached his maturity, that is, the limit of his strength and vigour in youth, which falls between eighteen and sixty years, or some say forty. One says "the man's ashudd passed", meaning his strength; it is a plural for which no singular of the same stem is heard, and by analogy its singular would be "shadd". He cites a line whose author the editor could not identify and which he found nowhere else, and a line of Humayd that refers to "al-ashudd" with four full years. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "ashudd" and notes uncertainty about the vowelling of some words in the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 21}

The interpreters differed on what God meant by reaching maturity. Some said thirty-three years; reports 18957 to 18959, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Amr ibn Muhammad, Sufyan and Ibn Abi Najih; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Layth; report 18960, from Mujahid, from Ibn Abbas: some thirty-odd years. Others said twenty years; report 18961, from al-Dahhak, through Ali ibn al-Musayyab and Abu Rawq. It is also reported from Ibn Abbas by an unsatisfactory route that it is between eighteen and thirty years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 22}

Al-Tabari said: the soundest view is that God reported that He gave Joseph, when he reached maturity, judgement and knowledge; maturity is the limit of his strength and youth. It may be that He gave it to him at eighteen, or at twenty, or at thirty-three; there is no indication in God's book, no report from the Messenger and no consensus of the community which of these it was, so the right course is to say what God said until a proof is established for what is said. On "We gave him judgement and knowledge" he says: We gave him then understanding and knowledge; report 18962, from Mujahid: intelligence and knowledge before prophethood. The editor refers to the explanation of "judgement". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 23}

On "thus We reward the doers of good" he says: as I rewarded Joseph, giving him judgement and knowledge for his obedience to Me, establishing him in the land and rescuing him from his brothers' hands. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 23}

It continues: and rescued him from the hands of those who meant to kill him; thus We reward whoever does good in his work, obeys Me in My command and keeps from what I forbade of disobedience to Me. The editor refers to the explanations of "reward" and "doing good". Al-Tabari adds that although the wording applies to every doer of good, the one meant is Muhammad, God's prophet; God says to him: as I did this for Joseph after what he met from his brothers and the trial he endured, establishing him in the land and smoothing the countries for him, so I shall do for you, saving you from the idolaters of your people who set out against you in enmity, establishing you in the land and giving you judgement and knowledge, for that is My reward to those who do good in my command and prohibition. Report 18963, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "the doers of good" means "the rightly guided". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 24}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
