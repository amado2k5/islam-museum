**Commentary on verse 34: "except those who repent before you have power over them; know that God is forgiving and merciful", pp. 277-282**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over this. Some said it means except those who repented of their polytheism and their waging war on God and His messenger and spreading corruption, by Islam and entering belief, before the believers had power over them: the believers have no way against them by any of the penalties God made the recompense of those who fought Him and His messenger, and no one has a claim against them for what they took in the time of their unbelief and war, of property, blood or sanctity; as for a Muslim who fights Muslims or covenanted people and does what calls for a penalty, repentance does not lift the penalty of his sin, being between him and God, and the ruler must apply the prescribed penalty and take him for people's rights.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 277}

Report 11872, from 'Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri, says the verse was revealed about polytheists, so whoever of them repented before being overcome had no case against him, and the verse does not protect a Muslim from the penalty if he killed, corrupted or fought and then joined the unbelievers before being overcome: the penalty for what he did is applied. Reports 11873 to 11876, from Mujahid, al-Dahhak and Ibn 'Abbas, say this is for polytheists who did something in their polytheism and then repented and became Muslims, and that whoever came repenting and entered Islam was accepted and not held to the past.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 278}

Reports 11877 and 11878, from Qatada and 'Ata' al-Khurasani, say this is for polytheists; whoever of them harmed Muslims while at war with them, took property and shed blood, and then repented before being overcome, had the past written off.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 279}

Others said the verse's ruling is about those who wage war on God and His messenger from among the people of Islam: whoever of them cut the road while staying in Islam and then asked for safe-conduct and was given it for his crimes, or did so as one who left Islam, joined the abode of war, then asked for safe-conduct and was given it; once the ruler gives him safety for past crimes, no one has a claim against him for blood or property taken before his repentance and the ruler's pledge. Report 11879, from al-Sha'bi, says Haritha ibn Badr went out as a warrior, terrorised the road, shed blood and took property, then came repenting before being overcome, and 'Ali ibn Abi Talib accepted his repentance and granted him a published safe-conduct for the blood and property he had taken.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 279}

Report 11880, from al-Sha'bi, says Haritha ibn Badr fought in 'Ali's time, went to al-Hasan ibn 'Ali to seek safe-conduct for him from 'Ali, who refused, then to Ibn Ja'far, who refused, and then to Sa'id ibn Qays al-Hamdani, who gave him safety and took him in; when 'Ali prayed the dawn prayer Sa'id came and asked what the recompense was for those who wage war on God and His messenger; he answered that they be killed, crucified, cut on opposite sides or banished, and then said "except those who repent before you have power over them"; Sa'id asked: even if it is Haritha ibn Badr? He said: even Haritha ibn Badr. Sa'id asked: so Haritha has come repenting and is safe? He said yes; Sa'id brought him, he gave allegiance and 'Ali accepted it and wrote him safety. Report 11881 is similar and adds that Haritha spoke verses praising Hamdan for fearing God and for its preacher ruling by the Book. The editor identifies Haritha ibn Badr and Sa'id ibn Qays al-Hamdani and mentions what Sa'id did after 'Ali gave him safety.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 280}

Report 11882, from al-Suddi, says his repenting before being overcome is that he writes to the ruler asking safe-conduct for what he killed and corrupted in the land, and the ruler is bound to grant it; once he does, he comes and puts his hand in the ruler's hand, and no one may pursue him or take him for blood he shed or property he took; if the ruler seizes him and he claims to have repented before the ruler gave him safety, the penalty is applied. Report 11883, from Makhul, says that if the ruler gives him safety he is safe and the penalty for what he did is not applied.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 281}

Others said the sense is that anyone who comes repenting from banditry before being overcome, whether he asked the ruler for safe-conduct and was given it or not, after coming submissive and abandoning war, is covered. Report 11884, from al-Sha'bi, says a man of Murad came to Abu Musa, governor of Kufa in 'Uthman's rule, after the obligatory prayer, saying this is the place of one seeking refuge in you: I am so-and-so of Murad, I fought God and His messenger and spread corruption, and I have repented before you had power over me. Abu Musa stood and said this is so-and-so, who fought God and His messenger and spread corruption and repented before being overcome, so whoever meets him should treat him only well. The man stayed as long as God willed, then went out, and God overtook him for his sins and he was killed.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 282}

Report 11885, from al-Sha'bi, is similar. Report 11886 has al-Walid ibn Muslim asking Malik about the warrior who terrorised the road, took blood and property, and joined the abode of war or held out within Muslim lands and then came repenting before being overcome; he said his repentance is accepted and he is not followed for any of his deeds, unless property is found with him that is identifiable, which is returned to its owner, or the guardian of someone he killed demands blood, proved by evidence or confession, in which case he is retaliated against; and for blood whose guardians did not seek it the ruler does not pursue him. Al-Awza'i said his repentance is accepted if he warred on the public and the leaders and had a stronghold or band he sheltered with, or joined the abode of war, and he is not followed for anything, as report 11887 says he heard from al-Zuhri. Report 11888, from al-Layth ibn Sa'd, says that if he declared war on public and leaders, took blood and property, and held out or joined the abode of war and then came repenting, his repentance is accepted and he is not followed for anything he did in his war, even if his guardian demands it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 283}

Report 11889, from al-Layth, says 'Ali al-Asadi fought, terrorised the road and took blood and property, was sought by the leaders and the public, and held out until he heard a man recite the verse of [[passage/quran-39-53|39:53]] and asked him to repeat it, sheathed his sword and came repenting to Medina, washed, prayed the dawn prayer in the Prophet's mosque and sat with Abu Hurayra; at daylight people recognised him and stood to him, and he said there was no way against him, he came repenting before they had power over him; Abu Hurayra said he spoke truly, took his hand to Marwan ibn al-Hakam governing Medina, and said this is 'Ali, who has come repenting, so there is no way against him, and he was left. He then went out repenting as a fighter at sea, met the Byzantines, leapt onto their ship, which listed with them and him, and they all drowned. The editor says he did not know the commander Musa ibn Ishaq or 'Ali al-Asadi. Report 11890, from 'Ata', is that for a man who stole and came with it repenting without being seized, there is no sin on him, and he recited the verse.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 284}

Report 11891, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, says if he came repenting, having neither seized property nor shed blood, he is left, which is the verse's sense. Others said the exception means one who repents of his warring after he joined the abode of unbelief in his warring; but one whose banditry was while living in the abode of Islam within the mass of the community, his repentance lifts nothing of God's prescribed penalties or Muslims' and covenanters' rights. Report 11892, from 'Urwa, says he was asked about one who robbed in Islam, committed offences and came repenting, and said his repentance is not accepted, for if accepted from them they would grow bold, but if he fled to the enemy and came repenting he would see no penalty on him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 285}

Report 11893, from 'Urwa, says the opposite, that the penalty is applied for what he fled from and no one may give safety for it. Others said that if his banditry was in the abode of Islam with no stronghold to shelter in, and he came repenting before being overcome, his repentance lifts nothing of the penalty or people's rights; but if he sheltered with a band that protected him from the Muslims' authority and then came repenting, his repentance lifts all he did in the days of his banditry, except where he committed a prescribed offence or told his companions to do what brings penalty or a fine to a Muslim or covenanter while not sheltering with a band, in which case he is held for it. Report 11894, from al-Awza'i, says that if a robber or band cut the road and took blood and property without a group to shelter in and entered the mass of the people for safety, then came repenting before being overcome, the repentance is not accepted and the penalty is applied.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 286}

Report 11895, from al-Awza'i, says that if he fled from his offence in the abode of Islam and a ruler gave him safety, the safety is not valid; if he joined the abode of war and asked a ruler for safety for his deeds, the ruler should not give it; if the ruler gave it without knowing his deeds he is safe; if he knew them, the ruler is liable and owes the blood-money for what he took in blood or property. Report 11896, from Rabi'a, says his repentance is accepted and he is not followed for his deeds in war, except where someone claims blood he shed in peacetime before his war, for which he is retaliated against. Report 11897, from al-Hakam ibn 'Utayba, says al-Hajjaj used to be discerning: he gave safety to a man who had fought and said look whether he did anything before he went out. Others said repentance lifts God's penalty owed through his warring but not human rights, among them al-Shafi'i, as report 11898 relays.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 287}

Al-Tabari chooses that the soundest view is that the repentance of a warrior who holds out by himself or with a band, before being overcome, lifts the worldly burdens of his days of warring and banditry, including prescribed penalties, fines, retaliation and talion, except property of Muslims and covenanters still identifiable in his hand, which is returned to its owners, by the agreement of all that this is the ruling for the band that holds out, wages war on God and His messenger and strives for corruption by leaving Islam; so it is for any who holds out and spreads corruption, one or many.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 287}

As for one who steals in secret, robs by catching someone off guard, or draws a weapon in the open on some passer-by and cannot resist when sought, God's ruling on him, repentant or not, stands, and he is held for the rights of one whose property he took or whose guardian he harmed by blood or deceit, his repentance being between him and God, by analogy with the agreement of all that if he did any of this while at peace with the Muslims and then fought them, his war does not lift any right of God or of a human.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 288}

He holds that "except those who repent before you have power over them" is clear proof for one who understands that the ruling God mentioned for warriors applies to Muslims and covenanters and not to polytheists who set up war on the Muslims; for were it a ruling for polytheists at war, their Islam would not drop what was due for killing and what Muslims have against polytheists at war, if they converted after our power over them, yet the Muslims agree that the Islam of a warring polytheist drops that after power over him as it would before it. He explains "know that God is forgiving and merciful" as: know, believers, that God does not hold to account one of those at war with God and His messenger who repented, but pardons and covers his sins and does not expose him by punishment in this world or the next, merciful to him in that pardon.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 289}
