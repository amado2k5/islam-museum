## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 15 to 16 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Then after that, you will surely die": Then you, O people, after your creation, are a mortal creation and We made you human beings upright, dead and returned to dust as you were. Then after your death, your promises will be worn-out remains, resurrected from dust as a new creation as We began you. It was said: "Then after that, you will die" because it is news about a state that happened to them that never happened. Similarly, the Arabs say to one who has not died: he is dead and dead for a little, but they do not say to someone who has died a hundred dead. Likewise, he is greedy for what you have if described as greed. If he is told he will act but does not, it is said he is coveting what you have tomorrow. The same applies to everything we mentioned.
