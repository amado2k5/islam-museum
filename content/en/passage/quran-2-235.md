## Summary
Verse 235 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 235 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-235|2:235]]) by saying that it means men are not at fault for alluding to marriage with women in their waiting period after their husbands' death, without spelling out a marriage contract. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 95} He gives what was said about the allusion that is permitted. From Ibn 'Abbas, it is to say, I want to marry, or I love a woman of such and such a sort, hinting to her in fair words (5098, 5099); it is what does not amount to openly proposing (5100, 5101); it is to tell the woman in her waiting period, I do not want to marry anyone but you, God willing, or I wish I could find a good woman, without openly proposing while she is in her waiting period (5102); it is to say, if you see fit do not marry before me, and I wish God would bring us together, and there is no harm (5103), and the like (5104). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 95} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 96} From 'Ubayda, he mentions her to her guardian and says, do not let anyone get ahead of me with her (5105); in Mujahid's report a man said to a woman at her husband's funeral, do not forestall me by yourself, and she said, I already have been forestalled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 96} Mujahid disliked saying, do not forestall me (5107), and held that the allusion is to say, you are lovely, you are valued, you are headed for good (5106, 5108, 5109); from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, to say, I want to marry, and if I marry I will treat my wife well (5110), or, I will give to you, be good to you, do such and such for you (5111). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 96} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 97} From 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim (5112) and al-Qasim ibn Muhammad (5113, 5115) it is to say, I desire you, I am eager for you, you please me, and the like; from 'Ata', to allude without disclosing anything, and she says, I hear what you say, without promising anything (5114). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 97} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 98} From Ibrahim, there is no harm in a gift as part of the allusion to marriage (5116, 5117). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 98} Al-Sha'bi (5118) and Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (5119) say much the same; al-Sha'bi adds that he must not take a pledge from her not to marry another (5120); Ibn Zayd from his father, everything short of resolving on the marriage contract is the permitted allusion (5121); Sufyan (5122). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 98} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 99} From Sakina bint Hanzala, Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn 'Ali came to her while she was in her waiting period and spoke of his kinship with the Messenger and his standing; she said, God forgive you, do you propose to me in my waiting period when people take religion from you? He said, I only told you of my kinship with the Messenger and my place; the Messenger went in to Umm Salama, who had been married to her cousin Abu Salama who died, and kept mentioning to her his rank with God, leaning on his hand until the mat left a mark on it, and that was not a proposal (5123). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 99} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 100} From Ibn Shihab, there is no blame on one who alludes to them in proposing before they become free, if they keep it in their hearts (5124); from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, to say, you are dear to me, I desire you, and God is sending you good and provision (5125). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 100} The editor identifies a narrator of 5123 and says he found the report only in al-Baghawi's commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 100}

Al-Tabari then gives the grammarians' differences on the word for proposal. Some said it means mention and also the testimony of faith, so that the sense is mentioning the women before them, which the editor says is al-Akhfash's view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 101} Others said the two forms are verbal nouns of the verb for seeking, and the word in surah Ta-Ha ([[passage/quran-20-95|20:95]]) is said to belong here, while the form with damma is what is spoken in a sermon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 101} Al-Tabari's own view is that the word is the noun of a single act from "he proposed to so-and-so", like the forms for a single sitting from "he sat". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 101}

The commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-235|2:235]]) continues. Al-Tabari says that "he proposed to so-and-so" means he asked her to marry him, which is his need, from the saying "what is your concern", meaning what do you need and what is your business; the allusion is the kind of speech by which the understanding hearer gets what its plain statement would convey; the editor says this wording is good and has no parallel in the dictionaries. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 102} He then explains the sentence about hiding the proposal in the heart: it means what you conceal in your hearts of proposing to them and resolving to marry them in their waiting period, and there is no blame in that either so long as you do not resolve on the marriage contract. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 102} He explains that the verbs for keeping something in the self mean to hide and cover, citing the passage in al-Saffat on protected eggs ([[passage/quran-37-49|37:49]]) and a line of verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 102} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 103} The interpreters said the same: Mujahid, concealing is mentioning her proposal in one's heart without showing it to her, all of which is lawful and known (5126, 5127); al-Suddi, that he enters, greets her and gives a gift if he wishes and says nothing (5128); al-Qasim ibn Muhammad the same (5129); Ibn Zayd, setting her marriage in his heart and keeping it secret (5130); Sufyan, secretly resolving to marry her (5131); al-Hasan, you kept it secret (5132). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 103} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 104}

Al-Tabari says that in God's allowing an allusion to marry a woman in her waiting period and forbidding the explicit statement, the difference between allusion and explicit statement in the meaning of speech is made clear. So an allusion to an accusation of adultery is not the same as stating it, and if the fixed penalty applied to allusion as to the explicit charge, the blame for alluding to proposal in the waiting period would equal that of resolving on the marriage contract; God's distinguishing between their rulings shows the difference of the rulings in the case of the accusation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 104} This is al-Tabari's reasoning, and the legal question of allusive accusation is disputed among jurists and not settled by the museum.

He then explains the sentence that God knows they will think of the women: that they will mention the widows in proposal in their hearts and with their tongues: al-Hasan says it means the proposal (5133, 5135); Mujahid, your thinking of her in your heart (5134). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 104} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 105}

Al-Tabari then gives the interpreters' differences over what "secret" is, which they were forbidden to arrange with the widows. Some said it is fornication: Jabir ibn Zayd (5136); Abu Mijlaz (5137-5140); al-Hasan (5141-5143, 5147, 5151, 5153), with Qatada's report from him saying indecency (5148); Ibrahim (5144, 5145); Qatada (5146); al-Dahhak (5149); Ibn 'Abbas, the secret is suspicion, a man would come in under suspicion while alluding to marriage, and God forbade it except for saying what is right (5150); al-Rabi', indecency and soft talk (5152). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 105} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 106} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 107} Others said it means not taking their pledges and promises during their waiting period not to marry anyone else: Ibn 'Abbas, do not say to her, I am in love, promise me you will marry no one else (5154); Sa'id ibn Jubayr, do not settle with her that she will not marry another (5155); al-Sha'bi, 'Amir, Mujahid and 'Ikrima, he does not take her pledge in her waiting period not to marry another (5156-5160); al-Suddi, he says, keep yourself for me, I will marry you, and takes a pledge from her (5161). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 107} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 108} The editor explains words in the manuscript and printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 107} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 108}

The reports on ([[passage/quran-2-235|2:235]]) continue. From Qatada, it concerns the man who takes a woman's pledge in her waiting period not to marry another; God forbade it, permitted proposing and fair words, and forbade indecency and soft talk (5162). From Sufyan, he secretly promises her something on the condition that she marry no other (5163); from Mujahid, the secret promise is taking a pledge and covenant from her to keep herself for him and marry no other (5164, 5165). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 109} Others said it means that the man says to her, do not forestall me by yourself: Mujahid (5166-5169), and that it is not lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 109} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 110} Others said it means not marrying them secretly in their waiting period: Ibn Zayd, he marries her secretly, then keeps her until she is free, then shows it and takes her in (5170, 5171). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 110}

Al-Tabari then chooses that the "secret" here is fornication, that is intercourse, because the Arabs call intercourse and a man's coming to a woman "a secret", since it takes place between men and women out of sight, citing lines by Ru'ba ibn al-'Ajjaj and al-Hutay'a. The word in their speech has three senses, what a person hides in himself, intercourse, and the best and noblest part, and the last is plainly not meant, so only what the self conceals and intercourse remain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 110} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 111} He argues that a promise in words is not what is meant: if the secret were taking a pledge not to marry another, the secret could no longer be what is hidden in hearts or said without being known, public things would count as secret, and that goes against what is understood in the language of those to whom the Qur'an came; and if the forbidden thing were only promising secretly between them, then promising marriage and proposal plainly in public would be allowed, which is outside the position of the whole community; and a proposal and marriage that the woman promised the man would be with a guardian and witnesses in public, so how could it be called secret. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 111} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 112} He concludes that the verse means: there is no blame on you for what you hinted to widows in their waiting period of your wish to marry them, without stating it, since you kept your need of them hidden in yourselves while they are in their waiting period; God knows you will mention proposing to them, so He allowed the hint and lifted blame from what your hearts hid; but He forbade you to promise them intercourse in their waiting period, by one of you saying to a woman in her waiting period, I have married you in my mind and am only waiting for your waiting period to end, and asking her thereby to let him have intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 112} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 113} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument; other scholars' views are given above under their own names with no museum ruling.

He then explains the exception for fair speech: it is, he says, an exception from a different kind, meaning "but", so God allowed fair speech to her in her waiting period, which is the hint He permitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 113} Sa'id ibn Jubayr, to say, I desire you and I hope we will be joined (5172); Ibn 'Abbas, saying, if you see fit do not forestall me by yourself (5173); Mujahid, the hint (5174, 5175); al-Suddi, the man goes in to the woman in her waiting period, praises her, mentions his desire and that what is decreed will be (5176); Sufyan (5177); Ibn Zayd, to say, I will give you this and that, all of it before the contract, and he says the prohibition on resolving the contract abrogated this (5178); al-Dahhak, a woman divorced or widowed, and the man says, keep yourself for me, I desire you, and she says, so do I (5179). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 113} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 114} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 115} The editor explains a word in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 114}

Al-Tabari then explains the ban on resolving on the contract: do not validate a marriage contract during the woman's waiting period, making it binding between you before the period ends, until what God prescribed reaches its term, the term He stated in the verse on the widow's waiting period ([[passage/quran-2-234|2:234]]); a man may not marry a woman in her waiting period and resolve on the contract until it ends: Mujahid, until the waiting period is over (5180). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 115}
