**Commentary on verse 36 (pp. 529-534)**

Al-Tabari says on "those who disbelieve spend their wealth to bar from God's path; they will spend it, then it will be a regret for them, then they will be overcome; and those who disbelieve will be gathered to Gehenna" [[passage/quran-8-36|8:36]]: those who disbelieved in God and His Messenger spend their wealth, giving it to others like them among the polytheists to strengthen themselves with it to fight the Messenger of God and those who believe in him, to bar the believers from belief; they will spend their wealth on that, then their spending will be a regret for them, that is a remorse, since their wealth goes and they do not gain what they hope for, putting out God's light and raising the word of unbelief over God's word, for God raises His word and makes the word of unbelief the lowest; then the believers overcome them and God gathers those who disbelieved in Him and His Messenger to Gehenna, where they are punished. He says it is a great regret and remorse for whoever lived and whoever died among them: the living man's wealth is stripped and goes in vain without any gain and he returns overcome, subdued and plundered, while the dead man is killed and stripped and hurried to God's fire. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "spending", "barring", "regret" and "gathering". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 529}

The one who undertook the spending God mentions in this verse was, it is said, Abu Sufyan. Report 16056, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: it came down about Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, who hired two thousand of the Ahabish of Banu Kinana at Uhud and fought the Prophet with them, they being those of whom Ka'b ibn Malik speaks in two lines describing the unbelievers' army as a wave with the unarmoured and the armoured among them, three thousand in number; report 16057, from Ibn Abza: he hired two thousand at Uhud besides those he summoned from the Arabs; report 16058, from al-Hakam ibn Utayba: he spent on the polytheists at Uhud forty ounces of gold; report 16059, from Qatada: when Abu Sufyan came with the caravan to Mecca he gathered the people and called them to fight until he campaigned against the Prophet the next year, Badr being in Ramadan, on the morning of the seventeenth, a Friday, and Uhud in Shawwal, on Saturday the eleventh, in the fourth year; report 16060, from al-Suddi: remorse on the day of resurrection, and woe; reports 16061 and 16062, from Mujahid: about Abu Sufyan's spending on the unbelievers at Uhud. The editor explains "the Ahabish", tribes that allied with Quraysh, and the words "unarmoured", "armoured" and "the choice", corrects the verse's transmission, explains "summoned" and "gathering", and found no entry for Khattab al-Usfuri except in Ibn Abi Hatim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 530} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 531} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 532}

Report 16063, from Ibn Ishaq from al-Zuhri, Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Hibban, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, al-Husayn ibn Abd al-Rahman and others of their scholars: when those of the unbelievers of Quraysh who were put in the well were killed at Badr and their routed men returned to Mecca and Abu Sufyan returned with his caravan, Abd Allah ibn Abi Rabi'a, Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl and Safwan ibn Umayya walked among men of Quraysh whose fathers, sons and brothers had been killed at Badr, and spoke with Abu Sufyan and those who had trade in that caravan, saying, Quraysh, Muhammad has wronged you and killed your best, so help us with this wealth against him, that we may take revenge for those of us who were killed; and they did; and about them God sent down this verse. Report 16064, from Ibn Ishaq, about the group who went to Abu Sufyan; report 16065, from Ata ibn Dinar: it came down about Abu Sufyan ibn Harb. The editor lists differences of the printed edition and manuscript from Ibn Hisham's Sira in the chain and in "as was reported from Ibn Abbas", where the Sira has "as one of the people of knowledge told me", and identifies Sa'id ibn Abi Ayyub and Ata ibn Dinar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 532} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 533}

Some said it rather means the polytheists of Badr; report 16066, from al-Dahhak: they are the people of Badr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 533}

Abu Ja'far said: the right saying to my mind is what we said, that God reported of those of Quraysh's polytheists who disbelieved in Him that they spend their wealth to bar from God's path, and did not tell us which of them He meant, but made the report general for those who disbelieved. It may be He meant those who spent their wealth to fight the Messenger and his Companions at Uhud, or those who spent that at Badr, or both groups; being so, the right course is to make it general as God made general the unbelievers of Quraysh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 534}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
