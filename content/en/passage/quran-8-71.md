**Commentary on verse 71 (pp. 75-76)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses His prophet: if these captives in your hands intend treachery against you, that is betrayal, scheming and deceit, showing in speech the opposite of what is in their selves, then they betrayed God before, that is they went against His command before the battle of Badr, and the believers were given power over them at Badr; God knows what they say with their tongues and what they hide, and is wise in managing them and the affairs of His creation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 75}

Report 16328, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and Ata al-Khurasani: it means al-Abbas and his companions in their statement that they believed and would advise sincerely; if their statement were treachery, they had betrayed God before, having disbelieved and fought the Prophet, and God gave him power over them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 76}

Report 16329, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was told that a man wrote for the Prophet, then apostatised and turned hypocrite and joined the polytheists at Mecca, saying Muhammad wrote only what he wished; an Ansari vowed that if God gave him power over the man he would strike him with the sword. On the day of the conquest the Messenger gave the people security except Abdullah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh, Miqyas ibn Subaba, Ibn Khatal and a woman who used to curse the Prophet every morning. Uthman brought Ibn Abi Sarh, his foster brother, saying he had come repentant and remorseful, and the Prophet turned away. When the Ansari heard of it he came with his sword hung on him and circled him, watching the Messenger in hope of a signal; then the Prophet held out his hand and took his pledge, telling the Ansari he had held back so that he would fulfil his vow. The man said he had feared the Prophet and asked why he had not signalled him, and the Prophet said it is not fitting for a prophet to signal covertly. The editor gives the spelling of Miqyas's name, explains words such as "circled", "held back" and "signal", and refers to a differently worded report in the Musnad of Ahmad from Anas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 76}

Report 16330 begins with its chain from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn and Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 76}

We stop here at the foot of p. 76, in the middle of the commentary on verse 71 (at the start of report 16330); the next batch begins at p. 77. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 76}
