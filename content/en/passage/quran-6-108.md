## Summary
Verse 108 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 108 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says that God tells His prophet Muhammad and the believers not to revile those whom the polytheists call upon besides God, of gods and rivals, lest the polytheists revile God out of ignorance of their Lord and in transgression without knowledge. Report 13738, from Ibn Abbas (through Ali ibn Abi Talha): they said, "Muhammad, you will stop reviling our gods or we will lampoon your Lord"; so God forbade them to revile their idols lest they revile God in transgression without knowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 33}

Report 13739, from Qatada: the Muslims used to revile the idols of the unbelievers, who would answer by reviling God, so God forbade them to bring reviling on their Lord, for they were an ignorant people with no knowledge of God (the editor's footnote explains that "to expose someone to reviling" is to draw abuse onto him, and cites a saying of Abu Hurayra on manners toward one's father in the same sense). Then report 13740, from al-Suddi: when death came to Abu Talib, Quraysh said, "Let us go to this man and ask him to tell his nephew to stop troubling us, for we are ashamed to kill him after Abu Talib dies, lest the Arabs say 'he protected him, and when he died they killed him'." Abu Sufyan, Abu Jahl, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, Umayya and Ubayy the sons of Khalaf, Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, Amr ibn al-As and al-Aswad ibn al-Bakhtari went, sending ahead a man called al-Muttalib to ask leave of Abu Talib. They entered and said: "You are our elder and chief, and Muhammad has harmed us and our gods; we would like you to call him and forbid him to mention our gods, and we will leave him and his God." Abu Talib called him, the Prophet came, and Abu Talib said: these are your people and your cousins. The Prophet asked what they wanted and they said: "We want you to leave us and our gods, and we leave you and your God." {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 34}

Report 13740 concludes: Abu Talib said, "Your people have been fair to you, so accept it from them." The Prophet said: "If I give you this, will you give me one word which, if you say it, you will rule the Arabs and the non-Arabs will submit to you through it and pay you tribute?" Abu Jahl said: "Yes, by your father, we will give it and ten like it; what is it?" He said: "Say: there is no god but God." They refused and recoiled. Abu Talib said, "Nephew, say something else, for your people are alarmed by it." He said: "Uncle, I am not one to say anything else until they bring me the sun and put it in my hand; even if they brought me the sun and put it in my hand I would say nothing else", meaning to make them despair. They grew angry and said: "Stop reviling our gods, or we will revile you and the one who commands you." That is the meaning of "lest they revile God in transgression without knowledge". The editor's footnotes settle some wordings of the report from the manuscript and from Ibn Kathir's commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 35}

Report 13741, from Qatada: the Muslims used to revile the idols of the unbelievers, and the unbelievers reviled God in transgression without knowledge, so God sent down the prohibition; report 13742, from Ibn Zayd: if you revile his god he will revile yours, so do not revile their gods. Al-Tabari says the authoritative readers of the regions agreed on reading "'adwan" with fatha on the ayn, sukun on the dal and a light waw, as a verbal noun from "so-and-so 'ada against so-and-so", meaning he wronged and transgressed against him ("ya'du 'adwan, 'uduwwan and 'udwanan"), "i'tida'" being the "ifti'al" form of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 35}

It is reported of al-Hasan al-Basri that he read "'uduwwan" with a doubled waw (report 13743, from Ahmad ibn Yusuf, from al-Qasim ibn Sallam, from Hajjaj, from Harun, from Uthman ibn Sa'd, on whom the editor gives a biographical note, adding that scholars criticised him). It is also mentioned that some Basrans read "'aduwwan" (the dal doubled), turning its sense to a plural, as in 26:77 and 60:1, so that its accusative is a circumstantial clause from "the polytheists", meaning: the polytheists, who are God's enemies, revile God without knowledge (Ibn Khalawayh attributes this reading to some Meccans, and al-Zamakhshari names Ibn Kathir). Al-Tabari concludes: the correct reading in my view is that of those who read with fatha on the ayn and a light waw, because of the agreement of the authoritative readers, and it is not allowed to go against what they have agreed on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 36}

He then explains "thus We made the deeds of every community seem fair to them; then their return is to their Lord, and He will tell them what they used to do": as We made worship of idols and obedience to Satan seem fair to those who set up idols and images as equals to their Lord, by Our abandoning them from obedience to the Merciful, so We made fair to every group that agreed on some deed, of obedience or disobedience to God, the deed on which they agreed; then their return and destination is to their Lord, who will make them stand and tell them of the deeds they did in this world and then repay them for them, good for good and evil for evil, or pardon by His bounty, so long as it is not associating others with Him or unbelief. The footnotes refer to earlier commentary on "zayyana", "umma" and "marji'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 37}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
