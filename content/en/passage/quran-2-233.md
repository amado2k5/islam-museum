## Summary
Verse 233 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 233 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari begins the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-233|2:233]]) by saying that "the mothers" means the women separated from their husbands who have children borne to them by those husbands, before the separation or after it from earlier intercourse, and that they have the better right to nurse them than others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 30} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 31} For him this is not an obligation from God upon the mother to nurse her child where the child's father is alive and well off, since God said in the surah of divorce ([[passage/quran-65-6|65:6]]), in sense, that if the parents disagree about the pay another woman will nurse for him, so He did not make nursing compulsory upon her; the statement about mothers therefore marks the end of the nursing period, which, when the parents differ after it, becomes the limit that separates their claims, and does not show that nursing is a duty upon them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 31} The editor explains that surah al-Talaq is the sixty-fifth surah and is called the shorter surah of women to distinguish it from the longer one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 31} He then explains the two years as two years, and Mujahid also says two years (4948, 4949). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 31} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 32} The root of the word for year is from the saying that a thing "turned", moved over. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 32}

The commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-233|2:233]]) continues. Al-Tabari asks what the word for completeness adds after naming two years, and answers that the Arabs sometimes say someone stayed in a place for two years, two days or two months when he stayed a day and part of another, so the word was added to tell the hearer that two whole years are meant, not one and part of another; they also use a year or a time for an act done in a moment, since they do not mean to count days but to name the time in which the thing happened. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 32} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 33} He links this to the passage in al-Baqara on the counted days and hastening in two days ([[passage/quran-2-203|2:203]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 32} He says that since nursing is permissible within the two years though they are not the whole span, and since the unqualified wording would allow for a year and part of another, the added word removes doubt and shows the time at which the limit of nursing is complete, at the end of both years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 33} The editor notes the corrections between manuscript and printed text and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 33}

Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over the limit that the verse points to, whether it is a limit for every infant or for some. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 33} Some said it is a limit for some and not others. From Ibn 'Abbas, for the woman who gives birth at six months, she nurses two full years; if at seven, twenty-three months, to make thirty months; if at nine, twenty-one months (4950); the same from 'Ikrima, not traced to Ibn 'Abbas (4951). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 34} From Abu 'Ubayd, the client of 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, a woman who gave birth at six months was brought before 'Uthman, who disapproved; Ibn 'Abbas said that when nursing is complete the pregnancy was six months, and he cited the verse in al-Ahqaf ([[passage/quran-46-15|46:15]]), so 'Uthman let her go (4952). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 34} The editor gives a note on Abu 'Ubayd and corrects the name from 'Abd al-Razzaq's Musannaf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 34}

Others said it is the limit of nursing for any infant whose parents differ over it, where one wants to reach it and the other to fall short: from Ibn 'Abbas, God made the nursing two full years for whoever wants to complete it, and then permitted weaning before or after by mutual consent and consultation (4953); from 'Ata', if the mother wants less she is bound to reach two years, and not to go beyond unless the father wishes (4954); from al-Thawri, if the father wants weaning before two years and the mother does not agree, he may not, and likewise if she wants it and he does not; if they agree, they wean him (4955). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 35} The editor notes that the printed text differs from the manuscript on who wills the extra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 35}

Others said that God showed there is no nursing after two years, and only nursing within them counts: Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn 'Umar did not hold nursing after two years to make anything forbidden (4956, 4957); Ibn Mas'ud said nursing after two years, or in them after weaning, is not nursing (4958); 'Alqama saw a woman nursing after two years and told her not to (4959); al-Sha'bi said that what is given by mouth, nose or breast within the two years makes things forbidden and what comes after does not (4960); Ibn Mas'ud again, no nursing after weaning or after two years (4961); Ibn 'Abbas, only what grows flesh and builds bone makes things forbidden (4962); he also said no nursing after the weaning of two years (4963), and with Abu al-Duha, none except in these two years (4964). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 36} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 37} The editor identifies narrators in these chains, among them Hilal ibn al-'Ala', and gives what the critics said of him and his father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 36} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 37}

Others said it shows that mothers are obliged to nurse their infants two years, and that God then eased this for whoever wants to complete the nursing, leaving the choice to parents: if they want to complete, they complete two years, and if they want weaning sooner, it is up to them, looking to the infant's good: Qatada (4965); al-Rabi', who says it means divorced women, and that the concession came afterwards (4966). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 37} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 38} From al-Suddi, it means a man divorces his wife and has a child by her, and she nurses his child for what another woman would take (4967). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 38} Al-Tabari says the mothers here are those separated from their husbands as he described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 38} Report 4968 begins at the foot of p. 38 and continues on the next page.

The commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-233|2:233]]) continues. From al-Dahhak, it means when a man divorces his wife while she nurses his child (4968, 4969). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 38} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 39} Al-Tabari then makes his choice: the soundest view, for him, is what 'Ali ibn Abi Talha reported from Ibn 'Abbas, which 'Ata' and al-Thawri agreed with, together with what is reported from Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn 'Umar; namely that the verse points to the end-point of nursing when the parents differ, that there is no nursing after two years that makes anything forbidden, and that it applies to every infant, whether born at six, seven or nine months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 39} His argument is that when God sets a limit, what lies beyond it cannot share the ruling of what lies within it, or the limit would mean nothing, and that complete nursing is the completion of two years, so there is no sense in adding to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 39} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 40} It applies to every infant because God made it general and did not single out some, and he has already shown in his work on the principles of rulings that restricting a general text without a statement from God or His Messenger is invalid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 40} The editor points out gaps and corrections in the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 39}

Al-Tabari gives an objection: God set pregnancy and weaning at thirty months in the verse in al-Ahqaf ([[passage/quran-46-15|46:15]]), so what pregnancy lacks is added to nursing. He answers that this would require that a pregnancy of two years allows only six months of nursing and one of four years allows none, or else the objector would claim pregnancy never exceeds nine months and so contradict what is observed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 40} To another question he replies that the verse in al-Ahqaf is not a limit God imposed on His servants, as the limit in the nursing verse is, because command and prohibition apply only where servants can do or leave something, and women cannot shorten or lengthen a pregnancy; so it reports that some of His creatures are carried and weaned within thirty months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 41} He adds that if someone thought it described all people, then every person reaching forty would have to say what that verse reports, yet we find some whose unbelief grows firm at forty, so it describes some of them only; and he notes that births at seven months are more numerous than births after two or four years, as births at nine months are more numerous than at six or seven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 42} The editor notes that the old division of the copied manuscript ends here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 41}

Al-Tabari then gives the readers' differences over the clause on completing the nursing: most of the people of Medina, Iraq and Syria read it with the yod and the accusative, and some Hijazis with the ta' and the nominative; he chooses the first because the widely transmitted reading came that way and because completion belongs to the parents; he also relates a heard form with a different vowel on the word for nursing and says that, if sound, both forms are allowed like two other pairs of words, but the reading is with the fatha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 42} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 43} On the clause about the child's father he says it means the fathers of the infants owe the nursing mothers their provision, that provision is what feeds them of food and nourishment, clothing is what they wear, and "in what is right" is what is due to a woman like her from a man like him, since God knows people differ in wealth and poverty, and so told each to spend on those he must support according to his means, as in the verse in al-Talaq ([[passage/quran-65-7|65:7]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 43} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 44} From al-Dahhak, when he divorces his wife while she nurses his child and they agree she will nurse two full years, her provision and clothing fall on the father according to his means (4970); from Sufyan, food and clothing are the father's (4971); from al-Rabi', it is on the father (4972). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 44}

The commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-233|2:233]]) continues. On the sentence stating that a soul is charged only with what it can bear, al-Tabari says it means a soul is loaded only with what does not make things narrow for it nor become impossible to find when it wishes; God does not oblige men to spend on the women who nurse their children, once separated, beyond what they can afford, as in the verse in al-Talaq ([[passage/quran-65-7|65:7]]); Sufyan says the meaning is only what she can bear (4973). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 45} He explains that the word for capacity comes from the verb meaning that a matter was within one's reach, and that giving from one's capacity differs from giving from one's strain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 45} He rejects the view of some of the people of qadar that the meaning is that a soul is charged only with the acts of obedience it has been given power for, because on that basis the verse in al-Isra' ([[passage/quran-17-48|17:48]]) would mean that the people were given power over what they were refused; he calls this a self-contradiction and a baseless claim, so what God charges souls with within their capacity is different from what He says they cannot find a way to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 45} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 46} This is al-Tabari's reply; the editor notes that the manuscript misquotes the sentence from copyist haste. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 45}

He turns to the clause forbidding a mother or a father to be harmed through the child. He reports that readers differ: most of the people of Hijaz, Kufa and Syria read it with the passive vowel as a prohibition, and some Hijazis and Basrans read it with the nominative, which cannot carry a prohibition but is a statement joined to the preceding sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 46} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 47} Some Basran grammarians said the nominative has the sense of "it is not fitting", citing a line by Abu al-Lahham al-Taghlibi; al-Tabari answers that what is heard from the Arabs is that they put such verbs in the subjunctive with an implied particle, so if this were meant it would have to be read that way, and that for him the nominative means that harming a mother through her child has no place in God's religion, His rule and the conduct of Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 47} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 48} He chooses the reading with the fatha, because it is a prohibition from God to each parent against harming the other through the child, forbidden to both by the consensus of the Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 48} The editor judges that the passage on the readings is corrupt in both the manuscript and printed text, with something fallen out, and supplies his reconstruction of its sense, which is his own. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 46} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 47}

The interpreters read it as a prohibition. From Mujahid, the mother is not to refuse to nurse it so as to burden the father, and the father is not to stop the mother nursing so as to grieve her (4974, 4975). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 49} From Qatada, God forbade harm: the father is forbidden to take the child from its mother when she accepts what another woman would be hired for, and the mother is forbidden to throw the child on its father to harm him (4976, 4977). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 49} From al-Hasan, once he has divorced her he may not take the child from her if she accepts what another woman would, and she may not burden him beyond his strength, if he is poor, by throwing the child on him (4978). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 49} From al-Dahhak, she is not to throw it on the father if he is alive or on his paternal relatives if he is dead, and the father is not to harm the woman who wants to nurse her child (4979). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 49} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 50} From al-Suddi, the man is not to take his child from his wife to give it to another woman at the pay she accepts, and the mother is not to cast it on him, but it is her duty to nurse it until he finds a nurse (4980). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 50} From Ibn Shihab, mothers have the better right to nurse their children as long as they accept what others would be paid (4981). From Sufyan (4982) and Ibn Zayd: he must not take it from her when she wants to nurse it, nor must she throw it on him when he can find no one to nurse it and has nothing to pay with (4983). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 50}

The commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-233|2:233]]) continues. From 'Ata' on the clause about harm: she must not leave the child and its nursing out of dislike, so as to harm its father, and he must not stop her so as to harm her (4984). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 50} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 51} Some said the mother whom he is forbidden to harm is the wet nurse, the woman who nurses another's child, and this is reported from 'Ikrima (4985). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 51} Al-Tabari says the meaning is that the father of the child must not harm the child's mother through her child, nor the mother the child's father through his child by her, the doer being left unnamed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 51} He answers some grammarians who said the letter has the fatha because it is the lightest vowel, saying that would hold only if the mother alone were forbidden, and that otherwise the kasra would be more eloquent, and that the unanimity of the readers on the fatha shows the speaker overlooked this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 52} He holds that anyone who supposed the mother to be the subject of an active verb contradicted the interpreters, since God forbade each parent to harm the other through their child, and did not forbid them to harm the child, who, being a suckling, cannot be imagined to harm anyone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 52} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 53} He answers those who allowed the kasra, who is al-Farra' according to the editor, by saying that the kasra changes the sense from the passive to the active. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 53} The editor says these passages confirm his own reconstruction of the corrupt place earlier in al-Tabari's text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 53}

Al-Tabari then says that since God forbade each parent to harm the other through their child, it is the duty of the leader of the Muslims, when a man wants to take his child from its mother after the separation while she cares for and nurses it for the pay another woman would be given, to oblige the father to hand the child to her as long as the child needs her, and likewise, where the child will take no other breast or the father is penniless and cannot hire a nurse, to oblige the separated mother to nurse and care for it, since harming the child is more fittingly forbidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 53} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 54} This is al-Tabari's view on the duty of the leader.

He begins the commentary on the sentence that extends the duty to the heir. The interpreters differed over who the heir is. Some said it is the child's heir, where the father is dead, who bears what the father bore while alive: Qatada (4986), al-Suddi (4987), Qatada (4988). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 54} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 55} Those then differed over the heir. Some said he is the heir on the father's side from the paternal relatives, whoever he is, whether brother, uncle, cousin or nephew: from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, 'Umar bound the distant cousins of a newborn to maintain him like the 'aqila (4989); the editor explains that the 'aqila are a man's paternal kin who pay blood money. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 55} From al-Hasan, it is on the paternal relatives (4990); from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab the same for nursing (4991). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 55} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 56} From al-Hasan, if a man dies while his wife is pregnant her upkeep is from her share, and her child's from his share of the estate, and if he has no property his upkeep is on his paternal relatives; he took it to mean men (4992, 4993). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 56} From Ibn Sirin, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Utba decided that an orphan's upkeep and nursing come from his own property, and said to the guardian, if he had none I would have ruled it against you (4994, 4995). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 56} From Ibrahim, the heir bears what the father bore when the child has no property, and if he has a cousin or paternal relatives who inherit from him, upkeep falls on them (4996). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 56} The editor refers to al-Bayhaqi's Sunan and corrects places in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 55} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 56}

The disagreement on who "the heir" is in ([[passage/quran-2-233|2:233]]) continues. From Mujahid, he is the guardian whoever he is (4997-4999); from 'Ata' and Qatada, on an orphan with nothing, his guardians are compelled to maintain him until he comes of age (5000); from al-Dahhak, if the infant's father dies and the infant has property the nursing is taken from it, and if not from the paternal relatives, and if they have none the mother is compelled (5001). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 57} Others said it is the child's heir, men and women according to what they inherit: Qatada (5002); from al-Zuhri, 'Umar made three heirs of the infant pay the nursing wage (5003); from Ibn Sirin, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Utba paid an infant's upkeep from his own property and said to his heir, if he had none we would have held you to his upkeep (5004). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 57} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 58} Others said it is those of his heirs who are within the forbidden degrees of kinship to the child, but not a cousin or a patron and the like, and al-Tabari reports that Abu Hanifa, Abu Yusuf and Muhammad said this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 58} Another group said it means the infant himself: from the judge Bashir ibn al-Nadr al-Muzani (5005), from Qabisa ibn Dhu'ayb (5006, 5007), and from al-Dahhak, the child who is nursed (5008). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 58} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 59} Al-Tabari says that for these the sense is that the infant bears what was on the father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 59} Others said it is the surviving parent after the death of the other: from Sufyan, for an infant with an uncle and a mother who nurses him, the nursing is shared between them, the uncle's share lessened by what the mother inherits, since the mother is compelled to maintain her child (5009). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 59} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 60} The editor identifies narrators in these chains, the judge Bashir ibn al-Nadr and Ibn Hujayra, and corrects names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 57} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 58}

The interpreters then differed over what the "like" refers to. Some said it is upon the infant's heir after the death of both parents what had been on his father of the nursing wage and upkeep, where the child has no property: Ibrahim says it is the nursing (5010, 5012) and the nursing wage (5011, 5013); 'Abd Allah ibn 'Utba says nursing (5014) and upkeep in what is right (5015); Ibrahim says the heir bears what the father bore of nursing if the infant has no property (5016), and nursing and upkeep (5017); al-Sha'bi, nursing and its wage (5019-5021); al-Hasan, nursing (5022, 5023), and that it concerns upkeep by the heir when the child has no property (5024). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 60} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 61} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 62} Mujahid says upkeep in what is right (5025, 5026), that the guardian bears the child's care and nursing if he has no property (5027), and that every heir bears the nursing described, with the care and nursing, if there is no property, and must not harm his mother (5028). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 62} Report 5029 begins at the foot of p. 62 and continues on the next page.
