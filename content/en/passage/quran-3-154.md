## Summary
Verse 154 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 154 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Security and drowsiness (pp. 315-316).** Abu Ja'far on verse 154: then God sent down on you, believers, after the grief He repaid with a grief that came before it, security, meaning safety, on the sincere and certain among you, not on the hypocrites and doubters. Then He explained what the security is, drowsiness, in the accusative as a substitute. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on 'safety'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 315}

**The two readings of the verb for overtaking (pp. 315-316).** Most readers of the Hijaz, Medina and Basra and some of Kufa read it masculine; a group of the Kufans read it feminine. Those who read it masculine take it that drowsiness is what overtakes the group of believers rather than the security; those who read it feminine take it that the security is what overtakes them. Abu Ja'far holds that both are well-known, widespread readings among the readers of the garrison towns, not differing in sense, since the security here is drowsiness and drowsiness the security, so whichever one reads is right. So too with similar cases in the Qur'an, for which he refers to [[passage/quran-44-43|44:43]] and what follows, [[passage/quran-75-37|75:37]] and [[passage/quran-19-25|19:25]]; the editor cites al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 240) and says he preferred the manuscript's connective in the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 315} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 316}

**Why the two groups parted (8072-8078, pp. 316-318).** The question is asked: what was the cause of the two groups' parting, one made secure until it dozed, the other absorbed in itself and thinking wrongly of God? The answer is that it was as follows. Al-Suddi (8072): the idolaters left Uhud after what happened and arranged with the Prophet to meet at Badr the next year; he said yes, and the Muslims feared they would descend on Medina, so the Messenger of God sent a man to watch them and said: if you see them sit on their baggage and lead their horses, they are going away; if you see them sit on their horses and lead their baggage, they are coming down on Medina; so fear God, be patient, and prepare them to fight. When the scout saw them sit hurriedly on their baggage he called out that they were going; the believers trusted their prophet and slept, while some hypocrites stayed thinking the enemy would come; God mentions that. Ibn 'Abbas through Ibn Jurayj (8073): He made them secure that day with drowsiness that overtook them, and only one who feels safe dozes. Abu Talha by several routes from Anas (8074-8078): I was among those overtaken by drowsiness at Uhud as security, so that it fell from my hand several times (al-Tabari glosses: his whip or sword) (8074); I raised my head and saw no one among the men but under his shield, swaying with drowsiness (8075); I was among those it was poured on (8076); my sword would fall from my hand and I would take it (8077); in al-Rabi''s report (8078), 'it was mentioned to us, and God knows best, from Anas', his sword kept falling and he kept taking it, and the other group were the hypocrites, who cared only for themselves. The editor explains that the baggage is the traveller's goods and the camels carrying it, that to lead a horse is to lead it at one's side, that the manuscript, printed edition and al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 87) have an extra preposition he says is undoubtedly to be dropped, that the 'hajafa' is a kind of shield of camel hide, and that the verb for swaying means to lean, move and sway. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 316} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 317} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 318}

**Reports on the drowsiness (8079-8082, pp. 318-319).** Al-Miswar ibn Makhrama (8079): I asked 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf about this verse and he said: sleep was cast on us at Uhud. Qatada (8080): that day they were two groups, and the believers God overtook with drowsiness as security from Him and mercy; al-Rabi' ibn Anas the same (8081). Al-Rabi' (8082): drowsiness was cast on them, and it was a security for them (the report is completed on p. 319). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 319}

**The rest of the reports on drowsiness (8083-8086, p. 319).** 'Abdallah (Ibn Mas'ud) through Abu Razin (8083): drowsiness in fighting is security, and drowsiness in prayer is from Satan. Ibn Ishaq (8084): God sent down drowsiness as security from Him on the people of certainty in Him, so they slept unafraid. Qatada through Ma'mar (8085): God cast drowsiness on them and it was security for them; it is mentioned that Abu Talha said drowsiness was cast on him that day and he dozed until his sword fell from his hand. Anas from Abu Talha, and Hisham ibn 'Urwa from 'Urwa from al-Zubayr (8086): we raised our heads at Uhud and looked, and each of them was leaning against the side of his shield; the narrator then recited the verse that mentions the security. The editor notes that 8084 is from Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 122), the continuation of the reports ending in 8067. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 319}

**The second group and the thought of the Jahiliyya (8087-8092, pp. 320-321).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning is a group of you, believers, whom their own selves preoccupied, the hypocrites, who care only for themselves; they are busy with fear of being killed and of death, sleep has flown from their eyes, and they think false thoughts of God, the thought of the people of the Jahiliyya among the idolaters: doubt about God's affair, denial of His prophet, and the supposition that God would abandon His prophet and give the unbelievers power over him. In a note the editor explains the verb 'hasiba' and its forms as meaning 'to think'. Qatada (8087): the other group are the hypocrites, who care only for themselves, the most cowardly of people, the most fearful and most ready to abandon the truth, people of doubt and suspicion about God's affair. Al-Rabi' (8088) is similar, the narrator reciting what the hypocrites said and God's answer. Ibn Ishaq (8089): the hypocrites were preoccupied by fear of being killed since they hoped for no outcome (from Ibn Hisham 3: 122, the continuation of 8084). Ibn Zayd (8090): these are the hypocrites. The thought of the Jahiliyya is the thought of the idolaters, according to Qatada (8091) and al-Rabi' (8092). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 321}

**Two ways of parsing 'a group' (p. 321).** Abu Ja'far: the nominative has two explanations: it is governed either by the pronoun that returns to it in the first clause, or by the clause after it that holds the thinking; the accusative would also be allowed, the 'wa' then serving the implied verb, meaning 'and their selves preoccupied a group', as in [[passage/quran-51-47|51:47]] (the reference is garbled in the scan; read from the sequence of the number). The editor refers to al-Farra's full treatment of this in Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 240-242). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 321}

**The hypocrites' saying and God's reply (8093, pp. 322-323).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning is the hypocritical group whom their selves preoccupied; they say they have no say in the matter, and the reply is that the matter is wholly God's; and that if they had had any say they would not have gone out to fight those who fought them and killed them. Ibn Jurayj (8093): 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy was told: the Banu al-Khazraj were killed today. He said: do we have any say? He was told: the matter is all God's. The editor says the printed edition set down a wording matching the verse's text, and that he kept the manuscript's. For Abu Ja'far this is a new statement from God, telling His prophet to say to them that the matter is all God's, who turns it as He wills and arranges it as He loves. Then it returns to reporting their hypocrisy: these people conceal in themselves unbelief and doubt about God which they do not show the Prophet; God then made His prophet aware of what they hid among themselves of hypocrisy and of the regret that struck them for having attended Uhud with the Muslims; their words mean that if going out had been to a war the idolaters brought to them, they would not have gone out and none of them would have been killed where they were killed at Uhud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 322} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 323}

**Who said it: Mu'attib ibn Qushayr (8094-8095, p. 323).** It is mentioned that among those who said this was Mu'attib ibn Qushayr, brother of the Banu 'Amr ibn 'Awf. Ibn Ishaq through Yahya ibn 'Abbad ibn 'Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr from his father from 'Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr from al-Zubayr (8094): I could hear Mu'attib ibn Qushayr's words while drowsiness was overtaking me, hearing them only as in a dream, when he said that if they had had any say they would not have been killed there. Sa'id ibn Yahya al-Umawi (8095) reports the same by a chain going back to Ibn Ishaq. The editor says he did not find the text of 8094 in Ibn Hisham's account of Uhud, but found its sense and an allusion to it before Uhud, in the mention of the hypocrites of the Ansar who joined the Jews (2: 169). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 323}

**The two readings of 'all' (pp. 323-324).** The readers differed. Most readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read 'all' in the accusative as an adjective of 'the matter'; some Basra readers read it in the nominative as a subject with what follows as predicate, like saying 'the matter, part of it is for 'Abdallah'; the accusative could also be a substitute. The editor refers to al-Farra (1: 243). Abu Ja'far: the reading in our view is the accusative because most readers agree on it, though the other is not wrong in sense or Arabic; had the nominative been widespread among readers, the two would be equal for him, since the meaning is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 323} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 324}

**Going out to the lying-places, the testing and the sifting (pp. 324-325).** Abu Ja'far: say, Muhammad, to those hypocrites you have been told of: had you stayed in your homes and not joined the believers in their war with their enemies, what you hid of your hypocrisy and concealed of your doubt in your religion would not have appeared to the believers (the printed edition has 'your associating' where the manuscript, which is right, has 'your doubt'); God had decreed killing for some of them, and they would have come out of their homes to the place of their fall and fallen where it was decreed (the editor refers for 'burooz' to 5: 354 and says the printed edition misread a word in the manuscript). The testing means that God examines what is in their breasts of doubt and so separates them, by what He shows the believers of their hypocrisy. Abu Ja'far has explained earlier that such expressions, though the description is outwardly attributed to God, mean His friends and the people obedient to Him: God's friends and the obedient would examine the doubt and sickness in their breasts, know them and tell them apart from the sincere and certain, and make clear what is in their hearts of belief in God, His messenger and the believers, whether hostility or allegiance. God has knowledge of what is in His creatures' breasts, good and evil, faith and unbelief; nothing is hidden from Him and He keeps it all until He repays each according to what he deserves. The editor refers to the commentary on 'testing' (7: 297 note 1), to p. 246 note 2 and 2: 160-162, and to 'purging' on p. 244. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 324} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 325}

**Ibn Ishaq's explanation and al-Hasan's answer (8096-8097, pp. 325-326).** Ibn Ishaq (8096): God mentioned the hypocrites' mutual blame and their regret over what befell them, then told His prophet to say to them that had they been in their homes and not attended this place where God showed of their inner secrets what He showed, He would have brought those on whom killing was decreed to another place to fall, so that what is in the breasts would be tested by it and what is in the hearts sifted, and nothing they hid is hidden from God (from Ibn Hisham 3: 122, the continuation of 8089; the editor records a difference between the printed edition and the manuscript in one phrase). Al-Hasan (8097), asked about it: God decreed that believers fight in His way; not everyone who fights is killed, but he is killed on whom God decreed killing. The editor gives notes on two men in the chain: al-Harith ibn Muslim al-Razi, the reciter, who narrated from al-Thawri and al-Rabi' ibn Sabih among others, and of whom he quotes Abu Hatim as saying he was a devout, trustworthy, truthful shaykh whom he saw and prayed behind (Ibn Abi Hatim 1/2/88); and Bahr al-Saqqa', Bahr ibn Kanaz al-Bahili, Abu al-Fadl, who narrated from al-Hasan, al-Zuhri and Qatada and was grandfather of 'Amr ibn 'Ali al-Fallas; al-Thawri narrated from him giving him a by-name without naming him, and the editor quotes Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan on this and Yahya ibn Ma'in's saying that his hadith is not to be written, and that he is abandoned (al-Tahdhib and Ibn Abi Hatim 1/1/418). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 325} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 326}
