## Summary
Verse 154 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 154 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Security and drowsiness (pp. 315-316).** Abu Ja'far on verse 154: then God sent down on you, believers, after the grief He repaid with a grief that came before it, security, meaning safety, on the sincere and certain among you, not on the hypocrites and doubters. Then He explained what the security is, drowsiness, in the accusative as a substitute. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on 'safety'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 315}

**The two readings of the verb for overtaking (pp. 315-316).** Most readers of the Hijaz, Medina and Basra and some of Kufa read it masculine; a group of the Kufans read it feminine. Those who read it masculine take it that drowsiness is what overtakes the group of believers rather than the security; those who read it feminine take it that the security is what overtakes them. Abu Ja'far holds that both are well-known, widespread readings among the readers of the garrison towns, not differing in sense, since the security here is drowsiness and drowsiness the security, so whichever one reads is right. So too with similar cases in the Qur'an, for which he refers to [[passage/quran-44-43|44:43]] and what follows, [[passage/quran-75-37|75:37]] and [[passage/quran-19-25|19:25]]; the editor cites al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 240) and says he preferred the manuscript's connective in the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 315} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 316}

**Why the two groups parted (8072-8078, pp. 316-318).** The question is asked: what was the cause of the two groups' parting, one made secure until it dozed, the other absorbed in itself and thinking wrongly of God? The answer is that it was as follows. Al-Suddi (8072): the idolaters left Uhud after what happened and arranged with the Prophet to meet at Badr the next year; he said yes, and the Muslims feared they would descend on Medina, so the Messenger of God sent a man to watch them and said: if you see them sit on their baggage and lead their horses, they are going away; if you see them sit on their horses and lead their baggage, they are coming down on Medina; so fear God, be patient, and prepare them to fight. When the scout saw them sit hurriedly on their baggage he called out that they were going; the believers trusted their prophet and slept, while some hypocrites stayed thinking the enemy would come; God mentions that. Ibn 'Abbas through Ibn Jurayj (8073): He made them secure that day with drowsiness that overtook them, and only one who feels safe dozes. Abu Talha by several routes from Anas (8074-8078): I was among those overtaken by drowsiness at Uhud as security, so that it fell from my hand several times (al-Tabari glosses: his whip or sword) (8074); I raised my head and saw no one among the men but under his shield, swaying with drowsiness (8075); I was among those it was poured on (8076); my sword would fall from my hand and I would take it (8077); in al-Rabi''s report (8078), 'it was mentioned to us, and God knows best, from Anas', his sword kept falling and he kept taking it, and the other group were the hypocrites, who cared only for themselves. The editor explains that the baggage is the traveller's goods and the camels carrying it, that to lead a horse is to lead it at one's side, that the manuscript, printed edition and al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 87) have an extra preposition he says is undoubtedly to be dropped, that the 'hajafa' is a kind of shield of camel hide, and that the verb for swaying means to lean, move and sway. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 316} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 317} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 318}

**Reports on the drowsiness (8079-8082, pp. 318-319).** Al-Miswar ibn Makhrama (8079): I asked 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf about this verse and he said: sleep was cast on us at Uhud. Qatada (8080): that day they were two groups, and the believers God overtook with drowsiness as security from Him and mercy; al-Rabi' ibn Anas the same (8081). Al-Rabi' (8082): drowsiness was cast on them, and it was a security for them (the report is completed on p. 319). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 319}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
