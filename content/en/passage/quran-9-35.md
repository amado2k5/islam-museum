**Commentary on verse 35 (pp. 229-232)**

Al-Tabari says: so give the news, Muhammad, to those who hoard gold and silver and do not take out God's dues from it, of a painful punishment "on the day it is heated in the fire of Hell", the "day" being connected with the painful punishment, as if to say He gives them news of a painful punishment with which God will punish them on the day it is heated. "Heated over it" means it is put into the fire and the fire kindled upon it, that is upon the gold and silver they hoarded, in the fire of Hell, "and their foreheads, flanks and backs are branded with it"; anything put into the fire has been "heated", as one says "I heated the iron in the fire". The words "their foreheads will be branded with it" mean with the hoarded gold and silver heated in Hell's fire, with which God brands, burning the foreheads, flanks and backs of those who hoarded it; "this is what you hoarded" is to say they will be told "this is what you hoarded in the world, unbelievers who withheld your hoards from God's obligatory dues in them, for yourselves", "so taste what you used to hoard", that is taste God's punishment for withholding God's dues from your wealth and hoarding it in rivalry and boasting. The words "and it will be said to them" are omitted because the sense shows them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 230}

Report 16675, from Abu Dharr, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya, Ayyub and Humayd ibn Hilal: Abu Dharr used to say to give the hoarders news of a brand on the foreheads, a brand on the flanks and a brand on the backs until the heat meets in their bellies. The editor says Humayd ibn Hilal is reliable but criticised for taking part in the ruler's work, and that al-Bazzar said he did not hear from Abu Dharr. Report 16676, from al-Ahnaf ibn Qays, through Ibn Ulayya, al-Jurayri and Abu al-Ala' ibn al-Shikhkhir: I came to Medina and, as I was in a circle with a company of Quraysh, a man came, rough of clothes, body and face, and stood over them and said to give the hoarders news of hot stones heated in Hell's fire, placed on the nipple of one of them until it comes out at the top of his shoulder blade, and on the shoulder blade until it comes out at the nipple, quivering; the people lowered their heads and I saw none of them reply; he turned away and I followed him until he sat by a pillar, and I said I saw these people dislike what you said, and he said these people understand nothing. The editor explains the words for the stones and the shoulder bone and says al-Bukhari and Muslim give it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 231}

Report 16677, from al-Ahnaf ibn Qays, through Ibn Humayd, al-Hakam, Amr ibn Qays, Amr ibn Murra and Abu Nasr: I saw in the Medina mosque a man in coarse clothes and shabby appearance going round the circles saying to give the owners of hoards news of a brand on their flanks, on their foreheads and on their backs, and then he went off muttering, "what can Quraysh do to me?" Report 16678, from Abu Dharr, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar and Qatada: give the owners of hoards news of a brand on the foreheads, on the flanks and on the backs. Report 16679, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki, his father, Sufyan and Qabus: on "the day it is heated in the fire of Hell" he said a snake coiling round his brow and forehead, saying "I am your wealth you were stingy with"; the editor says Qabus is weak and not relied on. Report 16680, from Thawban, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada, Salim ibn Abi al-Ja'd and Ma'dan ibn Abi Talha: the Prophet used to say that whoever leaves a hoard after him, it is made on the day of resurrection into a bald serpent with two black marks above its eyes that follows him; he says "woe to you, what are you?" and it says "I am your hoard that you left behind", and it keeps following him until he puts his hand in its mouth and it crunches it, and then it follows the rest of his body. The editor explains the serpent, bald and two marks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 232}
