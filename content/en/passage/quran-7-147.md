**Commentary on verse 147 (p. 116)**

Al-Tabari says: those who are arrogant in the land without right, and everyone who denies God's proofs, His messengers and His signs, who rejects that he will be raised after his death on the Day of Resurrection, and who denies meeting God in the Hereafter, their deeds have gone and become void and their burdens have settled upon them, because they acted for other than God and wearied themselves in what does not please Him, so their deeds became a calamity to them. On "will they be repaid except for what they did" he says: will they be rewarded other than with the reward of what they did? The reward of their deeds became eternity in a fire whose canopy encloses them, since their deeds were in obedience to Satan and not in obedience to the Merciful; and al-Tabari adds a prayer for refuge with God from His anger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 116}

He says he has explained the meaning of "being void", "repayment" and "the Hereafter" earlier, which spares repeating, and the editor refers to the earlier places and notes that the printed text reads "will they be told" where he kept the manuscript reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 116}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
