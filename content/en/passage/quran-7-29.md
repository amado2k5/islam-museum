## Summary
Verse 29 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 29 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Say: my Lord has commanded justice; set your faces right at every place of prayer, and call on Him, devoting your religion to Him; as He began you, you will return", God tells His Prophet to say to those who claim that God commanded them indecency, lying about God: my Lord did not command what you say, but "my Lord has commanded justice", meaning fairness; he gives (14469) from Mujahid and (14470) from al-Suddi that "qist" is justice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 379}

As for "set your faces right at every place of prayer", the commentators differ. Some said it means turn your faces, wherever you are in prayer, to the Kaaba: he gives (14471) and (14472) from Mujahid that it is to the Kaaba wherever you pray, in a church or elsewhere; (14473) from al-Suddi that "the masjid" is the Kaaba; (14474) from Mujahid that it is the Kaaba wherever you are; and (14475) from Ibn Zayd that the meaning is set them toward the qibla God commanded you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 380}

Others said the sense is rather: make your prostration to God purely, apart from other gods and rivals; he gives (14476) from al-Rabi' that it is in sincerity, that you call on none other and keep your religion pure for Him. Al-Tabari says the better of the two interpretations for the verse is that of al-Rabi', that the people were commanded to direct their prayer to their Lord and not to idols and images, and to make their supplication to God pure, not whistling and clapping. He says it is the better of the two because God addressed this verse to people from among the idolatrous Arabs who were not people of churches and synagogues, which belonged to the people of the two Books, and it is not rational to say to one who does not pray in a church or synagogue, face the Kaaba in a church or synagogue. The note says "muka'" is whistling and "tasdiya" clapping, as they circled the House naked, whistling with their mouths and clapping with their hands. As for "call on Him, devoting your religion to Him", he says: work for your Lord devoting religion and obedience to Him, not mixing it with shirk nor setting a partner in anything you do for Him; and he gives (14477) from al-Rabi' that it means you purify religion, supplication and work for Him, and then you face the Sacred House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 381}

Then, on "as He began you, you will return", he says the commentators differ. Some said it means as He began you wretched and blessed, so you will be raised on the Day of Resurrection: he gives (14478) from Ibn Abbas that God began the creation of the son of Adam believer and disbeliever, as He said in [[passage/quran-64-2|64:2]], "it is He who created you; some of you are disbelievers and some believers", and He returns them on the Day of Resurrection as He began their creation, believer and disbeliever; (14479) from him that the believer is raised as a believer and the disbeliever as a disbeliever; (14480) from Jabir that they are raised as they were, the believer in his faith and the hypocrite in his hypocrisy, and the note says Yahya ibn al-Duraysh is trustworthy; and (14481) from Abu al-Aliya that they returned to His knowledge of them, have you not heard His words about them, "as He began you, you will return", and His words "a group He guided, and a group deserved misguidance"? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 382}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
