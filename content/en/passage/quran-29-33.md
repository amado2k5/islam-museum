## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And when Our messengers came to Lot, he was displeased with them and distressed and said, 'Do not fear and do not grieve. Indeed, we are your deliverers and your family, except your wife was among the absent': 'And when Our messengers came, Lot' from the angels was 'bad with them,' meaning the angels disliked him for coming to him, because they hosted him and displeased him with that. Thus, the saying 'bad with them' means he did to them, whoever wronged him by that. It was mentioned from Qatadah that he used to say: He had a bad opinion of his people and grew weary of his guests. Ma'mar informed us from him: "He grew weary of them," meaning: He grew distressed by their hospitality because he learned of the wickedness of his people's actions. Also: hospitality out of fear for them because of what he knew of the evil of his people. In the Maqta, he quotes from Qatadah.
