## Summary
Verse 55 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 55 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Who believed and who turned away (pp. 482-483, reports 9831-9832, note 2).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: among these Jews, who were told to believe in what came down confirming what they had before the effacing of faces ([[passage/quran-4-47|4:47]]), some accepted what was sent down to Muhammad and some turned away from accepting it. Muhammad b. Amr (9831, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that the one who believed is the one who believed what was sent down to Muhammad; al-Muthanna (9832, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid likewise. The editor refers to earlier explanations of turning away. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 483}

**The lifting of the immediate threat and the postponed punishment (p. 483, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari holds that the verse indicates that for the Jews who turned away from what was sent down to Muhammad, those around his place of emigration, the threat of immediate punishment in this world in the earlier verse of the sura was lifted and their punishment postponed to the Day of Resurrection because some of them believed; for the threat of speedy punishment depended on all of them persisting in disbelief, and when some believed they were released from it, while the punishment of those who persisted in denial was postponed to the hereafter; so He told them that Hell would suffice them as a blaze. The editor refers to verse 47 of the sura and to p. 445. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 483}

**The sense of the blaze (p. 483, note 3).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: enough for you, you who deny what I sent down to my prophet, is the fire of Hell kindled upon you. Some said the word's origin is "kindled", from a verb said of Hell in the sura of the Rolling Up ([[passage/quran-81-12|81:12]]), but it was turned into the form fa'il, as one says a dyed hand and an anointed beard in the sense of dyed and anointed; the blaze is fuel. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 483}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 483}
