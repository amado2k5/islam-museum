## Summary
Verse 103 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 103 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Clinging to the rope (pp. 70-71).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: cling together to God's means, that is, hold to the religion God commanded and the covenant He made with them in His book, of harmony, gathering on the word of truth and surrender to His command; he refers to his earlier explanation of holding fast (pp. 62-63). The rope is the means by which one reaches an aim or need, and so security is also called a rope, since by it fear is removed and panic escaped. He cites a line of al-A'sha from his poem on Qays ibn Ma'dikarib (the editor traces it to the Diwan, Mushkil al-Qur'an, al-Ma'ani al-Kabir and Lisan al-Arab, notes it concerns his camel, quotes the lines before it, and explains the earlier line on pledges: tribes give a traveller a pledge and protection because they fear Qays, so whoever heads to him finds security). He also mentions the two ropes at [[passage/quran-3-112|3:112]] as the same usage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 70} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 71}

**First view: the community (7562-7563, p. 71).** From Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Hushaym, al-Awwam, al-Sha'bi, Abdullah ibn Mas'ud: the community (7562); from al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym (7563): the divine rope is the community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 71}

**Second view: the Qur'an and the covenant (7564-7572, pp. 71-72).** Others said it is the Qur'an and the covenant made in it. From Qatada (7564 via Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id): the firm rope God commanded them to hold, this Qur'an; and (7565 via al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar): God's covenant and command. From Ibn Mas'ud (7566 via Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Mansur, Shaqiq): the path is attended by devils who call, servant of God, come this way, to turn people from God's way, so hold the rope, for it is God's book; the editor says it is in Majma' al-Zawa'id in similar wording, attributed to al-Tabarani with his teacher called weak, and that al-Tabari's chain is sound. From al-Suddi (7567): God's book. From Mujahid (7568 via Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih): God's covenant. From Ata (7569 via al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj): the covenant. From Ibn Mas'ud (7570 via Abu Kurayb, Waki', al-A'mash, Abu Wa'il): the Qur'an. From al-Dahhak (7571 via al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abu Zuhayr, Juwaybir): the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 71} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 72}

From Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (7572 via Sa'id ibn Yahya, Asbat ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Sulayman al-Arzami, Atiyya) a report from the Prophet that God's book is the rope stretched from heaven to earth. The editor notes that Abd al-Malik is an imam whose reliability was shown earlier; that Atiyya is Ibn Sa'd al-Awfi, shown to be weak; that the words "from Atiyya" fell out of the manuscript and printed text and he restored them from Ibn Kathir. He traces the routes of the hadith, all turning on Atiyya al-Awfi, in Ahmad's Musnad and al-Tirmidhi's collection, where it is called good and strange; says the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam is sound and part of a long story reported by Ahmad, Muslim and Ibn Hibban in which God's book is the divine rope; that al-Haythami gives it in Majma' al-Zawa'id from al-Tabarani's al-Awsat with men in its chain disputed; and that al-Suyuti attributes it to Ibn Abi Shayba, Ibn Jarir, Ibn Sa'd, Ahmad and al-Tabarani. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 72} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 73}

**Third view: sincere devotion (7573-7574, p. 73).** Others said it is sincere devotion of oneness to God. From Abu al-Aliya (7573 via al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi): cling by sincerity to God alone; from Ibn Zayd (7574 via Yunus, Ibn Wahb): the rope is Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 73}

**The prohibition of division (p. 74).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: do not split away from God's religion and the covenant He made in His book, of harmony and gathering on obeying Him and His messenger and ceasing at His command. From Qatada (7575 via Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id): God dislikes division for you, warned beforehand against it and forbade it, and is pleased for you with hearing, obeying, harmony and community, so be pleased for yourselves with what God is pleased with for you if you can. From Abu al-Aliya (7576): do not be hostile on sincerity to God, be brothers upon it; the editor prefers the wording of the printed text and al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 74}

**The report on the division of the community (7577-7578, pp. 74-75).** From al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya ibn Salih, who says al-Awza'i related that Yazid al-Raqashi related that he heard Anas ibn Malik say that the Prophet said the Children of Israel divided into seventy-one groups and his community will divide into seventy-two, all in the fire except one; asked what that one was, he closed his hand and said the community, reciting the verse (7577). The like is related via Abd al-Karim ibn Abi Umayr, al-Walid ibn Muslim, al-Awza'i, Yazid al-Raqashi (7578). The editor says Yazid al-Raqashi is Yazid ibn Aban Abu Amr al-Basri the storyteller, who is weak; that al-Bukhari recorded that Shu'ba spoke against him, al-Nasa'i called him abandoned, and Ibn Sa'd said he was weak and a Qadarite; that Ibn Majah records it via al-Walid ibn Muslim from al-Awza'i from Qatada from Anas ending "all in the fire except one, the community", of which al-Busiri said the chain is sound with reliable men, and the editor agrees, concluding al-Awza'i took it from two shaykhs, one weak and one reliable, and the weak one added the recitation of the verse. On 7578 he cites al-Dhahabi that Abd al-Karim ibn Abi Umayr is unknown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 74} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 75}

**Ibn Mas'ud on obedience and community (7579-7580, p. 75).** From Abu Kurayb, al-Muharibi, Ibn Abi Khalid, al-Sha'bi, Thabit ibn Qutba al-Madani, Abdullah ibn Mas'ud: people, keep to obedience and the community, for that is the rope God commanded, and what you dislike in the community and obedience is better than what you like in division (7579); likewise via Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayyan al-Sukkari, Muhammad ibn Yazid, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, al-Sha'bi, Thabit ibn Qutba, who heard Ibn Mas'ud preaching (7580). The editor corrects the name and attribution of Thabit ibn Qutba (al-Madani, al-Thaqafi) with his entry from al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim, and corrects the name al-Sukkari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 75}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 75 in the commentary on verse 103 and continue from p. 76. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 75}
