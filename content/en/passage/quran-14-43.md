## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari continues the passage that began with the verse before it: In 'Muhta'ayn' there are two opinions among the interpreters — hastening, which is 'descendants' or something below it, according to Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Qatadah, deliberately heading toward the caller; and perpetuating the gaze at a distance from the other, which is 'al-tahmij,' according to Ibn Abbas, Al-Dahhak, and Abu Duha. And 'Muqna'i 'Rusahum': those who raise their heads with their heads raised so that their gaze does not turn back to them. And 'and their hearts are air': empty with no good and no comprehension of the horror of what you see. He lists the chains of transmission for these statements as is his custom in his book.
