**Commentary on verse 15 (pp. 573-576)**

On [[passage/quran-12-15|12:15]] al-Tabari says: something is omitted in the speech, left out because what remains shows it, namely 'so he sent him with them'; 'when they took him away and agreed', that is, their opinion agreed and they resolved to put him into the bottom of the well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 573}

Report 18831, from al-Suddi: he said, I will not send him with you, I fear the wolf may eat him while you are heedless; they said, if the wolf ate him while we are a group we would be losers; so he sent him with them, they took him out and he was honoured among them, but when they took him out into the open they showed their enmity; one brother would hit him and he would call on another who hit him, and he saw no mercy from any of them; they beat him until they nearly killed him, and he cried, father, Jacob, if you knew what the sons of slave women did to your son; and when they nearly killed him Judah said, did you not give me a pledge not to kill him? They took him to the well to throw him in, lowering him into the well while he clung to its rim; they tied his hands and took off his shirt and he said, brothers, give me my shirt back to cover myself in the well; they said, call the sun and moon and eleven stars to keep you company; he said, I saw nothing; they lowered him until half way and dropped him wanting him to die. There was water in the well and he fell into it, then took refuge on a rock in it and stood on it; when they threw him into the well he began to weep, and they called him and he thought mercy had reached them and answered them, and they wanted to crush him with a rock and kill him, but Judah rose and stopped them, saying you gave me a pledge not to kill him; Judah brought him food. The editor notes that the name here is Judah, unlike the Reuben and Simeon mentioned earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 574}

As for 'when they took him away and agreed', the waw was brought into the answer, as in the line of Imru' al-Qays, where the waw enters the answer of 'lamma', the speech being 'when we crossed the camp's yard, it turned us aside'; so too 'when they took him away and agreed', for 'agreed' is the answer. As for 'We inspired him: you will tell them of this deed of theirs', it means We inspired Joseph that he would tell his brothers of this deed they did to him, 'while they did not perceive', that is, did not know. Then the interpreters differ on what God meant by 'they did not perceive'. Some said He meant that God inspired Joseph that Joseph would tell his brothers of what they did to him in throwing him into the well; report 18832, from Mujahid: to Joseph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 575}

Reports 18833, 18834 and 18835, from Mujahid: We inspired Joseph that you will tell your brothers; God inspired Joseph in the well that he would tell them of what they did, and they did not perceive that inspiration. Others said the sense is We inspired Joseph of what his brothers were doing to him, and his brothers did not perceive God's telling him; reports 18836 and 18837, from Qatada: of what God let Joseph know of their affair while in the well, and 18838 is like it. Others said the sense is that Joseph would tell them what they did to him, and they would not perceive that he was Joseph; report 18839, from Ibn Jurayj: they did not perceive that he was Joseph; 18840, from Ibn Abbas: when Joseph's brothers came in and he knew them while they did not know him, the cup was brought and he put it on his hand and tapped it and it rang, and he said, this cup tells me you had a brother by your father called Joseph whom he kept close to him rather than you, and you took him and threw him into the bottom of the well; then he tapped it and it rang, and you came to your father and said the wolf ate him, bringing his shirt with false blood; and they said to one another, this cup is telling him of you; Ibn Abbas said, we think this verse came down only about them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 576}

The editor thinks this report of Ibn Abbas is not sound, and its gravest flaw is its going against the plain Qur'an, since had it agreed with it, it would better have been said to them when they came in to him, 'do you know what you did to Joseph and his brother when you were ignorant', at the end of the chapter; he also gives notes on its narrators. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 577}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
