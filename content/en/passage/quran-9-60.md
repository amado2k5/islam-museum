## Summary
Verse 60 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 60 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 60 (pp. 305-312)**

Al-Tabari says: the alms are only for the poor and the needy and those whom God, glorious is His praise, named. The editor notes that the printed text has an incoherent phrase and that the manuscript's reading is the correct one. The interpreters then differed over the description of the "poor" and the "needy". Some said the poor is the one in need who refrains from asking, and the needy the one in need who asks. Report 16818, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', Jarir and Ash'ath: the poor is he who sits in his house and the needy he who goes about; report 16819, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdallah, Mu'awiya and Ali: the needy are those who go round and the poor are the poor of the Muslims; report 16820, from Jabir ibn Zayd, through Ibn Waki', Abu Usama and Jarir ibn Hazim: the poor are those who abstain and the needy those who ask. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 305}

Report 16821, from al-Zuhri, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Ma'qil ibn Ubaydallah al-Jazari: the poor are those in their houses who do not ask and the needy are those who go out and ask; the editor notes that Ma'qil is reliable and that the printed text has al-Harrani in place of al-Jazari. Report 16822, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, al-Qasim, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Abd al-Warith and Ibn Abi Najih: the poor is he who does not ask and the needy he who asks; report 16823, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the poor are those who do not ask people, people in need, and the needy are those who ask people; report 16824, from Mujahid, to the same effect, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abd al-Warith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 306}

Others said the poor is the one among the needy with a chronic disability, and the needy the sound-bodied among them. Report 16825, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the poor is he who has a disability and the needy the sound person in need. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 306}

Report 16826, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: the poor is the one crippled by a disability, and the needy is the one without a disability. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 307}

Others said the poor are the poor of the Emigrants, and the needy are Muslims who did not emigrate and are in need. Report 16827, from al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Jarir ibn Hazim and Ali ibn al-Hakam: the poor are the poor of the Emigrants and the needy those who did not emigrate; the editor notes Ali ibn al-Hakam al-Bunani is reliable. Report 16828, from Ibrahim, through Abd al-Aziz, Sufyan and Mansur: the alms are for the poor Emigrants, and Sufyan said this means the bedouin are given nothing of them; report 16829, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Waki', his father, Sufyan and Mansur: it used to be said the alms are only for the poor Emigrants; report 16830, from him through Jarir and Mansur: the alms used to be put among the poor Emigrants and in God's way. Report 16831, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Sa'id ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abza, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub and Ja'far: some of the Emigrants had a house, a wife, a servant and a camel on which to perform the pilgrimage and fight, and God counted them poor and gave them a share in the zakat. Report 16832, from Ibrahim, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Mansur: it used to be said the alms are for the poor Emigrants and in God's way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 307}

Others said the needy is the one weak in earning. Report 16833, from Umar ibn al-Khattab, through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Ibn Ulayya, Ibn Awn and Muhammad: the poor is not the one without money but the one bare of earning, and Ibn Ulayya said that among them the word means the one denied his luck; report 16834, from Umar, through Ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar, Ayyub and Ibn Sirin: the needy is not the one without money but the one bare of earning. The editor explains that Umar meant that the greatest poor man is he who has sent nothing ahead for the hereafter and that worldly poverty is the lighter of the two, that the word comes from a phrase for a bare hill with no vegetation and that in the report of Fatima bint Qays, Mu'awiya is said to be bare of wealth, that the one denied his luck is the one given a small and limited portion, and that the printed text misread the manuscript at "weak in earning". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 308}

Some said the poor are of the Muslims and the needy of the People of the Book. Report 16835, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Umar ibn Nafi': do not call the poor Muslims "needy", the needy are only the needy of the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 308}

Al-Tabari then chooses: the poor is the person in poverty and need who nonetheless, in this place, abstains from asking and abasing himself before people, and the needy is the person in need who abases himself before people by asking. He gives as reasons the agreement of all the scholars that the needy is given from the obligatory alms by reason of poverty, and that neediness among the Arabs means abasement, as in the verse of al-Baqara that speaks of abasement and neediness, [[passage/quran-2-61|2:61]], meaning humiliation and abasement and not poverty; so since God made those apportioned from the alms into two classes by poverty, each class must differ from the other, and the one given under the plain name poor has no neediness in him, while the one given under neediness and poverty combines with his poverty the abasement of asking. The sense is therefore that the alms are for the poor: the abstaining among them who does not ask, and the self-abasing among them who does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 309}

A report of something like this has come from the Messenger of God. Report 16836, from Abu Hurayra, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Isma'il ibn Ja'far, Sharik ibn Abi Namir and Ata ibn Yasar: the needy is not the one whom a morsel or two, a date or two, turns away; the needy is the one who abstains, "recite if you wish" the verse in al-Baqara about those who do not ask people importunately, [[passage/quran-2-273|2:273]]. The editor notes that Sharik ibn Abdallah ibn Abi Namir is reliable, that al-Bukhari and Muslim relate him, with al-Bukhari in the Fath and Muslim in his Sahih, and that the printed text wrongly has "abasement and neediness" where the manuscript is correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 309}

Al-Tabari explains that the words "the needy is the one who abstains" follow people's usage of calling the poor "needy", and are not a distinction of needy from poor, as is shown by his citing the verse that describes those whom God began by describing as poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 310}

As for "those who work on them", they are the agents who collect them from their owners and put them with those who deserve them; they are given for their agency, whether rich or poor. Report 16837, from al-Zuhri, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Ma'qil ibn Ubaydallah: they are the agents; report 16838, from Qatada: its collectors, who gather and strive for it; report 16839, from Ibn Zayd: whoever works on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 310}

The interpreters then differed over how much the worker is given. Some said an eighth. Report 16840, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman, al-Hasan ibn Salih and Juwaybir: the workers have an eighth of the alms; report 16841, from Mujahid: the workers eat from the eighth share. Others said rather he is given according to his work. Report 16842, from Zuhayr al-Amiri: he met Abdallah ibn Amr ibn al-As and asked him about the alms, what kind of property they are, and he said the property of the lame, the one-eyed, the blind and everyone stranded; the man said the workers and fighters have a right, and he said the fighters are a people for whom it is lawful, and the workers according to their work; then he said the alms are not lawful for the rich or for one with sound strength. The editor explains the word for stranded, vouches for Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata al-Khaffaf and al-Akhdar ibn Ajlan, says he found Ata ibn Zuhayr only in Ibn Abi Hatim, and says al-Suyuti cites the report in al-Durr al-Manthur giving it only to Abu al-Shaykh, with "Abdallah ibn Umar", a mistake. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 311}

Report 16843, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the worker has a share if he works rightly, and Umar and those others did not give the worker an eighth but set a sum according to his work; report 16844, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', Jarir and Ash'ath: the workers used to be given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 312}

Al-Tabari chooses that the worker is given according to his work and the wage of his like, because God did not divide the alms of property among the eight classes in eight shares but made known to His creatures that the alms do not pass beyond these eight classes to others; so whoever is given a right from them is given according to the giver's judgement; and since the worker is given for his work, not for a need that ends with the giving, what he receives is a payment for his effort and work, which ends with his dismissal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 312}

As for "those whose hearts are to be reconciled", they were people won over to Islam whose support was not yet sound, to set right themselves and their clan, such as Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and Uyayna ibn Badr, and the discussion continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 312}

We stop here at the foot of p. 312, in the middle of the commentary on verse 60 (at the mention of those whose hearts are reconciled); the next batch begins at p. 313. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 312}
