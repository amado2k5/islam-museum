**Commentary on verse 123 (pp. 574-576)**

Al-Tabari says to the believers: you who believed God and His Messenger, fight the unbelievers near you, not those far away, that is, begin with the nearest in territory and then the next, not the distant first. Those near the people addressed at that time were the Byzantines, since they lived in Syria, and Syria was nearer to Medina than Iraq. But after God opened the lands to the believers, the duty on the people of each region is to fight their near enemies rather than the far, unless the people of another region of the Muslim lands are in need of them, in which case they must help and support them, since the Muslims are one hand against others. On that basis everyone who interpreted this verse took it as obliging the people of every region to fight the enemies near them. The editor refers to the language indexes for *wali*. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 574}

Report 17481 is from Ibn Umar, through Ibn Waki', his father, Sufyan and Shubayb ibn Gharqada: a man of the Banu Tamim asked about fighting the Daylam and he said, "Take the Byzantines." Report 17482 is from al-Hasan, through Ibn Bashshar, Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Ibn Waki', Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Yunus: "fight those near you of the unbelievers", he said, are the Daylam. Report 17483 is from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', his father and al-Rabi': when asked about fighting the Byzantines and the Daylam he recited this verse. Report 17484 is from Imran, brother of Ya'qub: he asked Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn about fighting the Daylam, and the report continues on the next page. The editor corrects the chain, saying Shubayb ibn Gharqada al-Sulami is a trustworthy Successor, and that a copyist or editor added "Urwa al-Bariqi" wrongly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 575}

The rest of report 17484: Imran asked Ja'far about fighting the Daylam and he said to fight them and keep guard against them, for they are among those of whom God said to fight the unbelievers near you. The editor notes that Ya'qub ibn Abdullah al-Qummi narrates from his two brothers Abd al-Rahman and Imran and that he found no notice of Imran. Report 17485 is from al-Hasan, through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym, Sufyan and al-Rabi': asked about Syria and the Daylam he said "fight those near you of the unbelievers" meant the Daylam. Report 17486 is from Abu Amr and Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz, through Ali ibn Sahl and al-Walid: each people guard the frontier posts and fortresses nearest to them, and they took the verse so. Report 17487 is from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: those near them among the unbelievers were the Arabs, and he fought them until he had finished with them; then God said to fight those who do not believe in God or the Last Day, in verse 29 up to "while they are humbled", and so he was commanded to strive against the People of the Book after finishing with the nearby Arabs, and striving against them is the best striving in God's sight. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 576}

"Let them find harshness in you" means: let these unbelievers whom you fight find firmness against them from you; "know that God is with those who fear Him" means be certain, when you fight them, that God is with you and will help you against them, for if you fear God by performing His obligations and avoiding disobedience, God helps and supports whoever fears Him. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of harshness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 576}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
