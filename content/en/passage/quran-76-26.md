## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 25-27 as a single section. He interprets the phrase "And during the night prostrate to Him and glorify Him a long [part of the] night" as meaning prayer and glorification. Ibn Zayd said, regarding the verse "And remember the name of your Lord morning and evening," that "morning" refers to the dawn prayer, and "evening" to the afternoon prayer. And regarding the phrase "And during the night prostrate to Him and glorify Him a long [part of the] night," he said that this was the first obligatory act. He recited: “O you who are wrapped in garments, arise [to pray] at night, except a little—half of it.” Then he said: “Indeed, your Lord knows that you stand [in prayer] for nearly two-thirds of the night, or half of it, or a third of it…” until he said: “So recite what is easy [for you] of the Qur’an…” to the end of the verse. Then he said: This was abrogated from the Messenger of God and from the people, and he made it supererogatory, so he said: “And from the night, pray with it as an additional [worship] for you.” He said: So he made it supererogatory. And in the passage, he quotes sayings from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd, and Sufyan.
