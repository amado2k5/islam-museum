## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari opens his commentary on the surah with two accounts of why it was sent down. In the first, the polytheists asked [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] to give them his Lord's lineage; he reports this from Ubayy ibn Ka'b through Abu al-Aliya, from Ikrima, from Abu al-Aliya (where those who ask are the leaders of the confederates), and from Jabir {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 727} {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 728}. In the second, a group of Jews asked him who created God; he reports this through Ibn Ishaq, in a report in which the Prophet grew angry, Jibril calmed him and brought the answer, and he reports it from Qatada {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 728} {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 729}. He then states the meaning: say to those who ask about your Lord's lineage and description that the Lord you ask about is God, to whom the worship of all things belongs, and worship befits no other {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 729}. On the nominative of "ahad" he sets out how the grammarians differed: some said it is raised by the name of God, with "huwa" as a supporting pronoun; some that it is nominative by resumption; and others that "ahad" means "one", denying that a supporting pronoun can open a sentence. He says the second saying is closer to the ways of Arabic {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 729} {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 730}. On the reading, he reports that the readers of the cities at large read "ahad" with tanwin, while Nasr ibn Asim and Abd Allah ibn Abi Ishaq are reported to have dropped it; he holds the reading with tanwin to be the correct one, as the more eloquent and better known usage and by the agreement of the authoritative readers {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v24 p. 730}.
