## In al-Tabari's commentary

On [[passage/quran-36-58|36:58]] al-Tabari begins with how the grammarians explain the nominative of "salam". From some Kufan grammarians he reports two ways: that it is the predicate of "what they call for" in [[passage/quran-36-57|36:57]], so that what they call for is theirs, secured and unmixed, with "qawl" in the accusative as an emphasis standing outside it; or that it is nominative as praise, meaning a greeting of peace to them, spoken by God {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 466} {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 467}. He notes that it was said to be in the accusative in the reading of Abd Allah, and reports from a Basran grammarian that "qawl" is accusative in place of a verb, as if to say "I say that as a saying" {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 467}. His own choice is that "salam" is the predicate of "what they call for" and explains it: they have what they call for, and that is a greeting of peace from God upon them, while the saying stands outside it {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 467}. He rests this on a report of Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, which he gives by three chains through Harmala from Sulayman ibn Humayd, who heard Muhammad ibn Ka'b tell it to Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz: that when God has finished with the people of Paradise and of the Fire, He comes to the people of Paradise rank by rank and greets them with peace, and they return the greeting; told to ask, they ask for His good pleasure {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 467} {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 468} {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 469}. Al-Tabari concludes that this report shows "salam" to be the explanation of what they call for, and he glosses "from a merciful Lord" as merciful to them, in that He did not punish them for the wrongs they had done before in the world {cite:tafsir-tabari-turki-v19 p. 469}.
