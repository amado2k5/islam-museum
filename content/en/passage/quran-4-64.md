## Summary
Verse 64 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 64 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Messengers sent to be obeyed (pp. 515-516, reports 9904-9906, note 1).** Al-Tabari says God sent not one messenger without making obedience to him an obligation on those he was sent to, and Muhammad is one of them; this is a rebuke to the hypocrites who claimed to accept what was sent down to the Prophet and then went to the taghut, turning from him, for whoever leaves obedience to the Messenger and satisfaction with his ruling and goes to the taghut has gone against God's command and wasted His obligation. He then says that whoever obeys the messengers does so with God's leave, meaning by His decree and prior determination in His knowledge and will. Muhammad b. Amr (9904, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that obedience to them is owed by whom God wills and no one obeys them except with His leave; al-Muthanna (9905, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid likewise; al-Muthanna (9906, from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Shibl) from Mujahid likewise. Al-Tabari says this is an allusion to those hypocrites: their leaving obedience to God and His messenger and satisfaction with his ruling arises from the abandonment that was decreed for them and the dominance of wretchedness over them, otherwise they would have been among those permitted to accept his ruling and hasten to obey. This is al-Tabari's reading, given as transmitted. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of permission. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 516}

**Repentance and asking forgiveness (p. 517, report 9907, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: if these hypocrites, when they wronged their own souls by earning great sin in taking their cases to the taghut and turning from God's book and His messenger's sunna when called to them, had come to you repenting and returning, and asked God to pardon the punishment of their sin by covering it, and the Messenger had asked the same for them, they would have met God turning toward them from what they dislike to what they love, and merciful toward them in not punishing the sin they repented. Mujahid (9907, from Muhammad b. Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) said the reference is to the Jew and the Muslim who went to Ka'b b. al-Ashraf. The editor refers to the language indexes for the meaning of seeking forgiveness and repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 517}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 517}
