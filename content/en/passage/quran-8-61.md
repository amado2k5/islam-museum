## Summary
Verse 61 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 61 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 61 (pp. 40-41)**

Al-Tabari says: God says to His Prophet Muhammad, if you fear treachery and perfidy from a people, throw back to them on equal terms and notify them of war; "if they incline to peace, incline to it", meaning if they lean toward making peace with you and leaving war, whether by entering Islam, giving the poll tax, or a truce and the like among means of peace and settlement, "incline to it" means turn to it and give them what they lean toward and ask of you. One says "he inclined to this", and Tamim and Qays, it is said, use the vowel "u" in the present while others use "i"; he cites a line of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani about Amr ibn al-Harith al-A'raj when he fled to Syria, with "inclining" in the sense of leaning, and the editor in the note gives the poem's lines about the birds that follow a raiding army and praises their quality. He refers to the earlier explanation of "peace". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 40}

Report 16245, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "if they incline to peace" means to settlement, and the verse about fighting the idolaters wherever found ([[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]]) abrogated it. Report 16246, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: this came before the sura of Bara'a, and the Prophet used to make truces with peoples for a term, after which they would either become Muslims or he would fight them; then this was abrogated in Bara'a by the verse of fighting the idolaters all together ([[passage/quran-9-36|9:36]]), each covenant-holder's covenant was thrown back, and he was ordered to fight them until they say there is no god but God and become Muslims, accepting nothing else from them; every covenant in this sura and others, and every settlement by which the Muslims made peace with the idolaters, Bara'a came to abrogate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 41}

Report 16247, from Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri, through Ibn Humayd, Yahya ibn Wadih, al-Husayn and Yazid: "if they incline to peace, incline to it" was abrogated by the verse in Bara'a about fighting those who do not believe in God or the last day, up to "while they are humbled" ([[passage/quran-9-29|9:29]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 41}

We stop here at the foot of p. 41, after report 16247 in the commentary on verse 61; the next batch begins at p. 42. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 41}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
