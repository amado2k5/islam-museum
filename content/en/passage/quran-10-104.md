**Commentary on verse 104 (pp. 217-218)**

On [[passage/quran-10-104|10:104]] al-Tabari says God tells His prophet: say, Muhammad, to these polytheists of your people who wondered that revelation was sent to you: if you are in doubt, people, about my religion to which I call you, and do not know that it is true from God, I do not worship those you worship besides God, of the gods and idols that neither hear nor see nor avail me anything, such that you should doubt its soundness. This is a subtle allusion in speech, and the sense is: if you doubt my religion you should not doubt it; it is rather what you are on, the worship of idols that understand nothing and neither harm nor benefit, that you should doubt. As for my religion, you should not doubt it, for I worship God who takes creatures and makes them die when He wills, and benefits and harms them if He wills, and the worship of One who is so is not denied by anyone of sound nature, whereas the worship of idols is denied by everyone of mind and sound reason. The editor explains 'lahn' as allusion and hinting without stating, and says the printed text and manuscript have 'and keeps away whom He wills', a copyist's slip, so he put what the context requires. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 217}

His words 'but I worship God, who takes you' mean the One who takes your souls and makes you die at your terms, and 'I was commanded to be among the believers' means He is the One who commanded me to be one of those who affirm what has come to me from Him. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on 'taking'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 218}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
