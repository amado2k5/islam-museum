## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and among the people is he who sells himself seeking God's pleasure": al-Tabari means: among the people is he who sells himself for what God promised the believers who fight in His way, and for which He bought their selves in "God has bought from the believers their selves and their wealth in return for Paradise" (9:111); he has shown elsewhere that "shara" means "sold". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 246} "Seeking God's pleasure" is in the accusative governed by "yashri", as if "for the sake of seeking"; he reports the opinion of some grammarians that it is accusative by the verb after the lam is dropped, citing a verse of Hatim, and another opinion on a verbal noun standing in the place of the cause, with the editor's notes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 246} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 247} The interpreters then differed over whom it was revealed about. Some said the Emigrants and the Helpers, meaning the fighters in God's way, from Qatada (4000). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 247} Some said particular men among the Emigrants; from 'Ikrima: it was revealed about Suhayb ibn Sinan and Abu Dharr al-Ghifari, Jundub ibn al-Sakan; Abu Dharr's people seized him and he slipped away and came to the Prophet, and when he returned as an emigrant they confronted him at Marr al-Zahran and he escaped again; Suhayb's people seized him and he ransomed himself with his wealth, then left as an emigrant and Qunfudh ibn 'Umayr ibn Jud'an overtook him, so he gave him what remained of his wealth and he let him go (4001). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 248} The editor corrects the printed "Munqidh" to "Qunfudh ibn 'Umayr", who became a Companion and was made governor of Mecca by 'Umar, then dismissed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 248} From al-Rabi': a man of Mecca had become Muslim and wanted to emigrate to Medina, but they stopped and held him; he said, I will give you my house and my wealth and all I own, so let me go; they refused, then one of them said take what he has and let him go, and they did, and he left; God sent down the verse on the Prophet at Medina; when he neared Medina 'Umar met him with some men and said, the sale has profited; he said, and your sale will not lose; what is that? 'Umar said, such and such was revealed about you (4002). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 248}

Others said it means everyone who sells himself in obedience to God, in fighting in His way, or in commanding the right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 248} From Muhammad ibn Sirin: Hisham ibn 'Amir charged the line until he broke it, and they said he cast himself with his own hand, so Abu Hurayra recited "among the people is he who sells himself seeking God's pleasure" (4003); the editor places this in the Kabul campaign, notes that his name in the pre-Islamic period was Shihab, and explains "cast himself with his hand" as into destruction; Qatada has the same (4005). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 249} From al-Mughira: 'Umar sent an army that besieged a fortress, and a man of Bajila went forward, fought and was killed, and people said he cast himself with his hand into destruction; this reached 'Umar, who said, they lie, does God not say "among the people is he who sells himself…" (4004). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 249} From al-Hasan: it was revealed about a Muslim who met an unbeliever and said to him, say there is no god but God and you will protect your blood and property except for what is due; he refused, and the Muslim said, by God I will sell myself to God, advanced and fought until he was killed (4006). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 249} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 250} From Abu al-Khalil: 'Umar heard someone recite the verse, said "we belong to God and to Him we return", and said, a man stood up commanding the right and forbidding the wrong and was killed (4007). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 250}

Al-Tabari says the view closest to the apparent wording is what is reported from 'Umar, 'Ali and Ibn 'Abbas, that it means the one who commands the right and forbids the wrong, because God described two groups: a hypocrite who says with his tongue the opposite of what is in him, who commits disobedience when able and, when forbidden, is seized by pride in sin; and one who sells himself seeking God's pleasure, so the apparent sense is that he sold himself in order to confront the wicked group. The report that it came down about Suhayb is not objectionable, since a verse may be revealed on an occasion while meaning everyone its wording covers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 250} The right view, he says, is that God described someone who sells himself seeking His pleasure, so everyone who gives himself in obedience to God until killed, or courts death though not killed, is meant, whether in fighting the enemy of the Muslims or in commanding the right and forbidding the wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 251}

On "and God is kind to His servants": the meaning of ra'fa, the tenderness of mercy, was explained before; so the sense is that God has wide mercy for the servant who sells himself to Him in fighting those who oppose His command among the polytheists and the wicked, and for His other believing servants, in this world and the next, fulfilling their reward for their trial in obeying Him and settling them in His gardens. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 251}
