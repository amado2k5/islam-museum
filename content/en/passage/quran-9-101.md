## Summary
Verse 101 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 101 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 101 (pp. 440-445)**

Al-Tabari says: among the people around your city of the Bedouin are hypocrites, and among the people of your city also their like, people who are hypocrites. As for "they have persisted in hypocrisy", it means they have been hardened in it and trained in it, and from this are the "rebellious devil", meaning the wicked and defiant one, and the saying that so-and-so "rebelled against his Lord", meaning was defiant, hardened in disobedience to Him and accustomed to it. Report 17119, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "and among the people of Medina who persisted in hypocrisy", he said they stayed in it and did not repent as the others repented; report 17120, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: that is, they insisted on it and refused anything else. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the rebellious one and to Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 440}

"You do not know them", God says to His Prophet Muhammad: you, Muhammad, do not know these hypocrites whose description I gave you, of those around you among the Bedouin and the people of Medina, but We know them. Report 17121, from Qatada, through al-Hasan, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: His words "among those around you of the Bedouin are hypocrites" to "We know them", he said, what is the matter with people who take it on themselves to know about others, so-and-so is in Paradise and so-and-so in the Fire! And when you ask one of them about himself he says "I do not know!" By my life, you know yourself better than you know people's deeds, and you have taken on something the prophets before you did not take on: the Prophet of God Noah said "what knowledge have I of what they used to do", [[passage/quran-26-112|26:112]], and the Prophet of God Shu'ayb said "what remains with God is better for you if you are believers, and I am not a guardian over you", [[passage/quran-11-86|11:86]], and God said to His Prophet "you do not know them, We know them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 441}

As for "We shall punish them twice", it means We shall punish these hypocrites twice, one in the world and the other in the grave. The interpreters then differed over what the one in the world is. Some said it is their exposure, God exposing them by uncovering their affairs and making their inner thoughts clear to the people through His Messenger. Report 17122, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Husayn ibn Amr al-Anqazi, his father, Asbat, al-Suddi and Abu Malik: the Messenger of God stood preaching on Friday and said "go out, so-and-so, for you are a hypocrite, go out, so-and-so, for you are a hypocrite", and drove some of them out of the mosque, shaming them; Umar met them as they were leaving the mosque, and hid from them in shame that he had not attended the Friday prayer, thinking the people had dispersed, and they hid from Umar, thinking he had learned of their affair; Umar came and entered the mosque and found the people had not yet prayed, and a man of the Muslims said to him "good news, Umar, God has exposed the hypocrites today!", so that was the first punishment, when he drove them out of the mosque, and the second punishment is the punishment of the grave. The editor notes that al-Haythami records the report from al-Tabarani's al-Awsat and calls al-Husayn ibn Amr weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 442}

Report 17123, from Abu Malik, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Sufyan and al-Suddi: "We shall punish them twice", he said, the Messenger of God used to preach and mention the hypocrites, and so punished them with his tongue; he said, and the punishment of the grave. Others said rather it is what befalls them of captivity, killing, hunger and fear in this world. Report 17124, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar and Ibn Abi Najih: killing and captivity; report 17125, from him, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: by hunger and the punishment of the grave, he said "then they will be returned to a great punishment" on the Day of Resurrection; report 17126, from him, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ja'far ibn Awn, al-Qasim, Yahya ibn Adam and Sufyan: hunger and killing, and Yahya said fear and killing; report 17127, from him, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Yaman and Sufyan: by hunger and killing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 442}

Report 17128, from Abu Malik, through Ibn Waki', Yahya ibn Yaman, Sufyan and al-Suddi: "We shall punish them twice", he said, by hunger and the punishment of the grave; report 17129, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Ibn Abi Najih: hunger and killing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 443}

Others said the meaning is: We shall punish them with a punishment in this world and a punishment in the hereafter. Report 17130, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "We shall punish them twice", the punishment of the world and the punishment of the grave, then they are returned to a great punishment; it was mentioned to us that the Prophet of God confided to Hudhayfa the names of twelve men of the hypocrites, saying "six of them will be dealt with by the abscess, a lamp of Hell's fire that takes hold in the shoulder of one of them until it reaches his chest, and six will die a death"; and it was mentioned to us that when a man died whom Umar thought to be one of them, he would look to Hudhayfa, and if he prayed over him he prayed over him, and if not he left him; and that Umar said to Hudhayfa "I adjure you by God, am I one of them?", and he said "no, by God, and I will clear no one of it after you". The editor explains the abscess as a large boil or swelling that appears in the belly and usually kills its owner. Report 17131, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the punishment of the world and the punishment of the grave; report 17132, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Muhammad ibn al-Ala', Badal ibn al-Muhabbar and Shu'ba: a punishment in the world and a punishment in the grave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 443}

Report 17133, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: the punishment of the world and the punishment of the grave, then they are returned to the punishment of the Fire. Others said that one of the two times of their punishment was the misfortunes in their wealth and children and the other in Hell. Report 17134, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "We shall punish them twice", he said, as for the punishment in the world, it is wealth and children, and he recited the verse "let not their wealth and children impress you, God only wishes to punish them with them in the worldly life", [[passage/quran-9-55|9:55]], by the misfortunes in them, which are a punishment for them and a reward for the believers; and a punishment in the hereafter in the Fire, "then they are returned to a great punishment", he said the Fire. Others said rather that one of the two is the prescribed penalties and the other the punishment of the grave, which is mentioned of Ibn Abbas by an unapproved route. Others said rather that one of the two is the taking of zakat from their wealth and the other the punishment of the grave, which is mentioned of Sulayman ibn Arqam from al-Hasan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 444}

Others said rather that one of the two is their punishment through the rage that enters them at the affairs of Islam. Report 17135, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "We shall punish them twice", he said, the punishment He promised them twice, as it reached me, being their distress at what they are in of the affair of Islam and the rage over it that enters them without reward, then their punishment in the grave when they come to it, then the great punishment to which they are returned, the punishment of the hereafter and abiding in it. The editor notes that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 444}

Al-Tabari holds that the best of the views is to say that God told that He punishes these who persisted in hypocrisy twice, and set for us no evidence by which to reach knowledge of the nature of those two punishments; it is possible that some of what we related of the speakers is it, but we have no knowledge which of them it is, except that in His words "then they are returned to a great punishment" is an indication that the punishment of both times is before their entry into the Fire, and the likelier of the two is that one is in the grave. As for "then they will be returned to a great punishment", it means that after God punishes these hypocrites twice, they will be returned to a great punishment, which is the punishment of Hell. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 445}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
