**Commentary on verse 22 (pp. 459-461)**

Al-Tabari says on "the worst of beasts with God are the deaf, the dumb, who do not reason" [[passage/quran-8-22|8:22]]: the worst of what crawls on the earth of God's creation, with God, are those who turn aside from the truth lest they hear it and take lesson from it and be admonished by it, and who turn back from it if they speak it, who do not understand from God His command and prohibition so as to apply them to their bodies. Report 15856, from Ibn Zayd: the "beasts" are the creatures; report 15857, from Ikrima: they used to say we are deaf and dumb to what Muhammad calls to, we do not hear it from him nor answer it with belief, and they were all killed at Uhud, being the standard-bearers; report 15858, from Mujahid: those who do not follow the truth; report 15859, from Ibn Zayd: it is not the deaf in this world or the dumb, but the deaf of heart, its dumb and its blind, and he recited [[passage/quran-22-46|22:46]]. There was disagreement over whom this verse means; some said it means a group of the polytheists, and reports 15860 and 15861, from Ibn Abbas: the deaf, the dumb who do not reason are a group of Banu Abd al-Dar who do not follow the truth. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "beast", "deaf" and "dumb". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 459} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 460}

…and the repeated report 15861 is in the same sense from Mujahid. Others said it means the hypocrites, and report 15862, from Ibn Ishaq: the hypocrites whom I forbade you to be like, dumb to good, deaf to the truth, who do not reason, who do not know what is on them in that of the vengeance and the reckoning. The editor says what is in brackets was doubtless dropped by the manuscript's copyist, since without it the report has no mention of the hypocrites, so he added it from the Sira, and explains "taba'a" as what carries a sin one is pursued for. Abu Ja'far said: the better of the two views is that of those who spoke as Ibn Abbas did, that this verse means the polytheists of Quraysh, since it is in the course of the report about them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 460} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 461}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
