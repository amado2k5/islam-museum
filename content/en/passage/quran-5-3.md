## Summary
Verse 3 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/farewell-sermon-at-arafat|Farewell Sermon at Arafat]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 3 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari then turns to the third verse, the one naming what God forbade: the carrion, blood, pig's flesh and what was dedicated to other than God. He says: God forbade you, believers, the carrion; 'carrion' is every creature with flowing blood, of land beasts and birds, that God permitted to be eaten, tame or wild, whose life left it without slaughter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 492}

On 'and the flesh of swine', the sense is that God forbade you the flesh of swine, the tame and the wild. Carrion and blood are general in outward form but particular in meaning; the flesh of swine is wholly forbidden inside and out, with nothing excepted. On 'what was dedicated to other than God', the sense is what was named on with other than God's name; the word comes from the cry of a newborn at birth, and from the call of the pilgrim who utters the talbiya; it means what was slaughtered for the gods and idols. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 493}

On 'the strangled', the commentators differ on how it happens: some say the animal puts its head between two branches of a tree and is strangled; report 11002, from al-Suddi: the one that puts its head between two branches of a tree and is strangled and dies; report 11003, from Qatada: the one that dies in its strangling. Others say it is tied and strangled by its tether; report 11004, from al-Dahhak: the sheep is tied and killed by its tether. Others say it is a beast of cattle the idolaters strangled until it died; report 11005, from Ibn Abbas, and report 11006, from Qatada, the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 494}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that the strangled is the one that is choked, whether in its tether or by putting its head where it cannot free itself and is choked to death; since the word describes it as strangled, not as strangled by another, which would be the strangled-one form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 495}

On 'the beaten to death', from the verb meaning to strike until one is near death: report 11007, from Ibn Abbas: the one beaten with wood until it dies; report 11008, from Qatada: the idolaters used to strike it with sticks, and when it died they ate it; report 11009, from Qatada through Shu'ba: they struck it until it died and then ate it; report 11010, from Ma'mar through Qatada: the one beaten until it dies; report 11011, from al-Dahhak: the one beaten until it dies; report 11012, from al-Suddi: the one struck until it dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 496}

Report 11013, from al-Dahhak: the animal of cattle or other, struck with wood for their gods until they killed it and ate it; report 11014, from Abu Abd Allah al-Sunabihi: the beaten is only in the case of livestock, and there is no beaten in game. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 497}

On 'the fallen', it means the one that falls from a high place, from a mountain or into a well or otherwise; falling is throwing oneself from high to low. Report 11015, from Ibn Abbas: the one that falls from the mountain; report 11016, from Qatada: the one that falls into the well and they eat it; report 11017, from Qatada: the one that falls into the well; report 11018, from al-Suddi: the one that falls from the mountain or into a well and dies; report 11019, from al-Dahhak: the one that falls into a well or from the top of a mountain and dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 498}

On 'the gored', it is the sheep gored by another so that it dies from the goring without slaughter; God forbade it to the believers unless they reach its slaughter before it dies. The word is a form of 'the gored one' changed from the passive to the form fa'ila. Some Basran grammarians keep the feminine marker because the word became like a noun, like tariqa and tawila, as if the gored were taken to mean the goring one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 499}

Al-Tabari explains the feminine ending of 'the gored': when the Arabs drop the noun modified by a fa'ila formed from a passive, they keep the feminine marker so it is known to be an attribute of a feminine; hence the marker is kept in 'the gored'; without it one could not tell if it were masculine or feminine. He holds this to be the soundest view, since the commentators widely say it means 'the struck'. Report 11021, from Ibn Abbas: a sheep that gores a sheep; report 11022, from Abu Maysara, who read 'the gored' with the passive; report 11023, from al-Dahhak: two sheep butt each other and both die; report 11024, from al-Suddi: the one gored by sheep or cattle and dying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 500}

Report 11025, from Qatada: two rams butted, one died, and they ate it; report 11026, the same; report 11027, from al-Dahhak: a sheep gores a sheep and it dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 501}

On 'what the beast of prey has eaten', the sense is the forbidding of what a beast of prey has taken, other than a trained hunting beast. Report 11028, from Ibn Abbas: what the beast of prey seized; report 11029, from al-Dahhak: the same; report 11030, from Qatada: in the Age of Ignorance, when a beast of prey killed something or ate from it, they ate what was left; report 11031, from Ibn Abbas through Ata ibn al-Sa'ib: he read 'the eaten by a beast of prey' in the form of a noun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 502}

On 'except what you have slaughtered', the sense is except what you have purified by the slaughter that God made a purifier. Commentators differ on what it excepts. Some say it is an exception from all that God named as forbidden, from what was dedicated to other than God, the strangled, the beaten, the fallen, the gored and what the beast of prey ate. Report 11032, from Ibn Abbas: whatever of these is caught alive so that its tail moves or its eye blinks, slaughter it and name God's name, and it is lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 502}

Report 11033, from al-Hasan: whichever of these you catch in time to slaughter, slaughter and eat; asked how one knows, he said: when it blinks its eye or strikes its tail. Report 11034, from Qatada: all of this, except swine's flesh, if you catch from it an eye that blinks, a tail that moves, or a leg that kicks, and you slaughter it, God has made it lawful for you. Report 11035, from Qatada through Ma'mar: the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 503}

Report 11036, from Ali: if the slaughter of the beaten, the fallen or the gored is caught while it moves a hand or foot, eat it; report 11037, from Ibrahim: when a beast of prey has eaten from the game, or the beaten, the gored or the fallen, and you catch its slaughter, eat it; report 11038, from Ja'far al-Sadiq through his father Muhammad al-Baqir from Ali: if it kicks with its leg, blinks its eye or moves its tail, that suffices. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 503}

Report 11039, from Ibn Tawus through his father: if you slaughter it and it wags its tail or moves, it is lawful for you; report 11040, from al-Hasan: if the beaten blinks its eye, kicks with its leg or wags its tail, slaughter and eat; report 11042, from Ubayd ibn Umayr: if it blinks its eye, wags its tail or moves, it is lawful for you; report 11043, from al-Dahhak: the people of the Age of Ignorance ate these; God forbade them in Islam except what is slaughtered, so whatever moves a man, tail or eye, and is slaughtered, is lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 504}

Report 11044, from Ibn Zayd: all of this is forbidden, except what is slaughtered from it. Al-Tabari says that on this view the beaten and the fallen are forbidden if they die from the fall, the beating, the goring or the beast's attack, unless you catch their slaughter before they die, and then eating is lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 504}

Others say that 'except what you have slaughtered' is an exception from the prohibition, not from the list of forbidden things, since carrion has no slaughter; the sense is: carrion, blood and all we named are forbidden, except what you have slaughtered by the slaughter God made lawful; this view is held by a group of the people of Medina. Report 11045, from Malik, asked about a sheep whose belly was torn by a beast until its intestines came out: he said he did not think it could be slaughtered or eaten; report 11046, from Malik through Ashhab: if the beast reached the chest, he did not think it should be eaten; if it only reached the limbs, there is no harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 505}

Al-Tabari prefers the first view, that 'except what you have slaughtered' is an exception from what precedes: what was dedicated to other than God, the strangled, the beaten, the fallen, the gored, and what the beast of prey ate, since each is described by the state in which it is before its death; whatever is caught alive before its soul leaves its body, and which God permitted, is lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 505}

He raises an objection: why list again what was forbidden, when the verse began with carrion, which includes all carrion? He answers that the listing is for clarity, since these things, though carrion in fact, are named by their names so it is known that their ruling is prohibition even if they are not yet dead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 506}

Al-Tabari answers: the reason for repeating it, though the ruling for what dies of the causes by which it is described was already given with carrion, is that those addressed by this verse counted as carrion only what died of an incidental illness other than strangling, falling, goring or a beast's attack; so God told them the ruling for these is that of what dies of incidental illnesses, and that the cause making carrion forbidden is not death by sickness or harm before it perished, but that it was not slaughtered by the slaughter that made it lawful. Report 11047, from al-Suddi through Asbat: this is forbidden because some Arabs ate it and did not count it as carrion, counting as dead only what died of sickness; so God forbade it to them except what had God's name said over it and whose slaughter was caught with life still in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 507}

On 'and what was slaughtered on the stone altars', it means God also forbade what was slaughtered on the nusub; 'what' is nominative, joined to the 'what' of 'what the beast of prey ate'. The nusub are stone idols, plural ansab, set up in a place where the idolaters offered to them, and they are not images. Ibn Jurayj described them (report 11048): the nusub are not images, which are shaped and carved, but stones set up, three hundred and sixty, some say three hundred of them belonging to Khuza'a; when they slaughtered they sprinkled the blood on what faced the House and cut the meat in slices and laid it on the stones. The Muslims said: the people of the Age of Ignorance honoured the House with blood, so we have more right to honour it; and God revealed that neither their flesh nor their blood reaches Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 508}

Report 11049, from Mujahid through Ibn Uyayna and Ibn Abi Najih: stones on which the people of the Age of Ignorance slaughtered; report 11050: stones around the Ka'ba on which they slaughtered, replacing them whenever they liked with stones they preferred; report 11051 gives the same through Shibl. Report 11052, from Qatada: the nusub are stones the people of the Age of Ignorance worshipped and slaughtered for, which God forbade; report 11053, from Qatada through Ma'mar: the stone altars of the Age of Ignorance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 509}

Report 11054, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha: stone altars on which they slaughtered and pronounced the call; report 11055, from Mujahid, in the sense given; report 11056, from al-Dahhak: stones for which they pronounced and on which they slaughtered; report 11057, from Ibn Zayd: what is slaughtered on the nusub and what is dedicated to other than God are one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 509}

On 'and to seek to divide by the arrows', it means to seek knowledge of what is apportioned to you or not by arrows; it is formed from 'qism', the apportioning of provision and needs. In the Age of Ignorance, when one of them wished to travel or raid, he cast the arrows, which are the azlam, and some were written 'my Lord has forbidden me' and others 'my Lord has commanded me'; if the arrow marked with the command came out he went on, and if the one marked with the prohibition came out he held back. The azlam are the plural of zalam, also zulam, the divining arrows described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 510}

Report 11058, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr through Sufyan and Abu Husayn: the arrows; when they wished to set out they made arrows for sitting and for setting out, and if setting out came up they went, and if sitting came up they sat. Report 11059, from him: white pebbles they cast; al-Tabari reports Sufyan ibn Wakī' saying it is chess, which the editor calls a very odd claim. Report 11060, from al-Hasan: they took three arrows, one marked 'command me', another 'forbid me', and left the third blank; then cast them, and if the blank one came out they cast again. Report 11061, from Mujahid: stones they wrote on, called the arrows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 511}

Reports 11062 and 11063, from Mujahid: the arrows cast for every journey, raid and trade; reports 11064 and 11065: the Persians' dice with which they gambled, and the Arabs' arrows. Report 11066, from Qatada: a man wishing to travel wrote on one arrow 'this orders me to stay' and on another 'this orders me to go', and put with them a blank one, then cast them: if the one ordering to stay came up he stayed, if the one ordering to go came up he went, and if the other came up he cast again. Report 11067 gives the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 512}

Report 11068, from al-Dahhak: they divided by the arrows in their affairs. Report 11069, from Ibn Zayd: the azlam were their arrows; one who wanted something wrote what he wanted on the arrows and cast them, and whichever came out he followed, even if it was what he liked least. Report 11070, from al-Suddi: the arrows kept in the Age of Ignorance by the soothsayers; a man who wished to travel or marry gave the soothsayer something and he cast the arrows for him; if what he liked came out he told him to do it, and if what he disliked came out he forbade it, as Abd al-Muttalib cast the arrows at Zamzam and over Abd Allah and the camels. Report 11071, from Abd Allah ibn Kathir: we heard that the people of the Age of Ignorance cast the arrows over departing and staying, and the arrow of departing came out and they departed, or staying and they stayed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 513}

We stop here at p. 513, before report 11072, Ibn Ishaq's account of the arrows, in the commentary on verse 3 of sura al-Ma'ida, and will continue from it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 513}
