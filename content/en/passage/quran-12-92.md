**Commentary on verse 92 (pp. 246-247)**

On "he said, no reproach upon you today; God will forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful", al-Tabari says: Joseph said to his brothers "no reproach", that is, no censure on you and no spoiling of what is between me and you of sanctity and the right of brotherhood, but you have from me pardon and forgiveness. The editor notes that the printed text and the manuscript have "taghyir" with a ghayn, that the correct form is with the unpointed 'ayn, and that he corrected it wherever it occurs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 246}

The interpreters said the same: report 19795, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "no reproach upon you", he did not reproach them for their deeds. Report 19796, from Sufyan, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr: no censure upon you. Report 19797, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: no blame on you today with me for what you did. Report 19798, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: they apologised to Joseph and he said "no reproach upon you today", he says, I will not mention your sin to you. "God will forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful" is Joseph's prayer for his brothers that God forgive them their sin in what they did to him and the wrong they committed against him; he says: God pardon your sin and wrong and veil it for you, "and He is the most merciful of the merciful" to whoever repents of his sin and turns to obedience to Him by repentance from disobedience. Report 19799, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "God will forgive you, and He is the most merciful of the merciful", when they confessed their sin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 247}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
