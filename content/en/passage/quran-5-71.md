**Commentary on verse 71: "They thought there would be no trial", pp. 478-480**

Al-Tabari says: these Israelites, whose covenant God took and to whom He sent messengers whom they denied or killed, thought that God would not try them with hardships and punishments for what they did, so they went blind to the truth and to keeping the covenant, and deaf to it. Then God turned to them, that is, guided them gently until they repented and returned from their sins to obedience; then many of them went blind and deaf again after His turning to them and His rescue of them from ruin. "God sees what they do" means He sees their deeds, good and bad, and will repay them for all of it on the day of resurrection. He also points to the discussion of why "many" is in the nominative, found in al-Farra and Abu Ubayda.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 478}

Report 12288, from Qatada, says the people thought there would be no trial; 12289, from al-Suddi, that they thought they would not be tested. Report 12290, from al-Hasan, says the trial is affliction, and 12291, from Ibn Abbas, that it is polytheism. Report 12292, from Mujahid, says they are the Jews.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 479}

Report 12293, from Mujahid and from Ibn Jurayj on Abdullah ibn Kathir, says the verse is about the Children of Israel and that the trial is affliction and testing.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 480}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
