## Place in the museum

**Commentary on verse 172 (pp. 222-235)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses His prophet Muhammad: remember, Muhammad, when your Lord brought the offspring of Adam out of the loins of their fathers, had them affirm His oneness, and made them bear witness over one another to that testimony and acknowledgement. The editor notes that al-Tabari already explained this verse earlier, at report 10855, and did not repeat it here, abridging. Report 15338, from Ibn Abbas from the Prophet: God took the covenant from the back of Adam at Nu'man, meaning Arafa, and brought out of his loins every offspring He had created, scattered them before Him like ants, then spoke to them face to face and said "Am I not your Lord?" and they said yes. The editor says the printed edition had a different word, the publisher thinking it was from recitation, and that the right reading is "qibalan", face to face and confronting, not from behind a veil, and he refers to earlier explanations of this word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 222}

The editor then writes at length on the tracing of this report: Ibn Abbas's report by way of Kulthum ibn Jabr from Sa'id ibn Jubayr is given by al-Tabari with five chains, this one and reports 15339 to 15341 and then 15350; the first alone is traced up to the Prophet and the rest stop at Ibn Abbas, and al-Tabari also gives it raised to the Prophet in his History. Ahmad gives it raised in the Musnad by the route of Husayn ibn Muhammad, which is al-Tabari's route; al-Hakim gives it raised in the Mustadrak by the route of Ibrahim ibn Marzuq al-Basri from Wahb ibn Jarir from his father and says the chain is sound though the two compilers did not include it, adding that Muslim relied on Kulthum ibn Jabr, and al-Dhahabi agreed; al-Haythami mentions it in Majma' al-Zawa'id and says its men are the men of the sound collection. The stopped form is given by Ibn Sa'd in the Tabaqat by the route of Ibn 'Ulayya and Hammad ibn Zayd from Kulthum. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir discussed it in his commentary and history and made the number of those who stopped it a flaw against the one who raised it, and that his brother Ahmad Shakir said in his commentary on the Musnad that raising is an addition from a trustworthy narrator and so accepted, and that the chain is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 223}

The editor identifies the transmitters: Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Tusi, al-Tabari's teacher, trustworthy; Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Bahram al-Tamimi, called "the teacher" and "the instructor"; Kulthum ibn Jabr, trustworthy among the younger Followers; and "Nu'man" is a valley of Hudhayl behind Arafa, two nights from it, that is Nu'man al-Arak, often named in their poetry. Report 15339, from Kulthum: I asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr about the verse and he asked Ibn Abbas, who said: your Lord wiped the back of Adam and brought out every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection at this Nu'man, pointing with his hand, and took their covenants and made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes. The editor identifies Imran ibn Musa al-Laythi, the sack-maker, al-Tabari's teacher, and Abd al-Warith ibn Sa'id ibn Dhakwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 223}

Report 15340, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Ibn 'Ulayya from Kulthum: your Lord wiped the back of Adam and every soul He creates came out until the Day of Resurrection at this Nu'man behind Arafa, and He took their covenant; the wording is that of Ya'qub's report. Report 15341, from Kulthum by way of his son Rabi'a, in the same sense, with the words "they said yes, we witness, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection that we were unaware of this". The editor identifies Ibn 'Ulayya as Ismail ibn Ibrahim al-Asadi and says this is the second of the reports stopped at Ibn Abbas, given by al-Tabari in the History with this chain and by Ibn Sa'd in the Tabaqat with this wording, its chain sound. In a note he explains that the singular form of the word for "their offspring" is one of the two readings and is the one used in his region, the other being the plural; al-Tabari does not mention this reading, the manuscript has the singular often and the plural in some reports, and the publisher had put the plural in all, so the editor follows the manuscript and keeps each as it stands there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 223} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 224}

Report 15342, from Ibn Abbas: when God first sent Adam down He sent him down at Dahna, a land in India, and God wiped his back and brought out of it every soul He creates until the Hour comes, then took the covenant from them and made them witness against themselves. The editor stops long at the name of the place: the printed edition has it with a jim, the manuscript with a ha, Ibn Sa'd gives it with a ha and a hamza, and al-Tabari's History has a form ending in hamza, which he thinks wrong. He then distinguishes two places. The first is the Dahna of the Arabian peninsula, named in the Sira from Ibn Ishaq when the Prophet returned from Ta'if by way of Dahna and halted at al-Ji'rana, and given by al-Bakri in his gazetteer and by Yaqut, who calls it one of the districts of Ta'if; the editor cites two poetic witnesses, by Rabi'a ibn Jahdar of Hudhayl and one from al-Asma'i, and thinks the commentator on the poetry made it a place on the sea coast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 224} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 225}

The second is the Dahna named in this report, described in it as a land in India. Al-Bakri mentions it under "Washim" from Ibn Ishaq: the people of the Book say that the place where Adam and Eve came down is on a mountain called Washim in the land of India, in the middle of its villages between Dahnaj and Mandal, and al-Tabari's History has something like it, from the people of the Torah, that Adam was sent down in India on a mountain called Wasim beside a valley called Buhayl, between al-Hanj and Mandal, two towns in India. The editor concludes that the reports which mention the creation or descent of Adam at Dahna without saying where are clarified by these reports, which place it in India, that Dahna with a ha is the Arabised form of Dahnaj, and that the Arabian one lies between Mecca and Ta'if. He says the author of Lisan al-Arab confused the two, that the Qamus gives it with a jim or a ha and refers one to the other, and that al-Zabidi in Taj al-Arus also confused the place of the Sira with the place of Adam's creation or descent, following al-Suhayli in al-Rawd al-Unuf; the cause of the confusion is the mention of Nu'man al-Arak, in the Arabs' land, in the report of Adam's creation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 225} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 226}

Report 15343, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Imran ibn Uyayna from Ata from Sa'id: Adam was sent down and God wiped his back and brought out of it every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection, then said "Am I not your Lord?" and they said yes, then recited the verse, and the pen dried from that day over what will be until the Day of Resurrection. The editor identifies Amr as Amr ibn Ali al-Fallas and Imran ibn Uyayna as the brother of Sufyan ibn Uyayna, giving the verdict of Ibn Ma'in and Abu Zur'a that he is "fit for hadith", Ibn Abi Hatim's that his hadith is not to be argued from because he brings objectionable reports, and al-'Uqayli's that there is error in his hadith; al-Tabari gives the report abridged in the History, as does Ibn Sa'd, and it will come by other chains, not through Imran, from Ata, without the mention of Dahna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 226} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 227}

Report 15344, from Ibn Abbas: when God created Adam He took his offspring from his back like ants and took two handfuls, saying to the people of the right hand "Enter Paradise in peace" and to the others "Enter the Fire and I do not mind". The editor identifies Yahya ibn Isa al-Nahshali, whom Ahmad and others held reliable and Ibn Ma'in and others held weak, and Habib ibn Abi Thabit, that is Habib ibn Qays ibn Dinar, trustworthy, and says the sense of the report is traced in the Musnads of a number of Companions, though he did not find this exact wording from Ibn Abbas except here and in al-Tabari's History. Report 15345, from Ibn Abbas by another chain: God wiped the back of Adam and brought out every good one in His right hand and every bad one in the other; the editor notes that al-Ajurri gives it in Kitab al-Shari'a by the route of Ali ibn Mus-hir from al-A'mash in other words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 227} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228}

Report 15346, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Shurayk from Ata from Sa'id: God wiped the back of Adam and drew out of it every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection; the editor calls the chain sound and refers back. Report 15347, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Amr ibn Abi Qays from Ata: when God created Adam He wiped his back at Dahna and brought out of his back every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection and said "Am I not your Lord?", they said yes, and "so they are thought that day" that the pen dried over what will be until the Day of Resurrection. The editor says Amr ibn Abi Qays al-Razi is trustworthy though he erred a little, that al-Tabari gives it in the History, that Ibn Sa'd gives it by the route of Mansur ibn Abi al-Aswad from Ata in much the same words, ending with Humayd's statement that they supposed the covenant was that day, that al-Durr al-Manthur attributes it to Ibn al-Mundhir alone, and that the passive "they are thought" means they suppose and reckon it so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228}

Report 15348, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of al-Mas'udi from Ali ibn Badhima from Sa'id: when God created Adam He took his covenant, wiped his back, took his offspring in the form of ants, and wrote their appointed terms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228}

Report 15348 from Ibn Abbas is completed: He wrote their terms, provisions and afflictions, and made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes. The editor says al-Tabari gave it by two chains, this and the next, and identifies al-Mas'udi as Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Utba ibn Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, trustworthy but confused late and with a changed memory, what the old teachers narrated from him being sound and Waki's hearing from him early; and Ali ibn Badhima al-Jazari, trustworthy, spoken of in ways that do no harm. He notes that al-Suyuti gives it in al-Durr al-Manthur, attributing it to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu al-Shaykh. Report 15349, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Yazid ibn Harun from al-Mas'udi: when God created Adam He took his covenant that He was his Lord and wrote his term and afflictions, drew out his offspring like ants, took their covenant, and wrote their terms, provisions and afflictions. The editor identifies Yazid ibn Harun al-Sulami as a famous memoriser and says he narrated mixed hadiths from al-Mas'udi after his memory changed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 229}
Report 15350, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Rabi'a ibn Kulthum from his father: God wiped the back of Adam while he was in the belly of Nu'man, a valley beside Arafa, and brought his offspring out of his back like ants, then made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes, we witness; the editor says this is another route for reports 15338 to 15341, whose tracing is given there. Report 15351, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Abu Hilal from Abu Jamra al-Duba'i: God brought the offspring of Adam out of his back like ants while it was in the "adhi" of water. The editor identifies Abu Hilal as Muhammad ibn Sulaym al-Rasibi, trustworthy and spoken of, quotes Ibn Adi that his hadiths are all or mostly unpreserved yet are to be written down, and Abu Jamra as Nasr ibn Imran ibn Isam al-Duba'i, trustworthy and reliable, noting that both printed edition and manuscript had a wrong name. He explains "adhi of water" as the layers the wind raises from its surface without waves, though it also means heavy waves, which is commoner, and the first sense is meant here; and that Ibn Kathir transmitted the report and al-Suyuti attributed it to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Manda in his refutation of the Jahmiyya, and Abu al-Shaykh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 229} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 230}
Report 15352, from Juwaybir: a son of al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, six days old, died, and al-Dahhak said: Jabir, when you lay my son in his grave, uncover his face and undo his knots, for my son is made to sit and is questioned. I did what he told me, and when I had finished I said: God have mercy on you, about what is your son asked, and who asks him? He said: he is asked about the covenant he acknowledged in the loins of Adam. I said: Abu al-Qasim, what is this covenant? He said: Ibn Abbas told me that God wiped the loins of Adam and drew out every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection, and took from them the covenant to worship Him and associate nothing with Him, and guaranteed their provisions; the Hour will not come until everyone who gave the covenant that day is born; whoever reaches the later covenant and keeps it, the first benefits him; whoever reaches it and does not keep it, the first does not benefit him; and whoever dies young before reaching the later covenant dies on the first covenant, on the natural disposition. The editor notes that the words "and who asks him" are missing from the printed edition and al-Durr al-Manthur but are in the manuscript and in Ibn Kathir, and that both manuscript and printed edition have a gap in the middle of the report which he restores from Ibn Kathir and al-Durr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 231}
The editor identifies the transmitters: Ali ibn Sahl al-Ramli, al-Tabari's teacher; Damra ibn Rabi'a al-Filastini, trustworthy; Abu Mas'ud, the narrator from Juwaybir, whom he fears to be Sa'id ibn Iyas al-Jurayri without being able to confirm it; and Juwaybir, a nickname, the name being said to be Jabir ibn Sa'id, which this report gives as Jabir. He notes that Ibn Kathir quoted it in his commentary and al-Suyuti in al-Durr. Then report 15353, from al-Aswad ibn Sari' of Banu Sa'd: I took part in four expeditions with the Messenger of God; the men took hold of the children after they had killed the fighters, and it reached the Messenger of God, who was much displeased and said: what is the matter with people who take hold of the children? A man said: are they not the children of the polytheists? He said: the best of you are children of polytheists; no soul is born but is born on the natural disposition and stays on it until its tongue speaks for it, and its parents make it Jewish or Christian. Al-Hasan said: God said this in His book, and he recited the verse. The editor remarks that the printed edition added an oath before "God said" and he does not know from where. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 231}
The editor identifies al-Sari ibn Yahya al-Shaybani, Abu al-Haytham, trustworthy and sure, who narrated from al-Hasan al-Basri, and al-Aswad ibn Sari' al-Tamimi, a Companion and famous poet, the first to give sermons in the mosque of Basra, quoting a line of his on the dead and listing the books that give his biography. He traces the report: Ahmad gave it in the Musnad by this route with a slight difference in wording and by others, among them Yunus ibn Muhammad al-Mu'addib from Aban ibn Yazid from Qatada from al-Hasan and then Yunus ibn Ubayd from al-Hasan, saying it happened in a raiding party the Messenger of God sent on the day of Hunayn; Ibn Sa'd gave it abridged, al-Bukhari in the History, Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Istiab, and Ibn Hajar mentions it in al-Isaba; al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak and said it is sound on the condition of the two shaykhs, al-Dhahabi agreeing; al-Bayhaqi gave it in the Great Sunan; al-Haythami mentions it in Majma' al-Zawa'id and says the men of some of Ahmad's chains are the men of the sound collection; and Ibn Kathir says al-Nasa'i gave it from Hushaym from Yunus ibn Ubayd from al-Hasan from al-Aswad without al-Hasan's words and his bringing the verse to mind. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 231} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 232}
Report 15354, from Abd Allah ibn Amr: the Messenger of God said of the verse: they were taken from his back as one takes with a comb from the head, and He said to them "Am I not your Lord?", they said yes, and the angels said "we witness, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection", the verse. The editor says al-Tabari gave it by three routes, the first raised to the Prophet and the other two stopped at Abd Allah ibn Amr, and that it has two chains: Sufyan al-Thawri from al-Ajlah, and Sufyan from Mansur. He found no biography of Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Walid al-Jurjani, al-Tabari's teacher; Ahmad ibn Abi Tayba is Ahmad ibn Isa ibn Sulayman al-Jurjani, judge of Qumis, of whom Abu Hatim said his hadith is written down, Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy, and Ibn Adi said he narrated hadiths most of them unusual; and al-Ajlah is Ibn Abd Allah ibn Hujiyya al-Kindi, spoken against and held reliable by Ibn Adi. Al-Suyuti gave the report as raised in al-Durr, while Ibn Kathir weakened its raising and showed that stopping it is sounder. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 232} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 233}
The editor notes that al-Tabari's own judgement of it comes at p. 250: he does not know it to be sound, because the trustworthy narrators relied on for memory and precision narrated this hadith from al-Thawri stopping it at Abd Allah ibn Amr, without raising it and without the point Ahmad ibn Abi Tayba mentioned. Reports 15355 and 15356, from Abd Allah ibn Amr by the chain of Mansur from Mujahid: He took them as the comb takes from the head; in the second Ibn Humayd's wording is "as one takes with a comb". The editor calls them the two sound stopped reports with a sound chain, says Ibn Kathir mentioned all three, and that al-Suyuti gave it as stopped, attributing it to Ibn Abi Shayba, Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Abu al-Shaykh and al-Lalaka'i in al-Sunna, and falling short in not naming Ibn Jarir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 233}
Report 15357, from Muslim ibn Yasar al-Juhani: Umar ibn al-Khattab was asked about the verse and said: I heard the Messenger of God say: God created Adam, then wiped over his back with His right hand and drew out offspring and said "I created these for Paradise and they act with the deeds of the people of Paradise"; then He wiped over his back and drew out offspring and said "I created these for the Fire and they act with the deeds of the people of the Fire". A man said: Messenger of God, then what is the action for? He said: when God creates a servant for Paradise He uses him in the deeds of the people of Paradise until he dies on one of their deeds and enters him into Paradise, and when He creates a servant for the Fire He uses him in the deeds of the people of the Fire until he dies on one of their deeds and enters him into the Fire. The editor notes that the printed edition had "wiped his back" and he follows the manuscript and the History. He identifies Ibrahim ibn Sa'id al-Jawhari, al-Tabari's teacher; Sa'd ibn Abd al-Hamid ibn Ja'far al-Ansari, trustworthy though weakened, which does no harm here since Rawh ibn Ubada is trustworthy and the mainstay of the report; Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa al-Jazari, trustworthy; Abd al-Hamid ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn al-Khattab, trustworthy and reliable; and Muslim ibn Yasar al-Juhani, a trustworthy Follower, of whom it is said he did not hear from Umar, Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a al-Azdi standing between them as in the next report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 233} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 234}
The editor traces the report: al-Tabari gave it with this chain in his History with a slight difference; Malik in the Muwatta, Ahmad in the Musnad and Abu Dawud in his Sunan (no. 4703) gave it; al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak, calling it sound on the condition of both compilers, and al-Dhahabi remarked that it has a break, but in another place al-Hakim called it sound on Muslim's condition and al-Dhahabi agreed there without the remark, which the editor finds strange; al-Ajurri gave it in al-Shari'a; Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Taqassi says its chain has two flaws, set out in al-Tamhid; and al-Tirmidhi gave it in the commentary section and called it good, adding that Muslim ibn Yasar did not hear from Umar and that some put a man between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 234}
Report 15358 has the chain of Muhammad ibn al-Musaffa from Baqiyya from Umar ibn Ju'thum al-Qurashi from Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa from Abd al-Hamid from Muslim ibn Yasar from Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a from Umar from the Prophet, in similar words. The editor adds that after writing this he found Ibn al-Qayyim giving the report in Shifa al-Alil, quoting Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Tamhid: al-Hakim said it is on Muslim's condition, which is not so, for it is broken, since Muslim ibn Yasar did not meet Umar and Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a stands between them, and both are unknown and not known for carrying knowledge, this not being the worshipper of Basra of that name but an unknown man of Medina; Yahya ibn Ma'in wrote over Muslim ibn Yasar "not known". Ibn Abd al-Barr says that although the chain is flawed, its meaning from the Prophet is reported by many routes, and lists the Companions who narrated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 235}
The editor notes that Ibn Kathir gave the report in his commentary and history and, after quoting al-Tirmidhi, said that Abu Hatim and Abu Zur'a said the same, Abu Hatim adding Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a between them; that Abu Dawud narrated it from Muhammad ibn al-Musaffa from Baqiyya; that al-Daraqutni said Umar ibn Ju'thum was followed by Yazid ibn Sinan Abu Farwa al-Ruhawi and that their word is nearer the truth than Malik's; and that Ibn Kathir thinks Malik dropped Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a deliberately because he did not know his state, Nu'aym being known only through this hadith. He identifies Muhammad ibn al-Musaffa ibn Bahlul al-Qurashi, a truthful memoriser spoken of and said to have practised a form of concealment in chains; Baqiyya ibn al-Walid; Umar ibn Ju'thum al-Qurashi, listed by Ibn Hibban among the trustworthy, the manuscript having Ja'far and the printed edition Ju'm, both wrong; and Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a al-Azdi, against whom neither al-Bukhari nor Ibn Abi Hatim records any criticism. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 235}
We stop here at the foot of p. 235, after the end of report 15358 and the editor's notes on it; the next batch begins at p. 236. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 235}
