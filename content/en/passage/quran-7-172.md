## Summary
Verse 172 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 172 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 172 (pp. 222-250)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses His prophet Muhammad: remember, Muhammad, when your Lord brought the offspring of Adam out of the loins of their fathers, had them affirm His oneness, and made them bear witness over one another to that testimony and acknowledgement. The editor notes that al-Tabari already explained this verse earlier, at report 10855, and did not repeat it here, abridging. Report 15338, from Ibn Abbas from the Prophet: God took the covenant from the back of Adam at Nu'man, meaning Arafa, and brought out of his loins every offspring He had created, scattered them before Him like ants, then spoke to them face to face and said "Am I not your Lord?" and they said yes. The editor says the printed edition had a different word, the publisher thinking it was from recitation, and that the right reading is "qibalan", face to face and confronting, not from behind a veil, and he refers to earlier explanations of this word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 222}

The editor then writes at length on the tracing of this report: Ibn Abbas's report by way of Kulthum ibn Jabr from Sa'id ibn Jubayr is given by al-Tabari with five chains, this one and reports 15339 to 15341 and then 15350; the first alone is traced up to the Prophet and the rest stop at Ibn Abbas, and al-Tabari also gives it raised to the Prophet in his History. Ahmad gives it raised in the Musnad by the route of Husayn ibn Muhammad, which is al-Tabari's route; al-Hakim gives it raised in the Mustadrak by the route of Ibrahim ibn Marzuq al-Basri from Wahb ibn Jarir from his father and says the chain is sound though the two compilers did not include it, adding that Muslim relied on Kulthum ibn Jabr, and al-Dhahabi agreed; al-Haythami mentions it in Majma' al-Zawa'id and says its men are the men of the sound collection. The stopped form is given by Ibn Sa'd in the Tabaqat by the route of Ibn 'Ulayya and Hammad ibn Zayd from Kulthum. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir discussed it in his commentary and history and made the number of those who stopped it a flaw against the one who raised it, and that his brother Ahmad Shakir said in his commentary on the Musnad that raising is an addition from a trustworthy narrator and so accepted, and that the chain is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 223}

The editor identifies the transmitters: Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Tusi, al-Tabari's teacher, trustworthy; Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Bahram al-Tamimi, called "the teacher" and "the instructor"; Kulthum ibn Jabr, trustworthy among the younger Followers; and "Nu'man" is a valley of Hudhayl behind Arafa, two nights from it, that is Nu'man al-Arak, often named in their poetry. Report 15339, from Kulthum: I asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr about the verse and he asked Ibn Abbas, who said: your Lord wiped the back of Adam and brought out every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection at this Nu'man, pointing with his hand, and took their covenants and made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes. The editor identifies Imran ibn Musa al-Laythi, the sack-maker, al-Tabari's teacher, and Abd al-Warith ibn Sa'id ibn Dhakwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 223}

Report 15340, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Ibn 'Ulayya from Kulthum: your Lord wiped the back of Adam and every soul He creates came out until the Day of Resurrection at this Nu'man behind Arafa, and He took their covenant; the wording is that of Ya'qub's report. Report 15341, from Kulthum by way of his son Rabi'a, in the same sense, with the words "they said yes, we witness, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection that we were unaware of this". The editor identifies Ibn 'Ulayya as Ismail ibn Ibrahim al-Asadi and says this is the second of the reports stopped at Ibn Abbas, given by al-Tabari in the History with this chain and by Ibn Sa'd in the Tabaqat with this wording, its chain sound. In a note he explains that the singular form of the word for "their offspring" is one of the two readings and is the one used in his region, the other being the plural; al-Tabari does not mention this reading, the manuscript has the singular often and the plural in some reports, and the publisher had put the plural in all, so the editor follows the manuscript and keeps each as it stands there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 223} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 224}

Report 15342, from Ibn Abbas: when God first sent Adam down He sent him down at Dahna, a land in India, and God wiped his back and brought out of it every soul He creates until the Hour comes, then took the covenant from them and made them witness against themselves. The editor stops long at the name of the place: the printed edition has it with a jim, the manuscript with a ha, Ibn Sa'd gives it with a ha and a hamza, and al-Tabari's History has a form ending in hamza, which he thinks wrong. He then distinguishes two places. The first is the Dahna of the Arabian peninsula, named in the Sira from Ibn Ishaq when the Prophet returned from Ta'if by way of Dahna and halted at al-Ji'rana, and given by al-Bakri in his gazetteer and by Yaqut, who calls it one of the districts of Ta'if; the editor cites two poetic witnesses, by Rabi'a ibn Jahdar of Hudhayl and one from al-Asma'i, and thinks the commentator on the poetry made it a place on the sea coast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 224} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 225}

The second is the Dahna named in this report, described in it as a land in India. Al-Bakri mentions it under "Washim" from Ibn Ishaq: the people of the Book say that the place where Adam and Eve came down is on a mountain called Washim in the land of India, in the middle of its villages between Dahnaj and Mandal, and al-Tabari's History has something like it, from the people of the Torah, that Adam was sent down in India on a mountain called Wasim beside a valley called Buhayl, between al-Hanj and Mandal, two towns in India. The editor concludes that the reports which mention the creation or descent of Adam at Dahna without saying where are clarified by these reports, which place it in India, that Dahna with a ha is the Arabised form of Dahnaj, and that the Arabian one lies between Mecca and Ta'if. He says the author of Lisan al-Arab confused the two, that the Qamus gives it with a jim or a ha and refers one to the other, and that al-Zabidi in Taj al-Arus also confused the place of the Sira with the place of Adam's creation or descent, following al-Suhayli in al-Rawd al-Unuf; the cause of the confusion is the mention of Nu'man al-Arak, in the Arabs' land, in the report of Adam's creation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 225} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 226}

Report 15343, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Imran ibn Uyayna from Ata from Sa'id: Adam was sent down and God wiped his back and brought out of it every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection, then said "Am I not your Lord?" and they said yes, then recited the verse, and the pen dried from that day over what will be until the Day of Resurrection. The editor identifies Amr as Amr ibn Ali al-Fallas and Imran ibn Uyayna as the brother of Sufyan ibn Uyayna, giving the verdict of Ibn Ma'in and Abu Zur'a that he is "fit for hadith", Ibn Abi Hatim's that his hadith is not to be argued from because he brings objectionable reports, and al-'Uqayli's that there is error in his hadith; al-Tabari gives the report abridged in the History, as does Ibn Sa'd, and it will come by other chains, not through Imran, from Ata, without the mention of Dahna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 226} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 227}

Report 15344, from Ibn Abbas: when God created Adam He took his offspring from his back like ants and took two handfuls, saying to the people of the right hand "Enter Paradise in peace" and to the others "Enter the Fire and I do not mind". The editor identifies Yahya ibn Isa al-Nahshali, whom Ahmad and others held reliable and Ibn Ma'in and others held weak, and Habib ibn Abi Thabit, that is Habib ibn Qays ibn Dinar, trustworthy, and says the sense of the report is traced in the Musnads of a number of Companions, though he did not find this exact wording from Ibn Abbas except here and in al-Tabari's History. Report 15345, from Ibn Abbas by another chain: God wiped the back of Adam and brought out every good one in His right hand and every bad one in the other; the editor notes that al-Ajurri gives it in Kitab al-Shari'a by the route of Ali ibn Mus-hir from al-A'mash in other words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 227} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228}

Report 15346, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Shurayk from Ata from Sa'id: God wiped the back of Adam and drew out of it every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection; the editor calls the chain sound and refers back. Report 15347, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Amr ibn Abi Qays from Ata: when God created Adam He wiped his back at Dahna and brought out of his back every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection and said "Am I not your Lord?", they said yes, and "so they are thought that day" that the pen dried over what will be until the Day of Resurrection. The editor says Amr ibn Abi Qays al-Razi is trustworthy though he erred a little, that al-Tabari gives it in the History, that Ibn Sa'd gives it by the route of Mansur ibn Abi al-Aswad from Ata in much the same words, ending with Humayd's statement that they supposed the covenant was that day, that al-Durr al-Manthur attributes it to Ibn al-Mundhir alone, and that the passive "they are thought" means they suppose and reckon it so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228}

Report 15348, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of al-Mas'udi from Ali ibn Badhima from Sa'id: when God created Adam He took his covenant, wiped his back, took his offspring in the form of ants, and wrote their appointed terms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228}

Report 15348 from Ibn Abbas is completed: He wrote their terms, provisions and afflictions, and made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes. The editor says al-Tabari gave it by two chains, this and the next, and identifies al-Mas'udi as Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Utba ibn Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, trustworthy but confused late and with a changed memory, what the old teachers narrated from him being sound and Waki's hearing from him early; and Ali ibn Badhima al-Jazari, trustworthy, spoken of in ways that do no harm. He notes that al-Suyuti gives it in al-Durr al-Manthur, attributing it to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu al-Shaykh. Report 15349, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Yazid ibn Harun from al-Mas'udi: when God created Adam He took his covenant that He was his Lord and wrote his term and afflictions, drew out his offspring like ants, took their covenant, and wrote their terms, provisions and afflictions. The editor identifies Yazid ibn Harun al-Sulami as a famous memoriser and says he narrated mixed hadiths from al-Mas'udi after his memory changed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 228} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 229}
Report 15350, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Rabi'a ibn Kulthum from his father: God wiped the back of Adam while he was in the belly of Nu'man, a valley beside Arafa, and brought his offspring out of his back like ants, then made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes, we witness; the editor says this is another route for reports 15338 to 15341, whose tracing is given there. Report 15351, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Abu Hilal from Abu Jamra al-Duba'i: God brought the offspring of Adam out of his back like ants while it was in the "adhi" of water. The editor identifies Abu Hilal as Muhammad ibn Sulaym al-Rasibi, trustworthy and spoken of, quotes Ibn Adi that his hadiths are all or mostly unpreserved yet are to be written down, and Abu Jamra as Nasr ibn Imran ibn Isam al-Duba'i, trustworthy and reliable, noting that both printed edition and manuscript had a wrong name. He explains "adhi of water" as the layers the wind raises from its surface without waves, though it also means heavy waves, which is commoner, and the first sense is meant here; and that Ibn Kathir transmitted the report and al-Suyuti attributed it to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Manda in his refutation of the Jahmiyya, and Abu al-Shaykh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 229} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 230}
Report 15352, from Juwaybir: a son of al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, six days old, died, and al-Dahhak said: Jabir, when you lay my son in his grave, uncover his face and undo his knots, for my son is made to sit and is questioned. I did what he told me, and when I had finished I said: God have mercy on you, about what is your son asked, and who asks him? He said: he is asked about the covenant he acknowledged in the loins of Adam. I said: Abu al-Qasim, what is this covenant? He said: Ibn Abbas told me that God wiped the loins of Adam and drew out every soul He creates until the Day of Resurrection, and took from them the covenant to worship Him and associate nothing with Him, and guaranteed their provisions; the Hour will not come until everyone who gave the covenant that day is born; whoever reaches the later covenant and keeps it, the first benefits him; whoever reaches it and does not keep it, the first does not benefit him; and whoever dies young before reaching the later covenant dies on the first covenant, on the natural disposition. The editor notes that the words "and who asks him" are missing from the printed edition and al-Durr al-Manthur but are in the manuscript and in Ibn Kathir, and that both manuscript and printed edition have a gap in the middle of the report which he restores from Ibn Kathir and al-Durr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 231}
The editor identifies the transmitters: Ali ibn Sahl al-Ramli, al-Tabari's teacher; Damra ibn Rabi'a al-Filastini, trustworthy; Abu Mas'ud, the narrator from Juwaybir, whom he fears to be Sa'id ibn Iyas al-Jurayri without being able to confirm it; and Juwaybir, a nickname, the name being said to be Jabir ibn Sa'id, which this report gives as Jabir. He notes that Ibn Kathir quoted it in his commentary and al-Suyuti in al-Durr. Then report 15353, from al-Aswad ibn Sari' of Banu Sa'd: I took part in four expeditions with the Messenger of God; the men took hold of the children after they had killed the fighters, and it reached the Messenger of God, who was much displeased and said: what is the matter with people who take hold of the children? A man said: are they not the children of the polytheists? He said: the best of you are children of polytheists; no soul is born but is born on the natural disposition and stays on it until its tongue speaks for it, and its parents make it Jewish or Christian. Al-Hasan said: God said this in His book, and he recited the verse. The editor remarks that the printed edition added an oath before "God said" and he does not know from where. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 231}
The editor identifies al-Sari ibn Yahya al-Shaybani, Abu al-Haytham, trustworthy and sure, who narrated from al-Hasan al-Basri, and al-Aswad ibn Sari' al-Tamimi, a Companion and famous poet, the first to give sermons in the mosque of Basra, quoting a line of his on the dead and listing the books that give his biography. He traces the report: Ahmad gave it in the Musnad by this route with a slight difference in wording and by others, among them Yunus ibn Muhammad al-Mu'addib from Aban ibn Yazid from Qatada from al-Hasan and then Yunus ibn Ubayd from al-Hasan, saying it happened in a raiding party the Messenger of God sent on the day of Hunayn; Ibn Sa'd gave it abridged, al-Bukhari in the History, Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Istiab, and Ibn Hajar mentions it in al-Isaba; al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak and said it is sound on the condition of the two shaykhs, al-Dhahabi agreeing; al-Bayhaqi gave it in the Great Sunan; al-Haythami mentions it in Majma' al-Zawa'id and says the men of some of Ahmad's chains are the men of the sound collection; and Ibn Kathir says al-Nasa'i gave it from Hushaym from Yunus ibn Ubayd from al-Hasan from al-Aswad without al-Hasan's words and his bringing the verse to mind. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 231} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 232}
Report 15354, from Abd Allah ibn Amr: the Messenger of God said of the verse: they were taken from his back as one takes with a comb from the head, and He said to them "Am I not your Lord?", they said yes, and the angels said "we witness, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection", the verse. The editor says al-Tabari gave it by three routes, the first raised to the Prophet and the other two stopped at Abd Allah ibn Amr, and that it has two chains: Sufyan al-Thawri from al-Ajlah, and Sufyan from Mansur. He found no biography of Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Walid al-Jurjani, al-Tabari's teacher; Ahmad ibn Abi Tayba is Ahmad ibn Isa ibn Sulayman al-Jurjani, judge of Qumis, of whom Abu Hatim said his hadith is written down, Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy, and Ibn Adi said he narrated hadiths most of them unusual; and al-Ajlah is Ibn Abd Allah ibn Hujiyya al-Kindi, spoken against and held reliable by Ibn Adi. Al-Suyuti gave the report as raised in al-Durr, while Ibn Kathir weakened its raising and showed that stopping it is sounder. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 232} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 233}
The editor notes that al-Tabari's own judgement of it comes at p. 250: he does not know it to be sound, because the trustworthy narrators relied on for memory and precision narrated this hadith from al-Thawri stopping it at Abd Allah ibn Amr, without raising it and without the point Ahmad ibn Abi Tayba mentioned. Reports 15355 and 15356, from Abd Allah ibn Amr by the chain of Mansur from Mujahid: He took them as the comb takes from the head; in the second Ibn Humayd's wording is "as one takes with a comb". The editor calls them the two sound stopped reports with a sound chain, says Ibn Kathir mentioned all three, and that al-Suyuti gave it as stopped, attributing it to Ibn Abi Shayba, Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Abu al-Shaykh and al-Lalaka'i in al-Sunna, and falling short in not naming Ibn Jarir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 233}
Report 15357, from Muslim ibn Yasar al-Juhani: Umar ibn al-Khattab was asked about the verse and said: I heard the Messenger of God say: God created Adam, then wiped over his back with His right hand and drew out offspring and said "I created these for Paradise and they act with the deeds of the people of Paradise"; then He wiped over his back and drew out offspring and said "I created these for the Fire and they act with the deeds of the people of the Fire". A man said: Messenger of God, then what is the action for? He said: when God creates a servant for Paradise He uses him in the deeds of the people of Paradise until he dies on one of their deeds and enters him into Paradise, and when He creates a servant for the Fire He uses him in the deeds of the people of the Fire until he dies on one of their deeds and enters him into the Fire. The editor notes that the printed edition had "wiped his back" and he follows the manuscript and the History. He identifies Ibrahim ibn Sa'id al-Jawhari, al-Tabari's teacher; Sa'd ibn Abd al-Hamid ibn Ja'far al-Ansari, trustworthy though weakened, which does no harm here since Rawh ibn Ubada is trustworthy and the mainstay of the report; Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa al-Jazari, trustworthy; Abd al-Hamid ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn al-Khattab, trustworthy and reliable; and Muslim ibn Yasar al-Juhani, a trustworthy Follower, of whom it is said he did not hear from Umar, Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a al-Azdi standing between them as in the next report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 233} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 234}
The editor traces the report: al-Tabari gave it with this chain in his History with a slight difference; Malik in the Muwatta, Ahmad in the Musnad and Abu Dawud in his Sunan (no. 4703) gave it; al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak, calling it sound on the condition of both compilers, and al-Dhahabi remarked that it has a break, but in another place al-Hakim called it sound on Muslim's condition and al-Dhahabi agreed there without the remark, which the editor finds strange; al-Ajurri gave it in al-Shari'a; Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Taqassi says its chain has two flaws, set out in al-Tamhid; and al-Tirmidhi gave it in the commentary section and called it good, adding that Muslim ibn Yasar did not hear from Umar and that some put a man between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 234}
Report 15358 has the chain of Muhammad ibn al-Musaffa from Baqiyya from Umar ibn Ju'thum al-Qurashi from Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa from Abd al-Hamid from Muslim ibn Yasar from Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a from Umar from the Prophet, in similar words. The editor adds that after writing this he found Ibn al-Qayyim giving the report in Shifa al-Alil, quoting Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Tamhid: al-Hakim said it is on Muslim's condition, which is not so, for it is broken, since Muslim ibn Yasar did not meet Umar and Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a stands between them, and both are unknown and not known for carrying knowledge, this not being the worshipper of Basra of that name but an unknown man of Medina; Yahya ibn Ma'in wrote over Muslim ibn Yasar "not known". Ibn Abd al-Barr says that although the chain is flawed, its meaning from the Prophet is reported by many routes, and lists the Companions who narrated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 235}
The editor notes that Ibn Kathir gave the report in his commentary and history and, after quoting al-Tirmidhi, said that Abu Hatim and Abu Zur'a said the same, Abu Hatim adding Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a between them; that Abu Dawud narrated it from Muhammad ibn al-Musaffa from Baqiyya; that al-Daraqutni said Umar ibn Ju'thum was followed by Yazid ibn Sinan Abu Farwa al-Ruhawi and that their word is nearer the truth than Malik's; and that Ibn Kathir thinks Malik dropped Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a deliberately because he did not know his state, Nu'aym being known only through this hadith. He identifies Muhammad ibn al-Musaffa ibn Bahlul al-Qurashi, a truthful memoriser spoken of and said to have practised a form of concealment in chains; Baqiyya ibn al-Walid; Umar ibn Ju'thum al-Qurashi, listed by Ibn Hibban among the trustworthy, the manuscript having Ja'far and the printed edition Ju'm, both wrong; and Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a al-Azdi, against whom neither al-Bukhari nor Ibn Abi Hatim records any criticism. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 235}
Report 15359, from Abu Muhammad, a man of Medina: I asked Umar ibn al-Khattab about the verse and he said: I asked the Prophet about it as you have asked me, and he said: God created Adam with His hand and breathed into him of His spirit, then seated him and wiped his back with His right hand and brought out offspring, saying "offspring I created for Paradise"; then He wiped his back with His other hand, both His hands being right, and said "and offspring I created for the Fire; they act in whatever work I will, then I seal for them the worst of their deeds and enter them into the Fire". The editor says the printed edition had "a man from Medina" and he keeps the manuscript reading; Umara is Umara ibn Umayr al-Taymi, narrated from by the compilers of the six books; he found no information on Abu Muhammad in any book. Ibn Abd al-Barr gives it in al-Tamhid with this chain and wording, except that it has "the evil of their deeds", and al-Suyuti gives it attributing it only to Ibn Jarir. At the head of the page the editor adds that al-Bukhari gives the report from Nu'aym ibn Rabi'a, abridged, in the Great History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 236}
Report 15360, from Ibn Abbas by the route of Ali ibn Abi Talha: God created Adam and brought out his offspring from his loins like ants and said to them "Who is your Lord?" and they said "God is our Lord"; then He returned them to his loins until everyone from whom a covenant was taken is born, with nothing added to them or taken from them until the Hour comes. The editor says this is a recurring chain in the commentary whose account is given earlier. Report 15361, from Ibn Abbas by the family chain of al-Awfi: when God created Adam He wiped his back and brought out all his offspring like ants, made them speak and they spoke, made them witness against themselves, and set light with some of them; and said to Adam: these are your offspring, from them I take the covenant that I am their Lord so that they associate nothing with Me, and their provision is on Me. Adam said: who is this one with the light? He said: David. He said: Lord, how long a term have You written for him? He said: sixty years. He said: and for me? He said: a thousand years, and I have written for every one of them how long he will live and stay. He said: Lord, add to him. He said: this book is laid down; give him of your age if you wish. He said: yes. The pen had dried over the terms of the rest of Adam's children, so forty years of Adam's term were written for David and his term became a hundred. When Adam had lived nine hundred and sixty years the angel of death came to him and Adam said: I have lived only nine hundred and sixty years and forty remain! The angel said: my Lord has told me of them, so go back to your Lord and ask. The angel returned to his Lord and said: Lord, I came back because I knew how You honour him. God said: go back and tell him that he gave his son David forty years. The editor notes that al-Suyuti attributed it to Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Lalaka'i in al-Sunna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 236} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 237}
Report 15362, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Ibn Jurayj from al-Zubayr ibn Musa from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: God struck Adam's right shoulder and every soul created for Paradise came out white and pure and He said "these are the people of Paradise"; then He struck the left shoulder and every soul created for the Fire came out black and He said "these are the people of the Fire"; then He took their covenants on belief in Him and knowledge of Him and of His command and on trust in Him and His command, and made them witness against themselves, and they believed, trusted, knew and acknowledged. It reached Ibn Jurayj that He brought them out on His palm like grains of mustard. From Mujahid: when He brought them out He said "servants of God, answer God", the answer being obedience, and they said "we have obeyed, God, obey us, here we are, God"; He gave this to Ibrahim in the rites as "here I am, God, here I am". From Ibn Abbas: He created Adam and brought out his offspring from his back like ants and spoke to them, then returned them to his loins, so there is no one who has not spoken and said "my Lord is God"; every creature created will come to be until the Day of Resurrection, and this is the natural disposition on which He made people. Sa'id ibn Jubayr said: the covenant was taken at Nu'man, which is behind Arafa, so that they should not say on the Day of Resurrection that they were unaware of this, that is of the covenant taken from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 237} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 238}
The editor identifies al-Zubayr ibn Musa al-Makki as trustworthy, and says al-Ajurri gave the report abridged in al-Shari'a by the route of Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn Thiql from Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak from Ibn Jurayj, that al-Suyuti gave it attributing it only to Ibn Jarir and Abu al-Shaykh, and that the printed edition repeated "God, obey us", which he drops in line with the manuscript. Then report 15363, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b: on that day He gathered them all, everything that will be until the Day of Resurrection, then made them speak, took the covenant from them and made them witness against themselves, with the words of [[passage/quran-7-172|7:172]] and [[passage/quran-7-173|7:173]]. He said: I make the seven heavens and the seven earths and your father Adam witness lest you say on the Day of Resurrection that you did not know this; know that there is no god but Me and no lord but Me, and do not associate anything with Me, and I will send you messengers to remind you of My covenant and compact and will send down My books to you. They said: we bear witness that You are our Lord and our God, we have no lord but You. They acknowledged obedience to Him that day, and their father Adam was raised over them and looked at them and saw the rich and the poor and the handsome and those below that, and said: Lord, if only You had made them equal! He said: I love to be thanked. The prophets were among them that day like lamps, and He singled out the prophets for another covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 238} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 239}
In this report Ubayy links verses to the sense: the other covenant singled out for the prophets, [[passage/quran-33-7|33:7]]; the natural disposition, [[passage/quran-30-30|30:30]]; "the first warners", [[passage/quran-53-56|53:56]]; and the verses on the covenants of most of them, [[passage/quran-7-102|7:102]] and [[passage/quran-10-74|10:74]]; and in His knowledge on the day they acknowledged it were those who would believe and those who would deny. The editor notes that the printed edition had "I will send" while the manuscript has "and I will send", the correct form being from the hadith sources; that the word "My books" is missing from the manuscript through a copyist's slip; that the bracketed addition is from other sources; and that the chain is sound. He traces the report: Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal gave it abridged in his additions to the Musnad from his teacher Muhammad ibn Ya'qub al-Rabali from al-Mu'tamir from his father from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, and al-Haythami quotes it in Majma' al-Zawa'id saying Abd Allah's teacher is of unknown condition and the rest of its men are the men of the sound collection; al-Hakim gave it at length in the Mustadrak by the route of Ubayd Allah ibn Musa from Abu Ja'far, Isa ibn Abd Allah ibn Mahan, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, calling the chain sound though not included by the two compilers, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 238} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 239} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 240}
The editor continues the tracing: al-Ajurri gave it in al-Shari'a by the route of Hakkam ibn Salm al-Razi from Abu Ja'far al-Razi from al-Rabi'; Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Tamhid by the route of Ahmad ibn Abd Allah ibn Salih from Ubayd Allah ibn Musa from Abu Ja'far, which is al-Hakim's route; Ibn Kathir mentions it in his commentary adding Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Mardawayh; and al-Suyuti adds Abd ibn Humayd, Abu al-Shaykh, Ibn Manda in his refutation of the Jahmiyya, al-Lalaka'i, Ibn Mardawayh, al-Bayhaqi in al-Asma wa al-Sifat and Ibn Asakir in his history. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 239} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 240}
Report 15364, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: He brought them out of Adam's back and gave Adam a life of a thousand years; they were shown to Adam, who saw a man of his offspring with a light that pleased him and asked about him, and was told he was David and that his life was set at sixty years, so Adam gave him forty years of his own. When Adam was dying he began to dispute with them over the forty years and was told that he had given them to David, and he kept disputing. Report 15365, from Sa'id: He brought his offspring out of his back like ants and showed them to Adam with their names, their fathers' names and their terms; David's spirit was shown to him in a bright light and he said: who is this? He said: this is of your offspring, a prophet and a successor. He said: how long is his life? He said: sixty years. He said: add forty years to him from mine. The pens were still wet and running, so the forty were recorded for David; Adam's life was a thousand years, and when he had completed it but for forty the angel of death was sent to him and said: Adam, I am commanded to take you. He said: do not forty years of my life remain? The angel of death returned to his Lord and said: Adam claims forty years of his life; He said: tell Adam he gave them to his son David while the pens were wet and it was recorded for David. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 240} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 241}
Report 15366 is similar from Sa'id by the chain of Ibn Waki', and reports 15367 and 15368, from Ata and then from another narrator: He brought them out of Adam's back until He had taken the covenant from them, then returned them to his loins. Report 15369, from al-Dahhak: when God dispersed His creation for Adam He created them and made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes; the editor says the phrase is strange but stands so in the manuscript and printed edition, that Muhammad ibn Ubayd is more likely al-Tanafisi, and that Abu Bistam is Muqatil ibn Hayyan al-Balkhi. Report 15370, from al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas: God created Adam, then brought out his offspring from his back and spoke to them and made them speak, saying "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes; then He returned them to his loins, so there is no one of creation who has not spoken and said "my Lord is God", and the Resurrection will not come until whoever witnessed against himself that day is born. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 241}
Report 15371, from al-Suddi: that is when He says that to Him submitted whoever is in the heavens and earth, willingly or unwillingly, [[passage/quran-3-83|3:83]], and when He says that to God belongs the conclusive argument, [[passage/quran-6-149|6:149]], meaning the day He took the covenant from them and then showed them to Adam. Report 15372, from al-Suddi: God brought Adam out of Paradise, before he had come down from heaven, then wiped the right side of his back and brought out his offspring like ants, white like pearls, and said to them "enter Paradise by My mercy"; and wiped the left side of his back and brought out black ones like ants and said "enter the Fire and I do not mind"; that is when He says the people of the right and the people of the left; then He took the covenant from them, "Am I not your Lord?", they said yes, and one group obeyed willingly and another unwillingly in dissimulation. The editor identifies Amr ibn Talha as Amr ibn Hammad ibn Talha al-Qannad, one of the men most often met in the commentary, the printed edition having "Umar" in error; says the report is part of a long report given by Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Tamhid from al-Suddi from his companions, Abu Malik and Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas, Murra al-Hamdani from Ibn Mas'ud, and some Companions of the Prophet; that al-Suyuti gave it at length attributing it only to Ibn Abd al-Barr; and that the printed edition reorders the description of the white and black ants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 242}
Report 15373, from al-Suddi, in the same sense, adding after "another group in dissimulation" that He and the angels said "we witness, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection that we were unaware of this, or say that our fathers associated before us"; so there is no one of Adam's children on earth who does not know that his Lord is God, and no polytheist who does not say to his son that they found their fathers on a creed, the creed being the religion, and are following in their footsteps, [[passage/quran-43-23|43:23]]; and that is when God says what is in [[passage/quran-7-172|7:172]], and when He says [[passage/quran-3-83|3:83]] and [[passage/quran-6-149|6:149]], meaning the day the covenant was taken. The editor thinks this report is part of the two preceding, 15371 and 15372, that Ibn Abd al-Barr gives it at length in al-Tamhid and al-Tabari abridged in his History, but that the opening of the report is not found in any source. Report 15374, from al-Kalbi: God wiped the back of Adam and brought out of it his offspring, all that will be until the Day of Resurrection, and took their covenant that He is their Lord and they gave it, so that you ask no one, unbeliever or other, "Who is your Lord?" but he says "God"; and al-Hasan said the like. The editor notes that al-Suyuti gave it from Ibn Abbas, attributing it to Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn al-Mundhir, and that it seems to come from al-Kalbi's commentary with his chain from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 243}
Report 15375, from Ali ibn al-Husayn: he practised withdrawal in intercourse and interpreted this verse. The editor says the printed edition had "used to say and interpret", which makes no sense, that withdrawal is when a man keeps his seed away from a woman's womb at intercourse so that she does not conceive, and that al-Suyuti added Ibn Abi Shayba. Report 15376, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: the spirits made their acknowledgement before their bodies were created; Ibn Abd al-Barr gives it in al-Tamhid by the route of Qasim ibn Asbagh from Muhammad ibn al-Jahm from Rawh ibn Ubada from Musa ibn Ubayda, and al-Suyuti added Ibn Abi Shayba. Report 15377, from Hisham ibn Hakim: a man came to the Messenger of God and said: do deeds begin, or has the decree been settled? He said: God took Adam's offspring from their backs, made them witness against themselves, then scattered them in His two palms and said "these are in Paradise and these in the Fire"; the people of Paradise are made ready for the deeds of the people of Paradise, and the people of the Fire for the deeds of the people of the Fire. The editor explains "scattered them in His palms" as making them dispersed and spread out, from the casting of arrows when the caster shakes them and they fall scattered, and notes that al-Tabarani's version reads "then He strewed them in His palms"; al-Tabari gave it with four chains through report 15380, and it was long ago noted that its chain is disordered, the ways of which he promises to set out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 244}
The editor identifies the transmitters at length: Ahmad ibn al-Faraj ibn Sulayman al-Kindi al-Himsi, Abu Uqba, known as al-Hijazi, came to Baghdad several times and narrated there from Baqiyya, and Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal, Ibn Jarir, al-Muhamili and others narrated from him; Ibn Abi Hatim said "his standing with us is truthfulness"; Ibn Adi said Muhammad ibn Awf al-Ta'i weakened him yet his hadith is written down, and Muhammad ibn Awf was harsh on him, saying he had no basis for Baqiyya's hadith from al-Zubaydi and that they were hadiths that came to him on the back of a sheet of paper; Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy, saying he errs and is known by his kunya; but al-Tabari later narrated this report from Muhammad ibn Awf and others, so what was said does no harm. Baqiyya ibn al-Walid al-Himsi is trustworthy, spoken against for concealment in chains, his report sound when he states that he heard, as he does here but not in the next. Al-Zubaydi is Muhammad ibn al-Walid ibn Amir, trustworthy, narrated from by the two compilers; Rashid ibn Sa'd al-Maqra'i al-Hubrani al-Himsi was held trustworthy by Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Ma'in, Ahmad said there is no harm in him, al-Daraqutni that he is considered if he does not narrate from the abandoned, and Ibn Hazm alone weakened him; he lost his eye at Siffin and died in 108. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 244} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 245}
The editor says the disorder in the chain of the report begins at Rashid ibn Sa'd's narration, in the naming of some of his men and in the wording, and collects the chains: first, al-Zubaydi from Rashid from Abd al-Rahman ibn Qatada al-Nasri from his father from Hisham ibn Hakim, found in al-Tabari 15377 and 15379, in al-Bukhari's Great History, with Ishaq ibn Rahwayh and in Ibn Kathir; second, al-Zubaydi from Rashid from Abd al-Rahman from Hisham, omitting "from his father", in al-Ajurri; third, Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Rashid from Abd al-Rahman from Hisham, in al-Tabari 15380; fourth, Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Rashid from Abd al-Rahman ibn Qatada al-Sulami, a Companion of the Messenger, "I heard the Messenger of God", in Ibn Sa'd, Ahmad, al-Hakim, Usd al-Ghaba and al-Isaba; fifth, al-Zubaydi from Abd al-Rahman from his father and Hisham ibn Hakim in al-Isaba. So the difference is that al-Zubaydi differs on Rashid, giving "from his father" once and dropping it once, and Mu'awiya differs on him, making it from Hisham once and a direct hearing from the Prophet once. He quotes Ibn Hajar that al-Bukhari held the hadith defective because Abd al-Rahman only narrated it from Hisham and Mu'awiya's "I heard" is an error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 245} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 246}
The third difference is in wording. The first wording is that which al-Tabari gives at 15377, and nearly the same is given by al-Bukhari in the Great History, al-Ajurri, Ishaq ibn Rahwayh, Majma' al-Zawa'id and al-Durr al-Manthur, which adds al-Bazzar, al-Tabarani, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bayhaqi in al-Asma wa al-Sifat, all from Hisham ibn Hakim; al-Haythami says that in the chain of al-Bazzar and al-Tabarani is Baqiyya, who is weak, though his hadith is strengthened by the many witnesses, and al-Tabarani's chain is good. The second wording is from Abd al-Rahman ibn Qatada al-Sulami: I heard the Messenger of God say that God created Adam, then took creation from his back and said "these are in Paradise and I do not mind, and these in the Fire and I do not mind"; someone said: Messenger of God, on what then do we work? He said: on the places where the decree falls. It is given by Ahmad in the Musnad, Ibn Sa'd, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Hakim, al-Haythami and Ibn Hajar; al-Hakim said it is sound, the two compilers having agreed to rely on its narrators up to the Companion, Abd al-Rahman being of Banu Salima among the Companions, and al-Dhahabi agreed; al-Haythami said Ahmad gave it and its men are trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 246} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 247}
The editor then turns to Abd al-Rahman ibn Qatada. In the first and second chains he is "al-Nasri", narrating from his father from Hisham, and it does not appear from the chain that he is a Companion; if he were, he would be a Companion narrating from a Companion from a Companion, which is strange and rare; if al-Bukhari is right that "from his father" is an addition, it is a Companion narrating from a Companion, or he may be a Follower. But no one has shown that al-Nasri is other than the Companion al-Sulami; Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Abi Hatim give a biography of him and say he narrated from Hisham ibn Hakim and Rashid ibn Sa'd narrated from him, and none says that this al-Sulami is called al-Nasri, which is also strange; they record his father Qatada al-Nasri in the Great History, saying he heard Hisham ibn Hakim and his son narrated from him, while Qatada al-Sulami is not mentioned. As to the attribution, "al-Sulami" is to Sulaym ibn Mansur ibn Ikrima, while al-Hakim says he was of Banu Salima of the Ansar; the editor thinks "al-Nasri" is to Nasr ibn Mu'awiya ibn Bakr ibn Hawazin, cousins of Sulaym, so that a man of Sulaym may have joined them and been attributed to them too, though he says he has no proof for this. He ends that the hadith is sound, narrated from a number of Companions by chains without this disorder, and that the disorder is old and was already noted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 246} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 247} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 248}
Report 15378, from Hisham ibn Hakim from the Prophet, the same, by the chain of Muhammad ibn Awf al-Ta'i from Haywa and Yazid from Baqiyya from al-Zubaydi. The editor identifies Muhammad ibn Awf ibn Sufyan al-Ta'i, al-Tabari's teacher, a trustworthy memoriser who narrated from Ahmad; Haywa ibn Shurayh ibn Yazid al-Hadrami, a learned and trustworthy jurist; and Yazid ibn Harun. Report 15379, from Hisham ibn Hakim: a man came to the Messenger of God, and he gave the like. The manuscript and printed edition read "Ahmad ibn Shabuya told me", and the editor adds "Abd Allah ibn" in brackets because al-Tabari's teacher is Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Thabit al-Marwazi, one of the imams of hadith, and because his father Ahmad ibn Shabuya died in 230 and al-Tabari did not reach him. He identifies Ishaq ibn Ibrahim ibn al-Ala al-Himsi al-Zubaydi, also called Ishaq ibn Zibriq, trustworthy and much spoken of; Amr ibn al-Harith ibn al-Dahhak al-Zubaydi al-Himsi, whom Ibn Hibban listed among the trustworthy and of whom al-Dhahabi said his probity is not known; and Abd Allah ibn Salim al-Ash'ari al-Wuhazi, held trustworthy by Ibn Hibban and al-Daraqutni, the manuscript and printed edition having "Muslim" in error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 248} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 249}
Report 15380, from Hisham ibn Hakim from the Prophet in similar words, by the chain of Muhammad ibn Awf from Abu Salih from Mu'awiya from Rashid ibn Sa'd from Abd al-Rahman ibn Qatada; the editor identifies Abu Salih as Abd Allah ibn Salih al-Misri, scribe of al-Layth ibn Sa'd, trustworthy though spoken against, and Mu'awiya ibn Salih as trustworthy, and refers to the note on report 15377. Then al-Tabari says: there is disagreement over the words "we witness, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection that we were unaware of this". Al-Suddi said: it is a report from God about Himself and His angels, that He and His angels said so when the children of Adam acknowledged His lordship, when He said to them "Am I not your Lord?" and they said yes. The editor notes that the manuscript has "when it was said to them" and that the printed edition made it "they said, yes" to follow the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 249}
On al-Suddi's view the sense is therefore: when your Lord took from the children of Adam, from their loins, their offspring and made them witness against themselves, "Am I not your Lord?", and they said yes, God and His angels said "we witness against you your acknowledgement that God is your Lord, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection that we were unaware of this". Al-Tabari notes that the report from him to this effect has already been given, as has the other report from Abd Allah ibn Amr from the Prophet in the same sense. Others said it is a report from God of what some of Adam's children said to others when God made them witness over one another, and that "made them witness against themselves" means He made some witness against others by their acknowledgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 250}
Al-Tabari then chooses: the better of the two views is what was reported from the Messenger of God if it is sound, and he does not know it to be sound, because the trustworthy narrators relied on for memory and precision narrated this hadith from al-Thawri stopping it at Abd Allah ibn Amr, without raising it and without the point Ahmad ibn Abi Tayba mentioned. If it is not sound from him, the apparent sense indicates that it is a report from God of what the children of Adam said to one another, for He says that He made them witness against themselves and they said yes, we witness; it is as if it were said that those who witnessed against their fellows when they acknowledged and said yes, said: we witness against you what you have acknowledged against yourselves, lest you say on the Day of Resurrection that we were unaware of this. The editor refers to reports 15373 and 15354 and to his note on the latter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 250}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
