## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when it is said to him, fear God, pride seizes him in sin; Hell is enough for him, and wretched is the resting place": al-Tabari says the sense is that when this hypocrite, whom God described to His Prophet, is told, fear God and be wary of Him in your corruption in His earth, your striving in it by what He forbade you, and your destroying the Muslims' crops and offspring, he becomes proud, is seized by pride and zeal for what God forbade him, and persists in his error; so burning in the fire of Hell is punishment enough for him, and wretched is the resting place for whoever burns in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 244} The interpreters differed over whom the verse means. Some said every wrongdoer and hypocrite; Abu Raja' al-'Utaridi heard 'Ali say of the verses from "among the people is he whose speech pleases you" to "God is kind to His servants": by the Lord of the Ka'ba, they fought (3998). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 244} From Ibn Zayd: after his voluntary prayer 'Umar ibn al-Khattab used to go into his date-drying yard and send for young men who had read the Qur'an, among them Ibn 'Abbas and the nephew of 'Uyayna, and they studied it until the midday rest; once they reached this verse and the one on those who sell themselves seeking God's pleasure, "these are the fighters in God's way", and Ibn 'Abbas said to the man beside him, did the two men fight? 'Umar overheard and asked, and he said, nothing, Commander of the Faithful, but 'Umar pressed him; Ibn 'Abbas said, I see here one who, when told to fear God, is seized by pride in sin, and one who sells himself seeking God's pleasure, who stands and bids the other to fear God, and when he does not accept and pride seizes him, the first says, I sell myself, and fights him, so the two fight; 'Umar said, God bless your land, Ibn 'Abbas (3999). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 245} The editor explains "subha" and "mirbad", identifies al-Hurr ibn Qays, nephew of 'Uyayna, and corrects the printed wording of 'Umar's exclamation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 245} Others said it is about al-Akhnas ibn Shariq, as already reported (3961). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 245} On "wretched is the resting place": the bed and couch of Hell, with which God threatened this hypocrite and which he prepared for himself by his hypocrisy, wickedness and rebellion against his Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 246}
