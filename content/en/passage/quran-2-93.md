## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "when We took your covenant and raised the mountain above you: take what We gave you with strength and listen; they said: we heard and we disobeyed", al-Tabari says the sense is: remember when We took your pledges to take what We gave you of the Torah with earnestness and zeal, acting on what is in it and refraining from what you were forbidden, and you gave the pledge when We raised the mountain over you. "Listen" means accept with obedience, as a man says "I heard and obeyed", with a verse of rajaz. "They said: we heard" is a report about the Jews that when told this they said: we heard your word and disobeyed your command; the speech turns from address to the third person, as Arabic customarily does. On "and the calf was made to sink into their hearts because of their disbelief" there are two views: that love of the calf sank into their hearts until it reached them, from Qatada, Abu al-'Aliya and al-Rabi'; or that they were made to drink water into which the filings of the calf were scattered, from al-Suddi (Moses burned it, ground it and scattered it in the sea, and for those who loved it gold showed on the drinker) and from Ibn Jurayj. Al-Tabari prefers the first, since one does not say of water that it "sank into the heart"; that is said of love of a thing, and "love" is dropped as the hearer understands, as in "ask the town", citing Zuhayr and Tarafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 356} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 357} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 359}
