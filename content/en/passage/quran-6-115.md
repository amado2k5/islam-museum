## Summary
Verse 115 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 115 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "and the word of your Lord is perfected in truth and justice; none can change His words, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing", al-Tabari says: the "word of your Lord" is perfected, meaning the Qur'an; He called it a "word" as the Arabs say of a poem that a poet recites, "this is so-and-so's kalima"; "in truth and justice" means that the word of your Lord is complete in truth and justice, both put in the accusative as a specification (tamyiz) of the word, as one says "I have twenty dirhams"; "none can change His words" means none can alter what He reported in His books will happen, in its occurrence at the time and term in which God reported it will occur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 62}

That is like His saying in al-Fath (48:15), "they want to change the word of God; say: you shall not follow us". Their wish to change God's word was their asking the Prophet of God to leave them to join the fighting with him, and their saying to him and the believers with him, "let us follow you", after the report God had given them in His book in al-Tawba (9:83), "if God brings you back to a party of them and they ask leave to go out, say: you shall never go out with me", so they tried to change God's word and His report that they would not go out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 62}

Al-Tabari completes his remarks on "none can change His words". Those who stayed behind wished to go out with the Prophet of God on a raid, though they would not fight an enemy with him, saying "let us follow you"; and God said to His prophet "they want to change", by making that request, the word of God and His report, "say: you shall not follow us; thus God said before". The meaning of "none can change His words" is likewise that none can alter what He reported will be, so as to void its coming, being and occurring as He reported, because the fabricators neither add to the books of God nor take from them; the Jews and Christians are, no doubt, the people of the books God sent down to His prophets, and He has reported that they alter, but not what He reported has no changer. Report 13789, from Qatada: truth and justice in what He ruled. As for "He is the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing", it means God hears what these who equate others with Him, who swear by God their strongest oaths that if a sign came to them they would believe in it, say, and what else creatures say, and knows what their oaths come to, of fulfilment, truth, falsehood and breaking, and other affairs of His servants (the editor refers to the lexical indexes for "sami'" and "'alim"). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 63}

The editor adds that at this place a part of the old division of the work, from which his copy was transcribed, comes to an end; the copy gives that part's closing line with a blessing on the Prophet, his family and his companions, and then the opening of the next part with the invocation and a prayer for success and help. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 63}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
