## Summary
Verse 41 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 41 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 41: "O Messenger, let not those who race to disbelief grieve you", pp. 301-303**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over whom the verse is about. Some said it was revealed about Abu Lubaba ibn 'Abd al-Mundhir for what he said to Banu Qurayza when the Prophet besieged them: it is slaughter, so do not surrender to Sa'd's ruling. Report 11918, from al-Suddi, says it was revealed about a man of the Ansar, said to be Abu Lubaba, to whom Banu Qurayza signalled on the day of the siege asking what the matter was and on what to surrender, and he signalled to them that it was slaughter.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 301}

Others said it was revealed about a Jew who asked a Muslim to ask the Prophet for his ruling on a man he had killed. Reports 11919 and 11920, from al-Sha'bi, say a Jew was killed by a man of his own faith, and the killer told their Muslim allies: ask Muhammad for me; if he has been sent to give the blood-money we will bring our dispute to him, and if he orders killing we will not come.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 302}

Others said it was revealed about 'Abdallah ibn Suriya, who left Islam after accepting it. Report 11921, from Abu Hurayra, says the Jewish rabbis gathered in the house of study when the Prophet came to Medina, a married man of theirs having committed adultery with a married Jewish woman, and they said: take this man and woman to Muhammad and ask him how to rule, giving him the ruling over them; if he rules as you do, with blackening, that is lashing with a tarred rope of fibre, then blackening their faces and mounting them on two donkeys facing the donkeys' rears, follow him, for he is only a king; but if he rules stoning, beware of him, lest he strip you of what is in your hands. They came to him saying this man had committed adultery after marriage with a married woman, rule on them, we have given you the ruling; so the Prophet went to their rabbis in the house of study and said: Jews, bring me your most learned, and they brought out the one-eyed 'Abdallah ibn Suriya, and some of Banu Qurayza relate that they also brought Abu Yasir ibn Akhtab and Wahb ibn Yahuda, saying these are our scholars.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 303}

The rest of report 11921 has the Prophet questioning them until their position was settled, to the point that they told Ibn Suriya this was the most learned left in the Torah. The Prophet took him aside, a young man, the youngest of them, and pressed the question: Ibn Suriya, I ask you by God and remind you of His favours to the Children of Israel, do you know that God ruled in the Torah that one who commits adultery after marriage is stoned? He said: yes, by God, Abu al-Qasim, they know you are a sent prophet, but they envy you. The Prophet went out and ordered the two stoned at the door of his mosque among Banu Ghanm ibn Malik ibn al-Najjar, and afterwards Ibn Suriya disbelieved, and God revealed the verse. The editor says the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira, corrects the lineage of Banu Ghanm, and traces it in Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Bayhaqi.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 304}

Report 11922, from al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, says the Prophet passed a Jew blackened and lashed, and called one of their scholars and asked: is this how you find the penalty for adultery among you? He said yes. Then he said: I ask you by Him who revealed the Torah to Moses, is this how you find the penalty for adultery among you? He said: no, and had you not asked by this I would not have told you, but it is stoning; yet adultery spread among our nobles, and when we took a noble we left him and when we took a weak man we applied the penalty, so we said let us gather and set something in place of stoning for noble and common alike, and we set blackening and lashing in place of stoning. The Prophet said: I am the first to revive your ruling when they let it die, and ordered him stoned, and God revealed "let not those who race to disbelief grieve you". The editor says Muslim, Ahmad, Abu Dawud and others gave it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 305}

Reports 11923 and 11924, from al-Zuhri, from a man of Muzayna, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, from Abu Hurayra, say a Jew came, they having taken counsel about a companion of theirs who had committed adultery after marriage; some said to others: this prophet has been sent, and you know stoning was made obligatory on you in the Torah and you hid it and settled on a lesser penalty, so let us ask him; if he rules as the Torah laid down, stoning, we will leave what we have, for the Torah is more fit to be obeyed. They came and said: Abu al-Qasim, a companion of ours who is married has committed adultery, what penalty do you see? The Prophet rose, we with him, and went to the Jews' house of study, found them studying the Torah and said: I ask you by God who revealed the Torah to Moses, what do you find in it as the penalty for one who commits adultery after marriage? They said: we find blackening and lashing. Their rabbi was silent at the side of the room, and when the Prophet saw his silence he pressed his question, and the rabbi said: since you ask us by God, we find stoning.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 306}

The report goes on that the Prophet asked: what was the first thing in which you relaxed God's command? He said a king's cousin committed adultery and was not stoned, then another man of a lesser family did, and the king wished to stone him, but his people stood up for him saying, by God you will not stone him until you stone so-and-so the king's cousin, so they agreed on a penalty short of stoning and left stoning. The Prophet said: then I rule by what is in the Torah, and God revealed on this the verse, up to [[passage/quran-5-44|5:44]]. The editor says the man of Muzayna is unknown and the chain is broken in Ahmad.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 307}

Others said the hypocrites were meant; report 11925, from 'Abdallah ibn Kathir, says they are the hypocrites, and report 11926, from Mujahid, says they are the hypocrites and are listeners to the Jews.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 307}

Al-Tabari chooses that the soundest view is that a group of hypocrites is meant, and it is possible that among those it takes in were Ibn Suriya, or Abu Lubaba, or others; but the best-established report is that from Abu Hurayra and al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, being from two Companions, so it is right to say 'Abdallah ibn Suriya was meant. The verse then means: Messenger, do not let those who race to deny your prophethood and disbelieve you grieve you, of those who said "we believe you, Muhammad, and know it with certainty from finding your description in our book". For in Abu Hurayra's report Ibn Suriya said they know you are a sent prophet but envy you, which was belief with the mouth and not confirmation in the heart, and God informed His prophet of Ibn Suriya's inner thought and that he did not believe in his heart.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 308}

**Commentary on verse 41, continued: "and of the Jews, listeners to falsehood, listeners for other people who have not come to you", pp. 309-310**

Al-Tabari says God tells His prophet not to grieve at the haste to disbelieve of the hypocrites who show belief in you with their tongues while holding you false, nor at the Jews' haste to deny your prophethood. He then described their traits and their evil deeds, and told him, consoling him for the grief their denial brings despite knowing his truth, that they treat the forbidden as lawful and the vile food of bribes and illicit gain, are people of lies about God and distortion of His book, and that He will bring His disgrace on them in this world and punishment in the next; they are "listeners to falsehood", listening to their rabbis' claim that the ruling for the married adulterer in the Torah is blackening and lashing, "listeners for other people who have not come to you", those who listen to the adulterer's people, who wished to bring the case to the Prophet but had not come and were set on coming. Report 11927, from Mujahid, says they are listeners for other people who had not come, along with those who did.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 309}

The interpreters differed about the two kinds of listeners. Some said the listeners for other people were the Jews of Fadak, and the people who had not come the Jews of Medina; report 11928, from Jabir, says the Jews who did not come were the Jews of Fadak, telling the Jews of Medina: if you are given this, take it; the editor thinks the correct reading reverses this heading, by the report itself. Others said those meant were Jews whom the people of the woman who committed adultery sent to ask the Prophet for the ruling, the senders being the other people, the woman's people, who had not come. Report 11929, from al-Suddi, says God sent down to the Children of Israel that if one of you commits adultery, stone him, and they kept to that until a man of their best did; when the Children of Israel gathered to stone him, the best and the nobles stood up and prevented it; then a man of the weak did.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 310}

The rest of report 11929, from al-Suddi, says the weak gathered and said: do not stone him until you bring your man and stone both; so the Children of Israel said this has become hard on us, let us put it right, and they dropped stoning and put in its place forty lashes with a tarred rope, mounting him on a donkey facing its tail, blackening his face and parading him, and so they did until the Prophet was sent and came to Medina. A woman of the Jewish nobles named Busra committed adultery, and her father sent some of his companions to the Prophet to ask about adultery and what was revealed to him on it, fearing he would expose them; if he gave the lashes take it, and if he ordered stoning beware. They came and asked and he said: stoning. God revealed "and of the Jews, listeners for other people who have not come to you, distorting words after their places" when they altered stoning into lashing.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 311}

Al-Tabari chooses that the soundest view is that the "listeners to falsehood" are the "listeners for other people"; they may be of the Jews of Medina with those listened for the Jews of Fadak, or others, but in any case they are a description of a group of Jews who heard falsehood about God on the ruling for the married woman who committed adultery among them, that her penalty in the Torah is blackening and lashing, who asked the Prophet about the ruling binding on her and heard what her people said before they came to him for judgement, and who only asked in order to tell the woman's people what his answer would be: if his ruling was not stoning they would accept him as judge, and if it was stoning they would be wary of him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 311}

Ibn Zayd spoke similarly, and report 11930 has him say they are listeners for other People of the Book who had not come, telling them the lie that Muhammad is a liar and this is not in the Torah, so do not believe him. The editor notes that here ended a part of the old division of the manuscript from which the copy was made, where the copyist closed the part with a prayer and began the next with the opening invocation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 312}

**Commentary on verse 41, continued: "distorting words after their places, saying: if you are given this take it, and if not, beware", pp. 313-316**

Al-Tabari says these listeners to falsehood, listeners for other Jews who had not yet come to him, distort the words; their distortion was changing God's ruling that He sent down in the Torah for married adulterers by stoning into lashing and blackening, the sense being the ruling of the words, with the mention of the report standing in for the mention of "ruling" because the hearers knew the sense, and likewise "after their places" meaning after God set it in its places, as in [[passage/quran-2-177|2:177]]. It might also mean they distort words from their places, "after" standing in for "from". He understands "if you are given this take it, and if not, beware" as these wrongdoers saying: if Muhammad rules lashing and blackening for our man, take it, and if he rules stoning, beware.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 313}

The interpreters said likewise. Report 11931, from Abu Hurayra, says those of them who were sent and those who stayed behind told them to distort the words, saying "if you are given this take it" for the blackening, and "if not, beware" meaning stoning. Reports 11932 and 11933, from Mujahid, say the sense is: if he agrees with you take it, and if not beware, the Jews saying it to the hypocrites. Report 11934, from al-Suddi, says they altered stoning into lashing. Report 11935, from Jabir, says they were the Jews of Fadak telling the Jews of Medina: if you are given this lashing take it, and if not beware of stoning.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 314}

Report 11936, from Ibn 'Abbas, says they were Jews among whom a woman committed adultery, and God had ruled stoning for it in the Torah, but they were loath to stone her and said: go to Muhammad, perhaps he has a concession, and if he does accept it; they came and said: Abu al-Qasim, a woman of ours committed adultery, what do you say? He said: how does God's ruling in the Torah read on the adulterer? They said: leave the Torah, what do you say? He said: bring me the most learned of you in the Torah sent down to Moses, and said to him: by Him who saved you from Pharaoh's people and split the sea for you, tell me God's ruling in the Torah on the adulterer. They said: its ruling is stoning, and he ordered her stoned. Report 11937, from Qatada, says this concerned a man of Banu Qurayza killed by Banu al-Nadir; when al-Nadir killed one of Qurayza they did not give retaliation, only blood-money, for their superiority, while when Qurayza killed one of al-Nadir they accepted nothing but retaliation; the Prophet came to Medina at the time of this killing and they wished to take it to him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 315}

A hypocrite among them told them: your slain man was killed deliberately, and when you take him to Muhammad I fear retaliation for you; if he accepts blood-money take it, and if not be on your guard. Report 11938, from Ibn Zayd, says those who had not come distort words from their places and do not put them where God sent them down, and that they are all Jews, some of them like others. Report 11939, from al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, says they say: go to Muhammad, and if he rules blackening and lashing take it, and if he rules stoning beware.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 316}

**Commentary on verse 41, continued: "whomever God wishes to try, you have no power for him against God", pp. 316-317**

Al-Tabari says this is God's consolation to His prophet from his grief over the haste of the Jews and hypocrites whose story He told, saying: do not let their haste to deny your prophethood grieve you, for I have decreed that they will not repent of their error nor return from their disbelief, by My earlier anger on them, and your grief does not help them. "Trial" here means going astray from the right path, so for one God wishes to leave astray you cannot rescue him from the confusion and error God willed for him, and should not grieve over missing his guidance to the truth, as report 11940 from al-Suddi has it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 317}

Then comes the commentary on "those whose hearts God did not wish to purify: for them is disgrace in this world and a great punishment in the hereafter"; al-Tabari says God tells His prophet not to grieve over those who race to disbelief among the Jews described to him, their haste being because God willed their trial and sealed their hearts, so they will never be guided; these are the ones whose hearts God did not wish to purify from the filth of disbelief.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 317}

We stop here at the foot of p. 317, in al-Tabari's commentary on "those whose hearts God did not wish to purify"; the rest of verse 41 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
