**Commentary on verse 41: "O Messenger, let not those who race to disbelief grieve you", pp. 301-303**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over whom the verse is about. Some said it was revealed about Abu Lubaba ibn 'Abd al-Mundhir for what he said to Banu Qurayza when the Prophet besieged them: it is slaughter, so do not surrender to Sa'd's ruling. Report 11918, from al-Suddi, says it was revealed about a man of the Ansar, said to be Abu Lubaba, to whom Banu Qurayza signalled on the day of the siege asking what the matter was and on what to surrender, and he signalled to them that it was slaughter.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 301}

Others said it was revealed about a Jew who asked a Muslim to ask the Prophet for his ruling on a man he had killed. Reports 11919 and 11920, from al-Sha'bi, say a Jew was killed by a man of his own faith, and the killer told their Muslim allies: ask Muhammad for me; if he has been sent to give the blood-money we will bring our dispute to him, and if he orders killing we will not come.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 302}

Others said it was revealed about 'Abdallah ibn Suriya, who left Islam after accepting it. Report 11921, from Abu Hurayra, says the Jewish rabbis gathered in the house of study when the Prophet came to Medina, a married man of theirs having committed adultery with a married Jewish woman, and they said: take this man and woman to Muhammad and ask him how to rule, giving him the ruling over them; if he rules as you do, with blackening, that is lashing with a tarred rope of fibre, then blackening their faces and mounting them on two donkeys facing the donkeys' rears, follow him, for he is only a king; but if he rules stoning, beware of him, lest he strip you of what is in your hands. They came to him saying this man had committed adultery after marriage with a married woman, rule on them, we have given you the ruling; so the Prophet went to their rabbis in the house of study and said: Jews, bring me your most learned, and they brought out the one-eyed 'Abdallah ibn Suriya, and some of Banu Qurayza relate that they also brought Abu Yasir ibn Akhtab and Wahb ibn Yahuda, saying these are our scholars.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 303}

We stop here at the foot of p. 303, in report 11921 from Abu Hurayra, where the Prophet questions the Jewish scholars; the rest of verse 41 follows in the next batch.
