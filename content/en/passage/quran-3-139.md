## Summary
Verse 139 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 139 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 234).** Abu Ja'far says this is God's consolation to the Companions of the Messenger for the wounds and killing they suffered at Uhud. The prohibition of weakness means they must not be weakened by what their enemy inflicted on them of killing and wounds so as to fall short in fighting and warring against him (from the saying "so-and-so grew weak in this matter, he is weak, weakness"); the prohibition of grief means they must not despair and lament what happened to them that day, since they are the uppermost, the prevailing over their enemy, and theirs is the issue in victory and help, if they believe His Prophet Muhammad in what he promises and in what he reports about where their affair and their enemy's will end. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 234}
- From al-Muthanna, Suwayd ibn Nasr, Ibn al-Mubarak, Yunus, al-Zuhri (7884): killing and wounds multiplied among the Companions of Muhammad until hardship reached every man of them, so God sent down the Qur'an and consoled the believers in the best way a Muslim people before them among the past nations had been consoled, from this verse to the verse at [[passage/quran-3-154|3:154]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 234}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7885): He consoles the Companions of Muhammad, as you hear, urges them to fight their enemy and forbids them to be helpless and weak in pursuing their enemy in God's way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 234}
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7886): He commands Muhammad, saying they must not weaken in going forward in God's way. The editor notes that the manuscript has an extra "and" and the printed text is clearer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 235}
- From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7887): do not weaken. From al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid, the same (7888). From al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7889): do not weaken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 235}
- From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj (7890): do not weaken in dealing with your enemy; and on the being uppermost he said: the Companions of the Messenger were routed in the ravine, saying what happened to so-and-so, announcing each other's deaths and telling one another that the Messenger had been killed, and they were in distress and grief. While they were so, Khalid ibn al-Walid climbed the mountain with the polytheists' cavalry above them, while they were below in the ravine. When they saw the Prophet they rejoiced, and the Prophet said: O God, we have no strength but in You, and none worships You in this land but this small band. A group of Muslim archers rallied, climbed up and shot at the polytheists' horses until God routed them and the Muslims rose over the mountain; that is the meaning of their being uppermost. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 235}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7891): do not weaken, and do not lament what has befallen you (the editor notes Ibn Hisham has "do not be despondent"), for the outcome and the ascendancy will be yours, if you believed My prophet in what he brought you from Me. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham (3: 116), the continuation of the preceding reports (the number is printed garbled). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 236}
- From Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, Ibn Abbas (7892): Khalid ibn al-Walid came wanting to climb the mountain above them, and the Prophet said: O God, let them not rise above us; and God sent down this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 236}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 236}
