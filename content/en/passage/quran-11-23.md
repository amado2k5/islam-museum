**Commentary on verse 23 (pp. 289-291)**

On [[passage/quran-11-23|11:23]] al-Tabari says: those who affirmed God and His Messenger and in the world worked in obedience to God, 'and humbled themselves before their Lord'. The interpreters differed on the meaning of 'ikhbat'. Some said it means they turned to their Lord; report 18095, from Ibn Abbas: 'ikhbat' is turning in repentance; report 18096, from Qatada: they turned to their Lord. Others said it means they feared; report 18097, from Ibn Abbas. Others said: they were at ease, reports 18098, 18099 and 18100, from Mujahid. Others said: they were humble; report 18101, from Qatada: 'ikhbat' is humility and lowliness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 290}

Al-Tabari says these sayings are close in meaning though their words differ, since turning to God comes from fear of God and from humility and lowliness before God in obedience, and ease toward Him is of humility toward Him, though the sense of 'ikhbat' itself among the Arabs is humility and lowliness. He says 'to their Lord' meaning 'for their Lord', since the Arabs often put 'li' in place of 'ila' and 'ila' in place of 'li', as in [[passage/quran-99-5|99:5]], meaning He inspired it to it; it may also be said because they are described as making for God by their humility. His words 'those are the people of Paradise, abiding in it' mean those so described are the dwellers of Paradise, who do not leave it nor die in it but remain in it without end. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 291}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
