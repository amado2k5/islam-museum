**Commentary on verse 65 (pp. 161-162)**

On "and when they opened their baggage they found their goods returned to them; they said, O our father, what more can we ask? Here are our goods returned to us; we shall bring provision for our family and guard our brother and gain an extra camel-load; that is an easy measure", al-Tabari says: when Joseph's brothers opened their baggage, which they had carried from Egypt from Joseph, they found their goods, the price of the food they had measured from him, returned to them; and they said to their father: what should we ask for? Here are our goods returned to us, to put his mind at ease about what had been done with them in returning their goods. Taken this way, "ma" is an interrogative in the accusative as the object of "nabghi". Qatada used to take this interpretation. The editor corrects the wording "returned to them" in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 161}

Report 19476, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "what more can we ask" means we ask nothing beyond this, for our goods have been returned to us and the measure given in full. On "we shall bring provision for our family", he says: we seek food for our family and buy it for them; one says "mara fulan ahlahu yamiruhum mayran". Al-Tabari cites a verse by a poet who sent a man as a provisioner who then stayed a year, the poet asking when his relief would come to those he would relieve; the editor says he does not know who said it and did not find the verse anywhere. "And guard our brother", whom you send with us, "and gain an extra camel-load", that is, we gain on our loads of food the load of a camel measured for us, "that is an easy measure", an easy load. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 162}

Report 19477, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Harith, al-Qasim and Hajjaj: each man of them had a camel-load, so they said send our brother with us and we gain a camel-load; and Ibn Jurayj said, from Mujahid, "a camel's measure" is a donkey's load, and that is a dialect; al-Qasim says Mujahid means that in some dialects the donkey is called "ba'ir". Report 19478, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: a camel's load. Report 19479, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: we count another camel with our camels, "that is an easy measure". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 162}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
