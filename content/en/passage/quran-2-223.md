## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari takes God's saying that women are a tilth for their husbands (see [[passage/quran-2-223|2:223]]) to mean that they are the field where their children are sown, so they may come to their field however and wherever they wish. The tilth is the sown crop, and women are called a tilth because they are among the means of the tilth, the sense being understood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 397} The interpreters said likewise: from Ibn 'Abbas, the place the child grows (4307); from al-Suddi, a field in which one ploughs (4308). The note says the editor restated al-Tabari's wording because it was garbled in the manuscript and the printed edition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 397}

The command to come to the tilth means: have intercourse with the field of your children from wherever you wish of the ways of approach; "approach" here is a euphemism for intercourse, and the editor refers to an earlier note. The interpreters differed over the meaning of "anna". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 398} Some said it means "how"; from Ibn 'Abbas, he comes to her however he wishes so long as it is not in the rear passage or in menstruation (4309); from him also, come to her however you wish, facing or turned away, so long as you do not come to her by the rear passage or in menstruation (4310); and from him, by the tilth he means the vulva, which you come to however you wish, facing it or turned from it, in whatever way you wish, provided you do not go beyond the vulva to anything else, which is what was said earlier about coming from where God commanded (4311). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 398} From 'Ikrima: however he wishes, so long as he does not do the deed of the people of Lut (4312); from Mujahid: however he wishes, but avoid the rear passage and menstruation (4313); from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: come to her lying, standing, turned aside, facing or turned away, however you wish, if it is in her front passage (4314). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 399} The long note on the chain of 4314 gives the narrators: 'Ubaydallah ibn Sa'd al-Zuhri is truthful, his uncle Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim trustworthy and reliable, his father Ibrahim ibn Sa'd trustworthy, Yazid ibn 'Abdallah ibn al-Had is listed among the trustworthy by Ibn Hibban, and ibn Ka'b is the tabi'i Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 399}

From Murra al-Hamdani: a Jewish man met a Muslim and asked whether one of them comes to his wife while kneeling, and he said yes; this was mentioned to the Prophet and the verse came down, meaning however he wishes provided it is in the vulva (4315). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 400} From Qatada: if you wish standing, sitting or on the side, if he comes to her from the way menstruation comes and does not go beyond that to anything else (4316); from al-Suddi: come to your field however you wish from its front, and do not come to her by the rear passage (4317). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 400} From 'Abdallah ibn 'Ali, who had heard that some Companions of the Prophet were sitting one day with a Jewish man near them, and one began saying he came to his wife lying down, another standing, another on her side and kneeling; the Jewish man said, you are no more than like beasts, whereas we come to her in a single posture; and God sent down the verse, the front passage being what is meant (4318). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 400}

Others said "anna shi'tum" means from wherever you wish and in whatever way you like. From Ibn 'Abbas, who disliked a woman being approached by the rear passage and said the tilth is only the front, from which offspring and menses come, forbidding the other, and said the verse came down meaning in whatever way you wish (4319). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 401} From 'Ikrima, a description of a posture which the narrator says refers to the rear passage (4320). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 401} The note says the narrator's name in 4319 is Sahl ibn Musa al-Razi and the editor discusses how it should be read. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 401}
