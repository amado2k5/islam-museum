## Summary
Verse 3 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 3 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 3 (pp. 112-118)**

Al-Tabari says the opening words mean an announcement from God and His messenger to the people on the day of the greater pilgrimage, the sense of "announcement" having been explained earlier. Sulayman ibn Musa gave it another sense in report 16380, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: the "announcement" is the narrative running from the opening of the sura until it ends at [[passage/quran-9-28|9:28]], twenty-eight verses; the editor identifies Sulayman ibn Musa al-Umawi al-Dimashqi al-Ashdaq as the jurist of Syria in his time. Report 16381, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: a notification from God and His messenger. The word is in the nominative by conjunction with the opening "disavowal from God", as if to say "this is a disavowal from God and His messenger, and an announcement from God". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 112}

On the "day of the greater pilgrimage" there is disagreement among the scholars. Some said it is the day of Arafa. Report 16382, from Abu al-Sahba' al-Bakri, through Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Abd al-Hakam, Abu Zur'a, Hayat ibn Shurayh, Abu Sakhr and Abu Mu'awiya al-Bajali: I asked Ali ibn Abi Talib about the day of the greater pilgrimage and he said the Messenger sent Abu Bakr to hold the pilgrimage for the people and sent him with forty verses of the sura, until he reached Arafa and addressed the people on the day of Arafa; when he finished his address he turned to Ali and said stand, Ali, and deliver the Messenger's message, and Ali stood and recited the forty verses to them; then we went back to Mina, where I threw the pebbles, slaughtered the sacrifice and shaved my head, and knowing that the people of the gathering had not been present at Abu Bakr's address on the day of Arafa, I went after them among the tents reciting it to them, so from this I think you reckoned it to be the day of sacrifice, when it was the day of Arafa. The editor explains the narrators, Abu Zur'a Wahb Allah ibn Rashid al-Misri, Abu Sakhr Humayd ibn Ziyad al-Kharrat and Abu Mu'awiya al-Bajali Ammar ibn Mu'awiya al-Duhni, calls the chain sound, corrects a name, and explains the words for returning and for tents. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 113}

Report 16383, from Abu Ishaq, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: I asked Abu Juhayfa about the day of the greater pilgrimage and he said the day of Arafa; I asked whether it was his own view or from the Companions of Muhammad and he said both. Report 16384, from Ata, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn Jurayj: the greater pilgrimage is the day of Arafa. Report 16385, from Umar, through Ibn Waki, his father, Umar ibn al-Walid al-Shanni and Shihab ibn Abbad al-Asari from his father: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of Arafa, and the narrator then asked Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, who reported from Ibn Umar that Umar said so. Report 16386, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Umar ibn al-Walid and Shihab ibn Abbad: I heard Umar ibn al-Khattab say this is the day of Arafa, the day of the greater pilgrimage, so let no one fast it; the narrator later asked about the best of the people of Medina and was sent to Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, who told him of one a hundred times better than himself, Umar or Ibn Umar, who forbade fasting it and said it is the day of the greater pilgrimage. The editor adopts a reading from Ibn Kathir's commentary at one point and says Abu Juhayfa is Wahb ibn Abdullah, called Wahb al-Khayr, and reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 114}

Report 16387, from Ibn al-Zubayr, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Abd al-Samad ibn Habib and Ma'qil ibn Dawud: this day of Arafa is the day of the greater pilgrimage, so let no one fast it; the editor says al-Bukhari and Ahmad judged Abd al-Samad ibn Habib weak and that he found no entry for Ma'qil ibn Dawud. Report 16388, from Ghalib ibn Ubaydallah: I asked Ata about the day of the greater pilgrimage and he said the day of Arafa, so leave it before the dawn; the editor calls Ghalib one whose reports are rejected. Report 16389, from Muhammad ibn Qays ibn Makhrama, through Ibn Waki, Muhammad ibn Bakr and Ibn Jurayj: the Prophet addressed the people on the evening of Arafa, saying after the praise that this is the day of the greater pilgrimage; the editor says Muhammad was a reliable Successor reporting from the Prophet without a Companion between. Report 16390, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Abd al-Wahhab: the day of Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 115}

Report 16391, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Harith, al-Qasim, Ishaq ibn Sulayman, Salama ibn Bukht and Ikrima: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of Arafa; the editor corrects the name Salama ibn Bukht and notes that Ahmad found nothing wrong with him. Report 16392, from Tawus, from his father, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: we asked what the greater pilgrimage is, and he said the day of Arafa. Report 16393, from Muhammad ibn Qays ibn Makhrama, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Idris and Ibn Jurayj: the Messenger addressed the people on the day of Arafa and said this is the day of the greater pilgrimage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 116}

Others said it is the day of sacrifice. Report 16394, from Ali, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abu Asim, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and al-Harith: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice; report 16395 is similar through Abu Kurayb, Mus'ab ibn Sallam and al-Ajlah, and report 16396 through Ibn Humayd, Hukkam and Anbasa. The editor explains that al-Harith here is al-Harith al-A'war, whose weakness has been noted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 116}

Report 16397, from Abdullah ibn Abi Awfa, through Ibn Abi al-Shawarib, Abd al-Wahid and Sulayman al-Shaybani: I asked him about the greater pilgrimage and he said the day of sacrifice; reports 16398 to 16403 give the same by several routes through Ayyash al-Amiri, Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr and al-Shaybani, some adding that it is the day on which blood is shed; report 16404, from Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr, through Ya'qub and Hushaym, calls it the day on which blood is shed and hair is shaved. The editor says Ayyash ibn Amr al-Amiri is reliable and that Abdullah ibn Abi Awfa al-Aslami was a Companion who took part in the pledge of al-Ridwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 117}

Report 16405, from Yahya ibn al-Jazzar, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abu Dawud, Shu'ba and al-Hakam: Ali went out on the day of sacrifice on a white mule toward the cemetery and a man took the bridle and asked him about the greater pilgrimage, and he said it is this day of yours, let her go. Report 16406, from Ali, through Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayan, Ishaq, Malik ibn Mighwal, Shutayr and al-Harith: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice; report 16407 is similar through Ibn Waki and Ibn Uyayna; report 16408, from Yahya ibn al-Jazzar, through Ibn Waki, his father, Shu'ba and al-Hakam, has a man meet Ali on the day of sacrifice, take his bridle and ask about the day of the greater pilgrimage, and Ali answers that it is this day. Report 16409, from Ibn Abi Awfa, through Ibn Waki, Yahya ibn Adam, Qays, Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr and Ayyash: it is the day on which blood is shed; report 16410, through Ibn Waki and Ibn Uyayna: the greater pilgrimage is the day on which blood is shed, hair is shaved and what was forbidden becomes lawful. The editor says Yahya ibn al-Jazzar is reliable though extreme in Shi'ism and heard only three reports from Ali, this being one. Report 16411 begins with its chain from Isa ibn Uthman ibn Isa al-Ramli. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 118}

We stop here at the foot of p. 118, in the middle of the commentary on verse 3 (at the start of report 16411, in the view that the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice); the next batch begins at p. 119. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 118}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
