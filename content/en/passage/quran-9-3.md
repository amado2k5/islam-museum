## Summary
Verse 3 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 3 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 3 (pp. 112-125)**

Al-Tabari says the opening words mean an announcement from God and His messenger to the people on the day of the greater pilgrimage, the sense of "announcement" having been explained earlier. Sulayman ibn Musa gave it another sense in report 16380, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: the "announcement" is the narrative running from the opening of the sura until it ends at [[passage/quran-9-28|9:28]], twenty-eight verses; the editor identifies Sulayman ibn Musa al-Umawi al-Dimashqi al-Ashdaq as the jurist of Syria in his time. Report 16381, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: a notification from God and His messenger. The word is in the nominative by conjunction with the opening "disavowal from God", as if to say "this is a disavowal from God and His messenger, and an announcement from God". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 112}

On the "day of the greater pilgrimage" there is disagreement among the scholars. Some said it is the day of Arafa. Report 16382, from Abu al-Sahba' al-Bakri, through Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Abd al-Hakam, Abu Zur'a, Hayat ibn Shurayh, Abu Sakhr and Abu Mu'awiya al-Bajali: I asked Ali ibn Abi Talib about the day of the greater pilgrimage and he said the Messenger sent Abu Bakr to hold the pilgrimage for the people and sent him with forty verses of the sura, until he reached Arafa and addressed the people on the day of Arafa; when he finished his address he turned to Ali and said stand, Ali, and deliver the Messenger's message, and Ali stood and recited the forty verses to them; then we went back to Mina, where I threw the pebbles, slaughtered the sacrifice and shaved my head, and knowing that the people of the gathering had not been present at Abu Bakr's address on the day of Arafa, I went after them among the tents reciting it to them, so from this I think you reckoned it to be the day of sacrifice, when it was the day of Arafa. The editor explains the narrators, Abu Zur'a Wahb Allah ibn Rashid al-Misri, Abu Sakhr Humayd ibn Ziyad al-Kharrat and Abu Mu'awiya al-Bajali Ammar ibn Mu'awiya al-Duhni, calls the chain sound, corrects a name, and explains the words for returning and for tents. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 113}

Report 16383, from Abu Ishaq, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: I asked Abu Juhayfa about the day of the greater pilgrimage and he said the day of Arafa; I asked whether it was his own view or from the Companions of Muhammad and he said both. Report 16384, from Ata, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn Jurayj: the greater pilgrimage is the day of Arafa. Report 16385, from Umar, through Ibn Waki, his father, Umar ibn al-Walid al-Shanni and Shihab ibn Abbad al-Asari from his father: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of Arafa, and the narrator then asked Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, who reported from Ibn Umar that Umar said so. Report 16386, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Umar ibn al-Walid and Shihab ibn Abbad: I heard Umar ibn al-Khattab say this is the day of Arafa, the day of the greater pilgrimage, so let no one fast it; the narrator later asked about the best of the people of Medina and was sent to Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, who told him of one a hundred times better than himself, Umar or Ibn Umar, who forbade fasting it and said it is the day of the greater pilgrimage. The editor adopts a reading from Ibn Kathir's commentary at one point and says Abu Juhayfa is Wahb ibn Abdullah, called Wahb al-Khayr, and reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 114}

Report 16387, from Ibn al-Zubayr, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Abd al-Samad ibn Habib and Ma'qil ibn Dawud: this day of Arafa is the day of the greater pilgrimage, so let no one fast it; the editor says al-Bukhari and Ahmad judged Abd al-Samad ibn Habib weak and that he found no entry for Ma'qil ibn Dawud. Report 16388, from Ghalib ibn Ubaydallah: I asked Ata about the day of the greater pilgrimage and he said the day of Arafa, so leave it before the dawn; the editor calls Ghalib one whose reports are rejected. Report 16389, from Muhammad ibn Qays ibn Makhrama, through Ibn Waki, Muhammad ibn Bakr and Ibn Jurayj: the Prophet addressed the people on the evening of Arafa, saying after the praise that this is the day of the greater pilgrimage; the editor says Muhammad was a reliable Successor reporting from the Prophet without a Companion between. Report 16390, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Abd al-Wahhab: the day of Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 115}

Report 16391, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Harith, al-Qasim, Ishaq ibn Sulayman, Salama ibn Bukht and Ikrima: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of Arafa; the editor corrects the name Salama ibn Bukht and notes that Ahmad found nothing wrong with him. Report 16392, from Tawus, from his father, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: we asked what the greater pilgrimage is, and he said the day of Arafa. Report 16393, from Muhammad ibn Qays ibn Makhrama, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Idris and Ibn Jurayj: the Messenger addressed the people on the day of Arafa and said this is the day of the greater pilgrimage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 116}

Others said it is the day of sacrifice. Report 16394, from Ali, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abu Asim, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and al-Harith: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice; report 16395 is similar through Abu Kurayb, Mus'ab ibn Sallam and al-Ajlah, and report 16396 through Ibn Humayd, Hukkam and Anbasa. The editor explains that al-Harith here is al-Harith al-A'war, whose weakness has been noted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 116}

Report 16397, from Abdullah ibn Abi Awfa, through Ibn Abi al-Shawarib, Abd al-Wahid and Sulayman al-Shaybani: I asked him about the greater pilgrimage and he said the day of sacrifice; reports 16398 to 16403 give the same by several routes through Ayyash al-Amiri, Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr and al-Shaybani, some adding that it is the day on which blood is shed; report 16404, from Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr, through Ya'qub and Hushaym, calls it the day on which blood is shed and hair is shaved. The editor says Ayyash ibn Amr al-Amiri is reliable and that Abdullah ibn Abi Awfa al-Aslami was a Companion who took part in the pledge of al-Ridwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 117}

Report 16405, from Yahya ibn al-Jazzar, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abu Dawud, Shu'ba and al-Hakam: Ali went out on the day of sacrifice on a white mule toward the cemetery and a man took the bridle and asked him about the greater pilgrimage, and he said it is this day of yours, let her go. Report 16406, from Ali, through Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayan, Ishaq, Malik ibn Mighwal, Shutayr and al-Harith: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice; report 16407 is similar through Ibn Waki and Ibn Uyayna; report 16408, from Yahya ibn al-Jazzar, through Ibn Waki, his father, Shu'ba and al-Hakam, has a man meet Ali on the day of sacrifice, take his bridle and ask about the day of the greater pilgrimage, and Ali answers that it is this day. Report 16409, from Ibn Abi Awfa, through Ibn Waki, Yahya ibn Adam, Qays, Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr and Ayyash: it is the day on which blood is shed; report 16410, through Ibn Waki and Ibn Uyayna: the greater pilgrimage is the day on which blood is shed, hair is shaved and what was forbidden becomes lawful. The editor says Yahya ibn al-Jazzar is reliable though extreme in Shi'ism and heard only three reports from Ali, this being one. Report 16411, from Abdullah ibn Sinan, through Isa ibn Uthman al-Ramli and al-A'mash: al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba addressed us on the day of sacrifice from a camel, saying this is the day of sacrifice and the day of the greater pilgrimage; reports 16412 and 16413 repeat it through Ibn Waki and his father. The editor says Abdullah ibn Sinan al-Asadi is reliable and that he corrected his name from "Ibn Yasar" in the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 119}

Report 16414, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Hammad ibn Salama, Simak and Ikrima: the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice; report 16415 is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr; report 16416, from Abu Juhayfa, through Ibn Waki, Ubaydallah, Isra'il and Abu Ishaq, says the same. Report 16417, from Abu Bishr, through Ibn Bashshar, Muhammad ibn Ja'far and Shu'ba: Ali ibn Abdullah ibn Abbas and a man of the house of Shayba disputed over the day of the greater pilgrimage, Ali saying it is the day of sacrifice and the other the day of Arafa; they sent to Sa'id ibn Jubayr, who said it is the day of sacrifice, since one who misses the day of Arafa has not missed the pilgrimage, but one who misses the day of sacrifice has. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 120}

Report 16418, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ya'qub, Hushaym and Yunus, tells the same dispute between Abdullah ibn Shayba and Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abdullah ibn Abbas. Report 16419 is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr through al-Shaybani; report 16420, from Qays ibn Ubada: the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja is the sacrifice and the day of the greater pilgrimage; reports 16421 and 16422, from Abdullah ibn Shaddad: the day of the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice and the lesser pilgrimage is the umra; report 16423, from Nafi' ibn Jubayr ibn Mut'im, and report 16424, from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, say it was said to be the day of sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 120}

Report 16425, from Amir al-Sha'bi: the day on which blood is shed and the forbidden becomes lawful; report 16426, from Ibrahim: the day of sacrifice, on which everything forbidden becomes lawful; report 16427, from Ali through al-Sha'bi: the day of sacrifice; report 16428, from Muhammad ibn Sirin: a day on which the Messenger's pilgrimage coincided with that of the desert dwellers; reports 16429 to 16431, from Mujahid: the day of sacrifice; report 16432, from al-Sha'bi, Ikrima and Mujahid, adds Mujahid's saying that it is the day on which the whole pilgrimage is brought together; reports 16433 to 16435, from Muhammad ibn Ali, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Ibn Abbas, and from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas: the day of sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 121}

Report 16436, from Ali and from al-Zuhri: the day of sacrifice. Report 16437, from Abu Hurayra, through Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Wahb, his uncle Abdullah ibn Wahb, Yunus, Amr, al-Zuhri and Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman: the Messenger sent me with Abu Bakr on the pilgrimage he put him in charge of before the farewell pilgrimage, among a group announcing to the people on the day of sacrifice that no polytheist is to perform the pilgrimage after the year nor circle the House naked; Humayd used to say the day of sacrifice is the day of the greater pilgrimage. The editor notes that al-Bukhari gives this in his Sahih and Abu Dawud in his Sunan. Reports 16438 and 16439, from Abdullah ibn Shaddad: the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice, the lesser the umra; report 16440, from Ibn Abi Awfa: the day on which hair is cut, blood is shed and the forbidden becomes lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 122}

Report 16441, from Ali; report 16442, from Ibn Abi Awfa, who when asked exclaimed that it is the day on which blood is shed, the forbidden becomes lawful and hair is cut, the day of sacrifice; report 16443, from Abdullah ibn Sinan, has al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba address them from a she-camel saying this is the day of sacrifice and the day of the greater pilgrimage; reports 16444 and 16445, from Ibrahim: the day of sacrifice. Report 16446, from Abu Bakra, through Ahmad ibn al-Miqdam, Yazid ibn Zuray', Ibn Awn, Ibn Sirin and Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakra: on that day the Prophet sat on a camel with someone holding its halter and asked what day this was; we were silent until we thought he would give it another name, and he said is it not the day of the pilgrimage. The editor points to al-Bukhari's fuller version, which has "the day of sacrifice". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 123}

Report 16447, from Ibn Umar, through Sahl ibn Muhammad al-Sijistani, Abu Jabir al-Harami and Hisham ibn al-Ghaz: the Messenger stood on the day of sacrifice at the pillars during the farewell pilgrimage and said this is the day of the greater pilgrimage. The editor spends long on correcting the narrators' names and tracing the report to Ibn Kathir, al-Bukhari in suspended form, Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah and al-Bayhaqi. Report 16448, from a Companion of the Prophet, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba, Amr ibn Murra and Murra al-Hamdani: the Messenger stood among us on a red she-camel with a cropped ear and asked whether we knew what day it was; they said the day of sacrifice, and he said you speak truly, the day of the greater pilgrimage; report 16449 is similar through Yahya ibn Sa'id. The editor suggests the Companion may be Ibn Mas'ud, explains the cropped ear, and cites al-Hakim's remark that the Companions and Successors differed over this day. Report 16450 begins, from Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, through Abu Kurayb and Ibn Idris, saying the Messenger sent Ali with four sayings when Abu Bakr led the people's pilgrimage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 125}

We stop here at the foot of p. 125, in the middle of the commentary on verse 3 (at the start of report 16450, after the reports that the greater pilgrimage is the day of sacrifice); the next batch begins at p. 126. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 125}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
