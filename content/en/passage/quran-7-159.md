**Commentary on verse 159 (pp. 172-174)**

Al-Tabari says: "of the people of Moses" means the Children of Israel, "a community" means a group, "who guide by the truth" means who are guided by the truth, that is, keep upright on it and act on it, "and by it do justice" means by the truth they give and take and deal fairly with themselves and do not wrong. A number of people have given accounts of the description of this community that God mentions in the verse, and al-Tabari gives what came to him of them. The editor refers to the explanations of "community", "guidance" and "justice". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 172}

Al-Tabari reports from al-Suddi that this community is a people between whom and you lies a river of "shahd". The editor explains that this means a river of honey, one of the rivers of Paradise, and that the word was corrupted in some books into "sahl" or "ramal" (sand); he notes that Ibn al-Khazin judged the story weak. The editor says that such a report is not to be taken up without a conclusive proof, that a report stopping at al-Suddi is no proof, and he gives al-Bukhari's note on the narrator Sadaqa Abu al-Hudhayl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 173}

Next he reports from Ibn Jurayj that it reached him that when the Children of Israel killed their prophets they disbelieved. They were twelve tribes; one tribe disowned what the rest had done, excused itself and asked God to separate it from them, so God opened a tunnel in the earth for them and they travelled in it until they came out beyond China, where they remain, upright Muslims who face our qibla. Ibn Jurayj adds from Ibn Abbas that this is what [[passage/quran-17-104|17:104]] means, that "the promise of the hereafter" is Jesus son of Mary, and that they will come out with him; and that they travelled in the tunnel for a year and a half. The editor observes that al-Tabari did not relate this report in his commentary on sura al-Isra and that this is part of his abridgment, which may point to the report's weakness in his eyes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 173} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 174}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
