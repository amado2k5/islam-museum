## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "from wherever you come out, turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque, and wherever you are, turn your faces toward it" al-Tabari says: from whatever place you leave, Muhammad, turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque, and wherever you are of God's earth, believers, turn your faces in prayer opposite it, toward it and to its aim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 199}

On "so that people have no argument against you, except those of them who do wrong, so do not fear them but fear Me", a group of interpreters said "the people" are the People of the Book, from Qatada and al-Rabi': they said, when the Prophet was turned to the Ka'ba, that the man longed for his father's house and his people's religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 199} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 200} Al-Tabari asks what argument the People of the Book had against the Prophet and his companions in their praying toward Jerusalem, and answers that they used to say Muhammad and his companions did not know where their qibla was until we guided them, and that he differs from us in our religion and follows our qibla, an argument of dispute and deception of the ignorant among the polytheists; "hujja" here means dispute and wrangling, and God cut it off by turning the qibla to that of Abraham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 200}

On "except those of them who do wrong" they are the Arab polytheists of Quraysh according to the interpreters, from Mujahid, al-Suddi, al-Rabi', Qatada and 'Ata'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 200} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 201} He asks what argument the Quraysh polytheists had and whether polytheists can have an argument against believers, and answers that "hujja" here means dispute and wrangling; the sense is that no one among the people should have a dispute or false claim against you, except the Quraysh polytheists who do have a false claim in saying Muhammad has returned to our qibla and will return to our religion, so God excepted them in denying that anyone has an argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 201} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 202}

He reports from Mujahid that their argument was their saying "you have returned to our qibla", and from Qatada and Mujahid that the Arab polytheists said, when the qibla was turned, he has returned to your qibla and will soon return to your religion, and God said "so do not fear them but fear Me". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 202} From al-Suddi on the authority of Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and some of the Companions, the polytheists of Mecca said, when the Prophet was turned to the Ka'ba, Muhammad's religion bewildered him, so he turned his qibla to you, knowing you were better guided, and will soon enter your religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 203} From 'Ata' and Mujahid, Quraysh said he cannot do without us, he has taken our qibla, and that was their argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 203}

Al-Tabari holds that the exception in "except those of them who do wrong" is the familiar kind, affirming for them what is denied of those before the particle, as in "no one among the people went except your brother"; so it is denied that anyone has a dispute or false claim against the Prophet and his companions for facing the Ka'ba except the Quraysh who wronged themselves, who do have a dispute and false claim in saying you turned to us and our qibla because we are better guided than you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 204} He rejects the view that "illa" means "and", since "illa" has that sense only after an earlier exception, and because the interpreters unanimously reject it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 204} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 205} He also rejects the view that "those who do wrong" are Arabs who were Jews and Christians whose argument is broken, the view that "illa" means "but", and the view that it begins a new clause, because the interpreters report that it tells of those who do wrong arguing as described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 205} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 206}

He reports from al-Rabi' that a Jew disputed with Abu al-'Aliya, saying Moses used to pray toward the rock of Jerusalem; Abu al-'Aliya replied he prayed at the rock toward the Sacred House, and the Jew said between me and you is the mosque of Salih, and he said I have prayed in it and its qibla is toward the Sacred House, adding that he passed Dhu al-Qarnayn's mosque and its qibla is toward the Ka'ba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 206}

On "so do not fear them but fear Me" he says: do not fear these wrongdoers in their argument, wrangling and saying that Muhammad has returned to our qibla and will return to our religion, nor that they can do you harm in your religion or turn you from the guidance God gave you, but fear Me, fear My punishment if you oppose My command; it is an exhortation to the believers to keep to their qibla and a prohibition on facing another. From al-Suddi: do not fear that I will return you to their religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 206} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 207}
