**Commentary on verse 75: "The Messiah son of Mary was only a messenger", pp. 484-486**

Al-Tabari says: this is God's argument for His prophet against the Christian parties in what they say of the Messiah, refuting the Jacobites who say he is God and the others who say he is God's son. He is the son of Mary, whom she bore as mothers bear their children, which is a human attribute and not that of the creator of humans; he is a messenger like those before him, at whose hands God worked signs and lessons as He willed, as proof of his truthfulness and that he was sent to those to whom he was sent, as He did at the hands of earlier messengers. The editor notes that a stretch of this page cannot be read with certainty in the printed copy and that a supplied word was added to make the sentence stand; the museum records that the foot of the page is partly illegible in the scan.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 484}

"His mother was a truthful woman" means that she was siddiqa, a form from truthfulness, as one calls a man siddiq, and the same word appears in the surah of the women in the mention of the truthful. Al-Tabari adds that it was said that Abu Bakr was called al-Siddiq for his truthfulness, and also that it was for believing the Prophet about his journey in one night from Mecca to Jerusalem and back. "They both ate food" reports that the two needed nourishment for their bodies like other humans; one who is so cannot be a god, since one needing food is sustained by another, which is clear proof of weakness, and the weak can only be a created servant, not a lord.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 485}

On "see how We make the signs clear to them", God tells His prophet to look at how He makes plain to these unbelieving Jews and Christians the proofs and arguments against what they say of God's prophets, their invention against Him and their claim that He has a son, and yet they do not stop even when decisive arguments come; then "see how they are turned away" means by what route they are diverted from the explanation and guidance. The Arabs say of anyone turned from something that he is "afuk" from it, and "I turned so-and-so from that".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 486}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
