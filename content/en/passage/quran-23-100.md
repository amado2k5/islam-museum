## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 100 to 101 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "until, when death comes to one of them, he says, 'My Lord, return'": until when one of these polytheists comes to death and witnesses the descent of Allah's command upon him, he says to the great one who witnesses the punishment that comes upon him, regretting what he has missed and eagerly seeking what he neglected before in obedience to Allah and asking Him for removal: "My Lord, return" to the world, return me to it, "Perhaps I may do righteousness," meaning: so that I may do righteous deeds in what I left behind before today of deeds I have neglected and neglected. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Zayd and Ibn Jurayj.
