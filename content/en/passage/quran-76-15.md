## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 15-16 as a single unit. He interprets the phrase “And its shade is close upon them, and its fruits are made easily accessible” as meaning that God, may He be exalted, means by His words, “And its shade is close upon them,” that the shade of its trees is near to them. There are several possible grammatical cases for “close” (daniyah): one is that it is connected to the phrase “reclining therein,” and the second is that it is connected to the phrase “they will not see therein any sun,” since the latter is in the accusative case, meaning: reclining therein on couches, not seeing any sun therein. The third interpretation is that it is used for praise, as if it were said: "Reclining therein on couches, and its shade close upon them," just as one might say, "So-and-so has a beautiful maidservant," and "a young, fresh young woman." This "waw" (و) implies a verb governing the noun "young woman" when praise is intended, not mere rhyme. "Daniyah" (دانية) is feminine because "shade" (ظلال) is plural. The passage also includes statements attributed to Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd, and Sufyan.
