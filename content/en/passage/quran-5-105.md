## Summary
Verse 105 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 105 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "O you who believe, you are responsible for your own souls; whoever goes astray cannot harm you if you are guided": believers, take care of your own souls, put them right, work for their deliverance from God's punishment and look to what brings them near their Lord, for "whoever goes astray", whoever disbelieves and takes a way other than the way of truth, cannot harm you if you are guided, believe in your Lord and obey Him in what He commanded and forbade, forbidding what He forbade and permitting what He permitted. The accusative of "your souls" is the accusative of urging, and the Arabs urge with prepositions and adverbs such as "alayka", "indaka", "dunaka" and "ilayka". The interpreters differ on its meaning. Some said it means: take care of your own souls when you have commanded the right and forbidden the wrong and it has not been accepted from you. Report 12848, from al-Hasan: this verse was read to Ibn Mas'ud, who said: this is not its time; say it as long as it is accepted from you, and when it is rejected by you, take care of your own souls (the editor gives notes on Sawwar ibn Abd Allah and Abu al-Ashhab). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 138}

Reports 12849 and 12850, from al-Hasan, are similar: a man said to Ibn Mas'ud, has God not said "O you who believe, you are responsible for your own souls"? He said: this is not its time; say it as long as it is accepted from you, and when it is rejected by you, take care of your own souls (the editor traces the report in al-Haythami and notes that al-Hasan al-Basri did not hear from Ibn Mas'ud). Report 12851, from Sufyan ibn Uqal: Ibn Umar was told, if only you would sit out these days and neither command nor forbid, for God says "you are responsible for your own souls; whoever goes astray cannot harm you if you are guided"! Ibn Umar said: it is not meant for me or my companions, because the messenger of God said, let the one present convey it to the one absent, and we were the present and you are the absent; this verse is for people who will come after us, who if they speak will not be heeded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 139}

We stop here at the foot of p. 139, in the middle of the commentary on verse 105, after report 12851 from Ibn Umar.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
