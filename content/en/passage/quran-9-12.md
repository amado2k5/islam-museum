**Commentary on verse 12 (pp. 153-157)**

Al-Tabari says: if these polytheists you covenanted with for the safety of Quraysh break their covenants after having bound themselves not to fight you or back any of your enemies against you, and "attack your religion", that is slander Islam, reviling and finding fault with it, then fight the leaders of unbelief in God, for they have no oaths, that is no covenant, "so that they may desist", that is be made to stop attacking your religion and backing others against you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 153}

The interpreters differed over who is meant by the leaders of unbelief. Some said they are Abu Jahl ibn Hisham, Utba ibn Rabi'a, Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and their like, while Hudhayfa used to say its people had not yet come. Report 16520, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: it means the covenant polytheists, whom He called leaders of unbelief, as they were; God tells His prophet that if they break the covenant between him and them he is to fight them, they being leaders of unbelief with no oaths. The editor says he kept the manuscript wording, which the printed text altered. Report 16521, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: among the leaders of unbelief were Abu Jahl ibn Hisham, Umayya ibn Khalaf, Utba ibn Rabi'a, Abu Sufyan and Suhayl ibn Amr, who were the ones who intended to expel him; report 16522 is similar through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 154}

Report 16523, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Bashshar, Ghundar and Shu'ba: Abu Sufyan is among them. Report 16524, from al-Suddi, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Asbat: these are Quraysh; if they break their covenant made on Islam and attack it, fight them. Report 16525, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd: the heads of the polytheists of Mecca. Report 16526, from Qatada, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, Umayya ibn Khalaf, Utba ibn Rabi'a, Abu Jahl ibn Hisham and Suhayl ibn Amr, who broke God's covenant and intended to expel the Messenger; and it is not, he says, as interpreted by the people of doubts, innovations and falsehood against God and His book. The editor explains that he means the Kharijites, who cite this verse to justify fighting those of the qibla who differ from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 155}

Report 16527, from Hudhayfa, through Ibn Waki, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Zayd ibn Wahb: the people of this verse have not yet been fought; reports 16528 and 16529 repeat it through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Habib ibn Hassan, and through Abu al-Sa'ib and al-A'mash. The editor says al-Bukhari gives it at length in other wording, and that Habib ibn Hassan, also called Habib ibn Abi al-Ashras, is a narrator of rejected reports who is abandoned. Report 16530, from Sila ibn Zufar, through Ibn Waki, his father, Sufyan, Isra'il and Abu Ishaq: "no oaths" means no covenant; report 16531, from Mujahid: their oaths are their covenant; report 16532, from al-Suddi: their covenant made on Islam; report 16533, from Ammar ibn Yasir: no covenant; report 16534, from Hudhayfa, through Muhammad ibn Ubayd al-Muharibi, Abu al-Ahwas, Abu Ishaq and Sila: no covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 156}

Al-Tabari says "breaking" originally means undoing, as one says a man "broke the strands of his rope" when he unravelled them, and "oaths" is the plural of the oath. The reciters differed over the words "they have no oaths": the reciters of the Hijaz, Iraq and elsewhere read it with a fatha, meaning they have no covenants; it is reported that al-Hasan al-Basri read it with a kasra, meaning they have no Islam, and that his reading could carry another sense, that they are to be given no security, that is do not grant them safety but kill them wherever found, taking the noun from "I granted him security". He then says the correct reading, which he does not allow reciting otherwise, is that with the fatha, because of the consensus of the authoritative reciters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 157}
