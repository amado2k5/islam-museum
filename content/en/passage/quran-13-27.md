**Commentary on verse 27 (pp. 431-432)**

On "those who disbelieve say: why has no sign been sent down to him from his Lord? Say: God leads astray whom He wills and guides to Himself whoever turns to Him", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: the polytheists of your people say to you, Muhammad, why was no sign sent down to you from your Lord, either an angel to be with you as a warner, or a treasure cast to you? Say: God leads astray whom He wills among you, people, abandoning him from believing me and believing what I brought you from my Lord; "and guides to Himself whoever turns", returning to repentance from his disbelief and to faith in Him, enabling him to follow me and believe me in what I brought you from his Lord. The straying of whoever of you strays is not because no sign was sent down to me from my Lord, nor is the guidance of whoever is guided among you because one was sent down; that is in God's hand: He enables whom He wills among you to believe and abandons whom He wills so that he does not believe. I explained the meaning of "inaba" in several places in this book with evidence, which spares repeating it here; in a note the editor says al-Tabari did not in fact treat "inaba" with evidence in the two nearby places he refers to, and seems to have taken what is coming to have been already covered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 431}

Report 20357, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and guides to Himself whoever turns", that is, whoever repents and comes forward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 432}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
