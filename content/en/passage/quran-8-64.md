**Commentary on verse 64 (p. 48)**

Al-Tabari says: God says to His Prophet Muhammad, "Prophet, God is enough for you", and for those of the believers who follow you, God. He says to them: rise up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 48}

The sentence continues: rise against your enemy, for God suffices you against them; do not let their large number and your small number alarm you, for God will strengthen you with His help. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "enough". Report 16265, from al-Sha'bi, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Mu'ammal ibn Isma'il, Sufyan and Shudhab Abu Mu'adh: God is enough for you and God is enough for those of the believers who follow you; report 16266 from him: God is enough for you and for those with you, through Ahmad ibn Uthman al-Awdi and Ubayd Allah ibn Musa; report 16267, from Amir, the same except that it says "and for those who were present with you", through Ibn Waki. Report 16268, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: God is enough for you and for them. The editor gives the biography of Shudhab Abu Mu'adh, freedman of al-Bara ibn Azib, and corrects his name in the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 49}

On this reading from al-Sha'bi, the "whoever" in "and whoever follows you of the believers" is accusative, joined to the sense of the "you" in "God is enough for you", not its form, since in form it is in the genitive but in sense accusative, the meaning being: God suffices you and suffices those who follow you of the believers. Some of the grammarians, namely al-Farra in Ma'ani al-Qur'an, said it is nominative, joined to the name "God", as if he had said "God is enough for you, and those of the believers who follow you in fighting the enemy, not those of them who sit back from you", and he cited "urge the believers to fight". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 50}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
