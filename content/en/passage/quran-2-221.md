## In al-Tabari's commentary

The interpreters differed on this verse, "do not marry polytheist women until they believe": was it revealed intending every polytheist woman, or was some of them intended by its ruling and not others, and was anything of it abrogated after the ruling became binding? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 362}

Some said it was revealed to forbid marriage to every polytheist woman for every Muslim, of whatever kind of polytheism, whether an idol worshipper, Jewish, Christian, Magian or other, then the prohibition on marrying People of the Book was abrogated by the verse of al-Ma'ida (5:4-5), which permitted the chaste women of those given the Book before you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 362} From Ibn 'Abbas: marriage to polytheist women was forbidden, then he excepted the women of the People of the Book and made them lawful when you give them their dowries (4212); the editor corrects the narrator's name in the chain to 'Ali ibn Dawud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 362} From 'Ikrima and al-Hasan: the women of the People of the Book were abrogated out of it and made lawful for Muslims (4213); from Mujahid: they are the women of Mecca and other polytheists, and then the women of the People of the Book were made lawful (4214, 4215); from al-Rabi': God forbade polytheist women in this verse then sent down al-Ma'ida and excepted the women of the People of the Book (4216). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 363}

Others said it was revealed with its ruling intended for the polytheist women of the Arabs, nothing of it abrogated or excepted, a verse general in its wording and specific in its meaning. From Qatada: it means the Arab polytheist women who have no scripture they read (4217). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 363}

From Qatada (via 'Abd al-Razzaq): the polytheist women are those not of the People of the Book, and Hudhayfa married a Jewish or Christian woman (4218); the editor says this is Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman, the Companion who knew the Prophet's list of hypocrites; from Qatada: it means the Arab polytheist women who have no scripture they read (4219); from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the idol-worshipping women (4220). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 363} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 364}

Others said it was revealed intending every polytheist woman of whatever kind of polytheism, idol worshipper, Magian or one of the Book, with nothing abrogated. From Ibn 'Abbas by Shahr ibn Hawshab: the Prophet forbade classes of women except believing emigrants, and forbade every woman holding a religion other than Islam; Talha ibn 'Ubaydallah had married a Jewish woman and Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman a Christian, and 'Umar became so angry that he nearly set upon them; they said, we will divorce them, Commander of the Believers, do not be angry; he said, if divorcing them is lawful then marrying them is lawful, but I shall take them from you humbled and abased (4221). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 364} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 365} The editor's note: 'Abd al-Hamid ibn Bahram was held trustworthy by Abu Dawud and Ibn Ma'in, and Shu'ba said he is truthful except that he narrates from Shahr, and they faulted his many narrations from Shahr, who is weak; Ibn Kathir after relating it said it is very strange (gharib jiddan) and the report is strange from 'Umar; and he explains "humbled and abased" as lowliness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 365}

Al-Tabari says the soundest of these views is Qatada's, that God meant by "do not marry polytheist women" those of them not of the People of the Book, and that the verse is general in its wording and specific in its meaning, with nothing of it abrogated, the women of the People of the Book not being included in it, since in al-Ma'ida God permitted the chaste women of those given the Book. He has set out elsewhere in his book that when two verses or reports are such that one negates the other's ruling in the nature of reason, neither may be judged to abrogate the other except by proof from a report that leaves no excuse; none exists here, so to say "this abrogates that" is a claim without proof, and arbitrary assertion is something anyone can do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 365} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 366} (The editor praises al-Tabari's arguments here as those of one perceptive in meanings and supported by reason.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 366}

As for what is reported from Shahr ibn Hawshab, from Ibn 'Abbas, from 'Umar, of his separating Talha and Hudhayfa from their two wives of the People of the Book, it has no meaning, since it contradicts what the community agrees is lawful by God's Book and His messenger's report. The opposite is related from 'Umar by a sounder chain, from Zayd ibn Wahb: 'Umar said, a Muslim may marry a Christian woman, but a Christian may not marry a Muslim woman (4222). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 366} The note says the chain is sound and connected to 'Umar, al-Bayhaqi relates it and Ibn Kathir grades its chain sound. 'Umar disliked Talha and Hudhayfa marrying Jewish and Christian women out of fear that people would follow them and lose interest in Muslim women, or for another reason, and told them to release them; from Shaqiq: Hudhayfa married a Jewish woman and 'Umar wrote, let her go; he wrote back, do you claim she is forbidden, that I should release her? 'Umar said, I do not claim she is forbidden, but I fear you would take up the prostitutes among them (4223). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 366} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 367} The note: al-Salt ibn Bahram is trustworthy; al-Bayhaqi relates it, Ibn Kathir cites it and grades the chain sound; and the word "mu'minat" (believing women) in the printed edition, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti is a corruption of "mumisat" (prostitutes), as in al-Bayhaqi, al-Jassas and al-Qurtubi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 367}

From Jabir ibn 'Abdallah: the Messenger of God said, we marry the women of the People of the Book and they do not marry ours (4224). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 367} Al-Tabari says that although there is something in this report's chain, following it, because all agree it is sound to hold, is better than the report of 'Abd al-Hamid ibn Bahram from Shahr ibn Hawshab. So the sense is: do not marry, you believers, polytheist women other than the People of the Book until they believe and affirm God, His messenger and what was sent down to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 367} The editor notes he found the report in no hadith collection besides this place; that Ibn Kathir quotes al-Tabari's remark and adds "so said Ibn Jarir"; that al-Tabari's "although there is something in its chain" perhaps alludes to the view that al-Hasan did not hear from Jabir, whereas the editor holds Hisham ibn Hassan's narration enough to establish that he did; and that its meaning is confirmed from Jabir as his own words (mawquf) in al-Shafi'i's al-Umm and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 367} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 368}

On "a believing slave woman is better than a polytheist woman": it means a slave woman who believes in God, His messenger and what he brought is better with God and more excellent than a free polytheist woman, though her lineage is noble and her origin honourable; that is, do not seek marriages among the noble women of the polytheists, for Muslim slave women are better to marry in God's sight. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 368} It is reported that the verse was revealed about a man who married a slave woman and was reproached for it, and a free polytheist woman was offered to him. From al-Suddi: it was revealed about 'Abdallah ibn Rawaha, who had a black slave woman; he was angry with her and slapped her, then took fright and went to the Prophet and told him about her; the Prophet said, what is she, 'Abdallah? He said, she fasts, prays, performs ablution well and testifies that there is no god but God and that you are His messenger; he said, she is a believer; 'Abdallah said, by the One who sent you with the truth, I shall free her and marry her, and did so; some Muslims criticised him and said, he married a slave woman, since they wanted to marry into the polytheists and have them marry theirs for the sake of their lineage, and the verse came down about them (4225). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 368} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 369} From Ibn Juraij: the polytheist women, because of their nobility (4226). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 369}

On "even if she pleases you": it means that even if a polytheist woman who is not of the People of the Book pleases you in beauty, lineage and wealth, do not marry her, for a believing slave woman is better with God than she; "law" is used in place of "in" because their sense and construction are close. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 369}
