## Summary
Verse 186 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 186 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Opening of the verse (p. 454).** Al-Tabari says the testing in wealth means trial by misfortunes in wealth, and in persons the trial by the loss of kinsmen and clan, among the people of support and community; the sentence continues on the next page. The notes refer to earlier explanations of the word for testing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 454}

**The rest of the commentary (p. 455).** Al-Tabari says "selves" here means the loss of kinsmen and clan among the people of support and community; those given the Book before them are the Jews and what they ascribed to God of fabrication and of other sayings, and the polytheists are the Christians; the harm from the Jews is what he mentioned, and from the Christians their saying about the Messiah and the like of their unbelief. By patience and fear of God he understands patience with God's command concerning them and others, and fear of God in what He commanded and forbade, which is among what God resolved and commanded. The notes refer to the explanations of "selves" (volume 6) and "harm" (volume 4), and explain the verb meaning to act on one's own before consulting: whoever does something without you and goes on with it without consulting you has forestalled you, from the word for forestalling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 455}

**First view: Finhas (pp. 455-456, report 8316).** Al-Tabari says it was said that all this came down about Finhas the Jew, chief of Banu Qaynuqa'. 'Ikrima (8316), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj: the verse came down about the Prophet, Abu Bakr and Finhas; the Prophet sent Abu Bakr to Finhas to ask his help with a letter, and told Abu Bakr not to act on his own before returning. Abu Bakr came girded with his sword and gave him the letter; when he read it he said "your Lord has come to need you to supply Him"! Abu Bakr was about to strike him with the sword, then remembered the Prophet's word and held back, and [[passage/quran-3-180|3:180]] came down. What lies between the two verses, up to [[passage/quran-3-184|3:184]], came down about Banu Qaynuqa'. Ibn Jurayj: it consoles His Prophet. Then, on this verse, God informed the believers that He would test them to see how they bore up in their religion, and that they would hear much harm from Jews and Christians, such as the Jews' saying about 'Uzayr ([[passage/quran-9-30|9:30]]) and the Christians' about the Messiah, and the Muslims would set up war when they heard their associating partners with God; God told them that patience and fear of God are among what He resolved and commanded, that is, among firmness. The first note refers to Finhas's story in reports 8300-8302; the second says the printed edition has a waw where the manuscript's letter looks like a dal, and the editor chose "when" as the right sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 456}

**Second view: Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf (pp. 456-458, report 8317).** Others said it came down about Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, who lampooned the Prophet and wrote love verses about Muslim women. Al-Zuhri (8317), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar: it is Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, who in his poetry incited the polytheists against the Prophet and his companions and lampooned the Prophet; five men of the Ansar went to him, among them Muhammad ibn Maslama and a man called Abu 'Abs (printed on the next page as Abu 'Atbas). They came to him in his people's gathering at al-'Awali, and he was startled; they said they had come on a need, and he asked one of them to come near and tell him; the man said they had come to sell him armour to spend the proceeds, and Ka'b said they must be in hardship since this man arrived among them. They agreed to come in the evening when people had quieted, came and called him, and his wife said they had not come at this hour for anything he liked; he said they had told him their story. The fourth note says this begins another report's wording, cut off from al-Zuhri's report, which is incomplete, and the compiler completed it with 'Ikrima's report inserted into its sequence. Ma'mar said: Ayyub told me from 'Ikrima that he looked down on them and spoke to them, and they wanted to sell him dates; he asked them to pledge their sons, and they said they were ashamed that their sons be taunted as the pledge for one load or two; he asked for their women, and they said he was the most handsome of men and they did not trust him, but would pledge their weapons, since he knew their need of them; he said bring your weapons. They said come down so that we take from you and you from us. He went to come down and his wife clung to him, telling him to send for others like them from his people; he said if they found him asleep they would not wake him; she said speak to them from above the house, but he refused. He came down with his scent wafting; they asked what it was, and he said the perfume of his wife. One of them drew near smelling it, then embraced him and told his companions to kill him; Abu 'Atbas stabbed him in the flank and Muhammad ibn Maslama struck him with the sword, and they killed him and returned. The Jews woke terrified and came to the Prophet saying their chief had been killed by treachery; the Prophet reminded them of what Ka'b had done, how he had urged the fight against them and harmed them, then invited them to have a peace agreement written between him and them; the document, it says, was with 'Ali. The notes on p. 457 explain: al-'Awali is the plural of the upland, a name for all of Medina's villages and buildings on the Najd side, with the lowland on the Tihama side, about four miles from Medina by one count, three by another, and eight at the farthest; to spend with money means to make it spending to meet one's need and one's family's; people quieted means they were still and asleep, and the manuscript has the form without the hamza, which the editor calls good and supports with lines of Ibn Harma about beasts of prey growing calm over their prey (described, not quoted); a load is a known measure, said to be a camel-load or sixty measures by the Prophet's measure; and the phrase "he went to come down" means he moved to come down, "went" being one of the helping words added to a sentence to depict motion or specify an act, not meaning actual going but the continuation of the act, a use the editor says is frequent though the dictionaries omit it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 458}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 458}
