## Summary
Verse 31 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 31 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The first view on the major sins: what is forbidden from the start of the sura to verse thirty (p. 233, reports 9168-9172).** Abu Jafar says the people of interpretation differed over the meaning of the major sins whose avoidance God promised to reward with the covering of the rest of their sins. Some said they are what God forbade His servants from the start of Surat al-Nisa to the head of its thirtieth verse. Ibn Bashshar (9168, from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from al-A'mash from Abu al-Duha from Masruq) reports from Abd Allah that the major sins are from the start of the sura to thirty of it; Ibn Bashshar (9169, from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Hammad from Ibrahim) reports the same from Abd Allah; al-Muthanna (9170, from Hajjaj from Hammad from Ibrahim) reports the same from Ibn Mas'ud; Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i (9171, from Waki from al-A'mash from Ibrahim from Alqama) reports from Abd Allah that the major sins run from the start of the sura to the place of this verse; al-Rifa'i (9172, from Abu Mu'awiya and Abu Khalid from al-A'mash from Ibrahim from Alqama) reports from Abd Allah to the same effect, and the wording of this report breaks off at the foot of the page and continues on the next.

**End of the first view: reports 9172-9178 and the editor's tracing (p. 234).** Report 9172 ends by saying that the range Ibn Mas'ud meant runs up to the place of this verse itself. Abu al-Sa'ib (9173, from Abu Mu'awiya from al-A'mash from Muslim) reports from Masruq that Abd Allah was asked about the major sins and said they lie between the opening of Surat al-Nisa and the head of thirty. Ibn Humayd (9174, from Jarir from Mughira from Hammad) reports the same from Ibrahim from Ibn Mas'ud, adding that he counted thirty verses of it. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9175, from Hushaym from Mughira) reports the same from Ibrahim from Abd Allah; Yaqub (9176, from Ibn Ulayya from Ibn Awn) reports from Ibrahim that they used to hold the major sins to lie between the start of the sura and this place. Al-Muthanna (9177, from Adam al-Asqalani from Shu'ba from Asim b. Abi al-Najud from Zirr b. Hubaysh) reports from Ibn Mas'ud that the major sins run from the start of the sura to thirty verses of it, after which Ibn Mas'ud recited the verse. Report 9178 (as printed, al-Muthanna from Ibn Waki from Mis'ar from Asim from Zirr) reports from Abd Allah the same. Editor Shakir notes that reports 9168-9178 are one report with many chains, recorded by al-Haythami in Majma al-Zawa'id (4:7), who says al-Bazzar narrated it and that its men are the men of the Sahih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 234}

**The second view: the major sins are seven (p. 235, report 9179).** Others said the major sins are seven. Tamim b. al-Muntasir (9179, from Yazid from Muhammad b. Ishaq from Muhammad b. Sahl b. Abi Hathma) reports from his father that he was in the mosque of Kufa while Ali was addressing the people from the pulpit, and Ali said the major sins are seven, repeating it three times until the people listened closely, and then asked them whether they would ask him about them. When they did, he listed associating partners with God, killing the soul God made sacred, slandering a chaste woman, consuming an orphan's property, consuming usury, fleeing on the day of the battle line, and returning to the desert life after the migration. Muhammad b. Sahl says he asked his father what that last one was and how it came to be in this list, and his father answered that nothing is graver than a man who migrated and then, when his share fell among the spoils and fighting became obligatory on him, cast that off his neck and went back to being a Bedouin as before. In the footnotes the editor says the printed edition and Ibn Kathir word the mention of Ali differently and he kept the manuscript's wording; that the two forms of address to the father mean the same; that 'ta'arrub' means going back to desert dwelling like the Arabs of the desert; and that a phrase in the manuscript is written confusedly, so he left the printed wording since it agrees with Ibn Kathir. He also gives a note on Muhammad b. Sahl b. Abi Hathma al-Ansari, who narrated from his father and his uncle, is in al-Bukhari's al-Tarikh al-Kabir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Ta'jil al-Manfa'a; al-Bukhari mentions no criticism of him, and Ibn Hibban lists him among the trustworthy. Al-Bukhari alluded to this report in his entry, and Ibn Kathir (2:422) traces it, quoting Ibn Mardawayh's version that goes back to the Prophet through Ibn Lahi'a from Ziyad b. Abi Habib from Muhammad b. Sahl from his father; Ibn Kathir adds that its chain needs scrutiny, that raising it to the Prophet is a gross error, and that the correct form is what Ibn Jarir narrated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 235}

**Reports 9180 and 9181: seven, each with a verse (pp. 235-237).** Muhammad b. Ubayd al-Muharibi (9180, from Abu al-Ahwas Sallam b. Sulaym from Ibn Ishaq) reports from Ubayd b. Umayr that the major sins are seven and each has a verse in God's Book: associating partners ([[passage/quran-22-31|22:31]]), consuming orphans' property ([[passage/quran-4-10|4:10]]), usury ([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), defaming believing women of good standing who are unaware ([[passage/quran-24-23|24:23]]), fleeing from the battle line ([[passage/quran-8-15|8:15]]), returning to the desert after migration ([[passage/quran-47-25|47:25]]), and killing a soul. Ibn Humayd (9181, from Jarir from Mansur from Ibn Ishaq) reports from Ubayd b. Umayr al-Laythi that the major sins are seven, and he pairs each with a passage: association ([[passage/quran-22-31|22:31]]), killing ([[passage/quran-4-93|4:93]]), usury ([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), orphans' property ([[passage/quran-4-10|4:10]]), slander of the chaste ([[passage/quran-24-23|24:23]]), flight from the battle line ([[passage/quran-8-16|8:16]]), and the one who turns back to the desert after migrating ([[passage/quran-47-25|47:25]]). A footnote begins at the foot of p. 236 and finishes on the next page: it gives the biography of Muhammad b. Ubayd b. Muhammad b. Waqid al-Muharibi, Abu Jafar al-Nahhas of Kufa, a teacher of al-Tabari, from whom Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi, Abu Hatim and others narrated; al-Nasa'i said there is no harm in him and Ibn Hibban lists him among the trustworthy. It also covers Abu al-Ahwas Sallam b. Sulaym; Ibn Ishaq, who is Muhammad b. Ishaq; and Ubayd b. Umayr b. Qatada al-Laythi, who narrated from his father and from Umar, Ali, Ubayy b. Ka'b, Abu Musa, Abu Hurayra and others, and is a trustworthy senior Successor. The editor says the printed edition wrote his name wrongly and the manuscript is right, and refers to his note on report 9189. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 237}

**Reports 9182-9184: the statement of Ubayda (p. 237).** Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9182, from Ibn Ulayya from Ibn Awn) reports from Muhammad b. Sirin that he asked Ubayda about the major sins and he listed associating partners with God, killing the soul God made sacred without right, flight from the battle line, consuming an orphan's property, usury and false slander, adding that people also mention the desert life after migration. Ibn Awn asked Muhammad about sorcery and he answered that false slander gathers much evil. Abu Kurayb (9183, from Hushaym from Mansur and Hisham) reports from Ibn Sirin from Ubayda that the major sins are association, forbidden killing, usury, slander of the chaste, an orphan's property, flight from the battle line and the apostate who becomes a Bedouin after migrating. Report 9184 (Yaqub from Hushaym from Hisham from Ibn Sirin from Ubayda) is to the same effect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 237}

**The ground of this view: report 9185 (pp. 237-238).** Abu Jafar gives as the ground of those who held the number seven the report of al-Muthanna (9185, from Abu Salih from al-Layth from Khalid from Sa'id b. Abi Hilal) from Nu'aym al-Mujmir from Suhayb, client of al-Utwari, who heard Abu Hurayra and Abu Sa'id al-Khudri say that the Messenger of God addressed them one day and swore three times, then bowed his head, and every man of them bowed his, weeping and not knowing what he had sworn on. Then he raised his head with joy on his face, which was dearer to them than red camels, and said that no servant who prays the five prayers, fasts Ramadan, pays the alms and avoids the seven major sins will fail to have the gates of Paradise opened for him, and he is then told to go in, greeted with safety. Editor Shakir says in the footnote that the chain is sound; that Khalid is Ibn Yazid al-Misri; that Nu'aym b. Abd Allah al-Mujmir of Medina, client of the family of Umar b. al-Khattab, is a trustworthy Successor whom the authors of the main collections used; that Suhayb, client of al-Utwari, is a trustworthy Medinan Successor; that al-Utwari is a nisba to Utwara, a clan of Kinana, as Ibn al-Athir says; and that Ibn Kathir's print has a misprinted form. He records that al-Bukhari gave the report briefly in his entry on Suhayb, that al-Nasa'i narrated it, that al-Hakim gave it in his Mustadrak and called it sound on the condition of the two shaykhs although they did not record it, that Ibn Hibban has it in his Sahih by another route, and that al-Suyuti adds Ibn Majah, Ibn Khuzayma and al-Bayhaqi. He also explains the words: to bow the head is to lower it and look at the ground; 'al-na'am' is camels and similar livestock, here camels; and red camels are the best of camels and the most patient in the midday heat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 238}

**Report 9186: the statement of Ata (p. 238).** Al-Muthanna (9186, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Ata that the major sins are seven: killing a soul, usury, consuming an orphan's property, slandering the chaste, false witness, disobedience to parents and flight from the battle line. As transmitted, this list differs from the earlier ones in including false witness and disobedience to parents and leaving out association and the return to desert life. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 238}

**The third view: the major sins are nine (pp. 239-240, report 9187).** Others said they are nine. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9187, from Ibn Ulayya from Ziyad b. Mikhraq) reports from Taysala b. Mayyas: 'I was with the Najadat and committed sins I took to be among the major sins, so I met Ibn Umar and told him; he asked what they were and I told him, and he said that is not among the major sins; they are nine, and I shall count them for you: associating partners with God, killing a soul unlawfully, flight from the battle line, slandering the chaste, usury, consuming an orphan's property wrongfully, profaning the Sacred Mosque, the one who seeks sorcery, and the weeping of parents through disobedience.' Ziyad says Taysala added that when Ibn Umar saw his fear he asked whether he feared the Fire and wished to enter Paradise and whether his parents were alive; Taysala said his mother was with him, and Ibn Umar said that if he softened his speech to her and fed her he would enter Paradise as long as he avoided the certain-damnations. In the footnotes the editor says he printed 'I committed' in all three places because it suits the sense, the manuscript at the first place and Ibn Kathir; that the print dropped the word at one point and he restored it from the manuscript; and that the manuscript's wording after 'that is not among the major sins' is the right one, meaning the sin Taysala did not name is not a major sin. He writes at length on the word for 'seeks sorcery': the printed edition, al-Bukhari's al-Adab al-Mufrad and Ibn Kathir read it with a different letter that would mean 'mocks', which he says is not a major sin and not found in any report; the manuscript and al-Durr al-Manthur have an undotted letter, and he judges the reading 'seeks sorcery', meaning asking a sorcerer to work it or teach it, to be right, because the reports name sorcery among the major sins, comparing the formation of a verb meaning to ask a diviner to cast pebbles; he admits the verb is absent from the dictionaries available to him. The footnotes on p. 240 add that fear is intense dread; that the chain of 9187 is sound; that Ziyad b. Mikhraq al-Muzani of Basra is trustworthy (Ibn Ma'in and al-Nasa'i); that Taysala b. Mayyas is Taysala b. Ali al-Nahdi of the next chain, his father being Ali and his nickname Mayyas, and al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim hold them one man whose correct nisba is al-Bahdali; that al-Bukhari recorded it in al-Adab al-Mufrad and Ibn Kathir mentioned it; and that al-Suyuti gave it in abridged and slightly corrupt form adding Ibn Rahawayh, Abd b. Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Qadi Ismail. The Najadat were a Kharijite (Haruri) group named after Najda b. Amir al-Haruri al-Hanafi, according to Lisan al-Arab; the print had a misreading of the name, the manuscript's form is undotted, and the right form stands in al-Adab al-Mufrad and the Azhari manuscript of Ibn Kathir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 240}

**Reports 9188 and 9189 (pp. 240-241).** Sulayman b. Thabit al-Jazzar al-Wasiti (9188, from Sallam b. Salam from Ayyub b. Utba) reports from Taysala b. Ali al-Nahdi: 'I came to Ibn Umar in the shade of an arak tree on the day of Arafa as he poured water over his head and face and asked him about the major sins; he said they are nine: associating partners with God, slandering the chaste (and when I asked whether that came before killing he said yes), killing a believing soul, flight from the battle line, sorcery, usury, an orphan's property, disobedience to Muslim parents, and profaning the Sacred House, your direction of prayer alive and dead.' Sulayman b. Thabit (9189, by the same chain to Ayyub b. Utba) reports from Yahya from Ubayd b. Umayr from his father from the Prophet the like of it, except that he began with killing before slander. In the footnotes the editor says the manuscript wrote the word for profaning in a form that the print wrote with the article, and that the copyist seems to have joined a letter to the alif; that 9188 is another chain for the preceding report; that he did not know who Sulayman b. Thabit al-Jazzar al-Wasiti, al-Tabari's teacher, is and found no biography, Ibn Kathir's print giving another form; that Sallam b. Salam is Abu al-Musayyab al-Wasiti with no criticism recorded; and that Ayyub b. Utba, Abu Yahya, judge of al-Yamama, is weak, as Ahmad, al-Bukhari and others held. He adds that Ibn Kathir mentions the report and that Ali b. al-Ja'd narrated it from Ayyub at length; that Ibn Hajar in al-Tahdhib points to other routes from Ayyub from Taysala; that Ayyub was not alone in it since Ikrima b. Ammar al-Ijli, a trustworthy man, also narrated it from Taysala, as al-Bukhari indicates briefly; and that al-Suyuti was too brief, attributing it only to Ali b. al-Ja'd. In the note on report 9189 he says Yahya is Ibn Abi Kathir, and that the manuscript and print both have 'Yahya b. Ubayd b. Umayr', a slip of the copyists turning 'from' into 'son of'.

**End of the note on report 9189 (p. 242).** The editor says the chain here lost Abd al-Hamid b. Sinan between Yahya b. Abi Kathir and Ubayd b. Umayr, and that this is not the copyists' doing but the error of Ayyub b. Utba. Ubayd b. Umayr al-Laythi is a well-known senior Successor and his father Umayr b. Qatada al-Laythi a Companion who was present at the conquest of Mecca and the farewell pilgrimage. He records that al-Hakim gave the report at length in the Mustadrak by way of Harb b. Shaddad from Yahya b. Abi Kathir from Abd al-Hamid b. Sinan from Ubayd b. Umayr from his father; al-Hakim said the two shaykhs relied on this report's narrators apart from Abd al-Hamid, and al-Dhahabi replied that they did not rely on Abd al-Hamid because he is unknown, though Ibn Hibban held him trustworthy; al-Hakim gave it again, calling the chain sound though the two did not record it, and al-Dhahabi agreed. He adds that al-Mizzi gave it in Tahdhib al-Kamal with two chains through Harb, that Abu Dawud gave it without its whole wording, Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Istiab briefly, and al-Nasa'i in abridged form with seven in place of nine. Ibn Kathir cites it from al-Hakim's first report, quotes al-Bukhari's remark that there is something to examine in Abd al-Hamid's report and Ibn Hibban's inclusion of him among the trustworthy, and then points to al-Tabari's version, which omits Abd al-Hamid. The editor takes this to show the omission is Ayyub b. Utba's confusion and not the copyists'. He also notes that Ibn Abi Hatim has an entry on Abd al-Hamid without criticism, which he counts as an endorsement, and that al-Suyuti also attributes the report to al-Tabarani and Ibn Mardawayh. Some page references in the note are smudged in the printed edition and are not given here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 242}

**The fourth view: the major sins are four (pp. 242-244, reports 9190-9200).** Others said four. Ibn Humayd (9190, from Hukkam b. Salm from Anbasa from Mutarrif from Wabara) reports from Ibn Mas'ud that they are associating partners with God, losing hope of relief from God, despair of His mercy, and feeling secure against His plan. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9191, from Hushaym from Mutarrif from Wabara b. Abd al-Rahman from Abu al-Tufayl) reports from Abd Allah b. Mas'ud that the greatest of the major sins are these, as does Abu Kurayb (9192, from Abu Mu'awiya from al-A'mash from Wabara) with the others in a different order; Abu Kurayb and Abu al-Sa'ib (9193, from Ibn Idris from Mutarrif from Wabara from Abu al-Tufayl) have the statement that they are four. Muhammad b. Umara al-Asadi (9194, from Abd Allah from Shayban from al-A'mash from Wabara from Abu al-Tufayl) heard Ibn Mas'ud say that the greatest is associating partners with God, and (9195, from Abd Allah from Israil from Abu Ishaq from Wabara from Abu al-Tufayl) reports the like. Ibn al-Muthanna (9196, its printed number smudged and read from the sequence and the editor's footnote, from Wahb b. Jarir from Shu'ba from Abd al-Malik from Abu al-Tufayl) reports from Abd Allah that the four are feeling secure against God's plan, losing hope of relief, and despair of mercy, with association first. By the same chain to Shu'ba, report 9197 (from al-Qasim b. Abi Bazza from Abu al-Tufayl) is the same, and Ibn al-Muthanna (9198, from Muhammad b. Ja'far from Shu'ba from al-Qasim) is to the like effect. Ibn Humayd (9199, from Jarir from Abd al-Aziz b. Rufay from Abu al-Tufayl) reports from Ibn Mas'ud four that are association, killing the soul God made sacred, feeling secure and losing hope of relief, so killing stands there in place of despair. Ibn Waki (9200, from his father from al-Mas'udi from Furat al-Qazzaz from Abu al-Tufayl) reports from Abd Allah the other four in a different order. In the footnotes the editor says the Abd al-Malik of 9196 is Abd al-Malik b. Sa'id b. Hayyan b. Abjar, known as Ibn Abjar, a trustworthy and firm narrator who followed the sunna and narrated from Abu al-Tufayl Amir b. Wathila, and that the manuscript and print both wrote 'Abd al-Malik b. Abi al-Tufayl', an evident error; that Furat al-Qazzaz is Furat b. Abi Abd al-Rahman al-Qazzaz al-Tamimi, trustworthy, who narrated from Abu al-Tufayl and others and from whom his son al-Hasan, Shu'ba and al-Mas'udi narrated; that al-Tabari gave this report from Ibn Mas'ud by many routes, Ibn Kathir (2:422) citing one and calling it sound to Ibn Mas'ud beyond doubt; that al-Suyuti (2:147) also credits Abd al-Razzaq, Abd b. Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, al-Tabarani and Ibn Abi al-Dunya in his book on repentance; and that Ibn Kathir also gives it from Ibn Abbas as a report back to the Prophet, saying its chain needs scrutiny and that it is more likely a statement of the Companion only. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 244}

**The fifth view: whatever God forbade is a major sin (pp. 244-246, reports 9201-9210).** Others said whatever God forbade is a major sin. Abu Kurayb (9201, from Hushaym from Mansur from Ibn Sirin) reports from Ibn Abbas that the major sins were mentioned in his presence and he said this. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9202, from Ibn Ulayya from Ayyub) reports from Muhammad that he was told Ibn Abbas used to say it, and that when the glance was mentioned he said it is the look. Then come reports from Ibn Abbas that the number exceeds seven. Muhammad b. Abd al-A'la (9203, from al-Mu'tamir from his father) reports from Tawus that a man asked Ibn Abbas to tell him the seven major sins and he said they are more than seven and seven, the narrator adding that he does not know how often he said it; Yaqub (9204, from Ibn Ulayya from Sulayman al-Taymi) reports the like from Tawus. Ibn Bashshar (9205, from Muhammad b. Jafar and Ibn Abi Adi from Awf) reports that Abu al-Aliya al-Riyahi stood up and said people say seven while he feared they were seventy or more. Ali (9206, from al-Walid from Abu Amr from al-Zuhri) reports that Ibn Abbas, asked whether they are seven, said they are nearer seventy. Al-Muthanna (9207, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Qays b. Sa'd) reports from Sa'id b. Jubayr that Ibn Abbas, asked by a man, said they are nearer seven hundred than seven, except that no major sin remains with seeking forgiveness and no minor sin with persistence. Ibn Humayd (9208, from Jarir from Layth) reports from Tawus that he said they are nearer seventy than seven. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9209, from Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar from Ibn Tawus) reports from his father that Ibn Abbas, asked if they are seven, said they are nearer seventy. Ahmad b. Hazim (9210, from Abu Nu'aym from Abd Allah b. Sa'dan from Abu al-Walid) reports that he asked Ibn Abbas and he said whatever God is disobeyed in is a major sin. In the footnotes the editor says the manuscript and Ibn Kathir read 'seven and seven' while the print has 'seven and nine' and he kept the manuscript; that the print's 'al-Tamimi' for Sulayman is wrong; and that he did not know Abd Allah b. Sa'dan or Abu al-Walid in 9210 and found nothing on them, and fears distortion or omission in the chain, Ibn Kathir having 'Abd Allah b. Ma'dan', equally unknown to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 246}

**The sixth view: three (p. 246, report 9211).** Others said three. Al-Muthanna (9211, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid from Ibn Mas'ud that the major sins are three: losing hope of relief, despair of mercy and feeling secure against God's plan. The editor refers to the earlier reports from Ibn Mas'ud, 9190-9200. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 246}

**The seventh view: every necessitating act and everything threatened with the Fire (pp. 246-247, reports 9212-9218).** Others said every necessitating sin, and whatever God threatened its people with the Fire for, is a major sin. Al-Muthanna (9212, from Abd Allah b. Salih from Mu'awiya from Ali b. Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas, commenting on this verse, that the major sins are every sin God sealed with the Fire, anger, a curse or punishment. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9213, from Ibn Ulayya from Hisham b. Hassan) reports from Muhammad b. Wasi that Sa'id b. Jubayr said every necessitating threat in the Qur'an is a major sin; Ibn Waki (9214, from his father from Muhammad b. Muhzim from Muhammad b. Wasi al-Azdi) reports from Sa'id b. Jubayr that every sin God ascribed to the Fire is among the major sins; Ali b. Sahl (9215, from al-Walid b. Muslim from Salim) heard al-Hasan say every necessitating threat in the Qur'an is a major sin; Muhammad b. Amr (9216, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid on the verse that they are the necessitating sins, and al-Muthanna (9217, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl) the same; Yahya b. Abi Talib (9218, from Yazid from Juwaybir) reports from al-Dahhak that the major sins are every necessitating sin for which God made the Fire the due of its people and every act for which the fixed penalty is applied. In the note on 9214 the editor says Muhammad b. Muhzim al-Sha'ab, also called al-Rammam, was held trustworthy by Ibn Ma'in and Ibn Hibban and 'no harm in him' by Abu Hatim, and is in al-Bukhari's al-Tarikh al-Kabir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Ta'jil al-Manfa'a; that the manuscript and print wrote a different form of his name, whose correct vocalisation he gives following those who vowelled it; and that al-Bukhari recorded this report in his entry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 247}

**Al-Tabari's choice: what the report from the Prophet establishes (pp. 247-248, report 9219).** Abu Jafar says what he holds on this is what the report from the Messenger of God establishes. Ahmad b. al-Walid al-Qurashi (9219, from Muhammad b. Jafar from Shu'ba from Ubayd Allah b. Abi Bakr) reports from Anas b. Malik that the Prophet mentioned the major sins, or was asked about them, and named associating partners with God, killing and disobedience to parents; then he asked whether he should tell them the greatest of the major sins, and said false speech, or false testimony, Shu'ba adding that he thinks most likely it was false testimony. Yahya b. Habib b. Arabi (9220, from Khalid b. al-Harith from Shu'ba) gives the report from Anas with association, disobedience to parents, killing and false speech, and Ibn al-Muthanna (9221, from Yahya b. Kathir, as printed, from Shu'ba) the like, with the question about the greatest of them answered with false speech. In the note on 9219 the editor says Ubayd Allah b. Abi Bakr b. Anas is a trustworthy Successor who narrates from his grandfather and that Ibn Kathir wrote his name wrongly; that Ahmad in the Musnad, al-Bukhari and Muslim narrated the report by way of Muhammad b. Jafar and al-Bukhari by another route; that Ibn Kathir cites it and credits the two Sahihs; and that al-Suyuti adds Abd b. Humayd, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Abi Hatim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 248}

**Reports 9222-9224 (pp. 248-249).** Muhammad b. al-Muthanna (9222, from Muhammad b. Jafar from Shu'ba from Firas from al-Sha'bi) reports from Abd Allah b. Amr from the Prophet that the greatest of the major sins are associating partners with God, disobedience to parents or killing a soul (Shu'ba being the one in doubt), and the false oath. Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i (9223, from Ubayd Allah b. Musa from Shayban from Firas from al-Sha'bi) reports from Abd Allah b. Amr that a Bedouin came to the Prophet and asked what the major sins are; he answered association, then disobedience to parents, then the false oath; Firas says he asked al-Sha'bi what that oath is and he said it is the oath by which one takes a Muslim man's property while lying in it. In the note the editor says 9222 and 9223 are two chains of one report with the same sense; that Firas b. Yahya al-Hamdani al-Kharifi is trustworthy and used by the authors of the main collections; that Ubayd Allah b. Musa is the hafiz al-Absi, the print writing his name wrongly; that Shayban is the grammarian Abu Mu'awiya b. Abd al-Rahman; and that the report is in Ahmad's Musnad, al-Bukhari, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Abu Nu'aym's Hilya, with Ibn Hajar in the Fath pointing to Ibn Hibban's version and Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti citing it. Al-Muthanna (9224, from Ibn Abi al-Sari Muhammad b. al-Mutawakkil al-Asqalani from Yahya b. Sa'd from Khalid b. Ma'dan from Abu Ruhm) reports from Abu Ayyub al-Ansari that the Messenger of God said whoever establishes the prayer, and the report breaks off there at the foot of the page.

**End of report 9224 and its note (p. 250).** The report from Abu Ayyub al-Ansari is completed: whoever establishes the prayer, pays the alms, fasts Ramadan and avoids the major sins has Paradise, and when asked what the major sins are the Prophet named associating partners with God, disobedience to parents and fleeing on the day of the march. In the note the editor says Ibn Abi al-Sari is Muhammad b. al-Mutawakkil b. Abd al-Rahman, the hafiz of Ascalon, trustworthy according to Ibn Ma'in and others, with a death date that is not clear in this printing; that Yahya b. Sa'id al-Attar al-Ansari al-Himsi was treated earlier (report 5753); that the printed edition had Muhammad b. Sa'd in his place, which he holds to be certainly a corruption, while his reading follows the manuscript, though the word Yahya there is not wholly clear. He allows that the name might be Bujayr b. Sa'd, who did narrate this report, but he found no Bujayr among the teachers of Ibn Abi al-Sari that al-Mizzi lists in Tahdhib al-Kamal, whereas Yahya b. Sa'id al-Attar is listed. Khalid b. Ma'dan al-Kala'i was treated at report 2070; Abu Ruhm Ahzab b. Usayd al-Sama'i is an early trustworthy Successor, counted by some among the Companions, and the editor prefers the first view. He traces the report to Ahmad's Musnad by two routes, both through Baqiyya, whom he calls trustworthy: some criticised Baqiyya for concealing his sources, but he states that he was told it directly in the first chain, which removes the doubt. Bujayr b. Sa'd al-Himsi is trustworthy according to Ahmad and Ibn Sa'd, and the editor judges the spelling Sa'id in some biographical works a plain error. Ibn Kathir copies the report from the second route in the Musnad and prints Yahya b. Sa'id for Bujayr b. Sa'd, which the editor takes as a copyist's or printer's slip; he adds that Ibn Kathir also ascribes it to al-Nasa'i, and that al-Suyuti adds Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim with a grading word that the scan renders unclearly. He notes that al-Tabari gives another chain by another route next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 250}

**Report 9225 and its note (pp. 250-251).** Abbas b. Abi Talib (9225, from Sa'd b. Abd al-Hamid b. Ja'far, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from Ibn Abi al-Zinad, from Musa b. Uqba, from Abd Allah b. Salman al-Aghar, from his father Salman al-Aghar) reports that Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, a man of al-Aqaba and Badr, said the Messenger of God said that no servant worships God without associating anything with Him, establishes the prayer, pays the alms, fasts Ramadan and avoids the major sins, but enters Paradise. They asked what the major sins are and he said associating partners with God, fleeing from the march and killing a soul. The editor says this is a second chain, by another route, for the preceding report. Abbas b. Abi Talib was treated at report 880, and Sa'd b. Abd al-Hamid b. Ja'far al-Ansari at report 3959, where the editor judged him trustworthy; both the print and the manuscript have a preposition reading Ja'far's name wrongly and he corrects it. The phrase from Ibn Abi Ja'far is given as it stands: if sound it would be Abd Allah b. Abi Ja'far al-Razi (report 7030), but the editor thinks it was added to the chain by the copyists' confusion, since Sa'd b. Abd al-Hamid is known for narrating from Ibn Abi al-Zinad (Abd al-Rahman, report 1694). The name Abd Allah b. Salman al-Aghar stands thus and the man is trustworthy, narrating from his father, but the editor thinks it should be Ubayd Allah in the diminutive, because Musa b. Uqba is not recorded as narrating from an Abd Allah, only from his brother Ubayd Allah, a well-known trustworthy narrator whom Malik and Musa b. Uqba reported from. Their father Salman al-Aghar, Abu Abd Allah of Medina, is a trustworthy Successor in the main collections. The editor says the report was traced earlier, but by this route, Salman al-Aghar from Abu Ayyub, he found it nowhere else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 251}

**Reports 9226 and 9227 and their notes (pp. 251-252).** Abu Kurayb (9226, from Ahmad b. Abd al-Rahman, from Abbad b. Abbad, from Ja'far b. al-Zubayr, from al-Qasim) reports from Abu Umama that some Companions of the Messenger of God mentioned the major sins while he was reclining and listed associating partners with God, devouring an orphan's property, fleeing from the march, slandering chaste women, disobeying parents, false speech, stealing from the spoils, sorcery and usury; the Prophet asked where they put the verse at [[passage/quran-3-77|3:77]]. The editor calls the chain weak and collapsed, says Ja'far b. al-Zubayr al-Dimashqi is very weak and narrated from al-Qasim from Abu Umama a forged copy, as shown at report 1939, that Ibn Kathir reproduces the report from this place, and that al-Suyuti cites it only to al-Tabari and calls the chain good, an error in the editor's judgement. Ubayd Allah b. Muhammad al-Farayabi (9227, from Sufyan, from Abu Mu'awiya, from Abu Amr al-Shaybani) reports from Abd Allah, evidently Ibn Mas'ud from the reports that follow, that he asked the Prophet about the major sins and was told: that you set up a rival to God who created you, that you kill your child because he would eat with you, or that you commit adultery with your neighbour's wife; and the Messenger recited to them what is at [[passage/quran-25-68|25:68]]. The editor says al-Farayabi, a teacher of al-Tabari, was treated at report 17, that al-Tabari will say on p. 254 that he erred in this report, meaning confused the sense, but that the editor does not agree, since this report and the next are one and differ only in wording; Sufyan is Ibn Uyayna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 252}

**Report 9228 and its note (pp. 252-253).** Abd Allah b. Muhammad al-Zuhri (9228, from Sufyan, from Abu Mu'awiya al-Nakha'i, who was in charge of the prison, having heard it from Abu Amr) reports from Abd Allah b. Mas'ud that he asked the Messenger of God which deed is worst and was told: that you make a rival to God who created you, that you kill your child so that he does not eat with you, or that you commit adultery with your neighbour's wife; and he recited the opening of the verse at [[passage/quran-25-68|25:68]]. The editor says Abd Allah b. Muhammad b. Abd al-Rahman b. al-Miswar b. Makhrama al-Zuhri, a teacher of al-Tabari, is trustworthy; that Abu Mu'awiya al-Nakha'i here and in the previous chain is Amr b. Abd Allah b. Wahb, trustworthy according to Ibn Ma'in and others; and that Abu Amr al-Shaybani is Sa'd b. Iyas, a leading Successor (report 5524). He adds that al-Tabari will give the report again at the commentary on verse 71 of the sura of the Criterion, from al-Farayabi and by other chains; that Ahmad gives it many times through Abu Wa'il Shaqiq b. Salama from Ibn Mas'ud, as do al-Bukhari and Muslim; that some of their versions put Amr b. Shurahbil between Abu Wa'il and Ibn Mas'ud, and the editor thinks Abu Wa'il heard it both ways, the addition being an extra link in a chain that is already connected, a point Ibn Hajar treats at length in the Fath; and that Ibn Kathir draws on one Musnad version and one later version of al-Tabari, while al-Suyuti adds al-Farayabi, Abd b. Humayd, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bayhaqi in Shu'ab al-Iman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 253}

**Al-Tabari's choice: reconciling the reports (pp. 252-254).** Abu Jafar says the soundest interpretation of the major sins is what the report from the Messenger of God establishes, rather than what others said, even though each of those who gave a view exerted himself and has a ground for it. On his reckoning the major sins are associating partners with God, disobedience to parents, killing the soul whose killing is forbidden, false speech (which can include false testimony), slander of chaste women, the false oath, sorcery, fleeing from the march and adultery with a neighbour's wife; killing a child so that he does not eat with one falls within killing the forbidden soul. Hence every report from the Prophet on the major sins is sound and they confirm one another: the report that they are seven is a detailed count, and the report that they are associating, killing, disobedience to parents and false speech is a summary, since false speech has many senses and gathers all of them. The report of Ibn Mas'ud that al-Farayabi gave is, he holds, a mistake of Ubayd Allah b. Muhammad, since the many well-supported reports on good routes from Ibn Mas'ud from the Prophet agree with al-Zuhri's version from Ibn Uyayna, and none of those narrators says that the Prophet was asked about the major sins; their transmission is more likely sound than al-Farayabi's. In the notes the editor says the printed edition had a different adjective for the routes than the manuscript and he does not know why it was changed, and that the printing had a phrase about keeping faith with it persistently where the manuscript's undotted word is, he thinks, to be read as the verb for being constant. Al-Tabari then says that whoever avoids the major sins for which God promised to expiate the rest of his evil deeds and admit him by a generous entering, and discharges the duties God laid on him, will find God's promise fulfilled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 254}

**Meaning of expiation and reports 9229-9231 (pp. 254-256).** Al-Tabari explains that God expiates for the believers, by their avoiding the major things their Lord forbids, the minor ones of their wrongdoings. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of expiation and of evil deeds in volume 7. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9229, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that what is meant is the minor sins; the editor says print and manuscript have al-Hasan for al-Husayn, that this chain recurs in the commentary, and that the nearest instance is 9133. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9230, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ibn Awn) reports from al-Hasan that some people met Abd Allah b. Amr in Egypt and told him they saw things in the Book of God that were commanded to be done and were not being done, and they wanted to meet the Commander of the Faithful about it. He travelled and they travelled with him. Umar asked him when he had come and whether he had come with permission, and he told him what the people had said, and Umar asked for them to be brought together (Ibn Awn thinks he said in a hall). Taking the humblest of them, Umar adjured him by God and by the right of Islam: had he read the whole Qur'an? He said yes. Had he counted it within himself? He said no, by God; and the narrator adds that had he said yes Umar would have bested him in argument. Had he counted it in his sight, in his speech, in his footsteps? Umar went through them to the last of them and said: may Umar's mother be bereaved, do you burden him with keeping people upright on the Book of God? Our Lord knew we would have evil deeds. He recited [[passage/quran-4-31|4:31]], then asked whether the people of Medina knew why they had come; they said no, and he said that had they known he would have made an example of them. The editor glosses the hall as the front room before the other rooms, to count a thing as to encompass and keep it, footsteps as the tread of a man's walking on the earth, and to out-argue as to defeat by proof, and he reports that the print and manuscript had a different word where he restored hall from Ibn Kathir's commentary. He notes that Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti (Durr, with the remark that Ibn Jarir gave it with a good chain) transmit the report, that Ibn Kathir calls the chain sound and the text good although al-Hasan's report from Umar has a break, such a report being well known enough to suffice, and he explains the phrase about making an example as meaning a punishment that would be a lesson for others. In his view the men had come complaining of their governor over Egypt, were severe and gave no ease, and wanted the caliph to drive people to what they could not themselves manage, the encompassing of every act of Islam; Umar was too great to be lax about Islam's rulings, and the editor adds that he wrote this out for fear a person of power might cite it to excuse leaving God's rulings unapplied, as he says rulers of his own time do. Yaqub (9231, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ziyad b. Mikhraq) reports from Mu'awiya b. Qurra that they came to Anas b. Malik, who said among other things that they had never seen the like of what had reached them from their Lord, and yet they had not left everything, family and property, for it; after a pause he said that by God their Lord had charged them with something lighter than that, having passed over for them what is beneath the major sins, so what had they to do with the major ones? Then he recited the verse. The editor says the second word then is not in the manuscript but he kept it because it is in the Durr and in Ibn Kathir, and that al-Suyuti also credits Ibn Abi Shayba and Abd b. Humayd. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 256}

**Reports 9232-9234 (pp. 256-257).** Bishr b. Mu'adh (9232, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that God promised forgiveness only to whoever avoids the major sins, and that it was mentioned to them that the Prophet said: avoid the major sins, hold to the right course, and rejoice. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9233, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from a man) reports from Ibn Mas'ud that five verses of the sura of the Women were dearer to him than the whole world, which the print numbers as [[passage/quran-4-31|4:31]], [[passage/quran-4-40|4:40]], [[passage/quran-4-48|4:48]] and [[passage/quran-4-116|4:116]], [[passage/quran-4-110|4:110]] and [[passage/quran-4-152|4:152]]. Al-Qasim (9234, from al-Husayn, from Abu al-Nadr, from Salih al-Murri, from Qatada) reports from Ibn Abbas that eight verses were sent down in the sura of the Women that are better for this community than all on which the sun has risen and set: the first three being [[passage/quran-4-26|4:26]], [[passage/quran-4-27|4:27]] and [[passage/quran-4-28|4:28]]; then he gave exactly as Ibn Mas'ud's list and added that he went on to explain the end of the verse, as pardon and mercy for those who committed sins. The editor adds for 9233 that al-Suyuti cites it in the Durr and also credits Abu Ubayd al-Qasim b. Sallam, Sa'id b. Mansur in his Fada'il, Abd b. Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, al-Tabarani, al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi in the Shu'ab. For 9234 he says Abu al-Nadr seems to be Ishaq b. Ibrahim b. Yazid al-Dimashqi al-Faradisi, a teacher of al-Bukhari and Abu Zur'a who was born in 141 and died in 227 and is trustworthy (report 8788); and that Salih al-Murri is Salih b. Bashir b. Wada' al-Murri the storyteller, who narrated from al-Hasan, Ibn Sirin and Qatada, a pious man but one who told reports the scholars rejected, so that the editor calls him one whose transmission is abandoned; he died in 172 or 176 as the print shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 257}

**The readers' dispute over the vowelling of mudkhal (pp. 257-259).** Al-Tabari says the readers differed over reading this word in the verse. Most of the readers of Medina and some of Kufa read it with a fatha on the mim, and likewise the one at [[passage/quran-22-59|22:59]], so that the sense is that He admits them and they enter a generous entering; on this reading the word could also mean the place of entering, because the Arabs sometimes open the mim in that sense. He cites a line of rajaz about praising God in the morning and at the hour of evening, and another line which, someone told him, he heard from the Arabs, which the editor ascribes to Umayya b. Abi al-Salt from the opening of one of his poems, referring to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, the Khizana and the Lisan; he adds that in a verb built on four letters the Arabs put damma on the mim, as in the example of rolling something, and carry the pattern of the causative verbs along with it. Most of the readers of Kufa and Basra read it with a damma on the mim, meaning that He will admit you an entering. Abu Jafar says the sounder of the two readings is the damma, for the reason he gave that the verbal noun of a verb on four letters takes that form, that the verbs for admitting and rolling are built on four, and that this is also the more eloquent form for verbal nouns of the causative pattern; he contrasts the two forms of the word for standing or staying, as at [[passage/quran-44-51|44:51]], and notes that the verse at [[passage/quran-17-80|17:80]] was read in the sense of admission and expulsion and he had not heard of anyone reading either word there with a fatha. The editor notes that he does not know who spoke the rajaz, that the manuscript has a blank for a few words after the rolling example, that the printed edition filled it with the verbal noun and he added the verb itself because the context needs it, that the printed edition has the wrong verb pattern where the manuscript is right, and that by the past form al-Tabari means the past verb and not the morphological pattern. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 259}

**Meaning of the generous entering (p. 259).** Al-Tabari says the entering described as generous is the good, pleasant one, honoured by the absence of afflictions and defects and the removal of worries and griefs and the entry of turbidity into the life of the one who enters; for that reason God called it generous. He then begins report 9235 (Muhammad b. al-Husayn, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal), and its chain is cut off at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 259}

**End of report 9235 (p. 260).** The chain of Muhammad b. al-Husayn, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, is completed: Asbat, from al-Suddi, said that the generous entering means the good one in Paradise. In his note the editor says the printed edition had Muhammad b. al-Hasan, which he holds to be an error, and refers to his comment on report 9229. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 260}

**Where verse 32 begins and how al-Tabari opens it (p. 260).** Al-Tabari begins the commentary on [[passage/quran-4-32|4:32]] by explaining that it forbids longing for the distinctions God gave to some people and not to others. He notes it was said to have come down about women who longed for the rank of men and for what men have, so God forbade His servants vain wishes and told them to ask Him for His gift, since wishes breed envy and unjust aggression in those who hold them. In a long note the editor adds his own opinion about his contemporaries: that people of his age had plunged into this kind of talk and confused it, that sincerity and a sound grasp of human nature are needed to separate vain wishes from real necessity, and that a community should be freed from imitating dominant nations; he distinguishes a community living a healthy life, men and women alike, free of defects, from one that drops every barrier between men and women. This is the editor's own voice, not al-Tabari's, and the museum gives no verdict on it.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 260}
