## Summary
Verse 31 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 31 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The first view on the major sins: what is forbidden from the start of the sura to verse thirty (p. 233, reports 9168-9172).** Abu Jafar says the people of interpretation differed over the meaning of the major sins whose avoidance God promised to reward with the covering of the rest of their sins. Some said they are what God forbade His servants from the start of Surat al-Nisa to the head of its thirtieth verse. Ibn Bashshar (9168, from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from al-A'mash from Abu al-Duha from Masruq) reports from Abd Allah that the major sins are from the start of the sura to thirty of it; Ibn Bashshar (9169, from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Hammad from Ibrahim) reports the same from Abd Allah; al-Muthanna (9170, from Hajjaj from Hammad from Ibrahim) reports the same from Ibn Mas'ud; Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i (9171, from Waki from al-A'mash from Ibrahim from Alqama) reports from Abd Allah that the major sins run from the start of the sura to the place of this verse; al-Rifa'i (9172, from Abu Mu'awiya and Abu Khalid from al-A'mash from Ibrahim from Alqama) reports from Abd Allah to the same effect, and the wording of this report breaks off at the foot of the page and continues on the next.

**End of the first view: reports 9172-9178 and the editor's tracing (p. 234).** Report 9172 ends by saying that the range Ibn Mas'ud meant runs up to the place of this verse itself. Abu al-Sa'ib (9173, from Abu Mu'awiya from al-A'mash from Muslim) reports from Masruq that Abd Allah was asked about the major sins and said they lie between the opening of Surat al-Nisa and the head of thirty. Ibn Humayd (9174, from Jarir from Mughira from Hammad) reports the same from Ibrahim from Ibn Mas'ud, adding that he counted thirty verses of it. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9175, from Hushaym from Mughira) reports the same from Ibrahim from Abd Allah; Yaqub (9176, from Ibn Ulayya from Ibn Awn) reports from Ibrahim that they used to hold the major sins to lie between the start of the sura and this place. Al-Muthanna (9177, from Adam al-Asqalani from Shu'ba from Asim b. Abi al-Najud from Zirr b. Hubaysh) reports from Ibn Mas'ud that the major sins run from the start of the sura to thirty verses of it, after which Ibn Mas'ud recited the verse. Report 9178 (as printed, al-Muthanna from Ibn Waki from Mis'ar from Asim from Zirr) reports from Abd Allah the same. Editor Shakir notes that reports 9168-9178 are one report with many chains, recorded by al-Haythami in Majma al-Zawa'id (4:7), who says al-Bazzar narrated it and that its men are the men of the Sahih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 234}

**The second view: the major sins are seven (p. 235, report 9179).** Others said the major sins are seven. Tamim b. al-Muntasir (9179, from Yazid from Muhammad b. Ishaq from Muhammad b. Sahl b. Abi Hathma) reports from his father that he was in the mosque of Kufa while Ali was addressing the people from the pulpit, and Ali said the major sins are seven, repeating it three times until the people listened closely, and then asked them whether they would ask him about them. When they did, he listed associating partners with God, killing the soul God made sacred, slandering a chaste woman, consuming an orphan's property, consuming usury, fleeing on the day of the battle line, and returning to the desert life after the migration. Muhammad b. Sahl says he asked his father what that last one was and how it came to be in this list, and his father answered that nothing is graver than a man who migrated and then, when his share fell among the spoils and fighting became obligatory on him, cast that off his neck and went back to being a Bedouin as before. In the footnotes the editor says the printed edition and Ibn Kathir word the mention of Ali differently and he kept the manuscript's wording; that the two forms of address to the father mean the same; that 'ta'arrub' means going back to desert dwelling like the Arabs of the desert; and that a phrase in the manuscript is written confusedly, so he left the printed wording since it agrees with Ibn Kathir. He also gives a note on Muhammad b. Sahl b. Abi Hathma al-Ansari, who narrated from his father and his uncle, is in al-Bukhari's al-Tarikh al-Kabir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Ta'jil al-Manfa'a; al-Bukhari mentions no criticism of him, and Ibn Hibban lists him among the trustworthy. Al-Bukhari alluded to this report in his entry, and Ibn Kathir (2:422) traces it, quoting Ibn Mardawayh's version that goes back to the Prophet through Ibn Lahi'a from Ziyad b. Abi Habib from Muhammad b. Sahl from his father; Ibn Kathir adds that its chain needs scrutiny, that raising it to the Prophet is a gross error, and that the correct form is what Ibn Jarir narrated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 235}

**Reports 9180 and 9181: seven, each with a verse (pp. 235-237).** Muhammad b. Ubayd al-Muharibi (9180, from Abu al-Ahwas Sallam b. Sulaym from Ibn Ishaq) reports from Ubayd b. Umayr that the major sins are seven and each has a verse in God's Book: associating partners ([[passage/quran-22-31|22:31]]), consuming orphans' property ([[passage/quran-4-10|4:10]]), usury ([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), defaming believing women of good standing who are unaware ([[passage/quran-24-23|24:23]]), fleeing from the battle line ([[passage/quran-8-15|8:15]]), returning to the desert after migration ([[passage/quran-47-25|47:25]]), and killing a soul. Ibn Humayd (9181, from Jarir from Mansur from Ibn Ishaq) reports from Ubayd b. Umayr al-Laythi that the major sins are seven, and he pairs each with a passage: association ([[passage/quran-22-31|22:31]]), killing ([[passage/quran-4-93|4:93]]), usury ([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), orphans' property ([[passage/quran-4-10|4:10]]), slander of the chaste ([[passage/quran-24-23|24:23]]), flight from the battle line ([[passage/quran-8-16|8:16]]), and the one who turns back to the desert after migrating ([[passage/quran-47-25|47:25]]). A footnote begins at the foot of p. 236 and finishes on the next page: it gives the biography of Muhammad b. Ubayd b. Muhammad b. Waqid al-Muharibi, Abu Jafar al-Nahhas of Kufa, a teacher of al-Tabari, from whom Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi, Abu Hatim and others narrated; al-Nasa'i said there is no harm in him and Ibn Hibban lists him among the trustworthy. It also covers Abu al-Ahwas Sallam b. Sulaym; Ibn Ishaq, who is Muhammad b. Ishaq; and Ubayd b. Umayr b. Qatada al-Laythi, who narrated from his father and from Umar, Ali, Ubayy b. Ka'b, Abu Musa, Abu Hurayra and others, and is a trustworthy senior Successor. The editor says the printed edition wrote his name wrongly and the manuscript is right, and refers to his note on report 9189. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 237}

**Reports 9182-9184: the statement of Ubayda (p. 237).** Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9182, from Ibn Ulayya from Ibn Awn) reports from Muhammad b. Sirin that he asked Ubayda about the major sins and he listed associating partners with God, killing the soul God made sacred without right, flight from the battle line, consuming an orphan's property, usury and false slander, adding that people also mention the desert life after migration. Ibn Awn asked Muhammad about sorcery and he answered that false slander gathers much evil. Abu Kurayb (9183, from Hushaym from Mansur and Hisham) reports from Ibn Sirin from Ubayda that the major sins are association, forbidden killing, usury, slander of the chaste, an orphan's property, flight from the battle line and the apostate who becomes a Bedouin after migrating. Report 9184 (Yaqub from Hushaym from Hisham from Ibn Sirin from Ubayda) is to the same effect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 237}

**The ground of this view: report 9185 (pp. 237-238).** Abu Jafar gives as the ground of those who held the number seven the report of al-Muthanna (9185, from Abu Salih from al-Layth from Khalid from Sa'id b. Abi Hilal) from Nu'aym al-Mujmir from Suhayb, client of al-Utwari, who heard Abu Hurayra and Abu Sa'id al-Khudri say that the Messenger of God addressed them one day and swore three times, then bowed his head, and every man of them bowed his, weeping and not knowing what he had sworn on. Then he raised his head with joy on his face, which was dearer to them than red camels, and said that no servant who prays the five prayers, fasts Ramadan, pays the alms and avoids the seven major sins will fail to have the gates of Paradise opened for him, and he is then told to go in, greeted with safety. Editor Shakir says in the footnote that the chain is sound; that Khalid is Ibn Yazid al-Misri; that Nu'aym b. Abd Allah al-Mujmir of Medina, client of the family of Umar b. al-Khattab, is a trustworthy Successor whom the authors of the main collections used; that Suhayb, client of al-Utwari, is a trustworthy Medinan Successor; that al-Utwari is a nisba to Utwara, a clan of Kinana, as Ibn al-Athir says; and that Ibn Kathir's print has a misprinted form. He records that al-Bukhari gave the report briefly in his entry on Suhayb, that al-Nasa'i narrated it, that al-Hakim gave it in his Mustadrak and called it sound on the condition of the two shaykhs although they did not record it, that Ibn Hibban has it in his Sahih by another route, and that al-Suyuti adds Ibn Majah, Ibn Khuzayma and al-Bayhaqi. He also explains the words: to bow the head is to lower it and look at the ground; 'al-na'am' is camels and similar livestock, here camels; and red camels are the best of camels and the most patient in the midday heat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 238}

**Report 9186: the statement of Ata (p. 238).** Al-Muthanna (9186, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Ata that the major sins are seven: killing a soul, usury, consuming an orphan's property, slandering the chaste, false witness, disobedience to parents and flight from the battle line. As transmitted, this list differs from the earlier ones in including false witness and disobedience to parents and leaving out association and the return to desert life. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 238}

**The third view: the major sins are nine (pp. 239-240, report 9187).** Others said they are nine. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9187, from Ibn Ulayya from Ziyad b. Mikhraq) reports from Taysala b. Mayyas: 'I was with the Najadat and committed sins I took to be among the major sins, so I met Ibn Umar and told him; he asked what they were and I told him, and he said that is not among the major sins; they are nine, and I shall count them for you: associating partners with God, killing a soul unlawfully, flight from the battle line, slandering the chaste, usury, consuming an orphan's property wrongfully, profaning the Sacred Mosque, the one who seeks sorcery, and the weeping of parents through disobedience.' Ziyad says Taysala added that when Ibn Umar saw his fear he asked whether he feared the Fire and wished to enter Paradise and whether his parents were alive; Taysala said his mother was with him, and Ibn Umar said that if he softened his speech to her and fed her he would enter Paradise as long as he avoided the certain-damnations. In the footnotes the editor says he printed 'I committed' in all three places because it suits the sense, the manuscript at the first place and Ibn Kathir; that the print dropped the word at one point and he restored it from the manuscript; and that the manuscript's wording after 'that is not among the major sins' is the right one, meaning the sin Taysala did not name is not a major sin. He writes at length on the word for 'seeks sorcery': the printed edition, al-Bukhari's al-Adab al-Mufrad and Ibn Kathir read it with a different letter that would mean 'mocks', which he says is not a major sin and not found in any report; the manuscript and al-Durr al-Manthur have an undotted letter, and he judges the reading 'seeks sorcery', meaning asking a sorcerer to work it or teach it, to be right, because the reports name sorcery among the major sins, comparing the formation of a verb meaning to ask a diviner to cast pebbles; he admits the verb is absent from the dictionaries available to him. The footnotes on p. 240 add that fear is intense dread; that the chain of 9187 is sound; that Ziyad b. Mikhraq al-Muzani of Basra is trustworthy (Ibn Ma'in and al-Nasa'i); that Taysala b. Mayyas is Taysala b. Ali al-Nahdi of the next chain, his father being Ali and his nickname Mayyas, and al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim hold them one man whose correct nisba is al-Bahdali; that al-Bukhari recorded it in al-Adab al-Mufrad and Ibn Kathir mentioned it; and that al-Suyuti gave it in abridged and slightly corrupt form adding Ibn Rahawayh, Abd b. Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Qadi Ismail. The Najadat were a Kharijite (Haruri) group named after Najda b. Amir al-Haruri al-Hanafi, according to Lisan al-Arab; the print had a misreading of the name, the manuscript's form is undotted, and the right form stands in al-Adab al-Mufrad and the Azhari manuscript of Ibn Kathir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 240}

**Reports 9188 and 9189 (pp. 240-241).** Sulayman b. Thabit al-Jazzar al-Wasiti (9188, from Sallam b. Salam from Ayyub b. Utba) reports from Taysala b. Ali al-Nahdi: 'I came to Ibn Umar in the shade of an arak tree on the day of Arafa as he poured water over his head and face and asked him about the major sins; he said they are nine: associating partners with God, slandering the chaste (and when I asked whether that came before killing he said yes), killing a believing soul, flight from the battle line, sorcery, usury, an orphan's property, disobedience to Muslim parents, and profaning the Sacred House, your direction of prayer alive and dead.' Sulayman b. Thabit (9189, by the same chain to Ayyub b. Utba) reports from Yahya from Ubayd b. Umayr from his father from the Prophet the like of it, except that he began with killing before slander. In the footnotes the editor says the manuscript wrote the word for profaning in a form that the print wrote with the article, and that the copyist seems to have joined a letter to the alif; that 9188 is another chain for the preceding report; that he did not know who Sulayman b. Thabit al-Jazzar al-Wasiti, al-Tabari's teacher, is and found no biography, Ibn Kathir's print giving another form; that Sallam b. Salam is Abu al-Musayyab al-Wasiti with no criticism recorded; and that Ayyub b. Utba, Abu Yahya, judge of al-Yamama, is weak, as Ahmad, al-Bukhari and others held. He adds that Ibn Kathir mentions the report and that Ali b. al-Ja'd narrated it from Ayyub at length; that Ibn Hajar in al-Tahdhib points to other routes from Ayyub from Taysala; that Ayyub was not alone in it since Ikrima b. Ammar al-Ijli, a trustworthy man, also narrated it from Taysala, as al-Bukhari indicates briefly; and that al-Suyuti was too brief, attributing it only to Ali b. al-Ja'd. In the note on report 9189 he says Yahya is Ibn Abi Kathir, and that the manuscript and print both have 'Yahya b. Ubayd b. Umayr', a slip of the copyists turning 'from' into 'son of'.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 241 in the commentary on verse 31 of sura 4, in the discussion of the meaning of the major sins, after the views of seven and of nine and at report 9189, and continue from p. 242. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 241}
