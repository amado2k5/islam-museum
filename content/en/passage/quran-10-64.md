## Summary
Verse 64 of [[passage/quran-10|Surat Yunus]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 64 of 109 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 64 (pp. 124-142)**

On [[passage/quran-10-64|10:64]] he says: the glad tidings from God in the life of this world and in the next belong to the friends of God who believed and were mindful. The interpreters then differed about these glad tidings and their nature. Some said they are the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him, and in the next life Paradise. Report 17717, from Abu al-Darda', that he asked the Prophet about the verse and was told: the good dream that the believer sees or that is seen for him. Report 17718, from Ubada b. al-Samit, that he asked the Prophet about the verse and was told: you have asked me about something no one asked me before you; it is the good dream that the righteous man sees or that is seen for him. In a note the editor lists the routes of the hadith of Abu al-Darda' and of Ubada, explains that the weakness of the chain of 17717 is the unknown shaykh from whom Abu Salih al-Samman related, and points to the later reports in which each route comes. The commentary continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 125}

Report 17719, from Ubada b. al-Samit, that he asked the Prophet about the verse and was told: it is the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him. Abu Dawud relates it from someone he does not name, from Yahya b. Abi Kathir, from Abu Salama b. Abd al-Rahman. Report 17720 is similar, through Aban b. Yazid al-Attar from Yahya b. Abi Kathir. The editor identifies the men of both chains. He says the first is weakened by the unknown narrator from Yahya and supported by the other routes from reliable men, and that Abu Salama did not hear from Ubada, so these reports are broken and weak and no sound collection includes them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 126}

Report 17721, from Ubada b. al-Samit: Abu Salama said he was told that Ubada asked the Prophet about the verse and was answered: you have asked me about something no one asked me before you; it is the good dream that a man sees or that is seen for him. The editor says its chain is disordered in both the printed text and the manuscript, which he thinks went wrong in the copyist's hands, and proposes how the names of al-Tabari's teachers in it should be corrected and the chain restored. He adds that it is also broken, because Abu Salama did not hear from Ubada, and that Ahmad and al-Darimi relate it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 127}

Then report 17722, from Abu al-Darda', through Ata b. Yasar from a man of Egypt: a man asked Abu al-Darda' about the verse, and he said he had been asked about something he had heard no one ask about since a man asked the Prophet, who answered: it is the good dream that a Muslim man sees or that is seen for him; his glad tidings in this life, and his glad tidings in the next are Paradise. The editor says the flaw in this chain is the unknown Egyptian from whom Ata related; Ibn Abi Hatim reports from his father that he is not known, and a copy of Fath al-Bari wrongly has 'Ubada' where 'Abu al-Darda'' is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 128}

Report 17723, from the same Egyptian: I asked Abu al-Darda' about the verse and he said no one had asked him about it since he asked the Prophet, except one man, and that the Prophet said no one had asked him about it since God sent it down, except one man; it is the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him. Report 17724 repeats it through Sufyan from Ibn al-Munkadir. The editor says the chain is sound as far as Ata apart from the unknown Egyptian, that Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi relate it, and that the report came earlier as 17717. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 129}

Report 17725, from Humayd b. Abd Allah al-Muzani: a man came to Ubada b. al-Samit and asked about the verse, and Ubada said no one had asked him before, that he had asked the Prophet, who said the same: it is the good dream that a believing servant sees in sleep or that is seen for him. The editor spends long on the names of the narrators in this chain and how they differ between the manuscript, the Musnad of Ahmad and the biographical works, and says the report is broken between Humayd and Ubada; it is the second route of the hadith of Ubada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 130}

Then al-Tabari's report 17726, from Abu Hurayra: the Messenger of God said the good dream is the glad tidings, that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him. Report 17727, from Abu Salih: Abu Hurayra said the good dream is glad tidings from God, and these are the mubashshirat. The editor says the first chain is sound and the second is a saying stopping at Abu Hurayra, also sound, and that the same appears next as a saying of the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 130}

Report 17728, from Abu Hurayra, from the Prophet on 'for them is the glad tidings in the life of this world': the good dream that a righteous servant sees or that is seen for him, and in the next life it is Paradise. The editor gives the men of the chain, among them Ammar b. Muhammad al-Thawri, a nephew of Sufyan al-Thawri whom Ibn Sa'd, Ibn Ma'in and al-Bukhari trusted and Ibn Hibban faulted. He calls the chain acceptable, and notes the good chain in Muslim, from al-A'mash: the Muslim's dream is one of forty-six parts of prophecy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 131}

Report 17729, from Abd Allah b. Amr b. al-As, from the Prophet on the verse: the good dream with which a servant is given glad tidings is one of forty-nine parts of prophecy. The editor names its narrators and calls the chain weak because of Rishdin b. Sa'd, says Abu al-Samh is Darraj b. Sam'an and that 'Abu al-Shaykh' in the printed text is a plain error, and says the report comes later with a sound chain as 17754. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 131}

Report 17730, from Ubada b. al-Samit, who said to the Prophet: we know the glad tidings of the next life, so what are those of this life? He said: the good dream that a servant sees or that is seen for him, which is one of forty-four parts, or sixty parts, of prophecy. The editor calls this the third route of Ubada's hadith. He judges the chain weak because of Musa b. Ubayda al-Rabadhi and broken because Ayyub b. Khalid did not relate from Ubada, and says he has kept 'sixty' from the manuscript in place of the printed 'seventy'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 132}

Report 17731, from Ubada b. al-Samit, that he asked the Prophet about the verse and was told: you have asked me about something none of my community asked before you; it is the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him, and in the next life Paradise. Report 17732, from Umm Kurz al-Ka'biyya, who heard the Prophet say: prophecy has gone and the mubashshirat remain. The editor says the first is another chain for 17718 and the first route of Ubada's hadith, then identifies the men of the second. He says there is discussion whether Siba' b. Thabit was a Companion, that Ibn Maja and al-Darimi relate it likewise, and that Ahmad relates it and cites his son saying that Sufyan b. Uyayna errs in these reports, since Ubayd Allah heard them from Siba' himself. His judgement is that the report is sound despite the difference and the slip is Sufyan's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 133}

Report 17733, from Abu al-Darda', from the Prophet on the verse: the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him, and in the next life Paradise, through al-A'mash from Abu Salih Dhakwan from a man. Report 17734 repeats 17722, with the Egyptian and his question to Abu al-Darda'. Report 17735, from Abu Salih from Abu al-Darda' directly, that he asked the Prophet and was told no one had asked about it but him. The editor says the first two are routes of Abu al-Darda's hadith and that Ahmad relates the first, that the third is the second route to which al-Tirmidhi points, and that Asim b. Bahdala is reliable but had something faulty in his memory, so this may be one of the reports he remembered badly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 134}

Report 17736, from Abu al-Darda', through Jarir from al-A'mash from Abu Salih from Ata b. Yasar: I asked the Prophet about it and he said no one had asked before me; it is the good dream that a servant sees or that is seen for him, and in the next life Paradise. Report 17737, through Ibn Uyayna from Amr b. Dinar from Abd al-Aziz b. Rufay from Abu Salih from Ata b. Yasar from a man of Egypt from Abu al-Darda', to the same effect. Report 17738, that Amr b. Dinar asked an Egyptian jurist who had come to them at one of the seasons, who told him he asked Abu al-Darda', who asked the Prophet. The editor calls the chain of 17736 good, the fourth route; 17737 is of the third route and is related by al-Tirmidhi and Ahmad; 17738 is the fifth route and is weak because Amr b. Dinar did not hear from Abu al-Darda' and relates from an unknown man. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 135}

The answer in the preceding report is: it is the good dream that a servant sees or that is seen for him. Then report 17739, from Ubada b. al-Samit, through Ali b. al-Mubarak from Yahya b. Abi Kathir from Abu Salama, and report 17740, repeating 17720, in the words: you have asked me about something no one asked me before you; it is the good dream that a righteous man sees or that is seen for him. Report 17741, from Abu Salih, who heard Abu al-Darda' asked about the verse and say no one had asked before him since he himself asked the Prophet about it. The editor says Ahmad, Ibn Maja and al-Hakim relate the first, that al-Hakim called it sound and al-Dhahabi agreed, that its flaw is the break between Abu Salama and Ubada, and that the third repeats 17735. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 136}

Report 17742, from Nafi' b. Jubayr, from a man among the Companions of the Prophet, on the verse: it is the good dream that a person sees or that is seen for him. Report 17743, from Ibn Jurayj, from Amr b. Dinar from Abu al-Darda', or from Ibn al-Munkadir from Ata b. Yasar from Abu al-Darda', that he asked the Prophet about it and was told: it is the good dream. Report 17744, from Urwa: it is the dream a man sees. Report 17745, from Yahya b. Abi Kathir: it is the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him. The editor says the unknown Companion in 17742 does no harm, that he may be Abu Hurayra, and that 17743 has two routes: the first weak because broken and the second the fourth route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 137}

Report 17746, from Hisham b. Urwa from his father: it is the good dream that a righteous servant sees. Report 17747, from Mujahid: it is the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him. Report 17748, from Ibn Abbas: it is the good dream that the Muslim servant sees for himself or for some of his brothers, a saying stopping at him. Report 17749, from Ibrahim: they used to say the dream is among the mubashshirat. Report 17750, from Qays b. Sa'd, that a man asked the Prophet about it and was told no one of his community had asked since it was sent down, and that it is the good dream a man sees for himself or that is seen for him, a mursal report. Report 17751, from Ibn Mas'ud: prophecy has gone and the mubashshirat remain; asked what they are, he said the good dream that a man sees or that is seen for him, a saying with a sound chain. Report 17752, from Ibn Abbas: this glad tidings in the world is what God said to His Prophet in [[passage/quran-33-47|33:47]], and it is the good dream that a believer sees or that is seen for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 138}

Report 17753, from Ata: it is the dream of the Muslim man, with which he is given glad tidings in his life. Report 17754, from Abd Allah b. Amr, from the Prophet: the good dream with which the believer is given glad tidings is one of forty-six parts of prophecy. Report 17755, from Urwa: it is the good dream a man sees or that is seen for him. Report 17756, from Humayd b. Abd Allah, that a man asked Ubada b. al-Samit about the verse and he said: I asked the Prophet what you ask, and he said you have asked me about something no one of my community asked; that is the good dream a believer sees for himself or that is seen for him. The editor says the hadith of Abd Allah b. Amr came earlier by a weak route and this route through Ibn Wahb is stronger than the one through Ibn Lahi'a, and that Ahmad and al-Haythami cite it; 17756 is the second route of Ubada's hadith, and the name Humayd differs between copies of the Musnad, which he thinks a copyist's slip. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 139}

Others said it is a glad tiding given to the believer in this world at death. Report 17757, from al-Zuhri and Qatada: it is the glad tiding at death in the life of this world. Report 17758, from al-Dahhak: he knows where he stands before he dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 140}

Al-Tabari says the soundest of the sayings is that God has told us that His God-fearing friends have glad tidings in the life of this world. Of this glad tidings is the good dream that a Muslim sees or that is seen for him, and of it is the angels' glad tidings to him as his soul departs, in God's mercy, as it was reported from the Prophet that the angels present say to his soul: come out to God's mercy and good pleasure. The editor says this is a report without a chain that he did not find in these words in a nearby place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 140}

Of it too is God's glad tidings to him of the great reward He promised in His Book and by the tongue of His messenger, as in [[passage/quran-2-25|2:25]]. Al-Tabari says all these meanings are God's glad tidings to him in the life of this world; God did not single out one over another, so all are included in His words, and in the next life it is Paradise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 141}

As for 'no change to the words of God', it means God does not break His promise nor change what He said, but carries out His promises for His creation and fulfils them. Report 17759, from Nafi': al-Hajjaj prolonged the sermon, and Ibn Umar laid his head in my lap; al-Hajjaj said Ibn al-Zubayr had changed the Book of God; Ibn Umar sat up and said: you cannot do that, nor can Ibn al-Zubayr; there is no change to the words of God. Al-Hajjaj said: you have been given knowledge, if only it benefited you. Ayyub said: when he turned on him over a matter touching himself, he fell silent. The editor says al-Hakim relates it, calls it sound and al-Dhahabi agreed, that he restored the sentence 'you have been given knowledge' from al-Mustadrak after the printed text and manuscript were corrupt, and he writes at length in praise of the Companions' courage and their rejection of wrong before governors in this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 141}

His words 'that is the great triumph' mean that these glad tidings in the life of this world and in the next are the great triumph, that is, gaining the need and the quest and deliverance from the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 142}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
