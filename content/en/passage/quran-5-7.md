**Commentary on verse 7: God's favour and his covenant (pp. 91-94)**

Al-Tabari says the sense is: remember, believers, God's favour upon you in the covenants you made with God for yourselves; remember his favour in that he guided you among covenants to what pleases him and enabled you to what saves you from error and ruin, among many other favours. Reports 11550 and 11551, from Mujahid, say that 'favours' are God's bounties. On 'his covenant by which he bound you', remember also his covenant by which he bound you, which is his pledge to you. Interpreters differed over which of his covenants is meant. Some said it is the covenant with the believing Companions of the Messenger of God when they pledged him hearing and obedience in what they liked and disliked, and to do all that God and his Messenger commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 91}

Report 11552, from Ibn Abbas, takes it to be when God sent the Prophet and revealed the Book to him and they said they believed in the Prophet and the Book and acknowledged what is in the Torah, so God reminded them of the covenant they had acknowledged and told them to keep it. Report 11553, from al-Suddi: God took our covenant and we said we hear and obey, upon belief in him and acknowledgement of him and his Messenger. Others said it is the covenant he took from his servants when he brought them out of Adam's loins and made them witness against themselves: am I not your Lord? They said: yes, we bear witness. Report 11554, from Mujahid, says it is the covenant by which he bound the children of Adam in Adam's back. The editor's note comments on the use of 'hayth' for 'hin'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 92}

Report 11555, from Mujahid, is similar. Al-Tabari then says the soundest view is that of Ibn Abbas, that the sense is: remember, believers, God's favour upon you in guiding you to Islam, and his covenant by which he bound you, his pledge when you gave his Messenger your oath of hearing and obedience in ease and dislike, hardship and plenty, when you said: we heard what you told us and obey what you commanded and forbade. Keep faith with God's covenant and he will keep faith with what he guaranteed you, completing his favour, admitting you to his garden and saving you from his punishment. He holds this sounder than the view of the covenant taken in Adam's loins, because after reminding the believers of his covenant, God mentions the covenant of the people of the Torah, in [[passage/quran-5-12|5:12]] and the verses after it, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 93}

alerting the Companions of the Messenger to where their share lies in keeping faith with God's pledge, making known the bad end of the People of the Book for neglecting his covenant, and warning them off breaking their pledges. Since the covenant used in the admonition was that of a people from whom it was taken after a messenger was sent and a book revealed, the state of the admonished must be like that of those they are admonished by, which shows the soundness of his view and the unsoundness of the other. 'fear God' is a warning from God to the believers among the Companions of his Messenger not to break his covenant by hiding in their hearts the opposite of what their tongues showed; fear God, lest you alter his pledge, for he sees what your breasts hide and would bring on you the punishment that fell on the Jews before you, transformation and every kind of retribution. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 94}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
