## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari explains "And We made for you livelihoods therein," which is the plural of livelihood. In "And for those for whom you are not a provider," interpreters differed: from Mujahid al-Dwab wa al-An'am, and from Mansur al-Wahsh in particular — based on this, "min" means "what," which is rare in Arab speech. Al-Tabari prefers that it refers to slaves, slaves, slaves, animals, and livestock, so the meaning is: We made for you 'Ma'ayesh' and made for you those whose livelihoods are not for you, as the Arabs place 'min' in the place of animals if they want to convey them to the children of Adam, and 'min' is in the accusative case as a conjunction with 'ma'aysh'; it is said to be a reduction on the kaf and meem in 'lakum', and it is thought that Mansur intended this meaning, which, although it has a basis, is somewhat distant, and he cites a verse for its ugliness.
