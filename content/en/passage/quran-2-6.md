## Summary
Verse 6 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari records that the exegetes differed over whom the verse concerns. From Ibn Abbas he reports that it is about those who disbelieve in what was sent down to Muhammad even if they say they believe in what came before, and that it was revealed about the Jews in the surroundings of Medina, as a rebuke for denying Muhammad's prophethood despite knowing it; he also reports that the opening of al-Baqara up to the hundredth verse was sent down about named Jewish rabbis and hypocrites of the Aws and Khazraj, and says he disliked lengthening the book by listing their names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 251}
A second report from Ibn Abbas, through Ali ibn Abi Talha, says the Prophet was eager that all people believe, and God told him that only those for whom happiness was decreed in the first remembrance would believe, and only those for whom misery was decreed would go astray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 252}
Al-Rabi' ibn Anas is reported to have said the two verses concern the leaders of the factions, who were killed at Badr, and he links them to two verses of Surat Ibrahim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 252}
Al-Tabari prefers Ibn Abbas's first reading. He reasons that the verse follows the account of the believers among the People of the Book, so it is fitting that an account of their disbelievers follow it, and that God argues against the rabbis of the Children of Israel by showing His Prophet what they hid and concealed, though he was unlettered and could neither write nor read, so that they would know the one who disclosed it is the one who sent down the book to Moses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 252} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 254}
He explains "sawa'" as balanced or equal, citing a verse of al-Anfal and lines of poetry, and says the question form in "have you warned them or not" stands in place of "which of the two" and is a statement in meaning; he also mentions a view of some Basran grammarians. He reads the verse as saying that warning and not warning are equal for the rabbis of the Jews of Medina who denied Muhammad's prophethood after knowing it and concealed his case, for they will not believe. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 256} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 257}
