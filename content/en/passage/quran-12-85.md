**Commentary on verse 85 (pp. 219-225)**

On "they said, by God, you will not cease to remember Joseph until you are consumed with illness or are among the perishing", al-Tabari says: the sons of Ya'qub who had returned to him from Egypt said, when he said "O my sorrow for Joseph": by God, you will not stop remembering Joseph. Report 19675, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "taft'a" means you wane from your love of him. Report 19676, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa: you wane from your love of him. Al-Tabari says: this is how al-Hasan said it in his report, and it is a mistake; it is rather "you do not cease to remember Joseph". The editor says al-Tabari's objection to al-Hasan ibn Muhammad's report shows that his source had something other than "you wane from his love", and he could not tell what the word was in al-Hasan's transmission. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 219}

Report 19677, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr and Waraqa: you do not wane from your love of him. Report 19678, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: you wane from your love of him. Report 19679, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa: you do not cease remembering Joseph. Report 19680, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki', Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima: you do not cease remembering Joseph, you do not wane from your love of him. Reports 19681 and 19682, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, and Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: you do not cease remembering Joseph; the editor notes that report 19682 is repeated in the printed text and the manuscript with its chain and wording, and removes the repetition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 220}

One says "ma fati'tu aqulu dhaka", and "ma fata'tu" is a dialect form, "afti'u" and "aftā", with the verbal nouns "fat'an" and "futuman"; the editor points out that he finds nothing in the dictionaries to support what al-Tabari says of this form and calls it very strange. "Ma afta'tu bihi" is also reported. Of this is a verse of Aws ibn Hajar describing the dust of horsemen that kept rising until it seemed like the tents of a windy day, for which the editor gives its source in his collection and another transmission of it; al-Tabari then cites another verse, also by Aws ibn Hajar. The rest of the explanation continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 220}

Al-Tabari cites a further verse of Aws ibn Hajar about horsemen that kept drawing near and hurrying, with the bare-headed and the slack following behind, meaning "they did not cease". The "la" has been dropped from "taft'a" though it is intended in the speech, because when the oath is followed by an affirmative statement it is not accompanied by negation and the lam that answers oaths is not omitted, as in "wa-llahi la-atiyannaka", while when what follows is negated it is met by "ma" or "la"; once its place is known it is dropped from the speech because the hearer understands the meaning. Of this is a verse of Imru' al-Qays in which he swears by God's oath that he will not leave his seat even if they cut his head and limbs there, dropping the "la" from "abrahu qa'idan", as in another verse, which the editor tends to ascribe to Tamim ibn Ubayy ibn Muqbil, swearing by the father of Dahma' that she will not cease to be dear among her people as long as a striker twists the fire-stick, meaning "la zalat". The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and corrects a word in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 221}

On "until you become harad", he says: until you are worn in body and bewildered in mind. The root meaning of "harad" is corruption in the body and mind from grief or love, as in a verse of al-Arji in which he says he is a man whom love persisted in and made harad, until he was worn out and sickness wasted him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 221}

By "fa-ahradani" he means it melted me and left me harad. One says "a man harad, a woman harad, a people harad, two men harad", in one form for masculine and feminine, dual and plural; some Arabs say "harid" for the masculine and "harida" for the feminine, and when it is used in that form it is made dual and plural, masculine and feminine, while "harad" is kept singular in every case and does not take the feminine because it is a verbal noun. Some reported by hearsay "a man muhrad" when he is in pain and recited a verse for it, whose poet the editor does not know and which he did not find; and a verse of Imru' al-Qays is said to be of this kind, describing a man with many camels who becomes muhrad like a young camel sick in its dwelling. The interpreters said the same: report 19683, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: "until you become harad" means the exhaustion of illness, worn out. Report 19684, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: short of death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 222}

Reports 19685 to 19690, from Mujahid: "harad" is what is short of death, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Fudayl and Layth; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; and al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa. Report 19691, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: until you wear out or grow old. Report 19692, from Qatada: until you are aged. Report 19693, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Abu Bakr al-Hudhali: aged. Reports 19694 and 19695, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muharibi and Juwaybir, and al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn and Hushaym: "harad" is the worn-out, perishing thing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 223}

Reports 19696 and 19697, from al-Dahhak, through Suwayd ibn Nasr, Ibn al-Mubarak, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman, and al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj: "harad" is the worn-out, the receding; the editor notes that the printed text has "worn and effaced" and that he keeps the manuscript reading. Report 19698, from al-Suddi: worn. Report 19699, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when Ya'qub mentioned Joseph, they, meaning his sons present at the time, said in ignorance and wrong, "by God, you will not cease to remember Joseph until you become harad", that is, corrupt with no mind, "or are among the perishing". Report 19700, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "harad" is the one returned to the lowest age so that he does not reason, or who perishes, being a perishing one before that. On "or are among the perishing" he says: or are among those who have perished by death. Report 19701, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Fudayl and Layth: death. Report 19702, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: of the dead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 224}

Reports 19703 and 19704, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', al-Muharibi and Juwaybir, and al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn and Hushaym: of the dead. Report 19705, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Awn and Abu Bakr al-Hudhali: of the dead. Reports 19706 to 19708, from Qatada and al-Suddi: or die; of the dead, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id; Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar; and Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 225}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
