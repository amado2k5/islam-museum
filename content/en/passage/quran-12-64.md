**Commentary on verse 64 (pp. 160-161)**

On "he said, shall I entrust him to you except as I entrusted you with his brother before? God is the best guardian and the most merciful of the merciful", al-Tabari says: their father Ya'qub said: shall I entrust you with your brother from your father, whom you ask me to send with you, except as I entrusted you with his brother Joseph before. The readers differed on "fa-llahu khayrun hifzan": most readers of Medina and some Kufans and Basrans read "hifzan", meaning God is the best of you in keeping; most readers of Kufa and some of Mecca read "hafizan", with an alif, taking "hafiz" as a specifying noun for "khayr", as one says "he is best as a man", the sense being God is best of you as guardian, with the kaf and mim dropped. Al-Tabari says the correct view is that these are two well-known readings, close in meaning, each read by people with knowledge of the Qur'an, and whichever the reader uses is right, because whoever describes God as best in keeping has described Him as the best keeper, and whoever describes Him as the best keeper has described Him as best in keeping. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 160}

"And He is the most merciful of the merciful", he says: God is the most merciful to His creation; He will have mercy on my weakness in my old age and my loneliness at the loss of my sons, and will not lose him, but will keep him until He returns him to me, out of His mercy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 161}
