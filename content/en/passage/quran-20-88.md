## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 88 to 89 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "They said, 'We did not break Your promise with our dominion, but we carried burdens of the ornament of the people and threw them.' Thus did the Samaritan throw them": The people of Moses said to Moses: We did not break Your promise, meaning by its appointed time is the covenant that was with them, as in Ibn Abi Nujayh, from Mujahid, regarding the phrase: "My promise," he said: My covenant, and that covenant and appointment is what we explained earlier. His saying: 'Bimalkanuna' indicates that they mistakenly acknowledged themselves and said: We did not pronounce ourselves correctly, nor did we control our affairs until we fell into the turmoil we fell into. The reciters differed in their reading: the majority of Medina's reciters read it as 'bimalkana' with a fatha on the mim, the majority of Kufa's reciters read it as 'bimlukna' with a damma on the mim, and some people of Basra read it as 'bimlukna' with a kasra. As for fatha and damma, they mean the same meaning, and they are by our power and ability, except that one is a verbal noun and the other is a noun. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd, and Ibn Jurayj.
