## Summary
Verse 236 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 236 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-236|2:236]]) by saying it means there is no blame on you in divorcing your wives so long as you have not had intercourse with them, and that "touching" here is a figure for intercourse: Ibn 'Abbas, touching is intercourse, but God uses what figure He wishes for what He wishes (5190); and from him, touching is marriage (5191). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 117} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 118} He gives the readers' difference: most of the people of Hijaz and Basra read it with a fatha on the first letter and no alif, from "I touched it", on the model of the agreed reading in the story of Maryam ([[passage/quran-3-47|3:47]]), while others read it with a damma and an alif after the mim, on the model of the agreed reading in the verse on zihar ([[passage/quran-58-3|58:3]]), taking it to mean each of the man and the woman doing it to the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 118} Al-Tabari holds that both readings are sound in meaning and agree in sense even though one has a little more in it, so neither can be judged better, and whoever reads either is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 119} He says it refers to women divorced before consummation in a marriage in which a dower was named, because every married woman has either a named dower or none, and from what the verse goes on to say it is known that it means the one with a named dower, since if it meant one without a fixed dower the next part would have no sensible meaning; so the sense is: no blame on you if you divorce those with a named dower before touching them, and those without before a dower is fixed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 119} The editor notes that in the manuscript Tabari's own base text read it with the alif. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 118}

He then explains the clause on fixing a due portion: or make a dower binding on them; Ibn 'Abbas says the due portion is the dower (5192); the root of the word is the obligatory, and he cites a line by al-Nabigha al-Ju'di, and says "the ruler fixed so-and-so two thousand" meaning he made it his due. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 120} He explains the command to provide a gift: give them from your property what they may enjoy, according to your means and standing, rich or poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 120} The interpreters differed over how much men were told to give. Some said the most is a servant, then silver, then clothing: Ibn 'Abbas (5193, 5194); al-Sha'bi, the middle gift for a divorced woman is her veil, her shift, her outer wrap and her covering sheet (5195, 5197), and Shurayh used to give five hundred; and Ibn 'Abbas, where a man marries a woman without naming a dower and divorces her before consummation, God told him to give her a gift according to his ease or hardship, a servant or the like if well off, and three garments or the like if poor (5196). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 121} The editor explains that the word for silver means minted dirhams. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 121}

The reports on the divorce gift in ([[passage/quran-2-236|2:236]]) continue. From al-Sha'bi, Shurayh used to give five hundred, and the middle gift is a woman's clothes at home, a shift, veil, wrap and cloak (5198-5200); from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, a man marries a woman without naming a dower, divorces her before consummation, and she has a gift in what is right but no dower, the least being three garments (5201); from Qatada the same, and for one who can afford it a wrap, cloak, shift and veil (5202); from 'Amir, he gives according to his means (5203). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 122} From the mother of Humayd ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, 'Abd al-Rahman gave Umm Abi Salama a black maid as a gift when he divorced her, and the Arabs call the gift "tahmim" (5204, 5205); from Ibn Sirin, he used to give a servant, upkeep or clothing, and al-Hasan ibn 'Ali gave ten thousand (5206); 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf gave a servant (5207); Ibn Shihab, the highest is a servant, the lowest clothing and upkeep (5208). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 123} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 124} Others said that if the couple differ the amount is half the dower of a woman like her, and this is the view of Abu Hanifa and his companions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 124}

Al-Tabari says the right view is what Ibn 'Abbas and those with him said: what is owed to the divorced woman is by the man's hardship or ease and not by the woman's standing; if it were by the dower of her like, up to half of it, the verse's mention of the man's means would have no sense; the dower of her like may be a great sum while the man is poor and owns nothing, and a judge who rules half of it would exceed God's ruling; it should instead go by the man's hardship or ease, not beyond a servant or its value if he is well off; if poor, the least clothing for her, three garments or so; and if unable, according to his ability, by the discretion of the just imam when it is brought to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 124} This is al-Tabari's choice, and the view of Abu Hanifa and his companions is given as theirs.

The interpreters then differed over whether the command to provide a gift is an obligation or a recommendation. Some said it is obligatory, enforced against the divorcer's property like other debts, for every divorced woman whoever she is: al-Hasan and Abu al-'Aliya, every divorced woman has a gift, whether consummated or not and even if a dower was fixed (5209, 5210, 5213); Sa'id ibn Jubayr (5211), citing the verse of the gift for divorced women ([[passage/quran-2-241|2:241]]), and (5212); al-Hasan on a woman with a fixed dower divorced before consummation, who has a gift, and when the questioner asked whether he did not read the verse on half of what was fixed ([[passage/quran-2-237|2:237]]) he said yes (5214). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 125} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 126}

Others said the gift is owed to every divorced woman except one with a fixed dower divorced before consummation, who has half the named dower and no gift: Ibn 'Umar (5215, 5216, 5225); Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, she had the gift in the verse of al-Ahzab ([[passage/quran-33-49|33:49]]), then when the verse in al-Baqara came down she was given half the dower if named and no gift, and if not named a gift (5217-5220); Mujahid (5221, 5222); Nafi' (5223); 'Ata' (5224); Shurayh, she has a gift within the half (5226, 5227). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 126} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 127} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 128} Others said the gift is a right of every divorced woman but in some cases it is enforced by judgment and in others it binds him only between him and God: al-Zuhri, there are two gifts, one the ruler enforces, the other a duty on the God-fearing (5228). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 128} Report 5229 begins at the foot of p. 128 and continues on the next page.
