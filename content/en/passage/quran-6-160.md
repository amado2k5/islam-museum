## Summary
Verse 160 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 160 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "whoever brings a good deed will have ten like it; whoever brings an evil deed will be repaid only with its like, and they will not be wronged", God says: whoever meets his Lord on the Day of Resurrection at the place of reckoning, of those who left their religion and became sects, with repentance, faith and desisting from the misguidance he persisted in, that is the good deed God mentions, and he will have ten like it, that is, ten good deeds like the deed he brought. "Whoever brings an evil deed" means whoever meets God on the Day of Resurrection having left the true religion and disbelieved in God will be repaid only with what grieves him, as he met God with his evil work. "They will not be wronged" means God does not wrong either party, neither the doers of good nor the doers of evil, by repaying the good with evil or the evil with good, but repays each party what is due to it, for He is wise and places nothing except where it deserves to be placed. Al-Tabari showed earlier that wrong means putting a thing in the wrong place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 274}

If one asks: if it is as you say, that "the good deed" here means faith in God, acknowledging His oneness and affirming His Messenger, and "the evil deed" means associating others with Him and denying His Messenger, does faith have likes by which the believer is repaid? And if it has, how is he repaid by it, when faith, in your view, is speech and action, and God's requital for it is honour in the Hereafter and the delights He has prepared for the people of His honour in the abode of eternity, which are things seen, felt and enjoyed, not speech that is heard nor the earning of limbs? The answer is that the meaning is not what you suppose; it is: whoever brings the good deed and meets God obedient with it will have the reward of ten good deeds like it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 275}

If he asks, does the saying "there is no god but God" have a like among good deeds? The answer is that it does, and it is the saying "there is no god but God"; that is what God promised whoever comes to Him with it, that He will repay him with a reward ten times what its sayer deserves; and likewise for whoever brings the evil deed that is shirk, except that its doer is repaid only what he deserves without multiplying. The note says the manuscript's wording here is unintelligible and that the editor placed the bracketed phrase so the reader would pause, fearing something had dropped from the text. He gives (14271) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that when the verse was revealed a man asked, is "there is no god but God" a good deed? He said yes, the best of good deeds; and (14272), (14273) and (14274) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that "the good deed" is there is no god but God and "the evil deed" is shirk. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 276}

He gives (14275) from Shaqiq that "the good deed" is there is no god but God, the word of sincerity, and "the evil deed" is shirk; (14276) from Mujahid and al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza that it is the word of sincerity and the evil deed is shirk and disbelief; (14277) from Ata; (14278) from Muhammad ibn Ka'b; and (14279), (14280) and (14281) from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i the same, and the note says it could not identify Abu al-Mujhil in the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 277}

He gives (14282) from Abu Ma'shar that Ibrahim used to swear by God, without making an exception, that "the good deed" is there is no god but God and "the evil deed" is shirk; (14283) from Ata, the word of sincerity; (14284) from Abu Salih; (14285) from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza that the evil deed is disbelief; (14286) from al-Dahhak; (14287) from al-Hasan; (14288) from Sa'id and (14289) from Mujahid that "the good deed" is there is no god but God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 278}

He gives (14290) from Ibn Abbas that "whoever brings the good deed" is whoever comes with there is no god but God and the evil deed is shirk; (14291) from Qatada that it was mentioned to him that the Prophet used to say deeds are six: two that make a thing obligatory, two that multiply and two that are likes. The two obligating: whoever meets God associating nothing with Him enters Paradise, and whoever meets Him associating others with Him enters the Fire. The two multiplying: a believer's spending in God's way is seven hundredfold, and his spending on his household is ten like it. The two likes: if a servant intends a good deed and does not do it, a good deed is written for him, and if he intends an evil and does it, one evil is written against him. And (14292) from Abu Dharr that he said, Messenger of God, teach me a deed that brings me nearer to Paradise and farther from the Fire; he said, when you do an evil deed, do a good one, for it is ten like it; I said, is "there is no god but God" among the good deeds? He said, it is the finest of good deeds. The note says the report is weak through the unknown shaykh from Taym. A group said the verse refers to the Bedouins, while the good deeds of the Emigrants are seven hundredfold or more. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 279}

He gives (14293) from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that this is for the Bedouins and for the Emigrants it is seven hundred, with a sound chain; (14294) from Abdullah ibn Umar that it was revealed about the Bedouins, and a man asked, what of the Emigrants? He said, something greater than that, and recited what is in [[passage/quran-4-40|4:40]], that God does not wrong by an atom's weight and, if it is a good deed, multiplies it and gives from Himself a great reward, adding that when God calls a thing great, it is great (the note says Atiyya al-Awfi, in the chain, is weak); and (14295) from al-Rabi' that it was revealed while they fasted three days a month and paid a tenth of their wealth, and the obligations came after, fasting Ramadan and zakat. If someone asks, how can it say "ten of its likes", adding the ten to the likes, which are themselves the likes, and can a thing be added to itself? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 280}

The answer is that it is added because the meaning is "ten good deeds like it"; the likes take the place of the thing counted and the ten is added to them, as one says "I have ten women"; and because the likes stand in for the deeds, the ten is put as the number of good deeds, and "mithl" is masculine but when put in the place of good deeds and since it serves for masculine and feminine it was made a substitute. One who says "I have ten of its likes" would not say "ten righteous things", because "righteous" is a deed that is not counted, while names are counted, and "mithl" is a name, so counting with it is allowed. It is reported that al-Hasan al-Basri read "he will have ten, its likes" with nunation and the nominative, a sound form in Arabic, but since the readers of the lands are against it al-Tabari does not allow going against them. The note corrects "number of verses" in the printed text and manuscript to "number of good deeds". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 281}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
