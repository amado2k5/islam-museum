**Commentary on verse 47: "let the people of the Gospel judge by what God sent down in it", pp. 374-376**

Al-Tabari says the readers differed in reading "let them judge": the readers of the Hijaz, Basra and some of Kufa read it with a sukun on the lam, as a command from God to the people of the Gospel to judge by what was sent down in it, with something omitted in the sentence, and a group of the people of Kufa read it with a kasra on the lam, meaning so that the people of the Gospel judge. He holds that they are two famous readings of close sense, so whichever one reads is right.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 374}

For God sent down no book to a prophet but that its people act by it and He commanded them to act by it; so too the Gospel, and it makes no difference whether it is read as a command with sukun or as a statement with kasra, since their senses agree. What is reported from Ubayy ibn Ka'b of a command reading is not soundly transmitted, and even if sound it would not make the other reading forbidden. So the sense with kasra is: We gave Jesus the Gospel with guidance and light, confirming the Torah, guidance and counsel for the God-fearing, so that the people of the Gospel might judge by what We sent down in it, but they changed its ruling and opposed it and went astray by opposing it; "those are the sinners", the ones who go out from God's command and contradict what He commanded and forbade in His book.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 375}

When read with sukun the sense is that We commanded its people to judge by what We sent down in it, but they did not obey Our command and opposed it, and those who opposed are the sinners. Ibn Zayd used to say that "sinners" here and elsewhere means the liars, and report 12103 has him say that whoever of the people of the Gospel did not judge thus, those are the sinners, the liars, reciting [[passage/quran-49-6|49:6]] where the sinner is the liar. Al-Tabari has explained the sense of sinning before. The editor notes that here ended a part of the old division of the manuscript from which the copy was made, the copyist closing it with a prayer and opening the next with the opening invocation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 376}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
