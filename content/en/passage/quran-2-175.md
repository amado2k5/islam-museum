## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "those are the ones who bought error for guidance and punishment for forgiveness" al-Tabari says they took error and left guidance, and took what brings God's punishment and left what brings His forgiveness and pleasure; naming the punishment and forgiveness suffices without naming their cause; he has explained earlier the sense of "bought error for guidance". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 330}

On "how patient they are with the Fire" al-Tabari gives the interpreters' differences. Some said: how bold they are in the work that brings them near the Fire, from Qatada, al-Hasan, Mujahid, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-Rabi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 331} Others said: how they work the works of the people of the Fire, from Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 331} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 332} They differed on "ma": some said it is a question, what makes them endure the Fire when they left the truth, from al-Suddi, 'Ata', Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash and Ibn Zayd; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 332} others said it is wonder, how bold they are toward what earns them the Fire, from Mujahid, al-Hasan and Qatada, and he links it to "may man be destroyed, how ungrateful he is". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 333} Al-Tabari chooses the view "how bold they are toward the Fire", meaning toward its punishment and how they work the works of its people, since the Arabs say "how patient so-and-so is against God" meaning how bold, and God makes His creation wonder by reporting about these people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 333} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 334}
