**Commentary on verse 73 (pp. 84-87)**

Al-Tabari says: those who disbelieved in God and His messenger are allies of one another, that is helpers and supporters of one another and more entitled to each other than the believers in God and His messenger. He recalls the view that the sense is that they are more entitled to one another's inheritance than their believing kin, and promises the rest of what he has. Report 16343, from Abu Malik, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan and al-Suddi: a man asked whether they should let their polytheist kin inherit, and the verse came down. Report 16344, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: it came down about the inheritances of the polytheists bound by a treaty. The editor corrects a garbled wording in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 84}

Report 16345, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb, on the passage from the believers who did not emigrate to "great corruption": the emigrant believer and the believer who had not emigrated did not inherit from each other even if they were believing brothers, because this religion was small in this land until the day of the conquest; when the conquest came and emigration ended they inherited by kinship wherever they were; the Prophet said there is no emigration after this conquest and recited the verse on those bound by kinship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 85}

Others said the sense is that the unbelievers support one another, and that one who stays in the land of war without emigrating cannot be a believer. Report 16346, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: a man would settle between Muslims and polytheists saying that if these prevailed he was with them and if those prevailed he was with them, and God refused him that and sent down this verse; it was said that the fire of a Muslim and the fire of a polytheist must not be in sight of each other, except for one who pays the poll tax and acknowledges the land tax. The editor explains that attributing sight to the fire is a figure for neighbourhood. Report 16347, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: God urged the believers to keep close ties, making the Emigrants and Helpers people of alliance in religion to the exclusion of others, and the unbelievers allies of one another; the editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 85}

On the closing clause of the verse, which warns of strife and great corruption if "it" is not done, the interpreters differed. Some said: unless you, believers, do what you were commanded, the Emigrants inheriting from one another through emigration and the Helpers through faith, to the exclusion of their bedouin Muslim kin and of unbelievers, there will be strife, a trial occurring in the land because of it, and great corruption, that is disobedience of God. Report 16348, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: unless you do this and leave them to inherit as they did; the Messenger accepted faith only with emigration. Report 16349, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: it means in inheritance, unless you take what I commanded in inheritance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 86}

Others said: unless you, believers, support one another in religion, there will be strife and great corruption. Report 16350, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: He made the Emigrants and Helpers people of alliance in religion to the exclusion of others and made the unbelievers allies of one another, then said the believer must ally with the believer rather than the unbeliever even if the latter is kin, or there will be confusion between truth and falsehood and corruption will spread when believers ally with unbelievers; then He returned inheritances to kin. The editor explains that the sentence was muddled in the printed text and manuscript by the repetition of the word "believer" and that he established it from Ibn Hisham's Sira. Report 16351, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: unless you cooperate and support one another in religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 87}

Al-Tabari then chooses, between the two readings of "those who disbelieved are allies of one another", the view that they support one another rather than the believers, and he finds in it an indication that God forbade the believer to remain in the land of war without emigrating: the known sense of "ally" in Arabic is helper and supporter, or cousin and kinsman, whereas "heir" is not a known sense except as the one who follows to carry out his inheritance after him, which is remote though the wording can bear it, and God's words should be directed to the most evident and well known sense. On that basis the better reading of "if you do not do it" is: unless you do what I commanded of cooperation and support for religion, since the verse begins by urging alliance in religion and mutual support, so it should end with it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 87}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
