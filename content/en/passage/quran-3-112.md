## Summary
Verse 112 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 112 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Being made to bear abasement (p. 110).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that the Jews who deny Muhammad are made to bear abasement wherever they are on earth, in whatever spot, in the lands of Muslims or polytheists, except with the rope mentioned after. The word for abasement is a form of "humiliation", which he explained with evidence elsewhere, and "caught" means met. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of both words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 110}

**Al-Hasan on abasement (7630, 7631, pp. 110-111).** From Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Hawdha, Awf, al-Hasan (7630): this community reached them while the Magians were collecting poll-tax from them. From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7631): God abased them so they have no protection and put them under the feet of the Muslims. The editor says the copyist dropped the wrath clause in the verse and he restored it on the form of recitation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 110} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 111}

**The meaning of the rope (7632-7642, pp. 111-113).** Al-Tabari says the rope here is the means by which they are safe from believers in their persons, property and children: a covenant and safe-conduct concluded for them before they were caught in the lands of Islam. He relates: from Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7632): a covenant, and the second rope is their own covenant; from Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7633): a covenant from God and a covenant from people; from al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar, Qatada (7634): the same; from Humayd ibn Mas'ada, Yazid, Uthman ibn Ghiyath, Ikrima (7635): the same; from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad, Asbat, al-Suddi (7636); from Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7637); from Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his father, Ibn Abbas (7638): a covenant from God and from people, as a man says "the pledge of God and the pledge of His messenger", which is the compact; from al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Mujahid (7639) in the same way, and Ata said the covenant is the rope. From Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Ibn Zayd (7640): a covenant, and they are Jews; this is like the saying of Abu al-Haytham ibn al-Tayyihan to the Prophet when the Ansar came to him at al-Aqaba, that they were cutting ropes between themselves and the people, meaning covenants; the Jews are safe in no land of God except by this rope. Ibn Zayd links it with [[passage/quran-3-55|3:55]], where the followers of the Messiah are set above those who disbelieved, so that wherever Christians are they are above the Jews, east or west, the Jews being abased in all lands, and with [[passage/quran-7-168|7:168]] on their being divided across the earth into communities. From Abu Mu'adh, Ubayd ibn Sulayman, al-Dahhak (7641) and from Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid, Juwaybir, al-Dahhak (7642): a covenant from God and a covenant from people. The editor notes that 7640 appeared in abridged form at no. 7155, and that the name Uthman ibn Ghiyath follows the printed text, which he judges correct over the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 111} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 112} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 113}

**The grammarians on the connective: the Kufan view (pp. 113-114).** Al-Tabari says the people of Arabic differed over what brings the connective "by" in the phrase. A Kufan grammarian, who the editor says is al-Farra (Ma'ani al-Qur'an 1:230), said an omitted verb brought it, the sense being: abasement is laid on them wherever they are caught unless they hold fast to a rope from God, the verb being left unsaid. He cited two lines: the first by Humayd ibn Thawr al-Hilali, about his camel which, when he came to her with her two ropes, shied away in fear, and he said the poet meant "came with her two ropes"; the second by Abu al-Tamahan al-Qayni, Hanzala ibn al-Sharqi, one of the long-lived (also ascribed to Adi ibn Zayd and to al-Misjah ibn Siba al-Dabbi), about a stalking hunter whom a viewer would think shackled though he is not, whom he glossed as meaning shackled with a shackle. The editor gives the places of both lines in Humayd's Diwan, Kitab al-Mu'ammarin, al-Farra's Ma'ani, al-Aghani, al-Buhturi's Hamasa, al-Qali's Amali, al-Sharif's Amali and others, with the variant transmission in the Diwan and the sense of two words describing the camel as sharp-spirited and easily startled, a praise and not a blame; says the printed text and manuscript gave only the first line, that dropping the witness line is the copyist's lapse so he restored it with al-Farra's remark, and corrects a misreading of the word for hunter, glossing it as one who stalks game, hidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 113} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 114}

**Al-Tabari's reply (p. 114).** He says requiring a omitted verb to act and its connected phrase to be displayed while the verb is dropped is weak in the Arabs' usage and remote from their speech, and that the lines do not prove the claim, since in the poet's phrase that she saw him with her two ropes there is plain indication that she saw him holding the rope, so what is stated needs no mention of holding, and the connective belongs to the verb of seeing, as in someone's saying "I am with God", self-sufficient and understood by the hearer, with no need for the connective to have a different governing word than the one expressed. The editor explains the technical term for the connected phrase and corrects the printed wording of the comparison. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 114}

**The Basran and other Kufan views (p. 115).** A Basran grammarian said the exception departs from the first part of the sentence, and is no harder than the exception in [[passage/quran-19-62|19:62]]. Others of the Kufans said it is a connected exception: abasement is laid on them everywhere except where there is a rope from God, as one might say abasement is laid on them in all places except this one. Al-Tabari answers that if it were connected, abasement would not be laid on them when they are caught with a covenant and pledge, which is not the Jews' description, since wherever they are caught, with a rope or without one, abasement is laid on them as the interpreters said, and this contradicts what God described; so that view is shown to be mistaken. The editor notes that some lines of this page are damaged in the scanned text, so only what is clear is given here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 115}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 115-116).** He says the right view is that the connective is brought by a verb that requires it and that the exception is disconnected from the first part, the sense being: but they are caught with a rope from God and a rope from people, as in [[passage/quran-4-92|4:92]], where the exception for error, though accusative by what acts before the exception, is not connected in the sense that he may kill him so, but means: but he may kill him by error. So here too the sense is not that abasement lifts from them if they are met with a covenant; abasement is fixed in every case, and the meaning is as stated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 115} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 116}

**Wrath, destitution and the killing of prophets (pp. 116-117).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that they took on God's wrath and returned with it, deserving it; he has explained its root with evidence, the meaning of destitution as the humility of need and poverty and its submissiveness, and the meaning of God's wrath, in earlier volumes. The distant pointing word means their returning with God's wrath and abasement laid on them is in exchange for their denying God's signs and proofs of His prophets' truthfulness and the duties He imposed, and for killing their prophets and God's messengers to them, in transgression against Him and bold falsehood, without any right that made them deserve killing. The editor refers to the earlier notes on the connective, wrath, "laid upon them" and destitution. For him the verse's sense is: they are made to bear abasement wherever they are met, except by a pledge from God and a pledge from people, and returned with God's wrath, bearing it, and made to bear the humiliation of need and the submissiveness of poverty, in exchange for denying His signs, proofs and arguments and killing His prophets without right, wrongfully and in transgression. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 116} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 117}

**Disobedience and transgression (7643, pp. 117-118).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God did that to them for their disbelief, killing of the prophets, disobedience of their Lord and transgression of His command, whose meaning he explained in many places before. He says God informed His servants what He did to these people of the Book, bringing abasement and disgrace on them in the present world besides what He stored for them later of punishment and painful torment, as a warning of the place of trial that whoever guards against comes to repentance, and an admonition to our community not to follow their ways and courses lest what befell them befall it. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7643): avoid disobedience and transgression, for by them those before you perished. The editor keeps the manuscript reading in the note and judges it better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 117} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 118}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 118}
