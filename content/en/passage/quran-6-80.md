## Summary
Verse 80 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 80 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "his people disputed with him; he said: do you dispute with me about God when he has guided me? I do not fear what you associate with him, unless my Lord wills something; my Lord's knowledge embraces everything; will you not remember?" al-Tabari says: Abraham argued with his people about God's oneness and his freedom from the idols, their argument with him being their saying that their gods, whom they worship, are better than his God. Abraham said "do you dispute with me about God" says: do you argue with me over my affirming God's oneness and making my work pure for him apart from all other gods, "when he has guided me" says: my Lord gave me success in knowing his unity and showed me the road of truth until I was certain nothing deserves to be worshipped but him; "I do not fear what you associate with him" says: I do not dread from your gods, whom you call on besides him, anything that would reach me in myself of harm or evil. That is because they said to him: we fear that our gods will touch you with harm, leprosy or madness, for your speaking ill of them; and Abraham said to them: I do not fear these gods you associate with God, that they reach me with harm or hurt, since they neither benefit nor harm; "unless my Lord wills something" says: but my fear is of God who created me and created the heavens and the earth, for if he wills to touch me in myself or my property with what he wills of perishing or lasting, increase or decrease, or otherwise, he does so, since he is able. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 489}

The editor refers for "disputing" and "guidance" to earlier passages, and says the printed text and manuscript have a word that has no sense, the right reading being as he set it.

Ibn Jurayj said the like (13466): his people called gods beside God and frightened him with their gods, that madness might strike him from them, and Abraham said "do you dispute with me about God when he has guided me?", he said I have come to know my Lord, I do not fear what you associate with him. "My Lord's knowledge embraces everything" says: my Lord knows everything, nothing hidden from him, as he is the creator of everything, not like the gods that neither harm nor benefit nor understand anything and are only carved wood and a shaped image; "will you not remember?" says: will you not take heed, ignorant ones, and see the error of what you persist in, worshipping a shaped image and carved wood that cannot harm or benefit and neither understands nor reasons, and leaving the worship of him who created you and created everything, in whose hand is good and who is able over everything and knows everything. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 489}

The editor says the printed text has "touch me in myself" dropping "with it", which the manuscript has, the publisher having misread the verb and dropped it; and refers for "capacity" and "remembering" to earlier passages.

Verse 80 closes here on p. 489, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-81|6:81]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
