## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "Then We sent Our messengers in succession; every nation whose messenger came back, they denied it, so We followed some of them and made them hadiths, and then separated them as a people who do not believe": "Then We sent" to the nations We established after Thamud. "Our messengers follow one another," meaning: some follow one another, and some follow one another. It is from the mutatarah, and it is a plural noun like 'something'; it is not said: so-and-so came to me with tatra, nor is it said: so-and-so came to me as a mutawatirah, which is a tanun but not a tanun, and it contains a ya. Whoever does not intend it is on the one who is tatarat. Whoever says 'tatra' mistakenly thinks the ya' is original, as it is said: ma'zza with ya, ma'zat, and bahmi biham, and the like. Sometimes it is performed and sometimes its application is omitted. Whoever makes it 'fa'la' pauses on it, wishing to kasra, and whoever makes it an alif of grammatical grammar does not point to it, because the grammatical alif is not broken. It is not said: 'I saw Zayd,' which refers to the kasra. In the section, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Ibn Zayd.
