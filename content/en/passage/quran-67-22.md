## Al-Tabari's commentary

His statement, “Is he who walks prone on his face better guided, or he who walks upright on a straight path?” explains: “Is he who walks,” O people, “prone on his face,” unable to see what is in front of him, or to his right and left, “better guided”: more upright on the path, and more guided, “or he who walks upright,” the way humans walk on their feet, “on a straight path,” meaning: on a path without any crookedness. It is said “prone” because it is an action that has not yet occurred. When it has not yet occurred, they add the letter “alif” to it, saying: “So-and-so prostrated himself on his face,” so he is prostrate. From this comes the saying of al-A'sha: “prostrate on his two sides.” He digs its root on the back of the naked one of the road, and he said: “He threw him down,” because it is an unrealized verb. If it were an unrealized verb, the alif would be omitted, so it would be said: “I threw so-and-so down on his face,” and “God threw him down on his face.” The passage quotes sayings of Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Qatadah.
