**Commentary on verse 67 (pp. 337-339)**

Al-Tabari says: the hypocrites, men and women, are those who show the believers faith with their tongues and hide unbelief in God and His Messenger; "some of them are of others" means they are one kind and their affair is one in announcing faith and concealing unbelief; "they command", those who accept from them, "the wrong", which is unbelief in God and in Muhammad and what he brought and denial of him, "and forbid the right", that is, they forbid them belief in God and His Messenger and in what he brought them from God. The editor refers to earlier explanations of hypocrisy, the wrong and the right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 337}

As for "and they close their hands", it means they hold their hands back from spending in God's way and withhold them from charity, so they refuse those for whom God made obligatory in their wealth the zakat their rights. Reports 16923, 16924, 16925 and 16926, from Mujahid, by several routes through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ishaq, Abdallah, Warqa', al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they do not stretch them out for spending in a right cause. Report 16927, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they do not stretch them out for good; report 16928, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they close their hands from every good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 338}

As for "they forgot God, so He forgot them", it means they abandoned God by not obeying Him and following His command, and so God abandoned them, withholding His success, guidance and mercy. We have shown earlier, with evidence, that forgetting means abandoning, which spares repeating it here. Report 16929, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they were forgotten of good and not forgotten of evil. As for "the hypocrites are the transgressors", it means that those who deceive the believers by showing them with their tongues belief in God while concealing unbelief are the ones who have departed from obedience to God and gone out of belief in Him and His Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 339}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
