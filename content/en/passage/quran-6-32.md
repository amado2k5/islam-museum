## Summary
Verse 32 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 32 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

This is God's denial of these unbelievers who deny the raising after death in their saying "there is nothing but our present life and we shall not be raised"; he says, belying their saying: the present life, people, is only play and amusement, that is, whoever seeks the pleasures of the life brought near to you in this abode, its delights and joys, and whoever takes pleasure in it and competes for it, is only in play and amusement, for it will soon pass from the one who enjoys it and takes his pleasure in its delights, or the days will bring him their calamities and turns and it will turn bitter and cloudy for him, like the player and amuser whose amusement and play quickly vanish and leave regret and grief; he says: do not be deceived by it, people, for he who is deceived by it will soon regret. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 329}

The editor refers for "present life" to volume 1, sets out the construction of "whoever seeks the pleasures of life ... its delights and joys in it", the raised "and whoever takes pleasure in it" being joined, keeps "it will turn" from the manuscript, from bitterness, meaning it becomes bitter after sweetness and cloudy after clarity, and keeps "soon".

"The abode of the hereafter is better for those who fear": working in obedience to him and preparing for the next abode with good deeds whose benefits remain for their people and whose people's joy there lasts is better than the abode that soon perishes, where no joy remains for those who work in it nor does delight last for them; "for those who fear" means those who fear God and so guard against him by obeying him, avoiding disobedience and hastening to his pleasure; "will you not understand?" means: do these who deny the raising not understand the truth of what we tell them, that the present life is play and amusement, though they see those among them cut off and those who perish and die, and those on whom misfortunes fall and who are stricken by calamities? In that, for one who reasons, is a warning and restraint from relying on it and making the soul its slave, and a clear proof that it has a director and disposer to whom creation owes pure worship, with nothing joined to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 330}

The editor explains "ukhturima" as death having taken the man from among his companions and left a gap in their rank.

Verse 32 closes here at p. 330, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-33|6:33]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
