**Commentary on verse 7 (pp. 141-145)**

Al-Tabari says: how, believers, and in what sense, can the polytheists have a covenant and protection with God and His messenger to be kept to, leaving them safe to roam the land? The sense is that they have no covenant and the believers must kill them wherever they find them, except those who gave a covenant at the Sacred Mosque, for God told the believers to keep faith with them and to be straight with them in it as long as they are straight with the believers. The interpreters differed over who is meant by those covenanted at the Sacred Mosque. Some said they are a people from Judhayma ibn al-Dil. Report 16490, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: they are the Banu Judhayma ibn al-Dil; the editor notes that the name is given differently here and in report 16491. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 141}

Report 16491, from Muhammad ibn Abbad ibn Ja'far, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they are Judhayma of Bakr of Kinana; the editor says Judhayma of Kinana are the Banu Judhayma ibn Amir ibn Abd Manat ibn Kinana, the people of al-Ghumaysa' on whom Khalid ibn al-Walid fell after the conquest, and that the Prophet sent Ali to make good Khalid's mistake, paying the blood money and compensating for property taken, and refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. Report 16492, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: how can the polytheists who with you were under the general covenant, that you would not frighten each other in the sanctuary or the sacred month, have a covenant with God and His messenger, except those you covenanted at the Sacred Mosque, the tribes of Banu Bakr who entered the covenant of Quraysh made at al-Hudaybiyya for the term between the Messenger and Quraysh; only this clan of Quraysh and the Banu al-Dil of Bakr broke it, and the covenant was to be completed to its term for those of Bakr who had not broken theirs. The editor explains the word for sanctity and gives the reading of the manuscript and the Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 142}

Others said they are Quraysh. Report 16493, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they are Quraysh; report 16494, from him, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the people of Mecca; report 16495, from him, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: they are a people who had a term with the Prophet, and no polytheist should enter the Sacred Mosque or avoid paying the poll tax to the Muslim, and "so long as they are straight with you" means the covenant polytheists. Report 16496, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: these are Quraysh, and this was abrogated by the months set for them; they betrayed and were not straight, so after the conquest they were given four months to choose, either to become Muslim or to join whatever land they wished, and they became Muslim before the four months were up and before any killing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 143}

Report 16497, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it was the day of al-Hudaybiyya; they were not straight and broke their covenant, that is they helped Banu Bakr, Quraysh's allies, against Khuza'a, the Prophet's allies. The editor explains that when the truce was written between the Prophet and Quraysh at al-Hudaybiyya, Banu Bakr said they were in Quraysh's pact and Khuza'a said they were in Muhammad's pact, and that after a time Banu Bakr and Quraysh joined against Khuza'a, one cause of the march on Mecca and its conquest. Others said they are a people of Khuza'a. Report 16498, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Ibn Uyayna and Ibn Jurayj: the covenant people of Khuza'a. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 144}

Al-Tabari then chooses as soundest the view that they are some of Banu Bakr of Kinana who remained in their covenant and did not join in breaking the covenant made between the Prophet and Quraysh at al-Hudaybiyya, which Quraysh broke by helping their allies of Banu al-Dil against the Prophet's allies of Khuza'a. His reason is that God told His prophet and the believers to complete the covenant with those who had covenanted at the Sacred Mosque as long as they were straight, and it has been shown that Ali proclaimed these verses in the ninth year, a year after the conquest of Mecca, when no unbeliever of Quraysh or Khuza'a in Mecca held a covenant with the Prophet to be kept, since those living there had broken it and been fought before these verses came down. Of "God loves the pious" he says it means God loves whoever fears God and watches Him in performing his duties, keeping his covenant with those he covenanted, avoiding disobedience and refraining from betraying covenants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 145}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
