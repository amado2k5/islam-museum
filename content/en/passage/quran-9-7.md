**Commentary on verse 7 (pp. 141-142)**

Al-Tabari says: how, believers, and in what sense, can the polytheists have a covenant and protection with God and His messenger to be kept to, leaving them safe to roam the land? The sense is that they have no covenant and the believers must kill them wherever they find them, except those who gave a covenant at the Sacred Mosque, for God told the believers to keep faith with them and to be straight with them in it as long as they are straight with the believers. The interpreters differed over who is meant by those covenanted at the Sacred Mosque. Some said they are a people from Judhayma ibn al-Dil. Report 16490, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: they are the Banu Judhayma ibn al-Dil; the editor notes that the name is given differently here and in report 16491. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 141}

Report 16491, from Muhammad ibn Abbad ibn Ja'far, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they are Judhayma of Bakr of Kinana; the editor says Judhayma of Kinana are the Banu Judhayma ibn Amir ibn Abd Manat ibn Kinana, the people of al-Ghumaysa' on whom Khalid ibn al-Walid fell after the conquest, and that the Prophet sent Ali to make good Khalid's mistake, paying the blood money and compensating for property taken, and refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. Report 16492, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: how can the polytheists who with you were under the general covenant, that you would not frighten each other in the sanctuary or the sacred month, have a covenant with God and His messenger, except those you covenanted at the Sacred Mosque, the tribes of Banu Bakr who entered the covenant of Quraysh made at al-Hudaybiyya for the term between the Messenger and Quraysh; only this clan of Quraysh and the Banu al-Dil of Bakr broke it, and the covenant was to be completed to its term for those of Bakr who had not broken theirs. The editor explains the word for sanctity and gives the reading of the manuscript and the Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 142}

Others said they are Quraysh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 142}
