## Summary
Verse 30 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 30 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "a group He guided, and a group deserved misguidance", al-Tabari takes up these same reports given for the preceding verse to explain "as He began you, you will return", which are reports 14478 to 14481 filed under verse 29, and continues the matter in the next batch. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 382}

He gives (14482) from Abu al-Aliya that "as He began you, you will return" means they were returned to His knowledge of them; (14483) from Muhammad ibn Ka'b that whoever God began his creation upon wretchedness goes to what God began his creation upon even if he does the works of the happy, as Iblis did the works of the people of happiness and then went to what his creation was begun upon, and whoever's creation was begun upon happiness goes to what it was begun upon even if he does the works of the wretched, as the magicians did the works of the wretched and then went to what their creation was begun upon, and the note says he means Pharaoh's magicians who believed in Moses; (14484) and (14485) from Mujahid that the Muslim is raised a Muslim and the disbeliever a disbeliever, and the note says Waqa' ibn Iyas Abu Yazid is spoken against; and (14486) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that it means as it was written for you, so you will be. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 383}

(14487) the like from Sa'id; (14488) from al-Suddi that it means as We created you, a group guided and a group astray, so you return and come out of your mothers' wombs; (14489) from Jabir that the Prophet said every soul is raised on what it was on, and the note says the chain is sound and that Muslim gave it in the wording "every servant is raised on what he died upon" and Ibn Majah "people are gathered on their intentions"; (14490) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that as it was written for you, you will be; (14491) from Mujahid that the believer is raised a believer and the disbeliever a disbeliever; and (14492) and (14493) from him that it means wretched and happy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 384}

Others said the meaning is: as He created you when you were nothing, you will return after annihilation. He gives (14494) and (14495) from al-Hasan that it means as He began you when you were nothing and gave you life, so He will make you die and then give you life on the Day of Resurrection; (14496) from Qatada that He began their creation when they were nothing, then they went, then He will restore them; (14497) from Ibn Abbas that it means as We created you the first time, so you return; (14498) from Mujahid that He will give you life after your death; and (14499) from Ibn Zayd that as He created them first, so He will restore them last. Al-Tabari says the soundest of the views is that of those who said the meaning is: as God began you as a creation after you were nothing, you will return after your annihilation as a creation like it, and He will gather you to the Day of Resurrection, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 385}

because God commanded His Prophet to inform by what is in this verse a people of idolaters of the Jahiliyya who did not believe in the return or affirm the resurrection, and so He commanded him to call them to acknowledge that God will raise them on the Day of Resurrection, reward whoever obeyed and punish whoever disobeyed; so He said to him, say to them, my Lord commanded justice, and that you set your faces right at every place of prayer, and that you call on Him devoting your religion to Him, and that you acknowledge that as He began you, you will return; the "and that you acknowledge that" is left out, as the "that" with "set right" was left out, since what is mentioned points to what was dropped. That being so, there is no sense in commanding the calling of one who denies the resurrection after death to acknowledge the manner in which those who are raised are raised; that call is made only to one who affirms the resurrection, whereas one who denies it is called to acknowledge it and then taught the conditions of the resurrection. Moreover, the report from the Messenger of God shows it: he gives (14500) and (14501) from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet, that he said people will be gathered naked and uncircumcised, and the first to be clothed is Abraham, and then he recited what is in [[passage/quran-21-104|21:104]], "as We began the first creation, We shall repeat it"; and (14502) from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet stood among them with an exhortation and said, O people, you will be gathered {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 386}

to God barefoot and uncircumcised, and then recited the same verse. The note says al-Bukhari, Muslim, Ahmad and al-Nasa'i gave the report, and that "ghurl" is the plural of "aghral", the uncircumcised. This shows the soundness of what al-Tabari said, that the creation return to God on the Day of Resurrection as living creation, as He began them in the world as living creation. One says "bada'a Allahu al-khalq yabda'uhum" and "abda'ahum yubdi'uhum ibda'an" for "He created them", two eloquent forms. Then He began a report of what preceded of His knowledge of His creation and what His decree brought about in them, saying: God guided a group of them, enabling them to righteous works, so they are guided, and misguidance became due on a group of them, away from guidance and right, by their taking Satan as an ally besides God. On that interpretation the first "group" is accusative by the working of "guided" on it, and the second by "became due" falling on its returning pronoun in "on them", as in [[passage/quran-76-31|76:31]], "He admits whom He wills to His mercy, and for the wrongdoers He has prepared a painful punishment"; the note refers to al-Farra's words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 387}

Whoever took the interpretation to be that, as He began you in the world as two kinds, disbeliever and believer, so you return in the Hereafter as two groups, a group guided and a group on whom misguidance became due, put the first "group" in the accusative by "you return" and made the second coordinated with it; and al-Tabari has shown what he thinks right on it. Then, on "they have taken the devils as allies besides God, and think they are guided", he says: the group on whom misguidance became due strayed from God's way and deviated from the straight course only by taking the devils as helpers and supporters besides God, out of ignorance of the error of what they were doing; rather they did it thinking they were on guidance and truth and that what they did was right. He holds this one of the clearest proofs against the saying of those who claim that God does not punish anyone for a disobedience he committed or a misguidance he held unless he does it after knowing the right way of it and commits it in stubbornness against his Lord; for were that so, there would be no difference between the group of misguidance who strayed while thinking themselves guided and the group of guidance, whereas God distinguished between their names and rulings in this verse. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "group", "ally" and "thinking". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 388}
