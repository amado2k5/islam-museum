**Commentary on verse 10 (pp. 30-33)**

He then explains "Their call in it will be: Glory be to You, O God, and their greeting in it will be: Peace, and the last of their call will be: Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds". The word for "their call" means their supplication, so their supplication there is "Glory be to You, O God"; the editor refers to the gloss on the word in the twelfth volume. Report 17563, from Ibn Jurayj, who says he was told that when a bird they desire passes them they say "Glory be to You, O God", and that is their call; the angel brings them what they desired and greets them, and they answer him, which is "their greeting in it will be peace"; when they have eaten they praise God their Lord, which is "the last of their call is: Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds". Report 17564, from Qatada: that is what they say there. Report 17565, from Sufyan: when they want something they say "O God", and what they called for comes to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 30}

"Glory be to You, O God" means exalting You, Lord, above what the polytheists ascribe to You of falsehood and slander against You; the editor refers to the gloss on "glory be" in the fourteenth volume. He then gives the reports: 17566, from Atiyya and others, that "glory be to God" is exalting God; 17567, from Musa ibn Talha, that the messenger of God was asked about "glory be to God" and said it is clearing God of evil; 17568, that Ibn al-Kawwa asked Ali about it and he said it is a word God chose for Himself; 17569, close to 17567 from Musa ibn Talha, with "exalting God above evil"; 17570, from Talha ibn Ubayd Allah, that he asked the messenger of God for the explanation of "glory be to God" and was told it is exalting God above all evil; and 17571, to that effect from Musa ibn Talha through his father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 31}

In the notes the editor identifies Sufyan al-Thawri, Uthman ibn Abd Allah ibn Mawhab (a trusted follower) and Musa ibn Talha (a trusted follower), says report 17567 is a mursal report that comes with a full chain in the next, and that the chains of these reports do not stand; he judges 17570 to have a ruined chain and 17571 a weak one, noting the narrators: Abd al-Rahman ibn Hammad is called of rejected traditions, Hafs ibn Sulayman weak, Talha ibn Yahya trusted by Ibn Ma'in but called of rejected traditions by al-Bukhari, and Sulayman ibn Ayyub transmitted rejected reports. These judgements are the editor's, not the museum's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 32}

Then "their greeting in it will be peace": the greeting of one to another is "peace", meaning you are safe and secure from what the people of the Fire are tried with; the editor refers to the gloss on "greeting" in the eighth volume. The Arabs also call kingship "tahiyya", and he cites two lines, one by Amr ibn Ma'dikarib about visiting Abu Qabus until he can halt his troops at his "greeting", that is his kingdom, and one by Zuhayr ibn Janab al-Kalbi saying he has obtained all that a young man can obtain except the "greeting", that is kingship (both described, not quoted); the editor lists where they are transmitted in the Amali, the Lisan and elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 33}

On "the last of their call is: Praise be to God, Lord of the worlds": the last of their supplication is that they say "praise be to God, Lord of the worlds"; that is why "that" is lightened and not doubled, since it is meant as a quotation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 33}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
