**Commentary on verse 99 (pp. 264-266)**

On "when they entered upon Joseph, he took his parents to himself and said, enter Egypt, God willing, in security", al-Tabari says: when Ya'qub, his children and their families entered upon Joseph, "he took his parents to himself", that is, joined them to himself, and said to them "enter Egypt, God willing, in security". If someone asks: how could Joseph say to them "enter Egypt" after they had entered it, when God reports that when they entered upon Joseph and he took his parents to himself, he said this to them? It is said: the interpreters differed on this. Some said that Ya'qub and his sons entered upon Joseph, and Joseph took his parents to himself, before entering Egypt; they say Joseph met his father out of honour to him before he entered Egypt, took him in, and then said to him and those with him "enter Egypt, God willing, in security" before the entering. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "i'wa'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 264}

Report 19877, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: they carried their families and dependants to him, and when they reached Egypt Joseph spoke to the king above him, and he and the kings went out to meet them; when they reached Egypt he said "enter Egypt, God willing, in security", "when they entered upon Joseph, he took his parents to himself". Report 19878, from Farqad al-Sabakhi, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Ja'far ibn Sulayman: when the shirt was cast on his face he regained his sight and said "bring me all your family"; Ya'qub and Joseph's brothers were carried, and when he drew near Joseph was told that he had come near, and went out to meet him, and the people of Egypt rode with him, for they revered him. As the two drew near each other, Ya'qub was walking leaning on a son of his called Judah; Ya'qub looked at the horses and the people and said: Judah, is this Pharaoh of Egypt? He said: no, this is your son! When each came near the other, Joseph went to greet him first but was prevented, since Ya'qub had more right to that and was more excellent, and he said: peace upon you, O remover of my sorrows. The editor explains the form "dhahib" and calls it unusual. Report 19879, from Hajjaj, through al-Qasim and al-Husayn: it reached me that Joseph and the king went out with four thousand to meet Ya'qub and his sons; and Farqad al-Sabakhi is reported to the same effect through one who heard Ja'far ibn Sulayman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 265}

Others said that "God willing" is an exception to Ya'qub's words to his sons, "I will ask forgiveness for you", and is of the postponed that means advanced; they said the meaning of the speech is: he said, I will ask my Lord's forgiveness for you, God willing, He is the Forgiving, the Merciful; when they entered upon Joseph he took his parents to himself and said, enter Egypt, and raised his parents. Report 19880, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "I will ask my Lord's forgiveness for you, God willing, in security", with what lies between them being a matter of the Qur'an's ordering. Al-Tabari says Ibn Jurayj means that words came in between "I will ask my Lord's forgiveness for you" and "God willing", whereas their place in his view is right after "I will ask my Lord's forgiveness for you". He says: the correct view in our opinion is what al-Suddi said, that Joseph said it to his parents and the children and families with them before they entered Egypt, when he met them, because the apparent text is so; there is nothing to show that what Ibn Jurayj said is right, and no ground for moving anything of God's book from its place or delaying it without clear proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 266}

It is also said that "his parents" means his father and his maternal aunt, those who said so holding that Joseph's mother had died earlier and that Ya'qub then had with him her sister, his aunt, whom he had married after her. Report 19881, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: his father and his aunt. Others said it was his father and mother: report 19882, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: his father and mother. Al-Tabari says: the better of the two views is Ibn Ishaq's, because that is the more usual in people's usage and the accepted meaning of "abawayn", unless it is shown by binding proof that Joseph's mother had died before that, in which case it is to be accepted. On "and said, enter Egypt, God willing, in security", he means secure from what you were in, in your desert, of drought and dearth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 266}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
