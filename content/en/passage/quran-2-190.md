## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "fight in the way of God those who fight you, but do not transgress; God does not love the transgressors", al-Tabari says the commentators differed. One group said it is the first verse revealed commanding the Muslims to fight the polytheists, in which they were commanded to fight those of the polytheists who fought them and to hold back from those who held back, and that it was then abrogated by Bara'a (al-Tawba). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 561} From al-Rabi', it was the first verse revealed about fighting at Medina, and the Prophet fought those who fought him and held back from those who held back until Bara'a was revealed; 'Abd al-Rahman did not mention "Medina". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 561} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 562} From Ibn Zayd, this has been abrogated; he recited "and fight the polytheists all together as they fight you all together" from al-Tawba and said this is the abrogating verse, and recited the opening of Bara'a to the verse "when the sacred months have passed, kill the polytheists wherever you find them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 562}

Others said that it is rather God's command to the Muslims to fight the unbelievers and was not abrogated, and the transgression they were forbidden is the killing of women and offspring, a prohibition that stands today, so nothing of the ruling of this verse was abrogated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 562} From Yahya ibn Yahya al-Ghassani, I wrote to 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz asking about the verse and he wrote to me, "that concerns women, offspring and those of them who did not set up war against you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 562} From Mujahid, "fight in the way of God those who fight you" was for the companions of Muhammad, who were commanded to fight the unbelievers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 562} From Ibn 'Abbas, do not kill women, boys, an old man, or one who offers you peace and holds back his hand; if you do this you have transgressed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 563} From Sa'id ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz wrote to 'Adi ibn Arta'a, "I found a verse in the Book of God, 'fight in the way of God those who fight you, but do not transgress', meaning do not fight one who does not fight you, meaning women, boys and monks". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 563}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the sounder of the two views is that which 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz stated, because a claimant's claim that a verse is abrogated, when it may be non-abrogated, without evidence for his claim is arbitrary assertion, and he refers to his earlier explanation of abrogation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 563} He explains the verse on this basis: fight, believers, in the way of God, which is His path that He made clear and His religion that He legislated for His servants, and call to it by hand and tongue those who turn away and are arrogant until they return to obedience or give the jizya in submission if they are People of the Book; they were commanded to fight those of the unbelieving fighters who fight, not those who do not fight, their women and offspring. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 563} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 564} The meaning of "do not transgress", he says, is do not kill a child or woman or one who has given you the jizya among the two peoples of the Book and the Magians, and "God does not love the transgressors" are those who exceed His limits and treat as lawful what He forbade them, the killing of these. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 564}
