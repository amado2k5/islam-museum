**Commentary on verse 28 (pp. 297-300)**

On [[passage/quran-11-28|11:28]] al-Tabari reports Noah's saying to his people when they denied him and rejected the counsel from God he brought them: 'my people, do you see, if I am on a clear proof from my Lord', that is, on knowledge, recognition and explanation from God of what binds me to Him and is due from me of pure worship and giving up setting idols up beside Him in it, 'and He has given me mercy from Him', that is, granted me from Him success, prophethood and wisdom so that I believed in Him and obeyed Him in what He ordered and forbade, 'and it was made obscure to you'. The readers differed: most readers of Medina and some of Basra and Kufa read 'fa-'amiyat' with fatha and light mim, meaning the mercy was obscure to you so you were not guided to it to acknowledge it and affirm your messenger on it; most Kufan readers read 'fa-'ummiyat' with damma on the ayn and doubled mim, following the reading of Abd Allah, which is reported as 'fa-'ammaha 'alaykum'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 298}

Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two readings is that of those who read 'fa-'ummiyat 'alaykum' with damma and doubled mim, for the reason those who read it that way give and because it is close to the words 'if I am on a clear proof from my Lord and He has given me mercy from Him', which attributes the mercy to God, so that making it obscure to the others is likewise better attributed to Him. This is one of the words in which the Arabs turned the verb from its place, for it is man who is blind to seeing the truth, and the truth is not described as blind except by the usage that speech has come to, which is allowed like their sayings 'the ring went into my hand' and 'the boot into my foot'; the rest of the explanation is on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 298}

...like the foot entering the boot and the finger the ring, but they used it so since the meaning is known; the editor says this is an abridgement of al-Farra's saying. His words 'shall we compel you to it while you dislike it?' mean: shall we take you into Islam, when God has made it obscure to you, while you dislike our compelling you? We shall not do that, but leave your affair to God, so that He decides in your matter what He sees and wills. Report 18106, from Ibn Jurayj: Noah said 'my people, if I am on a clear proof from my Lord', I have known it and by it known His command and that there is no god but He; 'and He has given me mercy from Him' is Islam, guidance, belief, judgement and prophethood. Report 18107, from Qatada: by God, if the Prophet of God could have, he would have compelled his people to it, but he could not nor did he have the power. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 299}

Report 18108, from Abu al-Aliya: in Ubayy's reading 'shall we compel you to it from our own selves while you dislike it'; reports 18109 and 18110, from Ibn Abbas, that he read it so, Abd Allah explaining it as from ourselves; report 18111, from Ubayy b. Ka'b: 'from the midst of our hearts'. The editor says this reading with its addition to the verse is irregular, since it adds to the mushaf, and no one may read it, and that he thinks 'from our own selves' or 'from our hearts' is an explanation worked into the writing of the verse and not a reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 300}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
