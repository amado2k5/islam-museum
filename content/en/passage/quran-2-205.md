## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when he turns away he strives in the land to spread corruption": by "tawalla" al-Tabari means this hypocrite turns his back and leaves you, Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 237} From Ibn 'Abbas: when he goes out from you, he strives (3980); some said it means when he is angry, from Ibn Jurayj (3981). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 237} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 238} Al-Tabari takes the sense to be that when this hypocrite leaves you angry he acts in the land in what God forbade him, seeking disobedience of God, highway robbery and the corruption of the road for God's servants, as was recounted of al-Akhnas ibn Shariq al-Thaqafi, about whom al-Suddi said the verse was revealed, burning the Muslims' crops and killing their donkeys. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 238} "Sa'y" in Arabic speech is work, and he cites a verse of al-A'sha about Qays ibn Ma'dikarib al-Kindi, with the editor noting the correct form in the Diwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 238} From Mujahid: "sa'a" means he worked (3982). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 238}

The interpreters differed over the "corruption" (ifsad) God ascribes to this hypocrite. Some said it is highway robbery and terrorising the road, as was recounted of al-Akhnas ibn Shariq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 238} Others said it is severing kinship ties and shedding Muslim blood; from Ibn Jurayj: cutting kin and shedding the blood of Muslims, and when asked why he did such a thing he says, I draw near to God by it (3983). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 239} Al-Tabari says the right view is that God described this hypocrite as one who, turning away from the Messenger of God, acts corruptly in God's earth; all acts of disobedience fall under ifsad, and God did not restrict the description to some of its senses. His corruption may have been highway robbery or something else, and whichever it was it was disobedience to God; yet what best fits the apparent wording is that he cut the road and frightened travellers, since the verse goes on to say he destroys crops and offspring, which resembles the act of one who terrorises the road more than one who severs kin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 239}

On "and destroys crops and offspring": they differed over the manner of his destroying. Some said it was burning the crops of some Muslims and hamstringing their donkeys, from al-Suddi (3984). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 239} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 240} From Mujahid: when he turns away he strives in the land with aggression and wrong, so God withholds the rain and crops and offspring perish; then he recited the verse of al-Rum on corruption appearing on land and sea (30:41) and said, it is not this sea of yours, but every town on flowing water is a "sea" (3985). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 240} Al-Tabari says Mujahid's view, though an interpretation the verse can bear, is less close to the apparent wording than what he reported from al-Suddi, and for that reason he chose the latter; "harth" is the crop and "nasl" the progeny and offspring. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 240} He adds that his destroying the crop is burning it, though it may have been as Mujahid said, by withholding of rain on account of his disobedience, or by killing those who tended it until it spoiled; likewise destroying offspring may be by killing the mothers or fathers so that their progeny ends. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 240} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 241} Al-Suddi said the verse was revealed about killing the Muslims' donkeys and burning their crops; al-Tabari holds that this is not incorrect, but that it is meant for everyone who follows his way in killing animals that may not be killed, or that may be killed in some cases when killed wrongfully, because God did not specify one thing over another but made it general, and a group of interpreters said the same of its generality. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 241}

Al-Tamimi asked Ibn 'Abbas about the crops and offspring, and he said, the crop is your tillage, and the offspring is the offspring of every beast (3986-3989); the editor says al-Tamimi is a man of Banu Tamim whose name is apparently unknown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 241} From Ibn 'Abbas in Ibn Sa'd's chain: the offspring of every beast, and of people too (3990); from Mujahid: the plants of the earth, and offspring of every creature that walks, people and animals (3991); from Qatada (3992), al-Dahhak (3993) and al-Rabi' (3994): the crop is the earth's plants and the offspring that of every beast; from 'Ata': the crop is the sown field and the offspring is of people and cattle, so he kills the offspring of people and cattle (3995); from al-Dahhak in another report: the crop is the root or origin (3996), which the editor calls good by figurative usage; from Makhul: the crop is what you till, and the offspring is the offspring of everything (3997). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 242} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 243}

Some readers read "wa-yuhliku" with the verb raised, joined to "he calls God to witness"; al-Tabari holds this reading impermissible, though Arabic allows it, because it goes against what the authoritative body of readers agree on, and because in the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b and his codex, as reported to him, it is "to spread corruption in it and to destroy the crops and offspring", which is among the clearest proofs of the correctness of the subjunctive joined to "to spread corruption". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 243}

On "and God does not love corruption": al-Tabari says it means God does not love acts of disobedience, cutting the road and frightening the way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 243}

Al-Tabari explains that "fasad" is the verbal noun of "fasada al-shay' yafsudu", like "dhahaba yadhhabu dhahaban", and that some Arabs make the verbal noun of "fasada" "fusudan" and of "dhahaba" "dhuhuban"; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 243} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 244}
