## Summary
Verse 35 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 35 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Fear as knowing, and the rift (pp. 318-319, notes 3 and 1).** Al-Tabari says fear here means knowing: if you, people, know that the spouses are in opposition, meaning each does what burdens the other. For the wife this is rebellion and leaving the duty God placed on her toward her husband; for the husband it is failing to keep her fairly or to let her go with kindness. Note 3 refers back to p. 298, note 2, on fear in the sense of knowing. He adds that the word for opposition is a verbal noun from 'so-and-so opposed so-and-so' and may amount to enmity, and Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9403, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) reports that when he strikes her and she refuses to return and opposes him, it means she became his enemy. Al-Tabari explains the word being joined to the shared word for 'between' by that word also being a noun, as at [[passage/quran-6-94|6:94]] on one reading; the editor notes (p. 319, note 2) that this reading takes it as a noun meaning the tie that joins you, and (note 3) that the printed edition misread the unpointed manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 319}

**Who is addressed to send the two judges (pp. 319-320, reports 9404-9406).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over who is commanded to send the two judges. Some said the ruler to whom the matter is brought. Ibn Bashshar (9404, from Abd al-Wahhab, from Ayyub) reports that Sa'id b. Jubayr said, of the woman seeking release, that he exhorts her, then forsakes her, then strikes her, and if she still does not desist the matter goes to the ruler, who sends one judge from his side and one from hers; each speaks for his side, and whichever party is the wrongdoer the ruler restrains, and if she is the rebel he orders her to seek release. Yahya b. Abi Talib (9405, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) reports from al-Dahhak that this is for the ruler. Others said those commanded are the man and the woman; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9406, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that if he strikes her and she returns, he has no way against her, but if she refuses and opposes him, he sends a judge from his family and she sends one from hers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 320}

**What the two judges are sent for (pp. 320-322, reports 9407-9410, with notes).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over what the judges are sent for and what they may decide. Some said the spouses send them as agents, and they may do only what they were authorised to do in matters where agency is allowed. Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9407, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ayyub, from Muhammad, that is Ibn Sirin, from Ubayda) reports that a man and his wife with a dispute came to Ali, each with a crowd of people (note 1, p. 320: the word for the crowd means a large group); he ordered the judges sent, then told them it was for them to join if they saw fit to join and to separate if they saw fit to separate; the woman said she accepted God's book in what was for her and against her, the man said he would not accept separation, and Ali said he had lied and would not leave until he affirmed what she had affirmed. On p. 321, note 1, the editor says the manuscript omitted part of the words and he restored them from the sources; note 2 says al-Shafi'i narrated it in al-Umm (5:177) through Abd al-Wahhab al-Thaqafi from Ayyub, and al-Bayhaqi in al-Sunan al-Kubra (7:305-306), and that al-Shafi'i said Ali's report is established with him. Mujahid b. Musa (9408, from Yazid, from Hisham b. Hassan and Abd Allah b. Awn, from Muhammad) gives it with small differences between Hisham's and Ibn Awn's versions of Ali's words; al-Qasim (9409, from al-Husayn, from Hushaym, from Mansur and Hisham, from Ibn Sirin, from Ubayda) says he witnessed Ali and relates the same (note 3: al-Bayhaqi gives the two in abridged form). Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9410, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that if he forsakes her and strikes her and she refuses and opposes him, he sends a judge from his side and she one from hers; she tells her judge she has entrusted her affair to him, to return if he so orders or part if he separates, and tells him of any wish about maintenance or any dislike, asking him to remove it; the man does likewise with his judge; the two judges then meet and each strives for his client; what they agree on, divorce or keeping, is valid; and if she sends a judge and he refuses to send one, he should not approach her until he does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 322}

**Second view: the judges are for reconciling, not separating (pp. 322-323, reports 9411-9413, with notes).** Others said the ruler sends them to learn who is wronged and who the wrongdoer, to hold each to what is due to the other, not to separate them. Ibn Bashshar (9411, from Abd al-A'la, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada and al-Hasan that the judges are sent to reconcile and to bear witness against the wrongdoer, and that separation is not in their hands. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9412, from Yazid b. Zuray', from Sa'id) reports the same from Qatada. Al-Muthanna (9413, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) reports from Qays b. Sa'd, asked about the two judges, that whatever they decide is valid; the husband's judge is alone with the husband and the wife's with the wife, each asks his client to speak truthfully, then they meet and take a pledge of truthfulness from each other, learn what each said and recognise the wrongdoer; if it is the woman they tell her no maintenance until she returns to what is right and obeys God, and if it is the man they tell him not to enter her house until he spends on her and returns to right and justice; if she refuses she is the wrongdoer and what is hers is taken, lawful for him, and if he is the wrongdoer he divorces her and none of her property is lawful to him; if he keeps her he keeps her as God ordered, spending on her and treating her well. In the notes (p. 323) the editor says he kept the manuscript reading at the start because the report appears to be part of a longer one, that the printed edition spoiled a sentence which he repaired by restoring the words and the conjunction, and, in note 3, he introduces Qays b. Sa'd al-Makki, client of Nafi b. Alqama, who narrated from Tawus (the note continues on the next page). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 323}

**End of the editor's note on report 9413 (p. 324).** Note 3 from the previous page continues: the editor says Qays b. Sa'd is trustworthy, narrates from Ata, Mujahid and Sa'id b. Jubayr, and has an entry in al-Tahdhib (the start of the note is on the previous page and its first words are cut in print). He then says the chain in the printed edition and the manuscript presents Qays as narrating from Mujahid; that the manuscript has marks after the name of Shibl and after the name of Mujahid showing that words in between were dropped, that the publisher did not know the scribes' convention and kept what should have been omitted; in his view Qays narrates from Mujahid, not the reverse, and the report looks like an answer to a question Qays put to Mujahid or Ata, as in some earlier chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 324}

**Reports 9414-9417 (pp. 324-325, with note 1).** Ibn Waki (9414, from his father, from Musa b. Ubayda) reports from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi that Ali used to send the two judges; the wife's judge asked the husband what he resented in his wife and, when he had said, asked whether, if she turned from what he disliked to what he liked, he would fear God regarding her and treat her as is due from him in maintenance and clothing; if he said yes, the husband's judge asked the wife likewise, and if she said yes he joined them. Ali said that through the two judges God unites and through them He separates. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9415, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) reports from al-Hasan that the two judges rule in bringing together and do not rule in separation. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9416, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) says that for a rebellious wife the husband may take release from her when the judges so order, after she has said to her husband things like that she will not honour an oath for him and will admit people into his house without his leave; the ruler says he will not allow release until she says she will not wash for him after major impurity nor pray for him, and then he says 'release the woman'. Yunus (9417, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd: he exhorts her; if she refuses and prevails, he forsakes her in her bed; if she prevails also over this, he strikes her; if she prevails over this too, two judges are sent from the two sides; if she prevails over this too and wants something else, then (Ibn Wahb says: my father said, or: my father used to say) the judges have nothing of separation in their hands: if they see wrong from the husband they tell him he is the wrongdoer and should desist, and if he refuses they refer it to the ruler; likewise if she is the wrongdoer. In note 1 the editor prefers the manuscript, corrects its wording, and says the speaker of the last sentence is Abd Allah b. Zayd b. Aslam, whose father is Zayd b. Aslam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 325}

**The view that the judges' ruling stands for uniting and separating (pp. 325-328, reports 9418-9427, with notes).** Others said the ruler sends them on the footing that their ruling is binding on the spouses in joining and in separating. Al-Muthanna (9418, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas): a righteous man from the husband's people and one like him from the wife's look at which is at fault; if it is the husband, his wife is kept from him and he is confined to maintenance; if it is the wife, she is confined to her husband and denied maintenance (note 2, p. 325: the verb means to hold and compel by force, from a root whose sin changes to sad); if they agree to separate or to join, their decision is valid; if they decide to join, one spouse is content and the other dislikes it and one then dies, the one who was content inherits from the one who disliked and not the reverse. Ibn Bashshar (9419, from Rawh, from Awf) reports from Ibn Sirin that the two judges separate and join if they see fit. Muhammad b. al-Muthanna (9420, from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Amr b. Murra) asked Sa'id b. Jubayr about the two judges and he said 'I had not been born then'; Amr said he meant the two judges in a quarrel of spouses, and he said they approach the one from whom the quarrel came; if he complies, well, else they approach the other; if not, they rule, and what they rule is valid. Note 1 (p. 326): Sa'id took the question to be about the two judges in the affair of Ali and Mu'awiya and their meeting at Dumat al-Jandal in 37 AH, hence 'not born'; al-Hajjaj killed him in 95 aged 49 (so born 46, after the arbitration), and another report has him telling his son at his execution that he was 57 (born 38, also after it). Note 2 says the printed edition misread the unpointed manuscript and explains the word as meaning dispute and mutual claim (see also report 9428). Abd al-Hamid b. Bayan (9421, from Muhammad b. Yazid, from Ismail) reports from Amir that whatever the judges decide is valid. Ibn Humayd (9422, from Jarir, from Mughira, from Dawud) reports from Ibrahim that what they rule is valid, whether they separate by three pronouncements, two or one, whether they rule a payment from his property against him, reconcile, or waive something (p. 327, note 1: the editor prefers the reading of a payment as compensation to the wife for the ill-treatment). Al-Muthanna (9423, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Abu Ja'far, from al-Mughira) reports from Ibrahim likewise, and that divorcing once, or divorcing her for a consideration, is valid. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9424, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Yahya b. Abi Kathir) reports from Abu Salama b. Abd al-Rahman that if they wish to separate they separate and if they wish to join they join. Al-Qasim (9425, from al-Husayn, from Hushaym, from Husayn) reports from al-Sha'bi that a wife rebelled, they brought the dispute to Shurayh, he ordered two judges, they decided to separate, the man disliked it, and Shurayh said 'then what were they for today?' and upheld their word (note 3: al-Bayhaqi, al-Sunan 7:306). Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9426, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Ibn Tawus, from Ikrima b. Khalid) reports Ibn Abbas saying that he and Mu'awiya were sent as two judges; Ma'mar adds that it reached him that Uthman sent them and told them to join if they saw fit and separate if they saw fit (p. 328, note 1: al-Bayhaqi). Al-Muthanna (9427, from Ishaq, from Rawh b. Ubada, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abi Mulayka) reports that Aqil b. Abi Talib married Fatima bint Utba and words passed between them; she came to Uthman, who sent Ibn Abbas and Mu'awiya; Ibn Abbas said he would separate them, Mu'awiya said he would not separate two elders of the Banu Abd Manaf; they came and found them reconciled (note 2: al-Shafi'i in al-Umm 5:177-178 through Muslim b. Khalid from Ibn Jurayj; al-Bayhaqi, al-Sunan 7:306). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 328}

**Report 9428 (p. 328).** Yahya b. Abi Talib (9428, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) reports from al-Dahhak that the two judges are just men and witnesses; when the man and woman quarrel and go to the ruler, he appoints two trustworthy judges, one from each side, who look from whom the corruption comes; if from the wife she is compelled to obey her husband, and he is told to fear God, treat her well and spend on her according to what God gave him; if the ill-treatment is from the man he is told to treat her well, and if he does not he is told to give her her due and let her go; the ruler undertakes this (note 3: see p. 326, note 2, on the sense of dispute). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 328}

**Al-Tabari's choice on who is addressed (pp. 328-330, with notes).** Al-Tabari says the soundest view is that God addressed the Muslims and commanded them to send the two judges when discord between spouses is feared, without singling some out; all agree the sending is for none but the spouses and the ruler who governs the Muslims' affairs or his deputy, and they differ over which of the two is commanded; nothing in the verse limits it to one spouse and there is no report on it from the Prophet, and the community differs; so what is excluded from the verse is only what all agree is excluded, and the spouses and the ruler both come under it. If the spouses each send a judge from his side to look into their affair, his authorising is valid for and against him; if he authorised only part, the judge's act stands within what he was authorised, for instance rights owed to the spouse but not duties; if neither spouse authorised anything but the two judges together, only what both agree stands, not what one does alone; if neither authorised them at all and they were sent only to look, to know the wronged from the wrongdoer and to testify before the ruler if needed, they may do nothing further, neither divorce nor taking money, and nothing binds the spouses. In the notes (pp. 329-330) the editor records where the printed edition and the manuscript differ in several places in this passage and justifies his readings from the context, notes a word he thinks the copyist dropped (supplied in brackets, meaning 'not permitted'), and says he removed a superfluous word from the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 330}

**The meaning of the two judges, and al-Tabari's rule (pp. 330-331, report 9429).** Al-Tabari asks what the word for the two judges means if matters are as he described, and says they differed. Some said it means just examination, as al-Dahhak said in the report already given; Yahya b. Abi Talib (9429, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) reports from him in effect that they are two judges deciding between the spouses in the manner he explained (the editor, note 5, says this sentence is al-Tabari's remark on al-Dahhak's words under 9428). Others said they are the judges who decide between them what the spouses delegated to them. Al-Tabari then says that whichever it is, neither of them may rule separation or taking money except with the consent of the one ruled against, apart from what binds one spouse toward the other by God's ruling, namely the husband's duty of maintenance and keeping well if he is the wrongdoer; otherwise it is not theirs nor anyone's, ruler or other. If the husband wrongs her the leader may bring him to what is due to her; if she is the rebel, God allowed him to take a ransom and made her divorce his, as explained in [[passage/quran-2|sura 2]] (note 1: a cross-reference to volume 4). So no one may separate a man and a woman without the husband's consent, nor take money from the wife without her consent, except by a proof that must be accepted from text or analogy. If the ruler sends them, they may not rule separation save by the husband's authorisation, nor money save with the wife's consent, which he says Ali's action and those who agree with him show (note 3: see reports 9407-9409); but they may reconcile the spouses and identify the wrongdoer in order to testify. The ruler sends them only when the spouses come to him, each complaining of the other, and he cannot tell the right party from the wrong; if he can, there is no point in sending them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 331}

**The last clause of the verse on intending reconciliation (pp. 332-333, reports 9430-9436).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: if the two judges intend reconciliation between the spouses between whom discord is feared, God will bring the two judges to agree on it, which happens when each is truthful in what he confides to the one sent to look into the spouses' affair. The reports: Ibn Bashshar (9430, from Yahya, from Sufyan, from Abu Hashim, from Mujahid): it is not the man and woman but the two judges; Ibn Humayd (9431, from Hukkam, from Amr, from Ata) from Sa'id b. Jubayr: they are the two judges; al-Muthanna (9432, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas: the two judges, and likewise every reconciler whom God guides to what is true and right; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9433, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: meaning the two judges; Ibn Humayd (9434, from Jarir, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib) from Sa'id b. Jubayr: if the judges intend reconciliation they reconcile; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9435, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from Abu Hashim) from Mujahid: God brings the two judges to agreement; Yahya b. Abi Talib (9436, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) from al-Dahhak: the two judges, when they give sincere counsel to woman and man alike. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 333}

**The close of the verse (p. 333, note 1).** Al-Tabari says God knew what the judges intended of reconciliation and otherwise and was informed of it and of the rest of the affairs of the spouses and of others, nothing hidden from Him, watching over them until He repays each with his due: good with good, and wrong with forgiveness or punishment. Note 1 refers back to the earlier treatment of the word for good (the cross-reference numbers are unclear in the scan). These statements are given as transmitted; the choices are al-Tabari's, the notes are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 333}
