**Commentary on verse 55 (pp. 295-297)**

Al-Tabari notes that the interpreters differed over this verse. Some said it means: let not the wealth of these hypocrites or their children impress you, Muhammad, in the life of this world; God only wishes to punish them with it in the hereafter, and they said the wording has a transposition of order. Report 16804, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: this is one of the transpositions of speech, meaning do not be impressed by their wealth or children in this world, God only wishes to punish them with it in the hereafter; report 16805, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: God wishes to punish them with it in the hereafter. The editor notes that this is the first time he finds the plural word for transpositions used in this commentary, and that the manuscript differs from the printed text, which took the passage from al-Durr al-Manthur, so he left it as it stood, and refers to al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 295}

Others said rather that it means God wishes to punish them with it in the life of this world through the obligations He has laid on them there. Report 16806, from al-Hasan, through al-Musayyab ibn Sharik and Salman: by the taking of zakat and spending in God's way. Report 16807, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: by the misfortunes in it, which are a punishment for them and a reward for the believers. The editor notes that people abandoned the reports of al-Musayyab ibn Sharik and that al-Bukhari said of him that they were silent about him, and that the manuscript has the name Salman al-Ansari where the printed text has al-Aqsari, about whom he could learn nothing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 296}

Al-Tabari prefers the interpretation given from al-Hasan, because it is the apparent sense of the revelation, and turning the interpretation to what the apparent sense indicates is better than turning it to a hidden sense with no proof of its soundness. Those who took it as a transposition did so because they saw no way in which God punishes the hypocrites in this world through their wealth and children, who are a joy to them, and it escaped them that it is a great punishment for such a person to be bound to what God made obligatory on him of His rights and duties, which are laid on him and taken from him when he is not willing, hoping for no recompense from God and no praise or thanks for what is taken, out of vexation and dislike. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 297}

As for "and their souls depart while they are disbelievers", it means their souls go out and they die in their disbelief in God and their denial of the prophethood of His prophet Muhammad. It is said of a soul that it has departed with either vowelling of the verb, and from it comes the saying that someone "sped ahead" among the people when he outstripped them, and that falsehood "vanished" when it passed away and was effaced. The editor says the lexicographers record only the one vowelling of the present tense and that he found no account of the other and left it unvowelled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 297}
