## Summary
Verse 204 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "among the people is he whose speech about worldly life pleases you, and he calls God to witness what is in his heart, yet he is the most contentious of opponents": al-Tabari says this is God's description of the hypocrites. He says: among the people is he whose outward speech and public show pleases you, Muhammad, and who calls God to witness what is in his heart, while he is the most contentious of opponents, that is, one who argues in falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 229} The interpreters then differed over whom the verse was revealed about. Some said it was al-Akhnas ibn Shariq, who came to the Messenger of God claiming he wanted Islam and swearing that he had come only for that, then went out and destroyed property of Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 229} From al-Suddi: it was revealed about al-Akhnas ibn Shariq al-Thaqafi, an ally of Banu Zuhra, who came to the Prophet at Medina and showed him Islam, which pleased the Prophet, and he said I came only wanting Islam and God knows I am truthful; then he left him and passed the crops and donkeys of some Muslims, burned the crops and hamstrung the donkeys, and the verse came down, "when he turns away he strives in the land to spread corruption"; "the most contentious" he explains as the most crooked in dispute. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 229} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 230} The editor notes that al-Tabari did not record this report in his commentary on the surahs of al-Humaza and al-Qalam, and that part of it will come as 3978. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 230}

Others said it was revealed about some of the hypocrites who spoke about the expedition of the Messenger of God that was lost at al-Raji'. From Ibn 'Abbas: when this expedition, the companions of Khubayb, was struck at al-Raji' between Mecca and Medina, some of the hypocrites said, alas for these tried ones who perished so; they neither stayed at home nor delivered their leader's mission; so God sent down about what the hypocrites said, and about the martyrdom and good that befell those men, "among the people is he whose speech about worldly life pleases you", that is, the Islam he shows by his tongue, "and he calls God to witness what is in his heart", that is, of hypocrisy, "and he is the most contentious", that is, one given to argument when he speaks to you, "and when he turns away", that is, goes out from you, "he strives in the land to spread corruption"; then "and among the people is he who sells himself seeking God's pleasure", those who gave themselves to God in jihad until they perished, meaning this expedition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 231} The editor notes the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and corrects the printed "the killed" to "the tried". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 230}

Others said that all the hypocrites are meant, and the difference between their inward and outward. From Sa'id al-Maqburi, who said to Muhammad ibn Ka'b: in some books it is said that God has servants whose tongues are sweeter than honey and hearts more bitter than aloe, who wear sheep's skins of gentleness for people and draw this world with religion; God says, are they tilling on Me and deluded about Me? By My might I will send on them a trial that leaves the forbearing among them bewildered; Muhammad ibn Ka'b said this is in God's book and recited the verse; Sa'id said you know about whom it was revealed; Muhammad ibn Ka'b said a verse is revealed about a man and then becomes general. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 231} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 232} From Nawf, who read the books: I find the description of some people of this community in the revealed book, and he gave similar words; al-Qurazi said I reflected on it in the Qur'an and found they were the hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 232} The editor identifies Khalid ibn Yazid al-Jumahi and Nawf ibn Fadala al-Himyari, son of Ka'b al-Ahbar's wife. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 232} From Qatada: he is the hypocrite; from Mujahid: his outward show in this world, and he calls God to witness in the dispute that he wants only what is right; from al-Rabi': this is a servant of fair words and bad deeds who comes to the Messenger of God and speaks well to him; from 'Ata': he says a thing while something else is in his heart, and God knows it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 232} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 233}

There are two readings of "he calls God to witness what is in his heart"; most readers read it as "yushhidu Allah", meaning that the hypocrite whose words please the Messenger of God calls on God to witness that his words match his belief and that he believes in God and His Messenger, while lying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 233} From Ibn Zayd: a man used to come to the Prophet saying I bear witness that you came with the truth from God, until the Prophet liked his words, and then say by God, Messenger of God, God knows that what is in my heart is as my tongue said; these, he says, are the hypocrites, and he recited the verse on the hypocrites in al-Munafiqun (63:1). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 233}

From al-Suddi on "he calls God to witness what is in his heart": God knows that I am truthful and that I want Islam; from Mujahid: he calls God to witness in the dispute that he wants only what is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 234} Others read "yashhadu Allahu", meaning that God Himself bears witness to the hypocrisy in his heart, that he conceals other than what his tongue shows, and to his lying; this is the reading of Ibn Muhaysin, and Ibn 'Abbas took it so in the earlier report (3962). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 234} Al-Tabari chooses the reading "yushhidu Allah", meaning he calls on God as witness to what is in his heart, because the authoritative body of readers agree on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 234}

On "he is the most contentious of opponents": the aladd among men is the one fierce in quarrel, and al-Tabari cites a verse whose poet is unknown; the editor notes variant wordings in Lisan al-'Arab and al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 235} The interpreters differed. Some said he is one given to argument: from Ibn 'Abbas, one given to argument when he speaks to you and answers back (3973); from Qatada, hard in disobeying God, arguing in falsehood, learned of tongue and ignorant in deed, speaking wisdom and acting in sin (3974), and in Ma'mar's version, arguing in falsehood (3975). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 235} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 236} Others said he is not straight in dispute but crooked: from Mujahid, unjust and not straight (3976), and one who does not keep straight in a dispute (3977); from al-Suddi, crooked in dispute (3978). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 236} Al-Tabari says both views are close in meaning, since crookedness in dispute belongs to argument and contentiousness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 236} Others said it means he is false in his speech; from al-Hasan, the liar in speech (3979), and al-Tabari says this too can carry the meaning of the first two if the speaker meant that he disputes with falsehood, in argument and in turning away from the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 236} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 237} He adds that this is God's report of the hypocrite whose speech pleases the Prophet and who calls God to witness that he is in the right, through the intensity of his quarrel and his arguing in falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 237}
