## Summary
Verse 108 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 108 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

By "that" al-Tabari means what he has said about the executors: when you doubt them and suspect them of betraying the property of the one who made them executors, you detain them after the prayer and make them swear to what the dead man's guardians claim against them. "Nearer that they bring the testimony in its proper form" means that doing this makes it likelier that they speak truly in their oaths, do not conceal, admit the truth and do not betray; "or fear that oaths will be returned after their oaths" means the executors fear that, if they are found to have earned sin in their oaths by God, their oaths will be passed to the dead man's guardians after oaths shown to be false, so that the guardians take what they claimed; so they then speak truly in oath and testimony, fearing shame and fearing to forfeit what they betrayed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 204}

The editor refers for "nearer" to earlier notes in volumes 6 and 7, and for "in its proper form" to volume 2.

The interpreters said much the same. Report 12979, from Ibn Abbas: if it is found that the two unbelievers lied, two of the guardians stand in their place and swear by God that the unbelievers' testimony is false and that they have not transgressed; the unbelievers' testimony is set aside and the guardians' allowed. "That is nearer" for the unbelievers to give testimony properly or fear that oaths be returned after their oaths. Muslim witnesses are not put to oath; oaths are only for unbelievers. Report 12980, from Qatada: that is likelier to make them truthful in their testimony and to fear the consequence ("aqib"). Report 12981, from Ibn Zayd on "or they fear oaths be returned": their oaths are voided and the others' taken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 205}

The editor corrects the printed "punishment" to "consequence" from the manuscript, and says the sentence that follows, given as another group's opinion, is broken in sense and does not fit the next report; he thinks something fell out, left it as it stood and put it in brackets, doubting it.

Report 12982, from al-Suddi: the two men are stood after their prayer in their own religion and swear by God that your companion made this will and this is his estate. The imam tells them before they swear: if you have concealed or betrayed I will shame you before your people, will not allow your testimony and will punish you; if he says this to them it is nearer that they bring testimony in its proper form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 206}

Al-Tabari then explains "fear God and listen, and God does not guide the transgressing people": fear God, people, and watch him in your oaths, that you do not swear falsely by them, nor take by them the property of one whose property is forbidden to you, nor betray one who trusted you; "listen" to what is said to you and the warnings given you, and act on them and stop; "God does not guide the transgressing people" means he does not grant success to one who transgresses from a command he was given, opposing it, obeying Satan and disobeying his Lord. Ibn Zayd said the transgressor here is the liar, and in report 12983 he says: the liars who swear to a falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 206}

The editor refers to his note on "entrusted" at p. 197 and to the explanation of "transgressor" in this sense by Ibn Zayd in volume 10, at report 12103.

Al-Tabari says that Ibn Zayd's view is not rejected by him, but God made the statement general that he does not guide all transgressors and did not single out some by a report or by reason, so it applies to all senses of transgression until something requiring acceptance singles out one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 207}

The scholars then differed on whether the ruling of these two verses is abrogated or fixed. Some said abrogated: report 12984, from Ibrahim, "it is abrogated"; report 12985, from Ibn Abbas, "it is abrogated", meaning the verse "testimony between you"; a group said it is fixed, and he has given the view of most of them. Al-Tabari holds that the ruling of the verse is not abrogated, because it is among God's rulings, on which the people of Islam have stood since the Prophet was sent until today, that one against whom a claim is made about what men own is cleared only by an oath, if the claimant has no proof for his claim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 207}

The editor corrects both manuscript and print, which have "the ruling of the verse is abrogated", to "not abrogated", because al-Tabari says so as will be seen.

And that if the claimant recognises in the defendant's hand an article that is his and claims it as his, and the holder says "it is mine, I bought it from this claimant", the word is that of the one who says the holder bought it from him, that is the claimant who recognised it, not the holder with his oath, if the holder has no proof of the purchase. As this is God's ruling, on which scholars do not differ, and as in the two verses the Prophet laid the oath on the two executors when the heirs made their claim, and laid nothing further on them once they swore, until the heirs recognised in the two's hands the bowl or ewer or other property of theirs, and the two said they bought it from the dead man, then the Prophet put the oath on the dead man's heirs, because the executors had become claimants, claiming that what they held was theirs by purchase; they were admitting the property was the dead man's and claiming a purchase, so needed proof, and the heirs, owners of the article, were nearer the oath. That is God's word "if it is found that they have earned sin". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 208}

At p. 208 the editor explains "i'tarafa" as recognised and distinguished, keeps the manuscript reading in the first sentence and adds "on him" in brackets, and restores "and became" which print omitted.

If the interpretation is so, there is no ground for anyone's claim that the verse is abrogated, because a ruling of God may be declared abrogated only on a report that cuts off excuse, either from God or his Messenger, or on widespread transmission; and since there is no such report and reason does not reject its soundness, it cannot be declared abrogated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 209}

Verse 108 closes here at p. 209, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-5-109|5:109]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
