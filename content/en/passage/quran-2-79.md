## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "woe", they differ: Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak, that it is the punishment upon them; Abu 'Iyad and Shaqiq, that it is the pus that flows at the base of Hell, or a valley of pus there; and a report is given from 'Uthman ibn 'Affan from the Prophet that "Wayl is a mountain in the Fire", on which the editor notes that the chain is problematic, with two narrators he could not trace and a scribal error in the text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 267} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 268}

Al-Tabari gives a report from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri from the Prophet that "Wayl is a valley in Hell into which the unbeliever falls for forty years before reaching its bottom"; the editor holds the chain sound, noting that al-Tirmidhi called it gharib and that Ibn Kathir regarded it as munkar because of one narrator. Al-Tabari concludes that, by what is reported of "wayl", the verse's meaning is punishment for the Jews who write falsehood with their hands and then say it is from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 269}
On "those who write the book with their hands", he says they are those among the Israelite Jews who altered God's book, writing a book in line with their own interpretations, contrary to what was sent down to Moses, and sold it to ignorant people for a paltry gain; this he reports from al-Suddi and Qatada, and from Mujahid and Abu al-'Aliya that they took what God had sent down in their book describing Muhammad and altered it, while Ibn Abbas, through al-Dahhak, says they are the unlettered ones. A report from 'Uthman from the Prophet is given that Wayl is a mountain in the Fire, and that they altered the Torah and erased Muhammad's name from it, on which the editor refers to his discussion of the problematic chain. 'Ata ibn Yasar says it is a valley in Hell in which mountains would melt if sent through it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 270} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 271} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 272}
To a questioner asking why "with their hands", since writing is by hand, he replies that a writing may be attributed to someone who did not write it, so God denies that some ignorant person wrote it at their scholars' order and affirms that the rabbis wrote it knowingly. On "for what their hands wrote and for what they earn", he says the punishment is for what they wrote and for the sins they commit and the proceeds they eat; "kasb" is work, reported from Abu al-'Aliya and al-Dahhak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 272} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 273}
