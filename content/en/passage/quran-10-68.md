**Commentary on verse 68 (pp. 145-146)**

On [[passage/quran-10-68|10:68]] al-Tabari says: these polytheists of your people, Muhammad, said God has taken a son, meaning their saying that the angels are the daughters of God. God, declaring Himself far above what they invented, says 'Glory be to Him', exalting Him above what they claim of their Lord. 'He is the Self-Sufficient': God has no need of any of His creatures and so no need of a child, for a child is sought by those who seek one as a help in their life and a remembrance after their death, and God has no need of any of that, neither of one to help Him manage nor of an heir after Him, since He does not perish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 145}

'To Him belongs what is in the heavens and on the earth': it is His as property, and the angels are His servants and His property, so how can a man's servant and property be his son? Do you not understand, people, how wrong you are in what you say? 'You have no authority for this' means you have no argument by which you can argue for your claim that the angels are God's daughters, authority being proof. 'Do you say of God what you do not know?' means do you say of God something whose truth and soundness you do not know, and attribute to Him what may not be attributed to Him, out of ignorance of what you say, with no proof or evidence? The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'glory be to Him', 'the Self-Sufficient' and 'authority'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 146}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
