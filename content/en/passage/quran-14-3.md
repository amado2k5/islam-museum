**Commentary on verse 3 (pp. 514-516)**

On "those who prefer the life of this world to the hereafter, and bar from God's path and seek to make it crooked; those are in far error", al-Tabari says: by "those who prefer the life of this world to the hereafter" He, mighty be His praise, means those who choose the life of this world and its enjoyment and disobedience to God in it over obedience to God {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 514}

and over the useful deeds that bring them near to His good pleasure in the hereafter; "and bar from God's path", he says, they prevent whoever wanted to believe in God and follow His Messenger in what he brought from God, of believing in Him and following him; "and seek to make it crooked", he says, they seek God's path, which is His religion with which He sent His Messenger, "crookedness", that is, distortion and alteration by lies and falsehood. "'Iwaj", with a kasra on the 'ayn and a fatha on the waw, is for religion, the earth and everything that is not upright, whereas for everything that is upright, such as a wall, a spear or a tooth, it is said with a fatha on both the 'ayn and the waw, "'awaj". God, mighty be His mention, says "those are in far error", meaning these disbelievers who prefer the life of this world to the hereafter, he says, they are far gone from the truth, travelling without guidance and swerving from the straight course of the path. The scholars of Arabic differed over why "'ala" enters in "'ala al-akhira". One of the Basran grammarians said the verb was joined with "'ala" as it is said "they struck him in the sword", meaning with the sword, since these particles are all joined with and dropped, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 515}

as in the Arabs' saying "nazaltu Zaydan" and "marartu Zaydan", meaning "I passed by him" and "I alighted on him". Another of them said it was inserted because the verb conveys the meaning of other verbs, so "they prefer the life of this world" means "they favour the life of this world over the hereafter", and for that reason "'ala" was inserted. I have explained this and its like in several places in the book, which spares repeating it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 516}
