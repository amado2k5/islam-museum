**Commentary on verse 71 (pp. 147-152)**

On [[passage/quran-10-71|10:71]] al-Tabari says God tells His prophet: recite to these polytheists of your people, who said God has taken a son, the story of Noah, that is, his report, 'when he said to his people: my people, if my standing has been burdensome to you', meaning if my staying among you has weighed on you and troubled you, 'and my reminding you of the signs of God', that is, my admonishing you with God's proofs and warning you, 'then in God I trust', meaning that if you have resolved to kill me or drive me out, my reliance is on God, my trust is in Him, and He is my support and back, 'so decide on your matter', that is, prepare your plan and resolve on what you intend against me. The editor says the printed text has 'and what you undertake' while the manuscript has an unpointed word, and he has put what he holds to be right, since 'ijma'' is firmness of intent and resolve. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 147}

One says 'ajma'tu 'ala kadha' meaning I resolved on it, and the Prophet's saying 'whoever has not resolved on the fast from the night has no fast' is the same, meaning has not resolved. The editor says al-Tabari gives it without a chain and that Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Maja relate it from Hafsa. He cites a line by a poet unknown to him, whom he guesses to be Abu al-Najm, of a man wondering whether he will set out one day with his plan settled, adding that wishes do not help. Report 17760, from al-A'raj on 'so decide your matter and your partners': settle your plan and call your partners. The editor discusses the men of its chain, among them Usayd the reciter who related the readings from al-A'raj and whose lineage is disputed, and refers to Ibn al-Jazari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 148}

'Your partners' is in the accusative through an implied verb, 'and call your partners', and 'partners' is joined to 'your matter' like a line of poetry about a man who saw your husband in battle girded with sword and spear, though a spear is not girded on; since the plain words indicate what is dropped, the poet made do with what he mentioned, and so it is with 'your partners'. The editor refers to al-Farra'. The reciters differ here. The readers of the cities read 'your partners' in the accusative and 'fa-ajmi'u' with a cut alif, from 'I resolved my matter'. It is reported of al-Hasan al-Basri that he read it with a cut alif and 'your partners' in the nominative, meaning decide your matter, and let your partners also decide their matter with you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 149}

Al-Tabari says the sound view is the reading with a cut alif and the partners in the accusative, because in the mushaf there is no wa, because the authority is agreed on reading it so and rejecting what differs, and because one who can err and forget is no objection to it. By 'partners' he means their gods and idols. 'Then let not your matter be a ghumma for you' means do not let your matter be obscure, confusing, unclear for you, from the saying 'the new moon was ghumma for the people' when it was obscure and they could not make it out. He cites a verse of al-Ajjaj in a rajaz about people covered over by a gloom that would have choked them had it not lifted. The editor says the printed text and manuscript have 'Ru'ba', which he thinks a copyist's slip, so he put 'al-Ajjaj' in brackets from Abu Ubayda. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 149}

It is also said to come from ghamm, grief, because the chest is tightened by it and its owner finds no outlet to ease what is in his heart, and a verse of al-Khansa is cited, on a man in distress whose throat Ibn Amr loosened so that his gloom lifted from his face. Report 17761, from Qatada on 'your matter a ghumma': let your matter not weigh on you. As for 'then carry out against me', it means then carry out against me what is in your souls and be done with it. Report 17762, from Qatada: carry out against me what you were going to carry out; and in an unnumbered report after it, from Mujahid: carry out against me what is in your souls. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 150}

Report 17764, from Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid, likewise. The people of knowledge of Arab speech differed on the meaning of 'then carry out against me': some said it means proceed against me, as one says 'so-and-so has qada', meaning he died and passed on; others said it means then be finished with me, qada' being completion, and 'he qada his debt' being from the same. Some readers are reported to have read it with 'afdu', meaning turn to me until you reach me, from 'the pain reached me'. 'And give me no respite' means do not delay, as one says 'I gave so-and-so respite on what he owes me'. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'qada' and 'respite' and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 151}

Al-Tabari says this is God's report of the saying of His prophet Noah to his people: he is sure of God's help against them and not afraid of their scheming and their harms, and he informs them that their gods neither harm nor benefit. He says to them: carry out what you tell yourselves about me, with sound resolve, and call for help, along with those who side with you against me, on your gods that you call on besides God, and do not delay it, for I have put my trust in God and am sure you cannot harm me unless my Lord wills. And though this is a report about Noah, it is an urging from God to His prophet Muhammad to follow his example and a showing of the way of right guidance in the message and the delivery entrusted to him. The editor says the printed text has a corrupt reading 'tawa'iq' and that 'bawa'iq' is the plural of 'ba'iqa', meaning their treachery, their evil and their wrongdoing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 152}
