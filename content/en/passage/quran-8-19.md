## Summary
Verse 19 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 19 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 19 (pp. 450-457)**

Al-Tabari says, on "if you seek a decision, the decision has come to you; and if you desist it is better for you, and if you return, We shall return, and your host will avail you nothing however many, and God is with the believers" [[passage/quran-8-19|8:19]]: God says to the polytheists who fought the Messenger of God at Badr, "if you seek a decision, the decision has come to you", meaning if you ask God to judge for the one of the two parties more cutting of kinship and the more wrongdoing of the two companies, and ask His help against it, God's judgement has come to you, and His help for the wronged against the wrongdoer and the one in the right against the one in falsehood. The interpreters said the like: reports 15831, from al-Dahhak, and 15832, from Ikrima: if you seek a judgement, the judgement has come to you; report 15833, from Ibn Abbas: He means the polytheists, if you ask help, the reinforcement has come to you; report 15834, from Ibn Abbas: if you ask for the judgement, and he used to say that "and if you desist it is better for you, and if you return We shall return" is to the polytheists; report 15835, from Mujahid: the unbelievers of Quraysh in their saying, our Lord, decide between us and Muhammad and his companions, and it was decided between them on the day of Badr, and report 15836 the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 450} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 451}

Report 15837, from al-Zuhri: Abu Jahl sought the decision and said, God, meaning Muhammad and himself, whichever of us was the more wicked toward You and the more cutting of kinship, destroy him today; God said, if you seek a decision, the decision has come to you; report 15838, from al-Zuhri: Abu Jahl ibn Hisham sought the decision and said so, meaning Muhammad and himself, and the two sons of Afra, Awf and Mu'awwidh, struck him and Ibn Mas'ud finished him; report 15839, from Abd Allah ibn Tha'laba ibn Su'ayr, ally of Banu Zuhra: that the one who sought the decision that day was Abu Jahl, who said when the people met, whichever of us is more cutting of kinship and brought us what is not known, destroy him this morning; that was his seeking of a decision and God sent down the verse about it; report 15840, from Qatada: Badr was a judgement and a lesson for whoever took lesson. The editor explains "ahanahu Allah" as destroyed him, identifies Abd Allah ibn Tha'laba ibn Su'ayr al-Adawi, noting that the Prophet wiped his face and head at the conquest, and traces the report to Ahmad and al-Hakim, who calls it sound on the condition of the two shaykhs, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 451} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 452}

Report 15841, from al-Suddi: when the polytheists went out from Mecca to the Prophet they took hold of the coverings of the Kaaba and asked God for help, saying, God, help the mightier of the two armies, the more honourable of the two parties and the better of the two tribes; and God said, if you seek a decision, the decision has come to you, meaning what you said has been helped, which is Muhammad; report 15842, from al-Dahhak: that was when the polytheists went out looking to their caravan, and the people of the caravan, Abu Sufyan and his companions, sent to the polytheists at Mecca asking their help, and Abu Jahl said, whichever of us is better in Your eyes, help him; and that is His saying "if you seek" meaning ask for help; report 15843, from Ibn Zayd: if you seek the punishment you were punished on the day of Badr, and their seeking was at Mecca when they said [[passage/quran-8-32|8:32]], and the punishment came to them on the day of Badr; and He told of the day of Uhud "if you return, We shall return"; report 15844, from Atiyya: Abu Jahl said on the day of Badr, God, help the better guided of the two parties, the better and more excellent, and it was revealed; report 15845, from al-Zuhri: that Abu Jahl was the one who sought the decision on the day of Badr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 453}

Reports 15846, 15847 and 15848, from Abd Allah ibn Tha'laba ibn Su'ayr: the one who sought the decision on the day of Badr was Abu Jahl, who said, God, he who has cut us off from kin and brought us what we do not know, destroy him this morning; and God sent down the verse; in report 15848 Ibn Ishaq says that God said "if you seek a decision, the decision has come to you" for Abu Jahl's saying, and said that "seeking a decision" is fairness in prayer; report 15849, from Yazid ibn Ruman and others: Abu Jahl said on the day of Badr, God, help the religion dearer to You, our old religion or theirs that is new; and God sent down the verse up to "God is with the believers". The editor refers to the tracing of report 15839 and to Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 454}

As for "and if you desist it is better for you", he says: if you desist, company of Quraysh and host of the unbelievers, from disbelieving in God and His Messenger and from fighting His prophet and those who believe in him, it is better for you in your world and your hereafter; "and if you return, We shall return" meaning if you return to fight him and fight his believing followers We shall return with a blow like the one struck at you on the day of Badr; "and your host will avail you nothing however many" meaning if you return We shall return to destroy you at the hands of My friends and rout you, and when I return to have you killed at their hands, captured and routed, your host, that is their army and company of polytheists, will avail you nothing, as they availed them nothing on the day of Badr despite their many and the believers' few; "and God is with the believers" meaning God is with those who believe in Him among His servants against those of them who disbelieve in Him, helping them against them and making them prevail as He made them prevail on the day of Badr over the polytheists. Report 15850, from Ibn Ishaq: He says to Quraysh "if you return, We shall return" with a like blow, "your host will avail you nothing however many", and God is with the believers, I help them against whoever opposes them. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "desisting", "availing", "company" and "with". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 454} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 455}
It is also said that the sense of "if you return, We shall return" is: if you return to seeking a decision We shall return to deciding for Muhammad. Al-Tabari says: this saying has no sense, because God had guaranteed His prophet when He permitted him to fight His enemies the prevailing of his religion and the exaltation of His word before Abu Jahl and his party sought a decision, so there is no sense, that being so, in saying "if you desist from seeking a decision it is better for you, and if you return We shall return", since God had promised His prophet the victory in His words [[passage/quran-22-39|22:39]], whether the polytheists sought a decision or not. Report 15851, from al-Suddi: "if you return, We shall return", if you seek a decision a second time We shall decide for Muhammad, "your host will avail you nothing however many, and God is with the believers", Muhammad and his companions. The editor notes that the printed edition differs from the manuscript and that he follows the text of Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 456}
The reciters differed over the reading of "and that God is with the believers": most reciters of Medina opened the "anna", in the sense "your host will avail you nothing however many, and because God is with the believers", "anna" being joined to the place of "however many", as if it said "because of its many and because God is with the believers", so that the position of "anna" is then accusative. Some people of Arabic claimed that its opening, when opened, is on "and that God weakens the plot of the unbelievers", "and that God is with the believers", joining the second to the first. Most Kufan and Basran reciters read "inna" with kasra as a fresh start, and argued that in the reading of Abd Allah it is "and God is with the believers" with an emphatic particle. Abu Ja'far said: the better reading is the kasra for a fresh start, since the report before it has completed what "and that God is with the believers" would require. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 456} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 457}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
