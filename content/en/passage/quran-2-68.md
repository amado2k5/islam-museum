## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari says that, once they knew the command came from God, the Israelites asked Moses to ask his Lord to make clear what the cow was, though God had spared them this, having told them to slaughter any cow they pleased with no limit. Their rough manners and coarse nature led them into captious questioning of their prophet, and God, in his reading, punished them by limiting the slaughter to one kind, which he reports from Ibn Abbas. He explains "la farid" as an old cow, citing Arabic verse for the sense of age. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 189} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 190}

On "la farid", the reports of Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, 'Ata al-Khurasani, al-Dahhak, Abu al-'Aliya, Qatada, al-Suddi and Ibn Zayd agree that it means an old, aged cow, al-Suddi adding "that bears no more". "Wa la bikr" is a young one: so say Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, Qatada, al-Dahhak and Abu al-'Aliya, while al-Suddi differs, saying it is one that has borne only a single calf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 191} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 192} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 193}
By "'awan" al-Tabari understands a middle-aged cow that has calved repeatedly, not a description of the young one, and he argues that "between that" cannot precede the old and the young, since it refers back to them. He cites verses of al-Akhtal, Tamim ibn Muqbil and al-Farazdaq for the word, and reports from Mujahid, Ibn Abbas (through Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, in Sharik's doubt) and al-Dahhak that it lies between young and old. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 193} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 194} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 195}
