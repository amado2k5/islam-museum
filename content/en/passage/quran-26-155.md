## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 154 to 156 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "He said, 'This is a she-camel that has drink, and you will have a drink on a known day.'" Salih said to Thamud when they asked him a sign by which they knew his truthfulness. So he brought them a she-camel, brought her out from a rock or a plateau: 'This is a she-camel, O people, it has drink, and you will have the like of it on another known day. You have no drinking. You have no right on the day it is received to drink from its drink, nor is it allowed to drink anything during your day.' By drinking, he means: the share and share of the water. He says: it has a share of water, and you have the same. Drinking, drinking, and drinking are all nouns with damma, fatha, and kasra. It has been narrated from the Arabs by hearsay: the last of these is the least in drinking and drinking. And His saying: "And do not touch it with harm," means: do not touch it with what harms it, such as harming it, killing, and the like. In the section, he quotes from Ibn Abbas.
