## Summary
Verse 200 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 200 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 200: patience, outlasting and keeping watch (pp. 501-506, reports 8386-8397).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the meaning of this command.

**First view: patience in religion, outlasting the disbelievers and keeping watch against them (pp. 501-502, reports 8386-8390).** Some said it means patience in the religion, outlasting the disbelievers and keeping watch against them. Al-Hasan (8386), through al-Muthanna from Suwayd ibn Nasr from Ibn al-Mubarak from al-Mubarak ibn Fadala: He commanded them to be patient in their religion and not to leave it for hardship or ease, pleasure or harm, and commanded them to outlast the disbelievers and to keep watch against the polytheists. Qatada (8387), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: patience in obedience to God, outlasting the people of error and keeping watch in the way of God. Qatada (8388), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar: outlasting the polytheists and keeping watch in the way of God. Ibn Jurayj (8389): patience in obedience, outlasting God's enemies and keeping watch in the way of God. Al-Dahhak (8390), through Yahya ibn Abi Talib from Yazid from Juwaybir: patience in what they were commanded, and outlasting the enemy and keeping watch against them.

**Second view: patience in religion and holding out for the promise (p. 502, report 8391).** Others said it means patience in the religion, holding out for the promise God made them for their obedience to Him, and keeping watch against their enemies. Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi (8391), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb from Abu Sakhr: be patient in your religion, hold out for the promise I made you, and keep watch against my enemy and yours until he gives up his religion for yours. In the editor's note Abu Sakhr is Humayd ibn Ziyad, who settled in Egypt; Ibn Hibban listed him among the reliable and he has an entry in al-Tahdhib (the scan garbles some of his epithets).

**Third view: patience in jihad (p. 503, reports 8392-8393).** Others said it means patience in jihad, outlasting the enemy and keeping watch against them. Zayd ibn Aslam (8392), through al-Muthanna from Ishaq from Ja'far ibn 'Awn from Hisham ibn Sa'd: patience in jihad, outlasting your enemy and keeping watch against him. Zayd ibn Aslam again (8393), through al-Muthanna from Mutarrif ibn 'Abd Allah al-Madani from Malik ibn Anas: Abu 'Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah wrote to 'Umar ibn al-Khattab mentioning hosts of the Byzantines and what he feared from them, and 'Umar wrote back: now then, whatever hardship befalls a believing servant, God makes relief after it, one hardship will never overcome two eases, and God says in His book the verse of Al 'Imran [[passage/quran-3-200|3:200]]. In the editor's note, Mutarrif ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Mutarrif ibn Sulayman al-Hilali al-Madani, client of Maymuna, mother of the believers, whose mother was Malik ibn Anas's sister, narrated from his maternal uncle Malik, Ibn Abi Dhi'b, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar al-'Umari and others, and al-Bukhari, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, al-Rabi' al-Muradi, Abu Hatim, Abu Zur'a and others narrated from him; Abu Hatim said he was unsettled in hadith but truthful, and Ibn Sa'd said he was reliable (the rest of the remark is garbled in the scan); he has entries in al-Tahdhib, al-Kabir and al-Jarh. The printed edition and the manuscript give an impossible attribution of origin and the right form is "al-Madani" or "al-Madini", as in the references and in Ibn Kathir (2: 337). The wording of the report in Ibn Kathir and in al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 114) has the particle "min" that dropped out of the printed edition and the manuscript, and the editor says it should be restored. He adds that it is a common mistake to say that "mahma" does not go with the past tense, since it occurs in reports, sayings and poetry, citing a saying of Abu Hurayra to al-Farazdaq in al-Kamil (1: 70) and a line of al-Aswad ibn Ya'fur in the Nawadir of Abu Zayd (159), which is described here and not reproduced. He says al-Hakim gave the report at length in al-Mustadrak (2: 300) with its chain, and the note continues on the next page: through Abu al-'Abbas al-Sayyari from 'Abd Allah ibn 'Ali from 'Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak from Hisham ibn Sa'd from Zayd ibn Aslam from his father from 'Umar that it reached him that Abu 'Ubayda was besieged in Syria and the people had massed against him, so 'Umar wrote to him with a greeting of peace and the same words on hardship and relief and one hardship not overcoming two eases, with the verse of Al 'Imran; Abu 'Ubayda wrote back with a greeting of peace, saying that God says in His book the verse of al-Hadid [[passage/quran-57-20|57:20]], to its end; 'Umar went out with the letter, sat on the pulpit, read it to the people of Medina and said: people of Medina, Abu 'Ubayda is only hinting to you to be eager for jihad. Al-Hakim said: this report is sound on the condition of Muslim, though the two did not issue it, and al-Dhahabi agreed.

**Fourth view: waiting for the prayer (pp. 504-506, reports 8394-8397).** Others said the keeping watch here is a keeping watch at the prayers, that is, waiting for one prayer after another. Abu Salama ibn 'Abd al-Rahman (8394), through al-Muthanna from Suwayd from Ibn al-Mubarak from Mus'ab ibn Thabit ibn 'Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr from Dawud ibn Salih: Abu Salama said to me: nephew, do you know about what this verse came down? I said: no. He said: nephew, in the time of the Prophet there was no raid on which one kept watch; it is waiting for prayer behind prayer. In the editor's note Mus'ab ibn Thabit's biography came earlier; Dawud ibn Salih al-Tammar al-Madani narrated from Abu Umama ibn Sahl ibn Hunayf, al-Qasim, Salim and Abu Salama, Ahmad said "I know no harm in him" and Ibn Hibban listed him among the reliable; Abu Salama ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf was a Successor who narrated from many Companions and Successors and was reliable, a jurist with much hadith; Ibn Kathir gave the report and quoted Ibn Mardawayh's version by another route, in which Abu Hurayra is the one who asked Abu Salama; al-Hakim gave it in al-Mustadrak (2: 301) through Sa'id ibn Mansur from Ibn al-Mubarak like al-Tabari's, except that in the answer Abu Salama says he heard Abu Hurayra say it; al-Suyuti gave it in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 113), attributing it to Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn al-Mundhir, al-Hakim, who declared it sound, and al-Bayhaqi in Shu'ab al-Iman; in all these places the wording is "after the prayer", while what is established in the manuscript is "behind the prayer", the copyist having first written "after" and then reshaped the letters into "behind", so he seems to have written it as he remembered it and then corrected it on seeing "behind" in the copy he was copying from.

'Ali (8395), through Abu al-Sa'ib from Ibn Fudayl from 'Abd Allah ibn Sa'id al-Maqburi from his grandfather from Shurahbil: the Messenger of God said: shall I tell you what God uses to expiate sins and errors? Perfecting the ablution despite hardships and waiting for one prayer after another: that is the watch. In the editor's note, Abu al-Sa'ib is Salm ibn Junada and Ibn Fudayl is Muhammad ibn Fudayl ibn Ghazwan; 'Abd Allah ibn Sa'id ibn Abi Sa'id al-Maqburi is very weak and was accused of lying, as shown earlier; he does not know who this Shurahbil is, and the chain is weak because of 'Abd Allah ibn Sa'id; were it sound he would think him Shurahbil ibn al-Simt al-Kindi, a senior Successor whose being a Companion is disputed and who was a contemporary of 'Ali, it being possible that Abu Sa'id al-Maqburi, who narrates from 'Ali directly, narrated from him; Ibn Kathir (2: 332) took the report from this place and attributed it to al-Tabari alone, and al-Suyuti (2: 114) alluded to it after Jabir's report, saying Ibn Jarir extracted the like from 'Ali; the sense is established from 'Ali by another sound route, but without the words "that is the watch"; al-Haythami (Majma' al-Zawa'id 2: 36) says Abu Ya'la and al-Bazzar narrated it and its men are the men of the Sahih; and al-Mundhiri (al-Targhib wa-l-Tarhib 1: 97) says Abu Ya'la and al-Bazzar narrated it with a sound chain, and al-Hakim, who said it is sound on the condition of Muslim.

Jabir (8396), through Musa ibn Sahl al-Ramli from Yahya ibn Wadih from Muhammad ibn Muhajir from Yahya ibn Yazid from Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa from Shurahbil: the Messenger of God said: shall I tell you what God uses to wipe out errors and expiate sins? We said: yes, Messenger of God. He said: perfecting the ablution in its places, many steps to the mosques and waiting for one prayer after another: that is the watch. In the editor's note, Muhammad ibn Muhajir ibn Abi Muslim al-Ansari al-Shami is reliable, as Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in and others declared; Yahya ibn Yazid al-Jazari, Abu Shayba al-Ruhawi, is described by al-Bukhari in al-Kabir as one whose hadith is not sound and is also listed in his book of the weak, while Ibn Abi Hatim reports from his father that there is no harm in him and that al-Bukhari should move him from there, and the editor judges that such a man's hadith is fair and that he did not narrate this one alone; Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa al-Jazari al-Ruhawi is reliable (Ibn Ma'in and others; Ibn Sa'd says reliable, much hadith, a jurist, a narrator of knowledge) and the six collections narrated from him; Shurahbil here is Ibn Sa'd al-Khatmi al-Madani, a client of the Ansar, disputed, but in the editor's view reliable, though confused at the end of his life when he passed a hundred, as he detailed in his commentary on the Musnad (no. 2104), and Ibn Khuzayma and Ibn Hibban drew on him in their Sahihs; Ibn Hibban showed in his Sahih that he is Shurahbil ibn Sa'd and gave the report through Abu 'Abd al-Rahim from Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa from Shurahbil ibn Sa'd from Jabir, Abu 'Abd al-Rahim being Khalid ibn Abi Yazid al-Harrani, reliable, so his transmission is a sound corroboration of Yahya ibn Yazid's; Ibn Kathir (2: 332) took it from al-Tabari's version; al-Mundhiri (1: 160-161) gave it from Ibn Hibban's; al-Haythami (Majma' al-Zawa'id 2: 37) attributed it to al-Bazzar and said its chain has Shurahbil ibn Sa'd, weak to the majority, though Ibn Hibban listed him among the reliable and extracted this report in his Sahih; and al-Suyuti (2: 114) attributed it to Ibn Jarir and Ibn Hibban.

Abu Hurayra (8397), through Abu Kurayb from Khalid ibn Makhlad from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from al-'Ala' ibn 'Abd al-Rahman from his father: the Messenger of God said: shall I tell you what God uses to lay down errors and raise by it ... and p. 506 breaks off here, with the text of the report continuing on p. 507.

The rest of report 8397 (p. 507): and raises ranks by? They said: yes, Messenger of God. He said: perfecting the ablution despite hardships, many steps to the mosques and waiting for one prayer after another; that is the watch, that is the watch. Abu Hurayra again (8398), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Isma'il ibn Ja'far from al-'Ala' ibn 'Abd al-Rahman from his father, from the Prophet, in similar words. In the editor's note on report 8397, Khalid ibn Makhlad is al-Qatawani and Muhammad ibn Ja'far is Ibn Abi Kathir, and a chain like this appeared in another report earlier; Ahmad gave it in the Musnad through Shu'ba from al-'Ala' from his father without the words "that is the watch", and also with those words through Malik from al-'Ala', and a third time through Malik with "that is the watch" three times at the end, which is the wording in the Muwatta; al-Nasa'i gave it (1: 34) through Malik, and Ibn Hibban in his Sahih through Malik; Ibn Kathir (2: 331) took it from Ibn Abi Hatim's version from Malik's report, like the Muwatta; Muslim gave it (1: 86) through Malik and through Shu'ba and says Malik's version with him has "that is the watch" twice; al-Mundhiri mentions it in al-Targhib wa-l-Tarhib (1: 128 and 97) and attributes it to Malik, Muslim, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i; and al-Suyuti (2: 114) adds al-Shafi'i and 'Abd al-Razzaq. In the note on report 8398, al-Qasim is Ibn al-Hasan and al-Husayn is Ibn Dawud al-Massisi, nicknamed "Sunayd"; the chain "al-Qasim from al-Husayn" recurs often in al-Tabari, in the commentary and in the history; he gave Sunayd's biography earlier, but found no biography for al-Qasim ibn al-Hasan, al-Tabari's teacher; Tarikh Baghdad (12: 432-433) has an entry for al-Qasim ibn al-Hasan ibn Yazid, Abu Muhammad al-Hamdhani the goldsmith, who died in 272, who might be the one meant, "but I am not at ease with it and cannot be certain, nor even prefer it", and he hopes to find something showing who this teacher was on another occasion. In the note on p. 508, Isma'il is Ibn Ja'far ibn Abi Kathir al-Ansari the reciter, reliable and trusted, and this report repeats the one before; Muslim and al-Tirmidhi also gave it through Isma'il ibn Ja'far, and al-Tirmidhi also through al-Darawardi from al-'Ala', saying that Abu Hurayra's report is fair and sound.

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 508-509).** Al-Tabari says the soundest interpretation of the verse is that of those who said that the address is to those who have believed God and His Messenger, and that the command to patience means patience in their religion and in obedience to their Lord, because God did not single out any sense of patience in religion and obedience, so none may be taken out of the plain wording of the revelation; thus the command is to patience in every sense of obedience to God in what He commanded and forbade, hard and severe, easy and light. As for outlasting, it means outlasting the enemies among the polytheists, because what is known of Arab speech about this form is that it is between two parties, or two or more, and is from one only rarely in a few words; so the believers were commanded to outlast their enemies until God gives them victory over them, raises His word and humiliates their enemies, and so that their enemy is not more patient than they are. In the editor's notes he refers to the earlier explanation of "patience" and says the printed edition has a mistaken wording whose right form is from the manuscript. Likewise keeping watch means keeping watch against their enemies and the enemies of their religion among the polytheists in the way of God. Al-Tabari sees the root of "ribat" as tying horses against the enemy, as the enemy tied their horses against them, and then the word came to be used for everyone who stays at a frontier post, defending those behind him from any enemy who wishes them harm and guarding those between him and them from aggressors, whether he has a horse he has tied or goes on foot with no mount. He says he took the word in this sense because it is the known sense of "ribat", and that speech is directed to the most prevalent known sense in people's usage and not the obscure one, until a proof comes to the contrary requiring it to be turned to the obscure sense, from the Book, or a report from the Messenger, or the agreement of the interpreters. In the editor's notes, the manuscript's wording on the enemy tying their horses is hard to read and may have been meant as "their steeds", but he left the printed edition's reading as a good one; "rujla" is going on foot without riding; "proof" is the subject of the later verb; and the printed edition and the manuscript have a different particle where the right form is the one he set, with another evident error in the printed edition in the same sentence.

**The closing of the verse (pp. 509-510, report 8399).** Al-Tabari says the command to fear God means: believers, beware of God, lest you oppose His command or go ahead of His prohibition, so that this may be the cause of your success, in that you remain in everlasting bliss and attain your requests with Him. In the editor's note the printed edition has a "wa" on the verb of going ahead and the right form is from the manuscript, and "go ahead of His prohibition" is used with a direct object as if he meant "get ahead of His prohibition", their getting ahead of it being that they hasten with their desires into forbidden things before God's prohibition turns them back from approaching them; he refers to earlier explanations of "perhaps" and of "success". Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi (8399), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb from Abu Sakhr, used to say of this place that the meaning is fearing God in what lies between God and you, in hope of success tomorrow when you meet Him.

**The end of Al 'Imran (p. 510).** The commentary on the sura of Al 'Imran closes at this place. In the editor's note, a part of the old division from which his manuscript was copied ends here, and it carries a text saying that the commentary on the sura in which women are mentioned follows, with a formula of blessing on the Prophet, his family and his companions; then comes what the editor set at the start of the commentary on the sura of al-Nisa. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 510}
