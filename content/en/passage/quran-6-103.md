## Summary
Verse 103 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 103 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari records that the interpreters differed on "eyes do not perceive Him, and He perceives the eyes". Some said it means that eyes do not encompass Him, while He encompasses them. Report 13694, from Ibn Abbas: no one's sight encompasses the King; report 13695, from Qatada: He is too great for eyes to perceive Him; then report 13696 begins, from Atiyya al-Awfi (through Sa'd ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam, from Khalid ibn Abd al-Rahman, from Abu Arfaja), and runs on to the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 13}

Report 13696 from Atiyya al-Awfi, on the verse "faces that day radiant, looking to their Lord" (75:22-23), concludes: they look at God, but their sight does not encompass Him because of His greatness, while His sight encompasses them; that is the saying "eyes do not perceive Him". The editor's footnote identifies the chain: Sa'd ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam al-Misri is trustworthy (the manuscript and print wrongly have "Yunus ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Hakam"); Khalid ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Khurasani al-Marwazi, of whom Abu Hatim said "a shaykh, no harm in him"; Abu Arfaja he could not identify; and Atiyya al-Awfi is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 14}

Al-Tabari gives the argument of those who held this view. God said of Pharaoh "until, when drowning overtook him" (10:90), describing the drowning as having "reached" Pharaoh, and drowning cannot be described as seeing him; so taking "do not perceive Him" to mean "do not see Him" is far-fetched, for one thing can overtake another without seeing it, as the companions of Moses said when Pharaoh's host drew near, "we are overtaken" (26:61), although God had promised Moses that they would not be overtaken (20:77). If a thing may see without overtaking and overtake without seeing, then the verse is far from meaning "eyes do not see Him"; it means "eyes do not encompass Him", because encompassing Him is not possible. The editor notes that the manuscript and print misquote the verse of Pharaoh and that he restored its wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 14}

They said: the believers and the people of paradise see their Lord with their eyes yet their eyes do not perceive Him, in the sense that they do not encompass Him, since it is not permissible to describe God as encompassed by anything. A parallel is that He is described as known yet not encompassed by knowledge, as in the Throne verse (2:255): denying that creatures encompass any of His knowledge is not denying that they know, and likewise denying that sight perceives Him is not denying that it sees Him; "perceiving" means encompassing, as Ibn Abbas said in the report above. If someone asks why the verse should not mean "eyes do not see Him", they answer that God reports in His book that faces on the Day of Resurrection are looking toward Him (75:22-23), and that His messenger told his community that they will see their Lord on that day as the moon is seen on the night of the full moon and as the sun is seen with no cloud beneath it. The editor notes that the manuscript drops the word for "full moon". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 15}

They continue: since God has reported what He has reported, and the messenger's reports make its meaning the looking of the eyes toward God, and God's book confirms itself in all its parts, and neither report can abrogate the other because reports are not abrogated (as al-Tabari has shown in his book "Latif al-Bayan 'an Usul al-Ahkam"), it follows that "eyes do not perceive Him" differs in meaning from "looking to their Lord": the people of paradise look at God with their eyes on the Day and do not perceive Him with them, so crediting both reports. The editor refers to the sound hadith on seeing the Lord on the Day of Resurrection in the collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim, and states that the two reports al-Tabari cites are sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 16}

Others said it means that eyes do not see Him while He sees the eyes. Report 13697, from al-Suddi: nothing sees Him, and He sees the creatures. Report 13698, from A'isha (through Hannad, Waki, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, Amir, Masruq): whoever tells you that the Prophet saw his Lord has lied; she cited this verse and 42:51 (a human is not spoken to by God except by revelation or from behind a veil), and said that he did see Gabriel in his own form twice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 16}

Report 13699, from Masruq: I asked A'isha, "Mother of the believers, did Muhammad see his Lord?" and she said, "Glory be to God, my hair stood on end at what you said!" and then recited this verse. Report 13700 is similar, through Dawud from al-Sha'bi; report 13701, from al-Sha'bi: A'isha said that whoever says anyone saw his Lord has made a grave lie against God. The footnotes say that Muslim reports the hadith of Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid from al-Sha'bi in abridged form, that "my hair stood on end" means it rose from fright, and that Muslim gives Dawud's hadith at length. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 17}

Al-Tabari says that the holders of this view take "perceiving" here to mean seeing, deny that God is seen by sight in this world and the next, and interpret "looking to their Lord" as awaiting God's mercy and reward. Some of them reinterpreted in various ways the reports from the Prophet confirming that the people of paradise will see their Lord, and others denied that they had come down from him, disputing that they were his words and referring the matter to their own reason, claiming that their reason rules out the possibility of seeing God with the eyes; they brought various kinds of embellishment and said much by way of inference. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 17}

Their chief proof, they claimed, was that sight sees only what is separate from it and not what touches it, and what is separate has a space or gap between; so if eyes saw their Lord on the Day of Resurrection as they see persons today, the Maker would have to be bounded, and whoever describes Him so describes Him with the attributes of bodies, which can increase and decrease. They added that the nature of sight is to perceive colours as the nature of hearing is to perceive sounds and of the one who sniffs to perceive scents; as it is not allowed to judge hearing apart from sounds, it is not allowed to judge sight apart from colours, and since God cannot be described as having colour, He cannot be described as seen. The editor corrects two spellings in the print and the manuscript in the footnotes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 18}

Others said it means that the eyes of creatures do not perceive Him in this world, while in the hereafter they do; "perceiving" for them is seeing. They argued that although perceiving may in some uses mean something other than seeing, seeing is one of its meanings, for it is not allowed that a person's sight reach a thing and see it and yet not perceive it, even if he has not encompassed all its parts by seeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 18}

They said: seeing what the viewer beholds is perceiving it, as against what he has not seen; and God has reported that on the Day of Resurrection some faces look to Him, so it is impossible that they look to Him and do not perceive Him by sight. Since there can be no contradiction in God's reports, "eyes do not perceive Him" must be particular, not general: they do not perceive Him in this world, while He perceives the eyes in this world and the next, God having made the exception He made with "looking to their Lord". Others of this group said the verse is particular, but it may mean any of these: the eyes of the wrongdoers do not perceive Him in this world or the next, while the eyes of the believers and the friends of God do; or the eyes do not perceive Him by reaching the limit and encompassing, but by seeing they do (the editor refers to the earlier note on the use of "bal"); or they do not perceive Him in this world and do in the next; or the eyes of one who sees Him do not perceive Him in the way the Eternal perceives the eyes of His creatures, so that what is denied of them is what He affirms of Himself, since their sight is weak and penetrates only what He strengthens it to penetrate, while all of it lies open to His sight. They added that there is no doubt the saying is particular and that God's friends will see Him with their eyes on that day, though we do not know which of the four senses of particularity is meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 19}

Others said the verse is general: no one's sight will perceive God in this world or the next, but on the Day of Resurrection God will create for His friends a sixth sense besides their five, and they will see Him with it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 19}

They argued that God denied of the eyes that they perceive Him without indicating, in the verse or elsewhere, that this is particular, while in another verse He reports that faces look toward Him; God's reports do not conflict, and both are sound in meaning as revealed (the editor follows the manuscript against the print here). They argued from reason as well: if it were possible to see Him in the next world with these very eyes, even with their powers increased, it would have to be possible to see Him in this world, however weakly, since every sense is created to perceive something and perceives it, however faint, unless it is destroyed; since that is not found in our eyes in this world, they cannot be otherwise in the next but keep their constitution in perceiving only what they perceive in this world. As God has reported that faces in the next world see Him, they see Him by other than the sense of sight, since His report cannot be false. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 20}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: the correct view with us is what the reports from the Messenger of God agree on, that he said that you will see your Lord on the Day of Resurrection as you see the moon on the night of the full moon and as you see the sun with no cloud beneath it; the believers see Him and the unbelievers are veiled from Him that day, as God says in al-Mutaffifin (83:15). He then begins his reply to what the deniers of seeing God with the eyes on that day argued, namely that sight sees only what is separate from it with a space or gap between. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 20}

Al-Tabari goes on with his reply to those who hold that seeing God with the eyes would give Him a limit and an end and is therefore void. They are asked: do you know of anything described as managing affairs, other than your Maker, that is not either in contact with you or apart from you? If they claim that they do, they are asked to show it, and there is no way; if they say they do not, they are told: have you not known Him as neither in contact nor apart, described by management and action, and did you hold knowing Him impossible? Why then do you deny that eyes, which as a rule see only what is apart from them with a gap between, may see Him although He is not apart from them and there is no gap, just as hearts know of no managing agent except one in contact or apart, and you hold that they know Him as neither? They are then asked for the difference, and whatever they say of the one, they will be bound to say of the other. The editor corrects a conjunction and two readings in the print and manuscript in the footnotes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 21}

They are likewise questioned about their argument that the nature of sight is to perceive colours as the nature of hearing is to perceive sounds: have you not known, among what you have witnessed, any agent of management and action except one with colour, while you have known Him as managing without colour? If they say yes, they cannot do otherwise than concede it, unless they lie and claim to have seen an agent without colour, in which case they are asked to show it and there is no way. Then: why do you deny that eyes perceive only colours in what you have witnessed, yet may perceive other things, as you did not find your minds knowing an agent of management except one with colour, and yet found them knowing Him without colour? Again they are asked for the difference. The editor notes in the footnote the variant wording of the print and manuscript here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 22}

Al-Tabari adds that those who hold this view raise questions that are confusing, and he dislikes to set them down and lengthen the book with them and their answers, since his aim in the book is not to expose their embellishments but to explain the meaning of the verses of the Qur'an; he gave the amount he did only so the reader would know that they go back in their view only to what Satan has confused for them, which is easy for the people of truth to show up as false, and that they go back to no firm verse and to no report from the Messenger of God, sound or weak. Then he explains "and He is the Subtle, the All-Aware": God is the one for whom perceiving the eyes and encompassing them is easy, including the seeing of what is hard for them to perceive and encompass; and "al-Khabir" is the one who knows His creation and their eyes and the cause of their being unable to perceive Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 22}

So by His power He dealt gently and fashioned the eyes of His creatures in a form that does not perceive Him, and in His knowledge He is aware how to order them and their affairs and what is best for His creation. Report 13702, from Abu al-Aliya: "al-Latif" means about drawing them out, and "al-Khabir" about knowing their place (through Hannad and Ibn Waki, from Waki, from Abu Ja'far al-Razi, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 23}
