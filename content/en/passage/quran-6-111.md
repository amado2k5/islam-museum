## Summary
Verse 111 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 111 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

God says to His prophet Muhammad: despair, Muhammad, of the success of these who set up idols and images as equals to their Lord, who say to you, "if you bring us a sign we will believe in you"; for if We sent down the angels to them so that they saw them openly, and the dead spoke to them through Our bringing them back to life as proof for you and a sign of your prophethood, and told them that you are right in what you say and that what you brought them is the truth from God, and if We gathered everything before them and made them face you, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 46}

they would not believe, nor credit you, nor follow you, unless God willed it for those of them whom He wills; "but most of them are ignorant": they are ignorant that it is so, supposing that belief is in their hands and unbelief too, believing whenever they wish and disbelieving whenever they wish. It is not so; that is in My hand: none of them believes except the one I guided to it and gave success, and none disbelieves except the one I forsook from right guidance and led astray. It is said that this was revealed about the mockers of the Messenger of God among the polytheists of Quraysh. Report 13755, from Ibn Jurayj: it was revealed about the mockers who asked the Prophet for the sign; he was told to say "signs are with God alone, and what will make you perceive that when it comes they will not believe", and about them was revealed "even if We sent down the angels to them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 47}

Others said that "they would not believe" is meant of the people of wretchedness, while "unless God wills" is an exception from "believe", meant of the people of belief and felicity. Report 13756, from Ibn Abbas: "they would not believe" are the people of wretchedness; then He said "unless God wills", and these are the people of felicity for whom it had already gone forth in His knowledge that they would enter belief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 47}

Al-Tabari chooses: the better of the two sayings is that of Ibn Abbas, because God, in "they would not believe", made general the people mentioned before in "they swore by God their strongest oaths that if a sign came to them they would believe in it"; it may be that those who asked for the sign were the mockers whom Ibn Jurayj says were meant, but there is no indication of that in the apparent sense of the revelation and no report by which proof stands, and God's report comes out as a general one, so the saying that it was meant for the people of wretchedness among them is better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 48}

The readers differed over "wa hasharna 'alayhim kulla shay'in qibalan". The readers of Medina read "qibalan" (qaf with kasra, ba with fatha), meaning "face to face", from "I met him qibalan", that is, openly and visibly. Most of the readers of Kufa and Basra read "qubulan" (damma on both qaf and ba), which has three possible aspects: one, that "qubul" is the plural of "qabil", as "rughuf" is the plural of "raghif" and "qudub" of "qadib", meaning guarantors and sureties, so that the sense is: We gathered everything before them as guarantors guaranteeing to them what We promise for their belief or threaten for their unbelief, and they would still not believe unless God willed; the second, that it means confronting and facing, from "I came to you qubulan, not duburan" (from the front, not the back); and the third, that the sense is: We gathered everything before them tribe by tribe, kind by kind and group by group, so that "qubul" would be the plural of "qabil", which is the tribe (the page ends here, in the middle of this third aspect). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 48}
