## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "you will find them the most eager of people for life, even more than those who associate others with God", al-Tabari says those meant are the Jews, the most eager of people for life in this world and the most averse to death, reported from Ibn Abbas, Abu al-'Aliya, al-Rabi' and Mujahid, their dislike of death arising from their knowledge of the long disgrace awaiting them in the next life. "And more than those who associate" means more eager than they, as one says "he is the bravest of people and than 'Antara", so "min" appears after the conjunction to restore the sense. God described the Jews as more eager than the polytheists because they believe in resurrection and know the punishment prepared for them, while the polytheists do not believe in resurrection or punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 369} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 370}

On who "those who associate" are, al-Tabari gives two views: that they are the Magians, who do not believe in resurrection, from Abu al-'Aliya and al-Rabi'; or that they are those who deny resurrection generally, from Ibn Abbas that the polytheist hopes for no resurrection after death and so loves a long life, while the Jew knows the disgrace awaiting him for squandering the knowledge he has; and from Ibn Zayd that the Jews are more eager than these. On "each of them would love to be granted a life of a thousand years", the polytheist who despairs of any resurrection after this world's end wishes for a long life, so that some greet one another with "ten thousand years"; from Ibn Abbas that this is the saying of the non-Arabs, "zeh hazar sal" (the editor explains the Persian phrase and notes that al-A'mash's report from Sa'id has a break in its chain), and from Qatada that error made long life dear to them. On "nor will it remove him from the punishment to be granted long life" he says that long life does not distance him from God's punishment; "huwa" is a supporting pronoun and "an yu'ammar" the subject of "muzahzihihi", which he holds nearest to right, setting out other views of the grammarians and the view of some interpreters, from Abu al-'Aliya, al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd, that "an" means "even if he is granted long life", which he says departs from known Arab usage. "Muzahzih" means one who removes and puts aside, for life must end and return to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 370} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 371} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 372} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 373} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 374} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 375}

On "nor will it remove him from the punishment", al-Tabari reports from Ibn Abbas that it will not put him aside from the punishment; from Abu al-'Aliya and al-Rabi' that even if granted long life it will neither aid nor save him; and from Ibn Zayd that were he granted life as long as Iblis's, it would not benefit him while he disbelieves. On "God sees what they do" he says He is the One who sees, nothing of their deeds hidden from Him, comprehending and keeping them until He gives them the punishment as recompense; "basir" is originally "mubsir", turned to the form "fa'il" as "musmi'" became "sami'" and "mu'lim" became "alim". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 376} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 377}
