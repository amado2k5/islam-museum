## Place in the museum

**Commentary on verse 100 (pp. 434-439)**

Al-Tabari says: those who were the first to precede the people to belief in God and His Messenger, "of the Emigrants", who left their people and kin and abandoned their homes and countries, "and the Helpers", who aided the Messenger of God against his enemies among the people of unbelief in God and His Messenger, "and those who followed them in goodness", that is, those who took their way in belief in God and His Messenger and in migration from the land of war to the land of Islam, seeking God's pleasure; and they have won God's being pleased with them and their being pleased with Him. The editor refers to earlier explanations of migration, the Helpers and doing good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 434}

The interpreters differed over who is meant by "the first forerunners". Some said they are those who pledged to the Messenger of God the Pledge of Good Pleasure or lived to see it. Report 17099, from Amir al-Sha'bi, through Ibn Waki', Muhammad ibn Bishr and Isma'il: he who reached the Pledge of Good Pleasure; report 17100, from him, through Ibn Fudayl and Mutarrif: the first Emigrants are those who reached the pledge under the tree; report 17101, from him, through Ibn Bashshar, Yahya and Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: the first Emigrants are those who were present at the Pledge of Good Pleasure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 435}

Report 17102, from al-Sha'bi, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Sufyan and Mutarrif: the first Emigrants are those who were before the pledge up to the pledge, and whoever was after the pledge is not of the first Emigrants; report 17103, from him, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hushaym, Isma'il and Mutarrif: they are those who made the Pledge of Good Pleasure; report 17104, from Amir, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Dawud: what separates the two migrations is the Pledge of Good Pleasure, which is the pledge of al-Hudaybiyya; report 17105, from al-Sha'bi, to the same effect, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid and Mutarrif: they are those who made the Pledge of Good Pleasure. Report 17106, from al-Sha'bi, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Anbar Abu Zubayd and Mutarrif: the first Emigrants are those who reached the Pledge of Good Pleasure. The editor notes that Anbar Abu Zubayd is Anbar ibn al-Qasim al-Zubaydi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 436}

Others said rather that they are those who prayed toward both qiblas with the Messenger of God. Report 17107, from Abu Musa, through Ibn Waki', Yahya ibn Adam, Qays, Uthman al-Thaqafi and a client of Abu Musa: the first Emigrants are those who prayed toward both qiblas with the Prophet; report 17108, from a client of Abu Musa, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Qays ibn al-Rabi', Uthman ibn al-Mughira and Abu Zur'a: I asked Abu Musa al-Ash'ari about the words "the first forerunners of the Emigrants and Helpers", and he said they are those who prayed toward both qiblas together. Report 17109, from Qatada, through Ibn Waki', his father and Abu Hilal: I said to Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab "why were they called the first Emigrants?", and he said whoever prayed with the Prophet toward both qiblas is among the first Emigrants; reports 17110 and 17111, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, say the same, through Ibn Bashshar, Yahya ibn Sa'id and Ibn Abi Aruba and through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id; reports 17112 and 17113, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Ibn Sirin, are alike; report 17114, from Ibn Sirin, through Ibn Bashshar, Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh and Ibn Awn: the first Emigrants are those who prayed toward both qiblas; report 17115, from Qatada, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: they are those who prayed toward both qiblas together. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 437}

As for those who followed the first Emigrants and Helpers in goodness, they are those whom God made Muslims in their Islam and who took their path in migration, support and good works. Report 17116, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Abu Ma'shar: Umar passed a man reciting this verse, "the first forerunners of the Emigrants and Helpers and those who followed them in goodness", and said "who taught you this verse?", and he said "Ubayy ibn Ka'b taught it to me", and he said "do not leave me until I take you to him", and he came to him and said "did you teach this verse?", and he said "yes", and he said "and did you hear it from the Messenger of God?", and he said "yes", and he said "I used to think we had been raised a rank no one after us could reach", and Ubayy said "the confirmation of that is in the opening of the verse in the beginning of al-Jumu'a, the middle of al-Hashr and the end of al-Anfal. As for the beginning of al-Jumu'a, 'and others of them who have not yet joined them', [[passage/quran-62-3|62:3]]; as for the middle of al-Hashr, 'those who came after them say, our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in belief', [[passage/quran-59-10|59:10]]; and as for the end of al-Anfal, 'those who believed afterward, migrated and strove with you, they are of you', [[passage/quran-8-75|8:75]]". The editor explains that Umar's question was about reading the verse with the Helpers in the genitive and with the "and" in "and those who followed them", and that the addition in brackets is from Ibn Kathir's commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 437}

Report 17117, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through Abu Kurayb, al-Hasan ibn Atiyya and Abu Ma'shar: Umar ibn al-Khattab passed a man reciting "the first forerunners of the Emigrants and Helpers" until he reached "and they were pleased with Him", and Umar took his hand and said "who taught you this?", and he said "Ubayy ibn Ka'b", and he said "do not leave me until I take you to him"; and when he came to him Umar said "did you teach this verse in this way?", and he said "yes", and he said "did you hear it from the Messenger of God?", and he said "yes", and he said "I used to think we had been raised a rank no one after us could reach", and Ubayy said "yes, the confirmation of this verse is in the beginning of Surat al-Jumu'a, 'and others of them who have not yet joined them' up to 'and He is the Mighty, the Wise', and in Surat al-Hashr, 'those who came after them', and in al-Anfal, 'those who believed afterward, migrated and strove with you, they are of you', to the end of the verse". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 438}

It is also reported of Umar, which is report 17118, from Habib ibn al-Shahid and from Ibn Amir al-Ansari, through Ahmad ibn Yusuf, al-Qasim, Hajjaj and Harun: Umar ibn al-Khattab read "the first forerunners of the Emigrants and the Helpers, those who followed them in goodness", putting the Helpers in the nominative and not adding the "and" before "those who", and Zayd ibn Thabit said to him "and those who followed them in goodness", and Umar said "those who followed them in goodness", and Zayd said "the Commander of the Faithful knows better", and Umar said "bring me Ubayy ibn Ka'b", and he came and he asked him about it, and Ubayy said "and those who followed them in goodness", and Umar said "then we follow Ubayy". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 438}

Al-Tabari says: the reading is with the Helpers in the genitive, joined to the Emigrants, though it is reported of al-Hasan al-Basri that he read the Helpers in the nominative, joined to "the forerunners". The reading al-Tabari allows no other than is the genitive on "the Helpers", because the authority of the reciters is agreed on it and because the forerunner was of both groups, of the Emigrants and of the Helpers, the report being intended about the forerunner of both groups and not about all of them, and with the addition of the "and" in "and those who followed them in goodness", because that is so in the codices of all the Muslims, the followers in goodness being other than the Emigrants and Helpers; and the forerunners are in the nominative through the pronoun that refers back to them in the statement of God's being pleased with them and their being pleased with Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 439}

The sense is: God's pleasure with all of them, because they obeyed Him and answered His prophet in what he called them to of His command and prohibition, and the pleasure with Him of the first forerunners of the Emigrants and Helpers and those who followed them in goodness, because He gave them abundant reward for their obedience to Him and their belief in Him and in His prophet; "and He prepared for them gardens under which rivers flow", which they enter, "abiding in them", staying in them "forever", neither dying in them nor leaving them; "that is the great triumph". The editor refers to earlier explanations of abiding, forever and triumph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 439}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
