**Commentary on verse 100 (pp. 434-435)**

Al-Tabari says: those who were the first to precede the people to belief in God and His Messenger, "of the Emigrants", who left their people and kin and abandoned their homes and countries, "and the Helpers", who aided the Messenger of God against his enemies among the people of unbelief in God and His Messenger, "and those who followed them in goodness", that is, those who took their way in belief in God and His Messenger and in migration from the land of war to the land of Islam, seeking God's pleasure; and they have won God's being pleased with them and their being pleased with Him. The editor refers to earlier explanations of migration, the Helpers and doing good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 434}

The interpreters differed over who is meant by "the first forerunners". Some said they are those who pledged to the Messenger of God the Pledge of Good Pleasure or lived to see it. Report 17099, from Amir al-Sha'bi, through Ibn Waki', Muhammad ibn Bishr and Isma'il: he who reached the Pledge of Good Pleasure; report 17100, from him, through Ibn Fudayl and Mutarrif: the first Emigrants are those who reached the pledge under the tree; report 17101, from him, through Ibn Bashshar, Yahya and Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: the first Emigrants are those who were present at the Pledge of Good Pleasure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 435}

Report 17102, from al-Sha'bi, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Sufyan and Mutarrif: the first Emigrants are those who were before the pledge up to the pledge, and whoever was after the pledge is not of the first Emigrants; report 17103, from him, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hushaym, Isma'il and Mutarrif: they are those who made the Pledge of Good Pleasure; report 17104, from Amir, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Dawud: what separates the two migrations is the Pledge of Good Pleasure, which is the pledge of al-Hudaybiyya; report 17105, from al-Sha'bi, to the same effect, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid and Mutarrif: they are those who made the Pledge of Good Pleasure. Report 17106 begins, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Anbar, and continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 435}

We stop here at the foot of p. 435, in the middle of the commentary on verse 100 (at the start of report 17106); the next batch begins at p. 436. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 435}
