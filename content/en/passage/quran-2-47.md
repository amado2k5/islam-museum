## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "O Children of Israel, remember My favour which I bestowed upon you and that I preferred you over the worlds" al-Tabari says the first part is explained as in verse 40, and that "I preferred you" is among the blessings recalled to them, meaning their forebears, since the forefathers' merits are the sons' merits. He holds that the wording is general but meant in a particular sense: I preferred you over the world among which you lived in your time. He reports from Qatada that He preferred them over the people of that time; from Abu al-Aliya that it was by the kingship, messengers and books they were given, over their age, since every age has its world; from Mujahid, over those among whom they lived; and from Ibn Zayd the same, citing the verse of surah al-Dukhan, adding that among them were those turned into apes, the most hateful of His creatures to Him, while this community was told "you are the best community". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 23} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 24} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 25}
Al-Tabari's evidence for the particular sense is what he reports from Bahz ibn Hakim, from his father, from his grandfather, who heard the Prophet say: "You complete seventy communities; you are the best of them and the most honoured by God". The editor traces it to al-Tirmidhi, Ahmad, Ibn Majah and al-Darimi, vouches for the narrators, and notes that Ibn Kathir reports al-Tirmidhi calling it good. Al-Tabari holds that this report shows the Children of Israel were not preferred over the community of Muhammad, and the verse means what he has set out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 25}
