## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 26 to 27 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Do they not prostrate to Allah, who brings forth the hidden in the heavens and the earth and knows what you conceal and what you reveal": The reciters differed in the reading of the phrase "do not prostrate to Allah." Some Meccans, some Medinans, and Kufans read "Ala" with a lighter meaning: "O these people, prostrate," so they concealed "these" as sufficient to use the prayer "Ya" over it. Some of them mentioned by hearing from the Arabs: 'Ala Irma, O you give charity to us.' He also cited Al-Akhtal's verse: 'O my Muslim, O Hind, the Prophet of Badr, even if he is alive except the end of time.' According to this reading, 'Prostrate in this place' is jussive, and there is no place for the phrase 'ala' in grammatical grammar. Most reciters of Medina, Kufa, and Basra read this as 'Ala Yasjudu' with a shadda ('ala'), meaning: Satan has made their deeds adornable for them so that they would not prostrate to Allah 'Ala' in the accusative case, as I mentioned of its meaning 'la'la', and they prostrate in the accusative case with 'an'. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid and Ibn Zayd.
