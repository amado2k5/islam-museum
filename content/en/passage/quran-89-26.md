## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 26 to 32 as a single section. He interprets the phrase, “He will say, ‘Oh, I wish I had sent forth [good deeds] for my [true] life!’” He explains that God, may He be exalted, is informing us of the longing of the son of Adam on the Day of Resurrection, and his regret for neglecting righteous deeds in this world that would have granted him eternal life in unending bliss: “Oh, I wish I had sent forth [good deeds] for my [true] life in this world, for this life after which there is no death, that would have saved me from God’s wrath and earned me His pleasure.” In this section, he quotes statements from Mujahid, Qatadah, and Al-Hasan.
