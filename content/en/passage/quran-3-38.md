## Summary
Verse 38 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 38 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the word for 'there', and Zechariah's hope.** Al-Tabari says the opening word of the verse means 'at that moment', that is, when Zechariah saw what he saw with Mary of God's provision and the favour He gave her without any human cause, and beheld with her fresh fruit out of season on earth; he conceived a hope for a child despite his age, from a barren woman. He hoped God would grant him a child in their condition, as He provided Mary, withdrawn from people, with summer fruit in winter and winter fruit in summer, though such is not the way of the world, just as a barren woman's bearing is against custom. So he turned to God for a child and asked for a blessed line, because, as is reported, the people of Zechariah's house had died out. The editor explains the grammar of the sentence, says he kept the preposition of the manuscript though the printed one is also right, and that a word repeated in both witnesses is a copyist's haste. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 359} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 360}

**Reports of al-Suddi, Ibn Abbas, Ikrima and Ibn Ishaq.** Report 6940 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says that when Zechariah saw her state he said that a Lord who gave her this out of season is able to give him good offspring; he longed for a child, stood and prayed, then called on his Lord in secret, and al-Tabari cites his supplications at [[passage/quran-19-4|19:4]] and following and [[passage/quran-21-89|21:89]]. Report 6941 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Yala ibn Muslim, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) says that on seeing summer fruit in winter and winter fruit in summer with Mary he said that He who brings this out of season is able to give him a child, and that was when he prayed. Report 6942 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Abu Bakr, from Ikrima) says he entered the sanctuary, shut the doors and communed with his Lord with the words of [[passage/quran-19-4|19:4]]-[[passage/quran-19-6|6]], and then the angels called to him as he stood praying, as in [[passage/quran-3-39|3:39]]. Report 6943 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from some scholars) says he prayed then, when he was old, childless and his household had died out, then complained to his Lord, and the angels called him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 360} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 361}

**The senses of offspring, blessed and 'from You'.** Al-Tabari says the word for offspring means progeny and 'good' means blessed; report 6944 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says blessed. The editor refers to earlier treatments of both words and notes that al-Tabari left offspring unexplained in the earlier places and explained it here, which he puts down to the abridgement of the book. Al-Tabari says the phrase 'from You' means 'from Your presence' and that the word for offspring is a collective that can be singular, and here is singular, since God reports Zechariah's request elsewhere in the singular, for an heir ([[passage/quran-19-5|19:5]]), showing he asked for one; the adjective is feminine because the word is feminine in form. He cites two lines: one of a poet telling a man that his father was a caliph whom another woman bore, with a feminine verb though the child is male because the word for caliph is feminine in form; the other of a poet on a silent mountain snake, with a feminine adjective for the form of the word for snake and then a masculine verb in sense, as he meant a male snake. He says this is allowed with nouns not applied to a named individual, such as 'beast', 'offspring', 'caliph', but if a man is named with one, his verb and attribute may not be made feminine. The editor says he does not know the poets, that both lines are in al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, the second also in Lisan al-Arab, that the printed text garbled it, cites lines of Antara on venom, glosses the words for 'silent' and 'toothless', and takes the poet to describe a shrewd man who gives no warning before he strikes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 361} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 362} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 363}

**The sense of the name of hearing.** Al-Tabari says the divine name closing the prayer means 'hearer of prayer' but is stronger in praise, meaning 'possessor of hearing'; the editor refers to earlier treatments. He reports that one of the Basran grammarians held it means 'You hear what You are called with'. Al-Tabari concludes that the sense of the verse is: at that moment Zechariah asked his Lord to grant him from Himself a blessed child, and God is the possessor of hearing for the prayer of whoever calls on Him. This is al-Tabari's reading. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 363}
