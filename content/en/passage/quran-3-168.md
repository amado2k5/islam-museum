## Summary
Verse 168 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 168 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 168: those who said of their brothers, and stayed behind (8199-8203, pp. 381-383).** Abu Ja'far: what is meant is those who dissembled, the relative phrase being in apposition to them and so in the accusative, though it may also be nominative as an explanation of what is implied in the preceding verb. So the sense is that God willed to know those who said of their brothers who were struck down with the Muslims in their war against the idolaters at Uhud and were killed there among their clans and people (these hypocrites having stayed behind from jihad with their brothers and clans): had those killed at Uhud obeyed us they would not have been killed there. Then God told His prophet: say, Muhammad, to those speakers, "repel"; one says "I warded killing off so-and-so" meaning I repelled it from him, and the editor refers to the earlier explanation of "dar'" (volume 2). Al-Tabari cites a line by al-Muthaqqib al-'Abdi in which a she-camel complains of her rider's constant travelling (this describes the sense of the line and does not quote its words); the editor says its tracing and explanation came in volume 2 and that citing it for the sense of repelling is odd from one like Abu Ja'far. Al-Tabari then explains the meaning: say to them, repel death from yourselves if you are truthful, hypocrites, in saying that our brothers, had they obeyed us in leaving jihad with Muhammad and the fight against Abu Sufyan and those with him of Quraysh, would not have been killed there by the sword and would have been alive by staying with you and lagging behind from joining the jihad against God's enemies with him; for you stayed behind from fighting them and lagged behind their jihad, and you are certain to die (pp. 381-382). The editor says the construction is "say to them: repel ... death from yourselves" and that he placed in brackets what is needed from the verse's own text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 381} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 382}

Ibn Ishaq (8199), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: it means those who were struck with you of their clans and people; that is, death is unavoidable, so if you can ward it off from yourselves, do; for they dissembled and left jihad only from eagerness to remain in this world and flight from death; the report is in the Sira (3: 125), a continuation of reports ending with 8194. Then those who said the speakers are the ones of whom God said that He knows the hypocrites: Qatada (8200), through Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that it was sent down about 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy (with a condemnatory epithet in Qatada's wording). Al-Suddi (8201), through Muhammad, from Ahmad, from Asbat: they are 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy and his companions. Ibn Jurayj (8202), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj: it is 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy, who stayed and said what he said to his brothers who went out with the Prophet on the day of Uhud; and from Mujahid, Jabir ibn 'Abdallah said: it is 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul. Al-Rabi' (8203), through 'Ammar, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father: it was sent down about 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy (p. 383). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 382} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 383}

**End of a part of the old division (pp. 383-384).** In a note on p. 383 the editor says that at this point a part of the old division from which his manuscript was copied comes to an end, and that the manuscript carries words to the effect that the interpretation of the next verse will follow, God willing; the note continues at the top of p. 384, giving the manuscript's closing formula (praise to God for His kindness and favour, with blessing and peace on the Prophet and his family) and the opening of the next part, with its basmala, a prayer for ease and the chain: Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Dawud told us, he said Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Jarir told us; and he refers to his comment on this chain earlier (the reference numbers are garbled in the scanned text). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 383} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 384}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 384}
