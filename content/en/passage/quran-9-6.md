**Commentary on verse 6 (pp. 138-140)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses His prophet: if any of the polytheists you were ordered to fight and kill after the sacred months asks you for protection, Muhammad, so as to hear God's word from you, which is the Qur'an, then grant him protection, that is give him security until he hears God's word as you recite it to him, and then convey him to his place of safety, that is send him back after hearing, if he refuses to become Muslim and takes no warning from what you recited, to where he is safe from you and those who obey you, until he reaches his home and his polytheist people; "that is because they are a people who do not know" means you do this, giving them security so they can hear the Qur'an and returning them to safety if they refuse Islam, because they are ignorant, who do not grasp God's proof and do not know what belief would bring them or the burden of sin in leaving it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 138}

Report 16481, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "if any of the polytheists seeks your protection" means any of those he was ordered to fight, whom he should protect; the editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. Report 16482, from al-Suddi: God's word is the Qur'an. Report 16483, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: a person who comes to hear what you say and what was sent down to you is safe until he comes and hears God's word and reaches the place of safety he came from; report 16484 is similar. Report 16485, from Sa'id, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub and Ja'far: the Messenger went out raiding and met the enemy; the Muslims brought out a polytheist and levelled spears at him, and the man said lift your weapons from me and let me hear God's word; they told him to testify that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger, to put away the rivals and disown al-Lat and al-Uzza, and he said I call you to witness that I have done so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 139}

Report 16486, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "then convey him to his place of safety" means that if what you recite and tell him does not suit him, convey him, and this is not abrogated. There was disagreement over whether the ruling of this verse is abrogated, some saying it is not and others that it is. Report 16487, from al-Dahhak, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Juwaybir: "kill the polytheists wherever you find them" was abrogated by the verse allowing favour afterward or ransom, [[passage/quran-47-4|47:4]]; report 16488, from al-Suddi, says the same. Others said rather that the words of 47:4 about favour were abrogated by "kill the polytheists"; report 16489, from Qatada, through Ibn Waki, Abda ibn Sulayman and Ibn Abi Aruba, says that the binding of captives in 47:4 was abrogated by the command to kill. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 140}

Al-Tabari says the sound view in his opinion is that this is not abrogated, since abrogation means removing a ruling established by another, and there is no valid proof that God's ruling in the case of polytheists was ever to kill them in every circumstance and was then abrogated by leaving them alive for ransom or favour; ransom, favour and killing were always among the Messenger's rulings concerning them from his first war, from the day of Badr, so the sense of the verse is: kill the polytheists wherever you find them, take them to be killed, favoured or ransomed, and besiege them. The editor refers to what al-Tabari says on abrogation in the indices. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 140}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
