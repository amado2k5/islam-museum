## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 54 to 55 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "reclining on its mats from the Istibriq and the reaping of the two gardens": "And whoever fears the standing before his Lord enjoys two gardens," "reclining on mattresses." Thus, reclining on the present is the meaning of the previous statement, because the preceding one means the news about one who fears the standing of his Lord that he is in blessing and joy, enjoying the two gardens. And His saying: "On the beds of their linings are of Istibraq and the reaping of the two Paradises," the Exalted says: The linings of these bedding are thick brocade, and among the Arabs, Istibraq means what is thick and coarse in brocade. Some scholars of Arab speech from Basra used to say: Goods that are not in the thickness of brocade or light sweat are called Istibraq. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, and Sufyan.
