## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "So eat and drink and be content with the eyes. But if you see any of the people, say: I have vowed to the Most Merciful a fast, so I will not speak to any human today": So eat from the fresh dates that fall upon you, and drink from the water of the pure water that your Lord has placed beneath you. Do not fear hunger or thirst. "And delight our eyes" means: "Kinden your soul and rejoice in your birth to Me and do not grieve." And the eye is established because it is the one described by the decree. The meaning of the statement is: 'Let your eye be fixed on your child,' then the verb was changed from 'al-'ayn' to the woman with the 'ayn,' so the 'ayn' was accusative, as the verb originally was for her, according to the interpretation, similar to what he did in his saying: 'If they are pleased with you from something of it,' it is: 'If their souls are good for you.' And His saying: 'And they are squeezed with a strain,' and from it is His saying: 'A ripe fresh fruit will fall upon you,' meaning 'the fresh fruit of the trunk' is merely 'the fresh stump of the trunk,' so the verb was changed to 'al-jad'a' in the reading of those who read it with a ya. In the passage, he quotes sayings from Ibn Abbas and Qatadah.
