## Summary
Verse 173 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 173 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 173 (pp. 404-405): the meaning of the wording.** Al-Tabari explains that the verse, connected with what precedes, means that God does not void the reward of the believers to whom the people said what they said; the relative pronoun stands in the genitive in apposition to "the believers", and is a description of those who answered the call of God and the Messenger. The first "people" are, as he was told, a group whom Abu Sufyan asked to discourage the Prophet and his companions who had set out after him from Uhud to Hamra' al-Asad; the second "people" are Abu Sufyan and the Quraysh with him at Uhud. By "they have massed" he understands that they gathered men to meet them and charge them in war; by the command to fear, that they should beware of them and shun meeting them, since they had no strength against them. Their increase in faith was that the intimidation added certainty to their certainty and belief in God, His promise and His Messenger's promise to their belief; it did not turn them from the course the Prophet had ordered, but they went on until they reached God's pleasure, and, trusting in God and relying on Him, they said the well-known formula of entrusting. He explains its first half as "God suffices us", and its second as "an excellent guardian for whoever takes charge of and sponsors". God described Himself so, he adds, because the "wakil" in Arabic usage is the one to whom the care of an affair is entrusted by the one who entrusts it; since these people had delegated their affair to God and trusted Him, He described Himself as taking it on for them. The note refers to the earlier explanation of "hasb" (the reference is printed as 244 and 4). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 404} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 405}

**The disagreement on the time of the saying (p. 406).** Al-Tabari reports that the interpreters differ over when the statement was made to the Prophet's companions. Some say it was said on their outing from Uhud to Hamra' al-Asad in pursuit of Abu Sufyan and those with him; others say it was said at the expedition of Badr al-Sughra, the year after Uhud, when the Prophet went to meet Abu Sufyan at the appointment he had made. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 406}

**Ibn Ishaq's report on Ma'bad al-Khuza'i (8243, pp. 406-409).** Through Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq from 'Abdallah ibn Abi Bakr ibn Muhammad ibn 'Amr ibn Hazm: Ma'bad al-Khuza'i passed the Prophet at Hamra' al-Asad. Khuza'a, Muslim and polytheist alike, were counsellors of the Prophet in Tihama, with a pact with him, hiding from him nothing that happened there; Ma'bad was then a polytheist. He told the Prophet that what had befallen him among his companions had grieved them and that they would have liked God to have spared him in them. He then left and met Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and those with him at al-Rawha', who had resolved to turn back against the Prophet and his companions, saying they had broken the edge of his companions, leaders and nobles, so how could they go back before wiping them out; they meant to charge the remnant. When Abu Sufyan saw Ma'bad he asked what lay behind him. Ma'bad said Muhammad had set out with his companions in pursuit in a host the like of which he had never seen, burning with rage against them; those who had stayed behind on the day of Uhud had joined him, regretting what they had done, and there was a fury in them he had never seen; he did not think they would leave before seeing the forelocks of the horses. When Abu Sufyan said they had resolved to return to wipe out the remnant, Ma'bad said he forbade them, for what he had seen had moved him to compose verses. As Al-Tabari gives them, the verses describe his mount nearly collapsing at the sounds, the short-coated horses surging in scattered troops with noble lions, his running off and thinking the earth tilted as they rose with a leader not forsaken, his warning to Abu Sufyan against meeting them when the valley seethes with horses, and his warning to the people of the sanctuary, openly and to every sensible man among them, of an army that was no rabble and whose warning was no idle talk (the verses are not reproduced here). This turned Abu Sufyan and those with him back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 406} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 408}

A party of 'Abd al-Qays then passed Abu Sufyan; he asked where they were going and they said to Medina for provisions. He offered that they carry a message to Muhammad from him, in return for which he would load their camels the next day with raisins at 'Ukaz when they arrived there; they agreed. The message was that they had resolved to march on him and his companions to wipe out the remnant. The party passed the Prophet at Hamra' al-Asad and told him, and he and his companions said the formula of entrusting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 409}

The editor's notes (pp. 406-409) explain: "'ayba" is a leather bag for belongings, and "a man's 'ayba" is the place of his secret, a hadith describing the Ansar as the Prophet's own and the place of his secret; "safqa" is the pledge, then the covenant and compact, from clapping hand on hand, since two parties to a covenant put one hand in the other's, a hadith holding that among the greatest of grave sins is fighting the people of one's pact, and an Ibn 'Umar hadith mentioning the clasp of the hand and the fruit of the heart; "'afahu" and "a'fahu" mean granting him well-being, and Ibn Hisham has "'afaka fihim", meaning that He guarded you from what befell your companions; the printed text has "from Hira' al-Asad", the correct form being from the manuscript, Ibn Hisham and the Tarikh; the printed text has a wrong reading of "we struck his companions at Uhud", the correct reading being from the manuscript; "hadd" is the edge of anything, and "we broke their edge" means we blunted it as a sword is notched so that they became weaker than before; "yataharraq" is to blaze with rage; the printed text has a variant "bi-shay'" unlike the manuscript, whose reading agrees with Ibn Hisham and the Tarikh. On the words of the verses (pp. 407-408): "hadda" a building is to shatter and demolish it; "jurd" are short-coated horses, a sign of nobility; "ababil" are scattered groups; he notes that Ma'bad claimed many Muslim horses, whereas the biographies say the Muslims at Uhud were a thousand, including a hundred in mail, with two horsemen, one for the Prophet and one for Abu Burda ibn Niyar; "tanabil" are short men, shortness being a fault because a short fighter cannot reach far with sword or spear, and the editor adopts al-Tabari's version in the tafsir and the Tarikh, which differs from the printed Ibn Hisham; he takes "kharq" to mean dazed and frightened, "mil" the plural of "amil", one who veers from his enemy through fear, and "ma'azil" the plural of "mi'zal", one who holds apart from the battle in fright, hardly fighting or helping one who calls on him; "taghamatat" of a pot is that its boiling grew fierce, and "bat-ha'" is the valley's torrent bed with fine gravel. The editor also quotes al-Suhayli's vocalisation of the word for horses in the Rawd al-Unuf (2: 144), a different reading with a jim in Abu Dharr al-Khushani, in the printed Ibn Hisham and in the Tarikh, and prefers the sense of a body of horses; "basl" is the forbidden, and "people of the basl" means Quraysh since Mecca is sacred; "dahiya" is openly; "irba" is insight into affairs; "wakhsh" is the base and small among people; "qanabil" are bands of men and horses. The note on p. 409 says the publisher dropped "and his companions" in the printed text although it stands in the Tarikh and the manuscript, to match Ibn Hisham's Sira. The report is in Ibn Hisham (3: 108-110), where it completes report 8233, and in the Tarikh (3: 28-29). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 406} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 409}

**Reports 8244-8247 (pp. 409-410).** Ibn Ishaq (8244), through Ibn Humayd from Salama: the "people" who said what they said were the group of 'Abd al-Qays to whom Abu Sufyan spoke, saying that he and those with him were coming back at them; he then notes that in the next verse God says they returned with a blessing and favour from God, untouched by harm; the note gives Ibn Hisham (3: 128) as completing the reports ending at 8235. Al-Suddi (8245), through Muhammad from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat: when Abu Sufyan and his companions regretted turning back and said to go back and wipe them out, God cast terror into their hearts and they were routed; they met a bedouin and gave him a small payment to tell Muhammad and his companions that they had massed against them; God informed His Messenger, who pursued until Hamra' al-Asad; they met the bedouin on the road and he told them, and they said the formula of entrusting and went back; the verse came down about them and the bedouin. Ibn 'Abbas (8246), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather: on his withdrawal from Uhud Abu Sufyan met a caravan bound for Medina with merchandise, bound by pacts with the Prophet; he said he would owe them their satisfaction if they turned Muhammad and his men back, should they find him pursuing, and told him he had gathered large forces. The caravan met the Prophet and told him that Abu Sufyan had massed forces and was coming to Medina, and that he might turn back if he wished; it added only to their certainty, and they said the formula, and the verse came down. The notes: "'ir" is a caravan carrying provisions, "hibal" the plural of "habl", a pact; the printed text omits the particle "fa" in "they said to him", and has a conjunction "wa" in "did not add to them", the manuscript being right in both. Qatada (8247), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: the Prophet and a band of companions set out after Abu Sufyan and his men left Uhud, as far as Dhu al-Hulayfa; bedouins and others came to say that Abu Sufyan was bearing down on them with a force, and they said the formula, and the verse came down about them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 409} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 410}

**The second view: the expedition of Badr al-Sughra (reports 8248-8250, pp. 410-412).** Others said this was said at the expedition of Badr al-Sughra, when the Prophet went the next year to meet Abu Sufyan at the appointment. Mujahid (8248), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: Abu Sufyan told Muhammad that their appointment was Badr, where they had killed his companions; the Prophet said "perhaps", and went to the appointment and camped at Badr, where they found the market and traded; this is the expedition of Badr al-Sughra. Ibn Jurayj (8249), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Mujahid, to like effect, adding that when the Prophet drew up his force for Abu Sufyan's appointment they met polytheists and asked about Quraysh, who said they had massed against them, to trick and frighten them, while the believers said the formula of entrusting, until they reached Badr and found its markets plentiful and uncontested. A man of the polytheists came and told Mecca about Muhammad's cavalry and composed verses on it, describing his she-camel shying from the company of Muhammad (the Emigrants and Ansar) and the 'ajwa dates of Yathrib, and saying he had made the water of Qudayd and then Dajnan his rendezvous (the verses are not reproduced here). Al-Tabari remarks that this is how al-Qasim recited it to him, which is an error, the true form being the one that follows on p. 412. The notes name the poet as Ma'bad ibn Abi Ma'bad al-Khuza'i, as Ibn Hisham (3: 220-221) and the Tarikh (3: 41) relate; the manuscript has "lamma 'abada", which the editor reads as "'abba'a", meaning he arrayed the army; "'afiya" means plentiful, like land whose growth no one has grazed, and refers to report 8252 to come; 'ajwa is a kind of the best dates of Medina, whose palm is the "lina" mentioned in Surat al-Hashr; "Yathrib" is the city of the Prophet; "munjad" is dark raisins; "tahwi" means hastens; "atlad" is the oldest; Qudayd is a watering place between Mecca and Medina, and Dajnan a mountain on the road from Mecca to Medina, a night's journey from Qudayd, from Abu 'Ubayd al-Bakri's Mu'jam ma sta'jam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 410} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 411} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 412}

'Ikrima (8250), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn 'Uyayna from 'Amr: Badr was a place of trade in the Jahiliyya; some Muslims set out for it and some polytheists met them and told them people had massed against them and warned them. The coward turned back, while the brave took equipment for fighting and for trade and said the formula; they went but met no one, and the verse came down about them. Ibn 'Uyayna then adds, from Zakariyya from al-Sha'bi from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr: it is the word of Ibrahim when he was thrown into the fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 412}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 412).** Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two views is that of those who said that what was said to the Prophet and his companions, that the people had massed against them, was said during the Prophet's setting out, with those who went with him, after Abu Sufyan and the Quraysh polytheists with him on their withdrawal from Uhud to Hamra' al-Asad; his argument follows on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 412}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 412 in the commentary on verse 173 and continue from p. 413. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 412}
