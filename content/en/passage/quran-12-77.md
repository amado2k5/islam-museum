**Commentary on verse 77 (pp. 194-201)**

On "they said, if he steals, a brother of his stole before; but Joseph kept it secret in himself and did not disclose it to them; he said, you are worse in position, and God knows best what you describe", al-Tabari says: "if he steals, a brother of his stole before" means his brother by his father and mother, Joseph. Report 19596, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: of Joseph. Reports 19597 to 19599, from Mujahid: Joseph, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 194}

The interpreters differed over the "theft" with which they charged Joseph. Some said it was an idol belonging to his maternal grandfather, which he broke and threw on the road. Report 19600, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ahmad ibn Amr al-Basri, al-Fayd ibn al-Fadl, Mis'ar and Abu Husayn: Joseph stole an idol of his mother's father, broke it and threw it in the road, and his brothers used to reproach him for it. The editor notes that Ahmad ibn Amr al-Basri is a teacher of al-Tabari and that no criticism is recorded of al-Fayd ibn al-Fadl al-Bajali al-Kufi, corrects a word in the printed text, and notes that al-Tabari reports it in his History. Report 19601, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it is said he stole an idol of his mother's father and they reproached him for it. Report 19602, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they meant to find fault with Joseph, and the theft they reproached him with was an idol of his mother's father that he took; God's prophet intended only good by it, but they reproached him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 195}

Report 19603, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: Joseph's mother told Joseph to steal an idol belonging to his maternal uncle which he worshipped, she being a Muslim. Others said: report 19604, from Ibn Idris and his father, through Abu Kurayb: Ya'qub's sons were at a meal when Joseph saw a bone with meat on it and hid it, and they reproached him for it, "if he steals, a brother of his stole before". The editor explains "irq" as a bone with meat on it, corrects the wording of the printed text and the manuscript, and says al-Tabari reports it at length in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 196}

Others said: report 19605, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Abd Allah ibn Abi Najih and Mujahid Abu al-Hajjaj: the first affliction that came to Joseph, as it reached me, was that his paternal aunt, the daughter of Ishaq and the eldest of Ishaq's children, held Ishaq's belt, which they inherited by seniority; whoever took it by stealth from its holder had the holder in his power, to do with as he pleased. When Joseph was born to Ya'qub, he had given him to his aunt to rear, so he was with her, and no one loved anything as she loved him. When he grew and had reached some years, and Ya'qub's heart longed for him, he came to her and said: sister, give Joseph back to me, for by God I cannot bear him to be absent from me for an hour! She said: by God, I will not give him up, I cannot bear him to be absent from me for an hour! He said: by God, I will not leave him! She said: then leave him with me some days, that I may look at him and grow calm about him, perhaps that will console me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 196}

When Ya'qub left her, she took Ishaq's belt and fastened it on Joseph beneath his clothes, then said: I have lost Ishaq's belt, look who took it and who has it! It was searched for. Then she said: search the members of the household! They searched them and found it with Joseph, and she said: by God he is my slave, I do with him as I wish. He said: Ya'qub came to her and she told him the matter, and he said to her: it is between you and him; if he did that, he is yours, I can do nothing else. She kept him and he could not get free of her until she died. That, he said, is what Joseph's brothers meant when he did to his brother what he did in taking him: "if he steals, a brother of his stole before". The editor explains words in the report and the sense of "salm", notes that al-Tabari reports it up to this point in his History, explains how "haddanahu" takes two objects, and that "his soul fell upon him" means he longed for him intensely. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 197}

Ibn Humayd said that Ibn Ishaq said: when Ya'qub's sons saw what Joseph's brothers did, and had no doubt that he had stolen, they said, in sorrow for him over what had come over them and as a reproof to him, "if he steals, a brother of his stole before". When Joseph heard it he said "you are worse in position" secretly within himself, and did not disclose it to them, "and God knows best what you describe". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 197}

On "but Joseph kept it secret in himself and did not disclose it to them", "asarraha" means he concealed it in himself. He says "asarraha" in the feminine because he meant by it the saying, which is "you are worse in position, and God knows best what you describe"; the masculine would also have been allowed, as it was said in Sura Hud "that is from the tidings of the unseen" and "that is from the tidings of the towns". He refers to the saying without any prior mention of it, and the Arabs often do that when the meant sense is understood by the hearers, like a verse of Hatim al-Ta'i in which he addresses a woman, saying that wealth does not help a man when his soul rattles in the throat and the chest grows tight. The editor notes that the verses after it describe his burial in a smooth-sided niche grave, as those he loves cover him with earth and shake off their hands, and refers to his collection and to the earlier discussion of "israr". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 198}

Hatim means: the chest grows tight for the soul, referring to it without any prior mention, since "when it rattles one day" tells the hearer what he means by "grows tight for it". Of the same kind is God's word in Sura al-Nahl, "your Lord after that is forgiving, merciful", where "after that" is said although no feminine noun had been mentioned. The interpreters said the same: report 19606, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: what he concealed in himself was his saying "you are worse in position, and God knows best what you describe". Report 19607, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: this saying. Report 19608, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: he concealed in himself his saying "you are worse in position, and God knows best what you describe". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 199}

On "and God knows best what you describe", he says: God knows best how you lie in what you charge his brother Benjamin with. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "wasf". The reports on this meaning follow on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 199}

Reports 19609 to 19611, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; and al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa, all from Ibn Abi Najih: "you are worse in position, and God knows best what you describe", they say Joseph said it. Report 19612, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "God knows best what you describe" means what you lie about. Al-Tabari says the sense is therefore: Joseph kept it secret in himself and did not disclose it to them, saying: you are worse in God's sight in standing than the one you charged with stealing, and fouler in position because of your past deeds, and God knows your lie, though many of those present were ignorant of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 200}

It is reported that when the cup was found in the baggage of Joseph's brother, the men blamed one another. Report 19613, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: when the stolen thing was brought out of the boy's baggage their backs were broken and they said: O sons of Rachel, we keep suffering affliction from you! When did you take this cup? Benjamin said: rather it is the sons of Rachel who keep suffering affliction from you; you took my brother and destroyed him in the wilderness! This cup was put in my baggage by whoever put the dirhams in your baggage! They said: do not mention the dirhams, or we will be seized for them! When they came in to Joseph he called for the cup and tapped it, then held it to his ear and said: this cup of mine tells me that you were twelve men and that you went off with a brother of yours and sold him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 200}

When Benjamin heard that he rose and prostrated to Joseph, then said: O king, ask this cup of yours about my brother, is he alive? He tapped it, then said: he is alive, and you will see him. He said: then do with me as you wish, for if he learns of me he will rescue me. Joseph went in and wept, then performed ablution and came out. Benjamin said: O king, I want you to strike this cup of yours so that it tells you the truth; ask it who stole it and put it in my baggage. He tapped it and said: this cup of mine is angry and says, how can you ask me who my companion is when you have seen with whom you were? The sons of Ya'qub, when angry, could not be borne, and Rubil grew angry and said: O king, by God, you will leave us or I will cry a cry such that no pregnant woman remains in Egypt without losing what is in her belly! Every hair on Rubil's body stood up and came out of his clothes. Joseph said to his son: go and stand by Rubil and touch him; for when one of Ya'qub's sons was angry and another touched him, his anger went. The boy passed by him and touched him, and his anger left, and Rubil said: who is this? In this land there are offspring of Ya'qub's seed! Joseph said: who is Ya'qub? Rubil grew angry again and said: O king, do not mention Ya'qub, for he is God's chosen, the son of God's offered one, the son of God's friend! Joseph said: then you are he, if you are truthful. The editor notes that al-Tabari relates this report at length in his History, explains "bazr" as offspring, and points to the History's wording "Isra'il Allah" in place of "siriyy Allah" and to differences among copies in some words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 201}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
