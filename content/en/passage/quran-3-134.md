## Summary
Verse 134 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 134 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning: spending in ease and hardship (pp. 213-214).** Abu Ja'far says he means that the Garden as wide as the heavens and the earth is prepared for the God-fearing, who give their wealth in God's way, either by spending it on someone in need or by strengthening a weak man to rise for jihad. The editor records that the printed text read "al-jihad" and that he keeps the manuscript's reading, and explains the word for a weakened man as one whose mount has weakened. Abu Ja'far then says "sarra'" is the state of gladness through abundant wealth and a comfortable life, a verbal noun from "this matter pleased me", and "darra'" a verbal noun from "so-and-so was afflicted", when narrowness and hardship strike his livelihood; the editor refers to the earlier explanation of "darra'" (3: 350-352). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 214}
- From Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, Ibn Abbas (7838): he means hardship and ease. Abu Ja'far adds that God reports that the Paradise described is for whoever feared Him and spent his wealth in His way in times of comfort and plenty and in times of narrowness and strain. The editor notes that the manuscript reads "in the state of contentment", which seems right too. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 214}

**Swallowing anger (pp. 214-215).** Abu Ja'far says the swallowers are those who gulp down their rage when their souls are full of it. One says "so-and-so swallowed his anger" when he gulped it and kept himself from doing what he was able to do, having power over the one who angered him and over the one who wronged him. The root is sealing a waterskin, said when it is filled with water; a man is called "kazim" or "makzum" when filled with grief and sorrow, and he cites [[passage/quran-12-84|12:84]], where Jacob's eyes whitened with sorrow; the water channels are called kazaim because they are full of water, and "I seized his kazamayn" means the passages of his breath. Ghayz is a verbal noun from "so-and-so enraged me", when one provokes and angers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 215}

**Pardon and doing good (p. 215).** Abu Ja'far says the pardoners are those who pass over the punishment of people's wrongs against them, though able to take revenge, and leave it; God's love is for whoever does these things for which Paradise was prepared, and these are the doers of good, their good being their doing of them.
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7839): that is the doing of good, and I love whoever acts by it. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 115). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 215}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7840): a people who spent in hardship and ease, strain and comfort; whoever can overcome evil with good should do so, and there is no power but with God. He then exhorts about the sip of patience one swallows while angry and wronged. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 215}
- From Musa ibn Abd al-Rahman, Muhammad ibn Bishr, Mahriz Abu Raja', al-Hasan (7841): on the Day of Resurrection it is said: let whoever has a reward due from God stand up, and only a person who pardoned stands; then al-Hasan recited the verse on the pardoners. The editor gives the narrators: Musa ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Masruqi, Muhammad ibn Bishr ibn al-Furafisa al-Abdi, and Mahriz ibn Abd Allah al-Jazari, a freedman of Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik, whom Ibn Hibban listed among the trustworthy, adding that he used to conceal his sources when narrating from Makhul. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 215}
- From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Dawud ibn Qays, Zayd ibn Aslam, a Syrian man called Abd al-Jalil, his paternal uncle, Abu Hurayra (7842): the Prophet said: whoever swallows anger while able to carry it out, God fills him with security and faith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 216}
- From Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, Ibn Abbas (7843): the swallowers are like those described in [[passage/quran-42-37|42:37]]: they are angry about a matter which, if they acted on it, would be forbidden, so they forgive and pardon, seeking God's face; and the pardoners are like what is said in [[passage/quran-24-22|24:22]]: do not swear not to give them anything of your spending, but forgive and overlook. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 216}

**The editor's note on report 7842 (p. 216).** Shakir accepts Dawud ibn Qays al-Farra' and Zayd ibn Aslam, says Abd al-Jalil the Syrian is unknown and his uncle more so, and gives al-Bukhari's remark on him, "not corroborated", as found in al-Dhahabi's Mizan, the Lisan and Ibn Abi Hatim; the chain is therefore weak because two narrators are unknown. He notes that Ibn Kathir took it from Abd al-Razzaq, that al-Suyuti attributed it to Abd al-Razzaq, al-Tabari and Ibn al-Mundhir, and that the Jami' al-Saghir (8997) attributes it to Ibn Abi al-Dunya's book on anger alone, at which he expresses surprise. He points to two reports in the same sense in Abu Dawud (4777, 4778) and to Ahmad's Musnad (6114), through Ali ibn Asim, Yunus ibn Ubayd, al-Hasan and Ibn Umar: no servant swallowed a sip better in God's sight than a sip of anger he swallows seeking God's face; he calls this chain sound. He says Ibn Kathir took it from Ibn Mardawayh and ascribed it to Ibn Jarir and Ibn Majah, that he himself did not find it at this place in al-Tabari, and that he does not know whether al-Tabari gave it elsewhere or the copyists dropped it, so he included it in the note as a precaution. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 216}
