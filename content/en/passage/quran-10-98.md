**Commentary on verse 98 (pp. 205-211)**

On [[passage/quran-10-98|10:98]] al-Tabari says: 'why was there not a town that believed' means why did a town not believe, and so it is in Ubayy's reading. The sense is: no town believed when it saw the punishment and God's anger came down on it because of its disobedience to its Lord and its deserving His punishment, so that its belief then benefited it, as Pharaoh's belief did not benefit him when drowning overtook him after he had persisted in his error, except the people of Yunus, whose belief did benefit them after the punishment came down and anger settled on them. God excepted the people of Yunus from the people of towns whose belief did not benefit them after the punishment came down in their courtyard and took them out of them, and told His creation that belief benefited them specially among all the other nations. The editor refers to the earlier uses of 'lawla' in the sense of 'halla'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 206}

If someone says: if it is as you describe and 'why was there not a town that believed and its belief benefited it' means 'no town believed', in the sense of denial, why is 'people' in the accusative, when you know that what comes before an exception, if it is a denial, is followed by the nominative, and the sound Arab usage is 'no one stood except your brother'? The reply: that is where what follows the exception is of the same kind as what precedes it, since the brother is of the kind of 'anyone', but if the kinds differ so that what follows the exception is not of the kind of what precedes it, the fluent speech of the Arabs uses the accusative, as when you say 'no one is left in the house except the peg', and 'we have no one except a dog or a donkey'. He cites a line of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani about the abandoned camp site, that it gave no answer and no one was in the site, and then excepts the tethering places, which he put in the accusative as an exception from another kind. So 'people of Yunus' is accusative, because they are a nation other than the nations from whom they are excepted, and of a different kind. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 206}

Al-Tabari says the people of Yunus are a nation other than the nations from which they are excepted in form, though they are of the children of Adam; this is the exception some of the people of Arabic call the disconnected exception, and had the people of Yunus been part of the nation from which they were excepted the speech would be in the nominative. The editor refers to al-Farra'. Report 17897, from Ibn Abbas: no town believed and had its belief benefit it when God's might came down on it, except the town of Yunus; Ibn Jurayj said from Mujahid: as the belief of the people of Yunus benefited them. Report 17898, from Qatada: this was not so in earlier nations; belief did not benefit a town that disbelieved and then believed when the punishment came, so that it was let be, except the people of Yunus. When they missed their prophet and thought punishment had drawn near, God cast repentance into their hearts; they put on sackcloth and separated every animal from its young, and cried out to God for forty nights, and when God saw the sincerity in their hearts and the repentance and regret for what had been, He lifted the punishment from them after it had hung over them; it was told us that the people of Yunus were at Nineveh in the land of Mosul. The editor says the word in the printed text and manuscript is unclear and he put 'separated' in brackets according to the reports that follow. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 207}

Report 17899, from Qatada: it has reached us that they went out and camped on a hill and separated every animal from its young, calling on God for forty nights until He turned to them; report 17900, from Sa'id b. Jubayr: the punishment covered the people of Yunus as the cloth covers a grave, which the editor explains as the grave being covered with the cloth when its occupant is put into it; report 17901, from Ibn Abbas: the punishment had come down on the people of Yunus until there was only two-thirds of a mile between them and it, and when they prayed God lifted it from them; report 17902, from Mujahid: as the belief of the people of Yunus benefited them, and in Abu Hudhayfa's version: no town believed when it saw the punishment and was benefited by its belief, except the people of Yunus, whom We let enjoy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 208}

Report 17903, from al-Rabi' b. Anas: a man who had kept the Qur'an in his heart in the governorship of Umar b. al-Khattab told us of the people of Yunus when he warned his people and they denied him, and he told them punishment would strike them and left them; when they saw it and it covered them they went out from their dwellings and climbed to a high place and cried out to their Lord, calling on Him sincerely in religion to lift the punishment from them and to return their messenger to them. He said: at that the verse was sent down, no town was covered by the punishment and then spared it except the people of Yunus in particular. When Yunus saw that he went away reproaching his Lord, and left in anger, thinking We would not straiten him, until he boarded a ship and a storm struck its people; and he told the story of Yunus. The editor says a word in the manuscript is unpointed and he put his reading of it in brackets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 208}

Report 17904, from Ibn Abi Najih: when they saw the punishment coming down they separated every female and her young, of people and cattle, then stood together and called on God, making their belief sincere, and saw the punishment lifted from them; Yunus said when it was lifted from them: shall I go back to them when I have called them liars? He had promised them the punishment by the morning of the third day, and then he left in anger and thought ill. Report 17905, from Sa'id b. Jubayr: when Yunus was sent to his people to call them to Islam they refused; it was said to him tell them that punishment will reach them in the morning; they said we have not tried him in a lie, so watch: if he spends the night with you it is nothing, and if he does not, know that punishment is coming at dawn. In the middle of the night he took a lump of dried cheese with ghee as provision and left; when they woke the punishment covered them as one is covered by the cloth in the grave, and they separated person from child and animal from young and cried out to God: we believe in what Yunus brought and affirm it; God lifted the punishment. Yunus went out to watch for it and saw nothing and said: they have proved me a liar; and he went off angry at his Lord until he reached the sea. The editor says 'ilatha' is dried cheese mixed with ghee. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 209}

Report 17906, from Amr b. Maymun, who said Ibn Mas'ud told us in the treasury: Yunus had promised his people the punishment and told them it would come within three days, so they separated every mother from her child, then went out and cried out to God and sought His forgiveness; God held the punishment back from them, and Yunus went out at dawn to see it and saw nothing; and whoever lied and had no proof was killed, so he went off in anger. Report 17907, from Abu al-Jald Jaylan: when the punishment covered the people of Yunus they went to an old man of the remaining scholars among them and said, the punishment has come down on us, what do you think? He said: say, O Living when there is none living, O Giver of life to the dead, O Living, there is no god but You; and the punishment was lifted from them and they were given enjoyment for a time. Report 17908, from Ma'mar: I was told that in Ibn Mas'ud's reading 'falawla' is 'fahalla'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 210}

His words 'when they believed, We lifted from them the punishment of disgrace in the life of this world' mean that when they affirmed their messenger and acknowledged what he brought them, after the punishment had shaded them and God's command had covered them and the trial come down on them, We lifted from them the punishment of humiliation and lowliness in their worldly lives, 'and gave them enjoyment for a time' meaning We deferred their terms and did not hasten the punishment on them, and left them in the world to enjoy their terms until the time of their death and the end of their lives which I had decreed. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'disgrace' and 'enjoyment'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 211}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
