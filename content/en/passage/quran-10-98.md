**Commentary on verse 98 (pp. 205-206)**

On [[passage/quran-10-98|10:98]] al-Tabari says: 'why was there not a town that believed' means why did a town not believe, and so it is in Ubayy's reading. The sense is: no town believed when it saw the punishment and God's anger came down on it because of its disobedience to its Lord and its deserving His punishment, so that its belief then benefited it, as Pharaoh's belief did not benefit him when drowning overtook him after he had persisted in his error, except the people of Yunus, whose belief did benefit them after the punishment came down and anger settled on them. God excepted the people of Yunus from the people of towns whose belief did not benefit them after the punishment came down in their courtyard and took them out of them, and told His creation that belief benefited them specially among all the other nations. The editor refers to the earlier uses of 'lawla' in the sense of 'halla'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 206}

If someone says: if it is as you describe and 'why was there not a town that believed and its belief benefited it' means 'no town believed', in the sense of denial, why is 'people' in the accusative, when you know that what comes before an exception, if it is a denial, is followed by the nominative, and the sound Arab usage is 'no one stood except your brother'? The reply: that is where what follows the exception is of the same kind as what precedes it, since the brother is of the kind of 'anyone', but if the kinds differ so that what follows the exception is not of the kind of what precedes it, the fluent speech of the Arabs uses the accusative, as when you say 'no one is left in the house except the peg', and 'we have no one except a dog or a donkey'. He cites a line of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani about the abandoned camp site, that it gave no answer and no one was in the site, and then excepts the tethering places, which he put in the accusative as an exception from another kind. So 'people of Yunus' is accusative, because they are a nation other than the nations from whom they are excepted, and of a different kind. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 206}
