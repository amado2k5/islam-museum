## Summary
Verse 187 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 187 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "it has been made lawful for you on the night of the fast to go in to your wives", al-Tabari says "uhilla" means released and permitted, "the night of the fast" means in the night of the fast, and "rafath" is a euphemism for sexual intercourse here; one says "rafath" and "rufuth", and it is reported that in the reading of 'Abd Allah it is "al-rufuth". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 487} The commentators said the same: from Ibn 'Abbas, "rafath is intercourse, but God is generous and speaks by allusion", and from him also that it is marriage relations; from Qatada, approaching women; and from Mujahid, Salim ibn 'Abd Allah and al-Suddi, intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 487} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 488} He notes that elsewhere "rafath" is foul speech, citing a line of al-'Ajjaj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 488}

On "they are a garment for you and you are a garment for them", al-Tabari says the sense is that your wives are a garment for you and you are a garment for them, and he poses the question how that can be, a garment being what one wears; he answers that there are two senses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 489} First, each was made a garment for the other because of their being bare together when sleeping, joined in one covering, each one's body pressed to the other's like what he wears on his body; he cites a line of al-Nabigha al-Ja'di and lines by Layla and Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali, and from al-Rabi', "they are a cover for you and you are a cover for them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 490} The other sense is that each is a garment for the other because he is a repose for him, as the night is called a garment, and a man's wife is his repose in whom he finds rest; Mujahid and others said this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 490} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 491} He also allows that the sense may be that each of you is a covering for the other, in what takes place between you of intercourse, from the eyes of other people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 491} It is reported from Mujahid, Qatada, al-Suddi and Ibn 'Abbas, "they are a repose for you and you are a repose for them", and from Ibn Zayd, "the union". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 491} The editor explains the lines of poetry and the rare words in footnotes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 488} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 490}

On "God knew that you were betraying yourselves, so He turned to you and pardoned you; so now lie with them and seek what God has written for you", al-Tabari asks what this betrayal was, and answers that their betrayal of themselves which God mentioned was in two things: intercourse with women, and eating and drinking during the time it was forbidden them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 492} He relates from Ibn Abi Layla that when a man broke his fast and slept he would not go to his wife, and when he slept he did not eat, until 'Umar ibn al-Khattab came wanting his wife and she said "you have slept", and thinking she was making an excuse he lay with her; and a man of the Ansar came and wanted to eat, and then this verse came down, "it has been made lawful for you on the night of the fast to go in to your wives". The copies of the text have a gap in this report (marked by dots), which the editor notes can be completed from the report of Waki' and 'Abd ibn Humayd given by al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-manthur, and he supplies what he judges to be its completion; what follows the gap in al-Tabari's text leads to the coming down of the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 492} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 493}

More reports on the occasion of the verse. From 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla: they used to fast three days of each month, and when Ramadan came, if a man did not eat at his breaking of the fast before sleeping he did not eat until the same time the next day, and if he or his wife slept he could not go in to her until the same time; an old man of the Ansar called Sirma ibn Malik came and said to his family "feed me", and she said "until I make you something hot", and his eyes overcame him and he slept; then 'Umar came, his wife said "I have slept", he did not accept her excuse, thinking she was making one, and lay with her; both spent the night tossing, and God sent down "eat and drink until the white thread becomes clear to you from the black thread of dawn", and said "so now lie with them", and God pardoned that and it became a practice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 494} From Mu'adh ibn Jabal, much the same, with the Ansari called Abu Sirma working on his land, sleeping at the time of breaking his fast and waking fasting exhausted; when the Prophet saw him he asked, was told, and a man had betrayed himself in the matter of women, and God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 494} The editor notes in footnotes that the first report is mursal, that Mu'adh's report is broken because Ibn Abi Layla did not meet Mu'adh, that the name differs among the reports between Sirma ibn Malik and Abu Sirma, and traces the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 494} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 495}

From al-Bara' ibn 'Azib: the companions of Muhammad, if a man was fasting and slept before breaking his fast, did not eat until the same time; Qays ibn Sirma al-Ansari was fasting and working on his land, and when the time of breaking the fast came he went to his wife and said "have you any food?", she said "no, but I will go and look for some", his eyes overcame him and he slept, and before noon he fainted; this was mentioned to the Prophet and the verse came down, and they rejoiced greatly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 495} The editor quotes Ibn Hajar's preferred identification of this Ansari and says the differing names come from narrators' errors. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 495} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 496} From Ibn 'Abbas through 'Ali ibn Abi Talha: in Ramadan, when the Muslims prayed the evening prayer, women and food were forbidden them until the same time next day; some of them, among them 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, then had food and women after the evening prayer and complained of it to the Prophet, and God sent down "God knew that you were betraying yourselves". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 496} From Ka'b ibn Malik: in Ramadan if a man slept after evening, food, drink and women were forbidden him until the next day; 'Umar returned one night from the Prophet, with whom he had been talking late, found his wife asleep, wanted her, she said "I have slept", he said "you have not slept", and lay with her; Ka'b ibn Malik did the same; 'Umar went to the Prophet in the morning and told him, and God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 496} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 497} From Thabit, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab lay with his wife one night in Ramadan and it weighed heavily on him, and God sent down "it has been made lawful for you on the night of the fast to go in to your wives"; the editor notes the chain is broken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 497}

From Ibn 'Abbas through 'Atiyya al-'Awfi: when people first became Muslims, a man fasting would fast his day and when evening came he would eat between then and the late-evening prayer, and once it was prayed, food was forbidden him until the evening of the next night; 'Umar ibn al-Khattab went to his wife for some need, and when he had washed he began weeping and blaming himself, then came to the Prophet and said, "I excuse myself to God and to you from this erring self of mine, which made it seem fair to me, so I lay with my wife; do you find a concession for me?", and he said "you were not fit to do that, 'Umar"; when he reached home he was sent for and told of his excuse in a verse of the Qur'an, which God commanded His Messenger to place in the middle hundred of Surat al-Baqara. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 498} The editor notes that al-Tabari's usual chain of weak narrators lies behind this report, and records a report from Abu Hurayra which al-Suyuti and Ibn Kathir attribute to al-Tabari but which he did not find in the printed al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 498}

From Mujahid: a man among the companions of Muhammad would fast by day, and when evening came he ate, drank and had intercourse with women, but once he slept all that was forbidden him until the same time next day; some of them betrayed themselves in this, and God pardoned them and made it lawful for them after sleeping and before it, throughout the night; in another report from him, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab was among those who betrayed himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 499} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 500} From 'Ikrima, an Ansari came one night fasting and his wife said "do not sleep until we make you food", and he slept; she came and said "by God, you slept", and he did not eat that night and woke fasting and fainted, and the concession was sent down for him; the editor notes it is mursal and discusses the standing of one narrator. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 500} From Qatada, at the beginning of the fast they were commanded three days of each month and two prayer units in the morning and two in the evening; food, drink and approaching women were lawful so long as they had not slept, and once they slept it was forbidden until the same time next day; their betrayal of themselves was that they took these after sleeping, and God then made them lawful until the dawn; in another report from him, some Muslims did this and God gave them a concession. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 501} The editor points out the oddity of the first report's wording and gives its counterpart in al-Durr al-manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 501}

From al-Suddi: Ramadan was prescribed for the Christians, and it was prescribed for them not to eat or drink after sleep, nor to approach women in the month of Ramadan; it was prescribed for the believers as it was for them, and the Muslims continued so until a man of the Ansar called Abu Qays ibn Sirma, who worked for pay in the date-gardens of Medina, brought dates to his family and said to his wife "exchange these for flour and make me a soft dish", she went and was slow in returning, he slept, she woke him, and he disliked disobeying God and His Messenger and refused to eat and woke fasting; the Prophet saw him in the evening and said "what is the matter, Abu Qays, you have become worn out", and he told him the story. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 502} 'Umar ibn al-Khattab had lain with a slave-girl of his, with some other believers who could not control themselves; when he heard Abu Qays's account he feared something would be revealed about Abu Qays and that he would be reminded, so he excused himself to the Prophet, who said "you were not fit for that, son of al-Khattab", and that was abrogated for them and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 502} From 'Ata': in Ramadan they would not touch women or eat or drink after they slept until the next night, but if they touched them before sleeping they saw no harm; a man of the Ansar was with his wife after he had slept and said "I have betrayed myself", and the Qur'an came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 502} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 503} From 'Ikrima, the same as Mujahid, adding that 'Umar told his wife "do not sleep until I return from the Prophet", she slept before he came back, he said to her "you are not asleep", and he lay with her until he came to the Prophet and told him; and that "eat and drink" came down about Abu Qays ibn Sirma of the Banu Khazraj, who ate after sleeping. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 503} From Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Hibban, Sirma ibn Anas came to his family one night, an old man fasting, and they had not prepared food; he laid his head down and dozed, his wife brought his food and he said "I have slept", she said "you did not sleep"; he woke hungry and exhausted, and God sent down "eat and drink". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 503}

On "so now lie with them", al-Tabari says that in Arabic "mubashara" is the meeting of skin with skin, a man's "bashara" being his outer skin, and God used it as a euphemism for intercourse; the sense is "now that I have made lawful for you the going in to your wives, lie with them in the nights of the month of Ramadan until the dawn rises". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 504} A number of commentators said the same: from Ibn 'Abbas, "mubashara is intercourse, but God is generous and speaks by allusion", and from him also "marry them" and marital relations; from 'Ata', intercourse, and that every mention of mubashara in the Qur'an is intercourse itself; from Ibn 'Abbas through Sa'id ibn Jubayr, "God alludes as He wishes by what He wishes"; from al-Suddi, "have intercourse with them"; and from Mujahid, mubashara in the Book of God is intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 504} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 505}

They differed over "and seek what God has written for you". Some said it is the child, from Mujahid, al-Hakam, 'Ikrima, al-Hasan, al-Suddi, Ibn 'Abbas, al-Rabi' and al-Dahhak; from Mujahid, "the child; if this wife does not bear, then that one". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 506} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 507} From Ibn Zayd, it is intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 507} Some said it means the Night of Qadr, from Ibn 'Abbas through Abu al-Jawza', Abu Hisham adding "so Mu'adh read it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 507} The editor in footnotes gives the circumstances of some narrators and corrects names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 505} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 506} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 507}

More on "and seek what God has written for you": from Ibn 'Abbas in another report through Abu al-Jawza', it is the Night of Qadr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 507} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 508} Others said it means what God made lawful and allowed for you: from Qatada, "what God made lawful for you", and also "seek the concession that I have written for you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 508} Some read it "and follow what God has written for you"; 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah asked Ibn 'Abbas how he read this verse, "seek" or "follow", and he said "whichever you wish", and then "keep to the first reading". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 508}

Al-Tabari chooses to say that "seek" means ask for, and "what God has written for you" means what God decreed for you, that is, seek what I have written for you in the Preserved Tablet that it will be allowed and released to you; seeking a child, if a man seeks it by lying with the woman, is among what God wrote for him in the Preserved Tablet, and so is seeking the Night of Qadr, and so is seeking what God has made lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 508} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 509} He says all the senses of good that are sought may enter the verse, but that the sense closest to the apparent wording is that of those who said "the child", because it follows "so now lie with them", meaning have intercourse; so the sense "seek what God has written in your lying with them of children and progeny" is closer to the verse than the other interpretations, which have no proof of their correctness from the apparent text of the revelation nor from a report of the Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 509}

On "eat and drink until the white thread becomes clear to you from the black thread of the dawn, then complete the fast until night", al-Tabari relates the commentators' differing views. Some said the white thread is the light of day and the black thread the darkness of night, so the sense is: eat at night in the month of your fast, drink and lie with your wives, from the start of the night until the light of day appears for you with the rising of dawn. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 509} From al-Hasan and al-Suddi, "the night from the day"; from Qatada, that they are two clear marks and limits, so do not let the call of a showy or witless muezzin keep you from your haste, for they call the prayer at a long stretch of the night, and a whiteness is sometimes seen near the pre-dawn which the Arabs call the false dawn, which should not stop you, because the true dawn is not hidden, a transverse streak on the horizon; eat and drink until the dawn is clear to you, and when you see it, stop. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 510} From Ibn 'Abbas, it means the night from the day, so intercourse, eating and drinking are lawful for you until the dawn is clear, and when it is clear they are forbidden to them until they complete the fast to night. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 510} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 511} Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash, asked about the white thread, said "this is the going of the night and the coming of the day", from al-Sha'bi on 'Adi ibn Hatim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 511} In a footnote the editor gives 'Adi ibn Hatim's report from the Musnad and the Sahih collections, in which he put a white cord and a black cord under his pillow and could not tell them apart, and the Prophet said to him "your pillow would then be wide; it is only the whiteness of the day from the blackness of the night". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 511}
