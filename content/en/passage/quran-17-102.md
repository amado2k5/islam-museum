## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "He said, 'I know that none of these have been revealed except by the Lord of the heavens and the earth with clear vision, and indeed I think you, Pharaoh, are reproachful.'" Reciters differed in the reading of the phrase "Indeed, I have known," and the majority of the reciters of the cities read it as "Indeed, I know" with a fatha on the 't', as addressed by Moses to Pharaoh. It was narrated from Ali ibn Abi Talib, may Allah be pleased with him, that he recited: 'Indeed, I have learned' with a damma on the 'ta', as a predicate from Moses himself. Whoever reads it according to this reading should follow his school of interpretation of the phrase 'I think you, O Moses, are bewitched,' meaning I think you have been bewitched, so you see that you are speaking correctly and not correctly. This is one aspect of interpretation. However, the reading followed by the reciters of the regions is different, and it is not permissible for us to contradict the evidence in what it brought of the reading, which is unanimous. In the excerpt, he quotes from Ibn Abbas and Sa'id ibn Jubayr.
