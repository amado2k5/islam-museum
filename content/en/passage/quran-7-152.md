**Commentary on verse 152 (pp. 133-136)**

Al-Tabari says: those who took the calf as a god will be reached by wrath from their Lord, God hastening it for them, and "dhilla", which is abasement, as God's punishment for their disbelief in their Lord, "in the life of this world", in the quick present before the later Hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 133}

Ibn Jurayj said in report 15147, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: this is for those of the calf-takers who died before Moses returned, and those of them who fled when Moses ordered them to kill each other. Al-Tabari says that although this has a ground, the plain sense of God's Book and the interpretation of most interpreters differ, for God made the report general for whoever took the calf, and reports from the Companions and Followers agree that God accepted the repentance of the calf-worshippers when Moses came back, through what He relates of Moses' words at [[passage/quran-2-54|2:54]], repenting to their Maker and killing themselves, which they did as their prophet ordered. God's command that they kill one another thus came from His anger at their worship of the calf; their killing each other was an abasement and humiliation by which God humbled them in the life of this world, and a repentance of theirs which He accepted. No one may take a report the Book gave in general terms as particular to some of those the plain sense covers, without proof from a report or reason, and no report is known that requires moving the plain sense to a specific inner meaning, nor does reason show it. The editor refers to the explanation of "abasement". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 134}

By "thus We repay the inventors of lies" he means: as I repaid those who took the calf as a god by bringing wrath on them and abasement in the life of this world for their disbelief in their Lord and their apostasy from their religion after believing in God, so We repay everyone who invents a lie against God, belies Him, affirms the divinity of another and worships something else, any of the idols, after affirming God's oneness and believing in Him, His prophets and messengers, if he does not repent of his disbelief before he is killed. A number of interpreters said the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 135}

Reports 15148 and 15149, from Abu Qilaba, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar and Ayyub, and through al-Muthanna, Abu al-Nu'man Arim, Hammad ibn Zayd and Ayyub: Abu Qilaba recited this verse and said it is for every inventor of lies until the Day of Resurrection, that God abases him. Report 15150, from Hammad ibn Salama, through Thabit and Humayd: Qays ibn Ubad and Jariya ibn Qudama came to Ali ibn Abi Talib and asked whether the Messenger of God had made a covenant with him about this matter he was in and called to, or whether it was an opinion he formed. He answered that the Messenger of God had made no covenant with him except a document in his sword's scabbard, and he drew it out, and in it was that every prophet has a sanctuary, and that he had made Medina sacred as Abraham made Mecca sacred, no weapon to be carried in it to fight, and that whoever innovates an innovation or shelters an innovator is under the curse of God, the angels and all people. When they left, one said to the other, do you not see this document? So they went back and left it, and said, we heard God say what is in this verse, and the people have forged a lie, and we think abasement will come upon them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 135}

In a long note the editor explains that Hammad is Ibn Salama, Thabit is al-Bunani, and Humayd is Humayd al-Tawil, Hammad's maternal uncle; that Qays ibn Ubad al-Qayni al-Dubai'i is reliable with few reports, came to Medina in Umar's caliphate and was killed by al-Hajjaj among those who went out with Ibn al-Ash'ath, and that Jariya ibn Qudama was a Companion of the Tamim, one of their nobles. He says he did not find the report with this chain and wording, that the document report from Qays ibn Ubad is in Ahmad's Musnad by various routes and in shorter and longer forms, none of which mentions Jariya ibn Qudama, but that al-Tabari's chain is sound, so it seems they were two different events. This is given as the report transmits it, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 136}

Report 15151, from Ibn Uyayna, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, on "thus We repay the inventors of lies": every follower of an innovation is abased. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 136}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
