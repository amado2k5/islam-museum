## Summary
Verse 65 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 65 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verses 65 and 66 (pp. 50-55)**

Al-Tabari says: God says to His Prophet Muhammad, "Prophet, urge the believers to fight", meaning urge those who follow you and believe you in what you urged them to of truth to fight those of the idolaters who turned away from the truth; "if there be twenty of you" men "steadfast" when meeting the enemy, offering themselves to God and standing firm against their enemy, "they will overcome two hundred" of their enemy and subdue them; "and if there be a hundred of you" then "they will overcome a thousand"; "because they are a people who do not understand" means because the idolaters fight with no hope of reward, no seeking of recompense and no offering to God, since they do not understand that God makes binding, for one who fights hoping for the reward and for God's promise, what He promised those who strive in His way, so they do not stand firm if the other side is truthful in meeting them, for fear of being killed and losing their worldly life. Then God lightened it for the believers since He knew their weakness: "now God has lightened it for you, knowing there is weakness in you", meaning weakness in the individual among them facing ten of their enemy; "if there be a hundred steadfast of you" at their meeting, standing firm against them, "they will overcome two hundred" of them, "and if there be a thousand of you they will overcome two thousand" of them "by God's leave", meaning by God's leaving them free to overcome them and helping them against their enemy and God's enemy, as a seeking of reward in their patience and of abundant recompense, "and God is with the steadfast" with help and victory. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "urging", "understanding" and "leave". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 51}

Report 16269, from Ata, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Muhammad ibn Mahbub, Sufyan and Layth: one was set against ten, then one was set against two, and he should not flee from two. Report 16270, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Jurayj and Amr ibn Dinar: for the Muslims one man was set against ten of the unbelievers, then it was lightened and one man was set against two; Ibn Abbas said, I would not like people to know how it was lightened for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 52}

Report 16271, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Abi Najih and Ata ibn Abi Rabah: when this verse came down it was heavy for the Muslims, who thought it great that twenty should fight two hundred and a hundred a thousand, and God abrogated it by the other verse "now God has lightened it"; when they were facing half their enemy they should not flee, and when fewer than that they were not bound to fight and could withdraw. The editor notes it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and that al-Tabari changed the order in which he transmitted it from Ibn Ishaq's commentary. Report 16272, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: each Muslim had ten, and should not flee from them, until God sent down "now God has lightened it", and He set two of the idolaters for each Muslim, abrogating the first command; and he said another time that God commanded the believer to fight ten of the unbelievers, which was hard for the believers, and God had mercy on them and commanded a man to fight two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 52}

Report 16273, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father and his father: ten of the enemy were set for each Muslim as an incitement, to make them resolve to campaign and know God would help them against the enemy; it was not a command that God laid on them or made obligatory but an exhortation and charge that God commanded His Prophet to give; then He lightened it for them, setting two for each man, so the believers would know God is merciful to them, and they relied on God, were patient and were truthful; had it been obligatory, every Muslim who held back from the unbelievers he met when they were more numerous would have been guilty. The narrator then answers those he heard say that it is not right for a Muslim to fight until there are two for each man and four for each two, citing the verse on the one who sells himself seeking God's pleasure ([[passage/quran-2-207|2:207]]) and the verse on being charged only with oneself and urging the believers ([[passage/quran-4-84|4:84]]); that is the urging sent down in al-Anfal, so do not hold back, fight, you have fallen among people as God willed them to be. The editor explains the word for incitement and corrects the printed text and manuscript in this passage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 54}

Report 16274, from Ikrima and al-Hasan, through Ibn Humayd, Yahya ibn Wadih and al-Husayn: He said in al-Anfal "if there be twenty steadfast of you", then abrogated it saying "now God has lightened it". Report 16275, from Ikrima, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mughira: one Muslim and ten idolaters, then it was lightened and it was set that a man should not flee from two. Report 16276, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: this was for the companions of Muhammad at Badr, when a man of them was set to fight ten of the unbelievers, and they cried out at it, so a man was set to fight two, as a lightening from God. Report 16277, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Ibrahim ibn Yazid, Amr ibn Dinar and Abu Ma'bad: the man was commanded to hold out against ten, and ten against a hundred, while the Muslims were few; when they grew many God lightened it and a man was commanded to hold out against two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 54}

Report 16278, from Ibn Abi Najih, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it was laid on them that when twenty met two hundred they should not flee, for if they did not flee they would overcome; then God lightened it. Report 16279, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: God set two for each man after ten had been set for each, and this is a report from Ibn Abbas. Report 16280, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki, Yazid ibn Harun, Jarir ibn Hazim, al-Zubayr ibn al-Khirrit and Ikrima: it was laid on the believers that each fight ten of the idolaters, which was hard for them, so God sent down the lightening and set one against two, and their victory was reduced by that amount. Report 16281, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: "they will overcome two hundred" means they fight two hundred, but they were weaker than that, so God abrogated it for them and lightened it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 55}

Report 16282, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar and Ibn Abi Najih: it was laid on them that when twenty met two hundred they should not flee, for if they did not flee they would overcome; then God lightened it for them. Report 16283, from al-Dahhak, through Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri and Juwaybir: it was obligatory that one should not flee from ten; report 16284, from Ata, the same, through al-Thawri and al-Layth. On "because they are a people who do not understand" he refers to what he already explained, and report 16285, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: they do not fight with intention or for a right in it, or with knowledge of good or evil. The editor notes it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and that al-Tabari moved it from its place and put report 16271 before it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 56}

Al-Tabari then says that this verse, "if there be twenty steadfast of you they will overcome two hundred", though it takes the form of a report, means a command, as shown by "now God has lightened it for you": lightening only comes after burdening. Had the standing of one against ten not been obligatory on them before the lightening, but only recommended, the lightening would have no point, because lightening is a permission to leave off one man's standing against ten of the enemy; if no burden preceded, the permission has no ground. So the ruling of "now God has lightened it for you, knowing there is weakness in you" abrogates the ruling of "if there be twenty steadfast of you". He has shown in his Kitab al-Bayan an Usul al-Ahkam that every report of God promising reward and recompense for a deed, or punishment for leaving it, though not in the form of a command, carries the sense of a command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 57}

The readers differed on "and knowing there is weakness in you": some of the Medinans and some of the Basrans read the weak-noun with a damma on the first letter everywhere in the Qur'an, as the verbal noun of "he was weak"; most of the Kufans read it with a fatha, also as the verbal noun; and some Medinans read "weak ones", as a plural of "weak" like "partners" from "partner". Al-Tabari holds the best readings are the fatha and the damma, because they are the two known readings and two eloquent dialect forms of one meaning, so whichever one reads is right; "weak ones" is anomalous among the readers, and though it has a sound way of being explained, he does not like anyone to read it. The editor corrects the title of his book between the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 58}
