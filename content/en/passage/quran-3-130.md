## Summary
Verse 130 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 130 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 204).** Abu Ja'far says: you who believe in God and His Messenger, do not take usury in your Islam, now that He has guided you to it, as you took it in your pre-Islamic days. That practice then was that a man was owed money by another for a term; when the term fell due he asked for it, and the debtor said: put off my debt and I will add to your money, and they did so; that is the doubling that is forbidden, and God forbade it to them in their Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 204}
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Mu'ammal, Sufyan, Ibn Jurayj, Ata' (7823): Thaqif used to lend to Banu al-Mughira in the pre-Islamic period, and when the term came they said: we will add to it if you delay; then the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 204}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7824): do not take in Islam, now that God has guided you to it, what you used to take when you were on something else, which is not lawful for you in your religion. The editor notes that Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 115) has "guided you by it" and that this is among the remaining reports ending with 7822. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 204}
- From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7825): the usury of the pre-Islamic period. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 204}
- From Yunus, Ibn Wahb, heard Ibn Zayd say, my father used to say (7826): usury in the pre-Islamic period lay in doubling and in age. A man would be owed an excess of debt and come to the debtor when the term fell due and say: will you pay me or add to it? If he had something he paid; if not, he moved it to the next age of animal: if it was a she-camel in her second year it became one in her third, then a fourth-year one, then a fifth-year one, then a sixth-year one, and so on upwards; and in cash he came to him, and if he had nothing he doubled it the next year, and again if he had nothing, so a hundred became two hundred the next year, and if he still had nothing it became four hundred, doubled each year or paid. He said: this is what they were forbidden. In his notes the editor explains that "age" here means the ages of camels, giving the terms for pregnant dams and for a camel in her second year, her third (milk-bearing mother), fourth, fifth, with a sixth-year one left out of the list and the seventh-year one named; that the word for cash means money, gold, silver and the like; and that the manuscript reads slightly differently in the debtor's question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 205}

**Godwariness and success (p. 205).** Abu Ja'far says the command means: be godwary toward God in the matter of usury and do not take it, and in other things He commanded or forbade, and obey Him in it; by success he means that they prosper and escape His punishment and reach the reward and eternity in His gardens He made them desire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 205}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7827): obey God so that you may be saved from the punishment He warned you of and reach the reward He made you desire. The editor notes that Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 115) reads in the present tense and that the report follows those ending with 7824. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 205}
