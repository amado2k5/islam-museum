## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 74 to 75 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Indeed, whoever comes to his Lord as a criminal, for him is Hell, where he will neither die nor live": Allah Almighty says, informing Pharaoh about the words of the magicians to Pharaoh: "Indeed, he is the one who comes to his Lord" from His creation as a criminal, meaning he has acquired disbelief in him. "For he has Hell," meaning: for he has Hell a refuge and dwelling, a recompense for his disbelief "in which he will not die," so his soul "will not live," so his soul will settle in its place and be reassured. But it depends on the throats among them: "And whoever comes to him as a believer" is monotheistic and does not associate partners with Him, "He has done righteous deeds," meaning: He has done what his Lord commanded him and has concluded what He forbidd: 'For those are the highest ranks,' meaning: those are the ones who have the highest ranks of Paradise.
