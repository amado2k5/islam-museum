## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 11 to 17 as a single section. He explains the phrase “noble and righteous”: “The righteous are the plural of ‘righteous,’ just as ‘disbelievers’ is the plural of ‘disbeliever,’ and ‘sorcerers’ is the plural of ‘sorcerer.’ However, it is known in the Arabic language that when they pronounce a singular noun, they say ‘a righteous man’ and ‘a righteous woman,’ and when they pluralize it, they revert it to the plural of the active participle, as they say ‘a noble man,’ and then they say in its plural ‘noble people,’ whereas the standard form for its singular would be ‘noble.’ It has been reported, based on what was heard from some Arabs, that they said ‘a good and righteous people,’ and the singular of ‘good’ is ‘good,’ and ‘righteous’ is ‘righteous.’ He then quotes in the section statements from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd, Al-A’mash, and Sufyan.”
