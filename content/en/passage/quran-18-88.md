## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "But whoever believes and does righteous deeds, he will have the best reward, and We will say to him 'Whoever is truthful and alone in Our affair is easy': As for those of whom Allah is truthful and alone, and acts upon His obedience, he will have the best reward with Allah, which is Paradise, meaning a reward for his faith and obedience to his Lord." Reciters have differed in reading this; the majority of the reciters of Medina and some from Basra and Kufa read it as: "So he will have the reward of the good" by raising the reward and adding it to the good. If this is read as such, it has two interpretations: the first is that the good deed is intended by it his faith and righteous deeds, so the meaning of the statement if it is intended is: either whoever believes and does righteous deeds will have its reward, meaning the reward for these good deeds. The second aspect is that the meaning of the good is Paradise, and the reward is added to it, as it is said: "And the abode of the Hereafter is better," and the abode is the Hereafter, as he said: "And that is the religion of value," and religion is the values. In the excerpt, he quotes from Mujahid and Ibn Jurayj.
