## Summary
Verse 203 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum

**Commentary on verse 203 (pp. 340-344)**

Al-Tabari says: when you do not bring these polytheists a sign from God, Muhammad, "they say, why have you not chosen it", meaning why have you not picked it out and selected it, from God's words "but God chooses whom He will of His messengers", [[passage/quran-3-179|3:179]], meaning picks and selects; he has shown that in its places with its witnesses. The interpreters then differed over it. Some said it means: why did you not invent it from yourself and make it up, meaning why did you not "choose" it by fabricating it, as the Arabs say "so-and-so chose this matter and picked it out as a fabrication". Report 15571, from Qatada: why do you not bring it to us from yourself, this is the saying of the unbelievers of Quraysh; report 15572, from Mujahid: why have you not improvised it, they said bring it out of yourself; report 15573, from Ibn Zayd: why have you not made it up and come with it from yourself; report 15574, from Ibn Abbas: why have you not received it, and another time, why have you not brought it about and produced it; report 15575, from al-Suddi: why have you not brought it about; report 15576, from Qatada: why have you not come with it from yourself. The editor says this is a very strange sense for "ikhtara" and he is in doubt of it unless the meaning is that the Arabs say in figure "he chose the thing as a fabrication" in the sense of making it up, and explains "iqtadaba" speech as improvising it without preparation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 340} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 341} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 342}

Others said it means why did you not take it from your Lord and receive it from Him. Report 15577, from Ibn Abbas: why have you not accepted it from God; report 15578, from Qatada: why have you not received it from your Lord; report 15579, from al-Dahhak: why have you not taken it and brought it from heaven. The editor notes the differences of wording between the manuscript and printed edition in these reports and hopes "received" is the right one. Then al-Tabari says: the better of the two interpretations is that of those who said it means why did you not bring it about from yourself, from the indication of God's words "say: I follow only what is revealed to me from my Lord, this is insight from your Lord", which shows that God commanded His prophet to answer them with a report about himself that he only follows what his Lord sends down and reveals to him, not that he produces speech from himself and fashions it and calls people to it. It is reported of al-Farra that he said "ijtabaytu al-kalam", "ikhtalaqtuhu" and "irtajaltuhu" when you made it up from yourself, report 15580, from al-Harith from al-Qasim from him. Abu Ubayda said that Abu Zayd used to say the Arabs say that of speech a man begins that he had not prepared in himself beforehand, and Abu Ubayda said "ikhtara'tuhu" is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 342} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 343}

Then al-Tabari says on "say: I follow only what is revealed to me from my Lord; this is insight from your Lord, and guidance and mercy for people who believe": He says to His prophet Muhammad, say, Muhammad, to those who say to you when you do not bring them a sign, why have you not made it up from yourself: that is not for me and it is not permitted me to do it, because God commanded me only to follow what is revealed to me from Him; I follow what is revealed to me from my Lord because I am His servant, I come to His command and obey Him alone. "This is insight from your Lord" means this Qur'an and revelation I recite to you are insights from your Lord, that is, proofs against you and a clear statement for you from your Lord, "insight" having the plural "basa'ir", as He says "these are insights for people and guidance and mercy for people who are sure", [[passage/quran-45-20|45:20]]. "This" is singular because the Qur'an and the revelation are meant. "And guidance" means a clear statement that guides the believers to the straight road, "and mercy" means God showed mercy by it to His believing servants and saved them by it from error and ruin, "for people who believe" means it is insight from God, guidance and mercy for whoever believes, that is, trusts in the Qur'an as God's sending down and revelation and acts on what is in it, not for whoever denied it, rejected it and disbelieved in it; rather for those who do not believe in it it is blindness and disgrace. The editor notes that the printed edition had a different word and the manuscript's unpointed word is rightly read "blindness", from God's words about the Qur'an, [[passage/quran-41-44|41:44]], and refers to the earlier explanations of "following", "revelation", "insight", "guidance", "mercy" and "belief". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 343} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 344}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
