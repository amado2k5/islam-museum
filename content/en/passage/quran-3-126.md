## Summary
Verse 126 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 126 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 190).** Abu Ja'far says the sense is: God made His promise to reinforce you with the angels whose number He mentioned only as glad tidings He gave you, and so that your hearts would be at rest in His promise and settle in it and not be alarmed at the size of your enemy and the fewness of your numbers; and your victory, if you win, comes only through God's help and not from the reinforcement of angels that reaches you. So they should rely on God and seek His help, not the crowds and numbers: if victory comes through God and with his help and five thousand of His angels are with them, then with large numbers of people it is all the more by God's help and strengthening; so let them be godwary and patient in fighting their enemy, for God will help them. In two notes the editor restores a "wa" dropped in the manuscript and printed text and explains the sentence's construction, then praises the imam for taking care, in explaining meanings, to keep God's oneness pure and guard His attributes from association: he removed from victory any idea that the angels' descent was its cause, and made clear that victory is from God for believers and angels alike and that the angels were only reinforcement as God said; the editor counts this among Abu Ja'far's understanding and insight and prays for God's mercy on him. This note is cut at the foot of p. 190 and finishes on p. 191. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 190}

**Reports on the meaning (p. 191).**
- From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7793): God made them only so that the believers would rejoice at them and be at ease with them; they did not fight alongside them that day, meaning Uhud; Mujahid said they did not fight with them that day, nor before it nor after it, except at Badr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 191}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7794): because God knows their weakness, and victory is from Him by His authority and power, for might and judgment are His and not any creature's. The editor says the manuscript and printed wording are garbled and that he fixed it from Ibn Ishaq's text in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 114), and that the report follows reports 7733 and 7741. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 191}
- From Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Ibn Zayd (7795): had He wished to give you victory without the angels, He would have. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 191}

**The two divine names (pp. 191-192).** He says the Mighty is so in His vengeance on those who disbelieve in Him, through the hands of His friends who obey Him, and the Wise in His management of the believers' affairs against their enemies and in His other affairs; so He tells them the glad news of His management for them and His help against their enemies if they obey what He commanded and are patient in fighting His enemy and theirs. The editor notes that a phrase in the manuscript is incoherent and the printed text is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 192}
