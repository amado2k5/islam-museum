## Summary
Verse 89 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 89 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary: the wish for disbelief, and emigration (p. 17, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says the hypocrites over whom the believers divided wish that you would deny your Lord's oneness and the truthfulness of your Prophet and become unbelievers like them, equal with them in idolatry. So do not make them your supporters until they leave the abode of idolatry and part from its people for the abode of Islam, seeking God's religion; then they become like you and have your status. Report 10066 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather) from Ibn Abbas: until they do as you did, meaning emigration in God's cause. The editor refers to earlier treatments of the words for wishing, equality, friendship and God's path. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 17}

**The order to seize and kill if they turn away (p. 18, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari: if these hypocrites turn away from acknowledging God and His Messenger and from emigrating from the abode of idolatry to the abode of Islam, then, believers, seize them and put them to death in their own lands and elsewhere, wherever you find them on God's earth; take from them neither a friend who stands by you nor a helper against your enemies, for they are unbelievers who spare no effort to ruin you. He reads the statement as proof that the hypocrisy of those over whom the believers disagreed was genuine, and as a warning to those who defended them. Report 10067 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, same chain) from Ibn Abbas: if they turn away from emigration, kill them. Report 10068 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: if they show their unbelief, kill them wherever you find them. The editor refers to earlier explanations of turning away, friend and helper. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 18}


These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 18}
