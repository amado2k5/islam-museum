Verse 28 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum

Then, on "when they commit an indecency they say: we found our fathers doing it, and God commanded us to; say: God does not command indecency; do you say about God what you do not know?", it is said that the meaning of "indecency" here is what (14462) is given from Mujahid: they used to circle the House naked, saying, we circle as our mothers bore us; the woman would put a strap or something on her private part and say, in a line whose sense is: today part of it shows, or all of it, and what shows of it I do not permit. The note says Abu Muhayat is Yahya ibn Ya'la, trustworthy, and that the "nis'a" is a broad braided strip of leather put on a camel's breast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 377}

He gives (14463) and (14464) from Mujahid that their indecency was that they circled the House naked; (14464m) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-Sha'bi the same; (14465) from al-Suddi that a tribe of the Arabs of Yemen circled the House naked, and when asked why they did it said, we found our fathers on it and God commanded us to; (14466) from Ibn Abbas that their indecency was their circling the House naked; and (14467) from Mujahid that it was in the circumambulation of the Hums in clothes while others were naked, and the note says the Hums are Quraysh, so called for their strictness in their religion; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 378}

and (14468) from Mujahid that their women circled the House naked, and that was the indecency in which they found their fathers. Al-Tabari says the meaning is therefore: when those who do not believe in God, whose allies God made the devils, do an ugly deed, which is the indecency, namely their stripping to circle the House and going bare for it, and are blamed and rebuked for their ugly deed, they say, we found our fathers doing the like of what we do, so we do as they did, follow their guidance and keep their way, and God commanded us to do it, so we are following His command in it. God says to His Prophet, say to them, Muhammad, that God does not command indecency, that is, He does not command His creatures to ugly and bad deeds; "do you say, people, about God what you do not know?" means do you relate of God that He commanded you to strip and go bare of garments and clothes for circumambulation, when you do not know that He commanded you to? The note says it thinks the better reading is "do you forge" in place of "do you relate", meaning do you speak falsehood and lies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 379}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
