**Commentary on verse 120 (pp. 561-564)**

Al-Tabari says: it was not for the people of Medina, the city of the Messenger of God, nor the Bedouin around them in the desert who stayed behind from the Messenger on the Tabuk campaign though believers, to remain with their families and homes, nor to prefer their own lives to his in accompanying him on his journey, striving with him and helping him in what he bore on his campaign. He says they were not permitted this because nothing afflicted them on the journey with him, neither thirst, nor weariness, nor hunger in God's way, that is, in establishing God's religion, supporting it and demolishing the beacons of unbelief, nor did they tread any land, and the sentence continues on the next page. The editor refers to earlier discussion of desire, hunger and God's way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 561}

Al-Tabari continues: nor do they tread any ground that angers the unbelievers, nor gain anything from the enemy of God and their enemy in their wealth, selves and children, but God records for them for all of it the reward of a righteous deed He has approved. "God does not let the reward of the doers of good be lost" means He does not leave any of His creatures who did good in their work, obeying what He commanded and avoiding what He forbade, without repaying and rewarding them for their righteous deeds; so for those of the people of Medina and the Bedouin around them who did this He recorded the reward for all they did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 562}

The interpreters differed over the ruling of this verse. Some said it is a binding rule and applied to the Messenger of God in particular: no one might stay behind when he himself campaigned, except for the excused, whereas for other leaders and governors, whoever of the believers wishes may stay behind them if the Muslims have no need of him. Report 17462 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: this is when the Prophet himself campaigned, so no one may stay behind; it was mentioned to us that he said that were it not for burdening his community he would not stay behind a detachment going out in God's way, but he found no ample means to take them with him and disliked to leave them behind. Report 17463 is from al-Awza'i, Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak, al-Fazari, al-Subay'i, Ibn Jabir and Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz, through Ali ibn Sahl and al-Walid ibn Muslim: it applies to the first and last of this community among those striving in God's way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 563}

Others said the verse was revealed when Muslims were few, and when they grew many God abrogated it and permitted staying behind to whoever wished, in verse 122. Report 17464 is from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: this was when Islam was small, and when it grew He said that the believers should not all go out together. Al-Tabari says that in his view God meant those He described in verse 90, the Bedouin excuse-makers who came for permission, and then said it was not for the people of Medina who stayed behind from the Messenger, nor the Bedouin around them who sat out jihad, to stay behind him. For the Messenger had called on everyone able to go out with him on that campaign, except those he gave leave or commanded to stay; those able to go had no right to stay. God then enumerated those who stayed, exposed the hypocrisy of those whose staying was hypocrisy, excused those with an excuse, and turned to those whose staying was negligence, without doubt or suspicion about God's command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 563}

Staying behind from him when he had no need was not forbidden, if it was not that he disliked it; and so is the rule for Muslims today toward their leader: it is not binding on all of them to march with him except when he needs them and Islam and its people must have them present and gathered, when obeying him becomes binding. If this is the meaning, neither of the two verses abrogates the other, since neither cancels the ruling of the other in every respect, and no report establishes abrogation. He says he has explained elsewhere that *makhmasa* is hunger. *Nayl* is the verbal noun of "it reached me" and "I got something", when one gets it by hand, and not from *tanawul*, which comes from *nawal*. He mentions a view of a scholar of Arabic usage that *nayl* has a waw changed to ya, rejects it as unknown in Arab speech, since the Arabs keep the waw in roots where it is silent after a fatha, as in *qawl*, *awl* and *hawl*. The editor observes that *nayl* had not been explained earlier in this way and that the observation helps in tracing the method by which al-Tabari composed his commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 564}
