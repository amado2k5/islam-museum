**Commentary on verse 12: the covenant of the Children of Israel and the chiefs (pp. 109-110)**

Al-Tabari says: this verse was sent down to inform the Prophet and believers of the character of those Jews who planned to stretch out their hands to them. Report 11567, from al-Hasan, says the Children of Israel here are the Jews among the People of the Book. What they planned, betrayal and breaking the covenant, is from their traits and those of their ancestors long ago; it also gave the Prophet an argument against the Jews by informing him of what was known to them and not to the Arabs of their hidden matters, and was a rebuke to the Jews for persisting in error and unbelief while knowing their position was wrong. God says to his Prophet: do not think much of those who planned to strike you or of the treachery they sought, for it is the character of their forebears. He then begins to report some of their betrayals and daring against their Lord and their breaking the covenant he took, despite the favours he gave them, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 109}

saying: God took the covenant of the forebears of those who planned to stretch out a hand to you, believers, of the Jews of the Children of Israel, to keep faith with him and obey what he commanded and forbade. Report 11568, from Abu al-Aliya: God took their covenants that they would be sincere to him and worship none but him. On 'and we raised up from among them twelve chiefs', he explains that it means twelve guarantors who stood surety for them to keep faith with God in the covenants they made. In Arab speech the 'naqib' is like the 'arif over a people, but above the 'arif; one says 'naqaba' over such a tribe, and 'naqiba' with 'naqaba' for becoming a chief; the 'manakib' are like assistants with the 'urafa'. Some Arabic scholars said he is the trustworthy surety over a people. The interpreters differed over its meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 110}

Some said the 'naqib' is the witness over his people; report 11569, from Qatada, says one man from each tribe as a witness over his people. Others said the 'naqib' is the commander; reports 11570 and 11571, from al-Rabi', say the 'chiefs' are the trustworthy ones. Al-Tabari then says that God ordered Moses to send the twelve chiefs from his people to the land of the giants in Syria to find out about them, since he meant Moses and his people to inherit their land and homes and make them dwellings for the Children of Israel after saving them from Pharaoh and bringing them out of Egypt. Report 11572, from al-Suddi: God ordered the Children of Israel to go to Jericho, the land of Jerusalem, and when they were near, Moses sent twelve chiefs from all the tribes; a man of the giants called Aj met them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 111}

He took the twelve and put them in the fold of his waist-wrapper with a load of firewood on his head, and went with them to his wife and said: look at these people who claim they mean to fight us, and threw them before her, and said: shall I not crush them with my foot? His wife said: no, let them go so that they tell their people what they saw, and he did. When they left, they told each other: if you tell the Children of Israel about these people they will turn from the Prophet of God, so keep it secret and tell the two prophets of God, who will decide; and they took a covenant from each other to keep it secret. Ten of them broke the pledge, and each told his brother and father what he had seen of Aj, while two kept it secret and came to Moses and Aaron and told them; that is when God says what he says in this verse. Report 11573, from Mujahid: twelve chiefs, a man from each tribe, whom Moses sent to the giants; they found two of the giants fitting in the sleeve of one of them, and one cluster of their grapes needing five men to carry on a pole. The notes record the editor's astonishment at the exaggerations of the narrators of Isra'iliyyat, and add notes on the words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 112}

and half a pomegranate, its seeds removed, holding five or four men. The chiefs returned, each forbidding his tribe from fighting them, except Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Yufanna, who urged the tribes to fight the giants and wage war; the tribes disobeyed these two and obeyed the others. Report 11574, from Mujahid, is similar. Report 11575, from Ibn Ishaq: Moses was ordered to go with the Children of Israel to the Holy Land, and was told: I have written it for you as a home, a settled place and a dwelling, so go out to it and fight its enemy, for I will help you; take twelve chiefs from your people, one for each tribe, to answer for his people's keeping faith; so Moses took twelve chiefs from them, guarantors for their people to keep God's covenant, choosing from each tribe its best and most faithful man; and he went with them to the Holy Land by God's command, until he reached the Wilderness between Egypt and Syria, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 113}

a land with no fruit and no shade; Moses prayed to his Lord when the heat troubled them, and he shaded them with cloud, and prayed for their provision, and God sent down manna and quails. God told Moses to send men to scout the land of Canaan which was given to the Children of Israel, one from each tribe. Ibn Ishaq then gives, as the people of the Torah relate, the names of the group who were sent: from the tribe of Reuben, Shammua son of Zakkur; from Simeon, Shaphat son of Hori; from Judah, Caleb son of Yefunneh. The editor's notes show that these names are in the Book of Numbers and that their spellings vary among the manuscript, the printed text, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and Ibn Habib's Muhabbar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 114}

from Issachar, Igal son of Joseph; from the tribe of Joseph, which is Ephraim, Joshua son of Nun; from Benjamin, Palti son of Raphu; from Zebulun, Gaddiel son of Sodi; from Manasseh son of Joseph, Gaddi son of Susi; from Dan, Ammiel son of Gemalli; from Asher, Sethur son of Michael; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 115}

from Naphtali, Nahbi son of Vophsi; from Gad, Geuel son of Machi. These are the names of those Moses sent to scout the land for him; on that day Hoshea son of Nun was named Joshua son of Nun. He sent them and told them: go up before the sun, cross the mountain, see what is in the land and what the people living there are like, strong or weak, few or many; see their land, whether it is fat or lean, wooded or not; and bring us some of the fruit of that land. It was the time when the first grapes ripened. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir said he had seen different names in the fourth book of the Torah, but that Ibn Ishaq's names agree with the Book of Numbers in the copy at hand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 116}

Report 11576, from Ibn Abbas: they were of the Children of Israel, sent by Moses to look at the city; they went and looked and brought back a single grape of their fruit, a man's load, and said: reckon the strength and might of a people whose fruit is this; thereupon they were put to trial and said they could not fight, and said to Moses what is in [[passage/quran-5-24|5:24]]. Report 11577, from Abu Mu'adh al-Fadl ibn Khalid: God told the Children of Israel to go to the Holy Land with their prophet Moses, and when they were near the city Moses told them: enter it. They refused and were afraid, and sent twelve chiefs to look at the people; they went, looked, and brought back a single grape of their fruit as a man's load, and said: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 117}

Report 11577 is completed: reckon the strength and might of a people whose fruit is this; thereupon they said to Moses what is in 5:24. Al-Tabari then explains 'God said: I am with you': God said to the Children of Israel that he will help them against their enemy and his, whom he told them to fight, if they fight them and keep his covenant and the pledge he took from them. There is an omitted word that the rest of the sentence makes unnecessary, since the sense is 'God said to them', and 'to them' was left out because the report is already about a people named. The oath then begins: if you, Children of Israel, keep up the prayer, and give the alms, that is, give them to those he commanded you to give, and believe in my messengers, that is, trust what my messengers brought of the laws of my religion, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 118}

Al-Rabi' ibn Anas said this was God's address to the twelve chiefs, and report 11578 from him says Moses told the twelve chiefs: go to the giants and bring me their news and affair, and do not fear, for God is with you as long as you keep up the prayer. Al-Tabari says: what al-Rabi' said is not far from sound, but it is among God's decrees for all creatures that he helps those who obey him and befriends those who follow his command and avoid disobedience, and since keeping up the prayer, giving alms and believing the messengers are part of obedience, the forgiveness of misdeeds and entry to gardens is not restricted to the chiefs, but is for the whole people. Interpreters differed on 'and you support them'. Some said: and you helped them; report 11579, from Mujahid, says you helped them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 119}

Report 11580, from Mujahid, is the same, and report 11581, from al-Suddi: you helped them with the sword. Others said it is obedience and help; report 11582, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd, says 'ta'zir' and 'tawqir' are obedience and help. The Arabic scholars differed on it. It is reported from Yunus that it means you praised them; report 11583 gives this from Abu Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna. Abu Ubayda himself said it means you helped them, assisted them, honoured them, magnified them and strengthened them, and quoted a verse about the many noble men and lions of a gathering. The editor comments on the attribution to Yunus and on an odd explanation of one word of the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 120}

Al-Farra' said 'azr' is repulsion: I repelled him if I saw him doing wrong and told him to fear God. Al-Tabari says the soundest view is that it means you helped them, because God says in the chapter of Victory, in [[passage/quran-48-9|48:9]], something showing that 'tawqir' is different from 'ta'zir'; when it is ruled out that it means magnifying, and help may be by hand or tongue, by hand defending with the sword or otherwise and by tongue with fair praise and defence of honour, it is right that it is help, since help includes the sense of each speaker. As for 'and you lend God a goodly loan', it means you spent in God's way, in fighting his enemy and yours, finding what is right in your spending and not overstepping God's limits. Asked why 'loan' and not 'lending', the verbal noun of 'aqrada', he answers that 'lending' would have been right, but that the phrase {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 121}

puts a verbal noun from the meaning and not from the wording, for in 'lend' is the sense of 'loan', as in 'give' is the sense of 'take'; the like is in [[passage/quran-71-17|71:17]], since 'made you grow' has the sense of 'you grew', and in Imru' al-Qays: I trained her and the hard one became tame, how complete a taming, since 'trained' has the sense of 'tamed'. He then explains 'I will wipe out your misdeeds and admit you to gardens': he means the Children of Israel, saying: if you, the people who gave me their pledge to obey me and follow my command, keep up the prayer, give the alms and do all else for which I promised you my garden, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 122}

I will cover with my pardon and overlooking of your punishment your past offences, the worship of the calf and other ruinous sins, and, with my covering that by my grace on the Day of Resurrection, I will admit you to gardens, meaning orchards. He glosses 'I will wipe out' as 'I will cover', because 'kufr' means denial, covering and veiling, as Labid says of a night when its clouds covered the stars. The Arabic scholars differed on the 'lam' in it. Some Basran grammarians said the first lam is for an oath and the second for another oath; some Kufan grammarians said the first stands in place of the oath, and the second is its answer, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 123}

arguing that 'if you keep up the prayer' is incomplete and needs 'I will wipe out', so it cannot be a fresh oath but must be the answer to an oath. On 'beneath which rivers flow', he says rivers flow beneath the trees of the orchards he will admit them to. He then explains 'whoever disbelieves after that among you has strayed from the right way': whoever of you, Children of Israel, denies any of what I commanded and leaves it, or does what I forbade, after I took the covenant to keep faith with me, has missed the straight course of the clear road and slipped from the right path. Error is going without guidance, 'sawa'' means middle, and 'sabil' means road, as he explained elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 124}
