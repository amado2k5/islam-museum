**Commentary on verse 129: unequal love between wives (pp. 284-287)**

Al-Tabari says the verse means that men cannot make themselves equal between their wives in the love of the heart, even if they try, because that is not in their power; so the heart will hold no more love for one than for the other. They are then told not to incline wholly to the one they love, leaving the other as though she were neither married nor unmarried. Report 10626, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih, says that being unable to be equal between them is an unavoidable fact. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 284}

Reports 10627 to 10633, from Ubayda through Ibn Sirin and others, give the equality as being of the self in love and intercourse, or in intercourse, or in love, or in affection; al-Hasan says it is love. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 285}

Report 10634, from Ibn Abbas, says you cannot be equal in desire between them, however much you try. Report 10635, from Qatada, says that it was reported that Umar used to say: O God, as for my heart I have no control; in other things I hope to be just. Report 10636, from Ibn Abbas, says the sense is love and intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 286}

Report 10637, through Ayyub from Abu Qilaba, says that the Prophet divided among his wives and was just, then said: O God, this is my division in what I possess; do not blame me for what You possess and I do not. The editor notes that Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah and al-Tirmidhi transmit it by several routes, one through A'isha; al-Tirmidhi judged a mursal version through Hammad ibn Zayd more sound than the one through Hammad ibn Salama. Al-Tirmidhi and some scholars explain what he does not possess as love and affection; Abu Dawud says it is the heart. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 286}

Reports 10638 to 10642: through Ibn Abi Mulayka, the verse was revealed about A'isha (10638); through al-Dahhak, from Juwaybir, the sense is desire and intercourse (10639 and 10640); from Sufyan, love and intercourse (10641); from Ibn Zayd, what happens in his body and heart, which he cannot possess (10642). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 287}

Reports 10643 to 10647, from Ubayda through Ibn Awn, Ibn Ulayya, Hisham, Hibban ibn Musa and al-Awza'i, say that the forbidden inclination is 'by himself', that is, of the self. Report 10645, through Hisham and Ibn Sirin, has Ubayda say that Hisham thinks it means love and intercourse. Report 10648, from al-Hasan, says it is in intercourse and division. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 288}

Reports 10649 to 10652 give Mujahid's view, through Ibn Abi Najih and Ibn Jurayj: do not intend to do harm; whoever deliberately inclines does wrong and oppresses. Report 10653, from Ibn Zayd, says the inclination here is in his work of staying the night with her and in what he takes of her goodness; report 10654, from al-Suddi, says he leans over her, does not maintain her, and gives her no day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 289}

Report 10655, from Mujahid, says he deliberately does harm; from Ibn Jurayj, he was told it means intercourse. Report 10656, from Abu Qilaba through Hammad ibn Zayd and Ayyub, and report 10657, through Abd al-Wahhab, from A'isha, have the Prophet divide among his wives justly and say: O God, this is my division in what I possess; do not blame me for what You possess and I do not. The editor notes that print and manuscript both give 'Abd Allah ibn Zayd', an error for Abd Allah ibn Yazid, A'isha's foster-son, and that the 'al-Khatmi' in the printed Abu Dawud is not sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 289}

Report 10658, from Abu Hurayra through Bashir ibn Nahik, al-Nadr ibn Anas, Qatada and Hammam ibn Yahya: whoever has two wives and inclines to one of them will come on the Day of Resurrection with one side fallen or leaning. The editor sets out its routes in Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah and al-Bayhaqi; al-Tirmidhi says Hammam traced it to Qatada, that Hisham al-Dastuwa'i reported it from Qatada with the words 'it used to be said', and that it is not known as a saying of the Prophet except through Hammam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 290}

Report 10659, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha and Mu'awiya, says the 'hanging' wife is neither unmarried nor married. Report 10660, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, says neither unmarried nor having a husband; reports 10661 and 10662, from al-Hasan, say neither divorced nor married. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 290}

Report 10663, from Qatada, says she is as one held back or imprisoned; report 10664, from Ma'mar through Qatada, says as one imprisoned. The editor notes that the manuscript has both words and the printed edition keeps one; the sense is that the husband has confined her, neither releasing her nor divorcing her. Reports 10665 and 10666, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, say neither divorced nor married; report 10667, from Mujahid, says neither unmarried nor married. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 291}

Reports 10668 to 10670, from Mujahid, al-Dahhak and al-Suddi, say she is neither unmarried nor married, and her case must not be left as though she had no husband. Report 10671, from Ibn Zayd, describes the 'hanging' wife as one not released, whose self seeks a husband, and who is in no state of a wife with her husband, neither with him nor separated, so she may seek for herself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 292}

Al-Tabari says God commanded men to be just between their wives in what they can do: division, maintenance, and not wronging one by leaving her to the other. He pardoned them for what they cannot control, the love and inclination of the heart. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 292}

The last part of verse 129 concerns reform, piety, forgiveness and mercy: men should put their dealings right, be fair in division, maintenance and honourable company, and fear God against the forbidden inclination. In forgiveness God covers the past inclination and wrong by withholding punishment for it; in mercy He accepts repentance and permits a settlement in which the wife releases her turn on condition that she is not divorced. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 293}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
