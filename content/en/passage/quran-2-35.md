## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "And We said, O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in the Garden" al-Tabari takes the verse as clear evidence that Iblis was expelled from the Garden after refusing to prostrate, that Adam was settled there before Iblis went down to earth, and that Iblis made them slip only after being cursed. He reports from al-Suddi that Iblis, once cursed and expelled and before descending to earth, swore by God's might to lead Adam, his descendants and his wife astray, except God's sincere servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 512} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 513}
He then records the difference over when Adam's wife was created. From Ibn Abbas through al-Suddi: Adam was settled in the Garden alone, slept, and woke to find a woman beside his head whom God had made from his rib; he named her Hawwa because she was made from a living thing, and the angels asked him her name to see how far his knowledge went. From Ibn Ishaq, relaying what he had heard from the People of the Book and others, she was created before his settling in the Garden, from a rib of his left side while he slept, and on waking he said she was his flesh, his blood and his wife and found rest in her. The report is given as what "they claim, and God knows best", and the editor glosses "qabilan" as face to face. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 513} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 514}
He notes that "zawja" with the feminine ending is more usual in Arabic for a man's wife than "zawj" without it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 514}
On "and eat from it freely" he explains "raghad" as wide, easy living that does not trouble its owner, citing Imru' al-Qays, and reports from al-Suddi "easy" and from Mujahid "no reckoning against them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 515}
