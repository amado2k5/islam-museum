## Summary
Verse 110 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 110 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The opening of the dispute about the praised community (p. 100).** Al-Tabari notes that the interpreters differed over who is meant by the verse's description of this community as the best brought forth for people. Some said: those who emigrated with God's messenger from Mecca to Medina. This view continues on the next page.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 100}

**First view: the emigrants (7606, 7607, 7611, pp. 100-101).** Some said they are those who emigrated with God's messenger from Mecca to Medina, specifically among his Companions. From Abu Kurayb, Amr ibn Hammad, Asbat, Simak, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas (7606): those who went out with him from Mecca. From Abu Kurayb, Ibn Atiyya, Qays, Simak, Ikrima, Ibn Abbas (7607): those who emigrated from Mecca to Medina. From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Isra'il, Simak ibn Harb, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas (7611): those who emigrated with the Prophet to Medina. The editor says Ahmad has it in the Musnad under several numbers (3321, 2989, 2928, 2463 and others) with a chain he judges sound, and that al-Hakim gives it in the Mustadrak (2:294), calling it sound on Muslim's condition though neither of the two shaykhs gave it, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 100} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 102}

**Second view: the special Companions (7608-7610, 7612-7613, pp. 101-102).** From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad, Asbat, al-Suddi (7608): Umar ibn al-Khattab said that had God willed, the address would have been put in a form covering everyone and all of us would be included, but He used the past form for a special group of God's messenger's Companions and those who acted as they did, who were the best of communities, commanding what is good and forbidding what is wrong. From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Ikrima (7609): it was revealed about Ibn Mas'ud, Salim the client of Abu Hudhayfa, Ubayy ibn Ka'b and Mu'adh ibn Jabal. From Abu Kurayb, Mus'ab ibn al-Miqdam, Isra'il, al-Suddi from one who told him (7610): Umar said it is for our first and not for our last. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7612): it was mentioned to them that Umar ibn al-Khattab, on a pilgrimage, saw poor conduct among the people, recited the verse and said: people, whoever is pleased to be of that community, let him fulfil God's condition in it. The editor glosses the word for conduct as guidance, state and manners, and God's condition as what He asked of that community. From Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid, Juwaybir, al-Dahhak (7613): they are the Companions of God's messenger specifically, the transmitters and callers whom God commanded the Muslims to obey; the editor refers to his note on "transmitters" at report 7597. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 102}

**Third view: conditional on the qualities (7614, 7615, pp. 102-103).** Others said the sense is that you are the best of communities provided you meet the conditions God described, commanding good, forbidding wrong and believing in God, brought out for the people of your time. From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7614): on this condition, meaning for those among them; and from al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Mujahid (7615) the same. Mujahid compares it with the choosing of a people with knowledge in [[passage/quran-44-32|44:32]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 102} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 103}

**Fourth view: the most beneficial to people (7616, 7617, p. 103).** From Ibn Waki, his father, Sufyan, Maysara, Abu Hazim, Abu Hurayra (7616): you were the best of people for people, bringing them in chains and putting them into Islam. From Ubayd ibn Asbat, his father, Fudayl ibn Marzuq, Atiyya (7617): the best of people for people. The editor says al-Bukhari has the first through Maysara ibn Ammar al-Ashja'i, a reliable Kufan, that Abu Hazim is Salman al-Ashja'i and that "Sulayman" in the Fath is an error, that al-Bukhari's wording has them bringing them in chains on their necks until they enter Islam, and that Ibn Hajar treated the meaning of the verse and most of these reports at length. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 103}

**Fifth view: the most responsive to Islam (7618, p. 103).** Others said it is said because they were the community most responsive to Islam. From Ammar ibn al-Hasan, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7618): no community was more responsive in Islam than this one, hence the description. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 103}

**Sixth view: they were so, and al-Tabari's choice (7619-7623, pp. 104-105).** Some said it means they were the best of communities. From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7619): what you hear of good has been in this community. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7620): al-Hasan used to say, we are its last and most honoured with God. Al-Tabari says the soundest of these for the verse is al-Hasan's, because Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim related from Ibn Ulayya, Bahz ibn Hakim, his father, his grandfather, who heard God's messenger say that they complete seventy communities and are the last and most honoured with God (7621); and from al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar, Bahz ibn Hakim, in the same sense with mention of the verse (7622); and from Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7623) that it was mentioned to them that the Prophet said one day, with his back to the Kaaba, that on the Day of Resurrection they complete seventy communities, being the last and best. The editor says 7621 and 7622 are one report with two chains, already given at no. 873, that al-Hakim has it in the Mustadrak (4:84) with a sound chain, that al-Hafiz cites it in the Fath (8:169) as good and sound, recording it in al-Tirmidhi (who graded it good), Ibn Majah and al-Hakim (who graded it sound), and that its sense also occurs in a long raised report from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri in Ahmad's Musnad with a sound chain. This is al-Tabari's choice; the other views are given as transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 104} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 105}

**Commanding, forbidding and believing (7624, p. 105).** Al-Tabari says commanding means commanding belief in God and His messenger and acting by His laws, and forbidding means forbidding associating others with God, denying His messenger and doing what He forbade. From Ali ibn Dawud, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya, Ali, Ibn Abbas (7624): they command them to testify to the word of oneness and acknowledge what God sent down, and fight them over it; that is the greatest good, and they forbid denial, the worst wrong. The root sense of the known good is whatever is known and its doing beautiful and approved, not disliked among believers, so obedience to God is called that because believers know it and do not disapprove its doing; the wrong is what God disapproved and believers deem ugly, so disobedience is called that because believers disapprove and regard its commission as grave. Belief means affirming God and devoting oneness and worship to Him. The editor, in a note, defends the manuscript's wording against those who thought it irregular, calling it plainly correct, and refers to his note on the two terms at p. 91. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 105}

**An objection on the past form (p. 106).** Al-Tabari raises a question: how can the verb be in the past when you say the verse means this community is the best of past communities, since the past is said of people who were good and then changed? He answers that the sense is the opposite of what the questioner supposes, and means "you are", as in [[passage/quran-8-26|8:26]] and [[passage/quran-7-86|7:86]], where the state is put in two forms with one meaning; adding or dropping the verb in such places is the same because the sense is known. If someone took it in the sense of completion, it would mean you were created or found the best of communities, a sound sense too. Some Arabists claimed the sense is that they were the best with God in the Preserved Tablet, brought out for the people. He says the first two accounts are closest to the sense of the report he gave earlier. Another gloss is the best people of a way, with the word for community meaning a way. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1:229) and to the earlier notes on the word for community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 106}

**The close of the verse: if the People of the Book had believed (7625, pp. 107-108).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: had the people of the Torah and Gospel, Jews and Christians, believed in Muhammad and what he brought from God, it would be better for them with God in their present world and the next; among them are believers who affirmed God's messenger in what he brought, namely Abdullah ibn Salam and his brother, Tha'laba ibn Sa'ya and his brother and their like who believed and followed what came to them, while most of them are those who left their religion. His reason is that the Jews' religion is following the Torah and believing in Muhammad, the Christians' following the Gospel and believing in it and in the Torah, and both books contain Muhammad's description, attributes and sending and that he is God's prophet, yet both groups deny him, which is their departure from the religion they claim to follow. From Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7625): God blamed most people. The editor corrects from the manuscript the name Tha'laba ibn Sa'ya (the printed text has Sa'id), says it comes again correctly in the report of his and his brother's conversion at 7644, refers to earlier explanations of the word for defiant wrongdoing, and corrects the phrase on the two books. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 107} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 108}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 108}
