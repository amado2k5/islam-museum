## Summary
Verse 102 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 102 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Beginning of the commentary: the Prophet among them and the prayer established with its limits (p. 141, note 1).** The commentary on verse 102 of sura 4 begins on p. 141. Al-Tabari says the sense is: when you, Muhammad, are among your companions who are travelling and fearing the trial from their enemy, and you establish the prayer for them with its limits, its bowing and prostration, without shortening it in the way permitted at the clash and advance of the lines (leaving the limits, bowing, prostration and other obligations), then let a group of your companions among whom you stand pray with you, and let the rest face the enemy. He says the text omits what the other groups, who are not praying, should do, because the wording points to it and what is said suffices for what is left unsaid; and he passes on to the command to take arms. The editor's note refers to al-Tabari's earlier explanation of the word for "group" in volume 6. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 141}

**The dispute over which group is told to take arms, and report 10344 (p. 142, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the group commanded to take arms. Some said it is the group praying with the Prophet, so that the sense is: let the praying group with you take its arms, the arms they were told to take in prayer being such things as the sword one hangs on oneself, a knife, or a dagger fastened to the coat of mail and clothes. Others said it is rather the group facing the enemy and not praying, and this is the view of Ibn Abbas. Report 10344 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas): when the group that stood with you in your prayer has finished prostrating, let them go behind you, lined up against the enemy in the place where the other groups are who did not pray with you or join your prayer. The editor notes that the printed text has "with the Messenger of God" and that he kept the manuscript reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 142}

**The disagreement on what happens when the first group has finished (pp. 142-143, notes 1-3 on p. 143).** Al-Tabari then gives the interpreters' disagreement about the group being "behind" the worshippers once they have prostrated. Some said it means that when they have prayed and finished their prayer they should be behind you. These in turn divided. Some said that when this group has prayed one rak'a with the imam, it gives the salam and leaves for its companions' place facing the enemy, with nothing to make up; in their view these are the ones for whom God removed blame in shortening, that is, making it one rak'a, and they report that the Prophet prayed the prayer of fear with one group one rak'a without making it up, and with another group one rak'a without making it up. Al-Tabari remarks that he has mentioned some of this earlier and that this suffices without listing everything. Others of them said the duty of this group, once it has finished the rak'a it was told to pray with the Prophet, is to stand where it prayed, complete the rest of its prayer by itself, give the salam and go to its companions' line, while the Prophet remains standing until that group finishes the rest of its prayer, if it is one whose number may be shortened from what is required of residents in safety; then the other group, which had been facing the enemy, comes and he prays another rak'a with it. He then says they disagree about the ruling on this second group, and one party among them says the Prophet had to... and the discussion continues on p. 144. The editor's notes: the printed text and manuscript misplace the word "they said", which he restored to the start of the sentence; the clause on making it "one rak'a" explains the shortening of the prayer; and he gives the grammatical connection of "rather the duty fell on this group ... to stand in its place". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 143}

**The second group's ruling and report 10345 (pp. 143-145, notes 1-2 on p. 144 and note 1 on p. 145).** Al-Tabari goes on: those who say the first group completes its prayer alone differ over the second group. One party says that when the Prophet finished his two rak'as and raised his head from the second prostration he sat for the tashahhud, and the group that prayed the second rak'a with him and missed the first, being occupied with the enemy, stood and made up its missed rak'a while the Prophet waited seated in his tashahhud until it finished and recited it, and then he gave the salam with them. Another party says the group that missed the first rak'a sat with him for the tashahhud, reciting it along with his, and when he finished he gave the salam, and then the group stood and made up its missed rak'a; al-Tabari adds that each of these speakers reports from the Prophet accounts that he acted as that speaker says. He then gives those who say the Prophet waited for both groups until each had made up its prayer and did not leave his own prayer until both had finished. Report 10345 (Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Wahb, from Malik, from Yazid ibn Ruman, from Salih ibn Khawwat, from one who prayed the prayer of fear with the Prophet on the day of Dhat al-Riqa'): one group lined up with him while another faced the enemy; he prayed a rak'a with those with him, then stayed standing while they completed for themselves; then the other group came and he prayed with them, stayed seated while they completed for themselves, and then gave the salam with them. The editor's notes on pp. 144-145: the manuscript drops a part found in the printed text, which he judges correct; the manuscript has "made up" in the singular and the printed text in the dual, and he put a bracketed addition by his own judgment so the sentence reads; and the word for "facing" is read with a kasra or damma on its first letter. On 10345 he says Yazid ibn Ruman al-Asadi, Abu Ruh of Medina, a teacher of Malik, is a learned, reliable narrator of much hadith; that Salih ibn Khawwat ibn Jubayr ibn al-Nu'man al-Ansari narrated from his father and his maternal uncle Sahl ibn Abi Hathma and is a reliable follower of little hadith, whose fear-prayer report the authors of the main collections give; and that Sahl ibn Abi Hathma was a companion who was eight years old when the Prophet died. He quotes Ibn Hajar's Tahdhib giving Ibn Abi Hatim's report from his father that Sahl pledged allegiance under the tree, attended all the battles but Badr and guided the Prophet on the night of Uhud, says he could not find this in al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil, and cites Ibn al-Qattan's reply that Abu Hatim's statement is not sound and that the appraiser and guide to Uhud was Sahl's father Abu Hathma, as Ibn Jarir and others state. He calls the report sound, given by Malik, al-Shafi'i, al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi, al-Tahawi, al-Bayhaqi and al-Jassas. He adds that Ibn Hajar in the Fath notes that the unnamed narrator is said to be Sahl, since al-Qasim ibn Muhammad narrated it from Salih from Sahl, but that Ibn Hajar holds it more likely to be Salih's father Khawwat ibn Jubayr, as al-Nawawi asserts, and later shows that Sahl was a child at the time, which supports this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 145}

**Reports 10346-10353 (pp. 146-148, notes 1-2 on p. 146, 1-2 on p. 147 and 1-2 on p. 148).** These are reports of Sahl ibn Abi Hathma and Salih ibn Khawwat on the form of the prayer. Report 10346 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Ubayd Allah ibn Mu'adh, from his father, from Shu'ba, from Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim, from his father, from Salih, from Sahl): the Prophet prayed with his companions in fear, put them in two rows behind him, prayed a rak'a with those next to him and then stood, remaining standing until those behind prayed a rak'a; they moved forward and those who had been in front stayed back, and he prayed a rak'a with them, then sat until those who stayed back prayed a rak'a, and gave the salam. Report 10347 (Sufyan ibn Waki', from Rawh, from Shu'ba, with the same chain, from the Prophet): one group stands before the imam and one behind; he prays a rak'a of two prostrations with those behind, sits in his place until they make up a rak'a of two prostrations, then they move to their companions' place and those come to theirs, and he prays a rak'a of two prostrations with them and sits until they pray, then gives the salam. Then comes the heading for those who say the second group sat with the Prophet until he finished and then made up what remained. Report 10348 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Wahhab, from Yahya ibn Sa'id, from al-Qasim, from Salih ibn Khawwat, from Sahl): the imam stands facing the qibla with a group, another group facing the enemy; he bows and prostrates with those with him and stands; when upright, those behind him bow for themselves one rak'a of two prostrations, give the salam and leave while the imam stands, and stand opposite the enemy; the others come and say the takbir in the imam's place, he bows and prostrates with them and gives the salam, and they stand and bow for themselves one rak'a of two prostrations and give the salam. Report 10349 (Ibn Bashshar, from Yazid ibn Harun, from Yahya ibn Sa'id, from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, from Salih, from Sahl) is similar, and report 10350 (Ibn Bashshar, from Yahya ibn Sa'id, from Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Ansari, from al-Qasim, from Salih, from Sahl) gives the same form, ending that it is two rak'as for him and one for them. In report 10351 Bundar says he asked Yahya ibn Sa'id about this report and was told it from Shu'ba, from Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim, from his father, from Salih, from Sahl, from the Prophet, like Yahya ibn Sa'id's report, and Yahya said to write it beside it as he did not remember it. Report 10352 (Nasr ibn Ali, from Abd al-A'la, from Ubayd Allah, from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr, from Salih): the imam forms two rows, one facing the enemy and one behind him; he prays a rak'a with those behind, they stand and pray one for themselves, give the salam and go to line up; the others come, he prays a rak'a with them and gives the salam, and they stand and pray one for themselves. Report 10353 (Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, from Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, from Ubayd Allah, from al-Qasim, from Salih, from a man among the Prophet's companions) is similar, and Ubayd Allah says he has heard nothing better in what is mentioned on the prayer of fear. In the notes the editor says that 10346-10347 and 10348-10350 are given by Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim, al-Bayhaqi, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Malik; that 10347 is the report traced to the Prophet to which al-Tabari alludes in 10351; that the Yahya ibn Sa'id of 10350 is al-Qattan; that al-Tirmidhi says Yahya al-Ansari did not trace it to the Prophet from al-Qasim and his students narrated it as the saying of a companion, while Shu'ba traced it through Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim; that he found no source for 10352-10353, that the Ubayd Allah there is Ibn Umar, and that al-Bayhaqi gives a differently worded version in which Salih narrates from his father rather than from an unnamed man; and that the printed text has an accusative where the manuscript has the bare word for "something", which he read with the preposition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 148}

**Report 10354 and the view that the first group leaves without the salam (pp. 148-150, notes 1-2 on p. 149 and note 1 on p. 150).** Report 10354 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas), on his reading of the clause about a group standing with the Prophet: this is for the prayer in fear; the imam stands with a group while another group takes its arms and stands facing the enemy; he prays a rak'a with those with him and sits in his posture; they stand and pray the second rak'a for themselves while he sits, then leave to their companions and take their position; the others come, he prays the second rak'a with them and gives the salam, and they stand and pray their second rak'a; so the Prophet prayed on the day of Batn Nakhla. Al-Tabari then gives another view on the group being behind the Prophet: when the group that entered his prayer prostrates the second prostration of its first rak'a, it goes behind the Prophet and behind his companions who have not prayed, facing the enemy. These speakers say this group did not give the salam after its prostrations with the Prophet but went to its companions' station with the rest of its prayer still due, and the other group came and joined the Prophet in the remainder of his prayer, so that he prayed with them the rak'a still due from him, which they take to be the sense of the clause telling the second group to pray with him and take its caution and arms. They then differ on how each group made up what remained after the Prophet's salam. Some said that the second group, when the Prophet gave the salam, stood and made up what it missed in its place, while the first group had not completed facing the enemy; when it finished it went to its companions' lines, and the first group came to the place where it prayed and completed the rest. The editor notes that the printed text adds a conjunction in the word for "upon them", and adds the word "its prayer" where the manuscript, which he keeps, is better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 150}

**Reports 10355-10357 and the editor's notes on Khusayf (pp. 150-151, notes 1-2 on p. 150 and note 1 on p. 151).** Report 10355 (Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Abi al-Shawarib, from Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad, from Khusayf, from Abu Ubayda ibn Abd Allah, from Abd Allah): the Prophet led us in the prayer of fear; one group stood behind him and another facing the enemy; he prayed a rak'a with those behind, they withdrew to their companions' place, and the others stood behind him and he prayed a rak'a with them and gave the salam; then these stood and prayed a rak'a for themselves, went to their companions' place facing the enemy, and the others returned to their place and prayed a rak'a for themselves. Report 10356 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Ibn Fudayl, from Khusayf, from Abu Ubayda, from Abd Allah) is similar, and so is 10357 (Tamim ibn al-Muntasir, from Ishaq, from Sharik, from Khusayf, from Abu Ubayda, from his father, from the Prophet). The editor says Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad al-Abdi is among the reliable authorities, and that Khusayf ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Jazari was criticised; he quotes Ibn Hibban that a group of their imams abandoned him and others relied on him, that he was a pious, jurist, devout elder but often erred in what he narrated and stood alone against well-known narrators with what is not corroborated, though truthful in his own narration, so that the fair course is to accept what agrees with the reliable and leave what is uncorroborated. He says Abu Ubayda is the son of Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud and Abd Allah is Ibn Mas'ud; that Abu Dawud and al-Bayhaqi give the report by several routes from Khusayf, the wording of Sharik's route (10357) differing from that of Abd al-Wahid; and that al-Bayhaqi calls it a mursal report because Abu Ubayda did not meet his father, and says Khusayf is not strong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 151}

**The view of making up with or without recitation, and reports 10358-10360 (pp. 151-152, notes 1-4 on p. 152).** Al-Tabari gives a further view: the second group does not make up the rest of its prayer after the Prophet's salam but goes first, taking the station of its companions who prayed the first rak'a with him, while the first group comes to its station and makes up its remaining rak'a; some said it makes it up without recitation and others with recitation; having made it up and given the salam it goes to its companions' lines facing the enemy, and the second group comes to its place where it prayed with him and makes up its second rak'a with recitation, then gives the salam and leaves. Report 10358 (al-Harith, from Abd al-Aziz, from Sufyan, from Hammad, from Ibrahim) on the prayer of fear: he lines one row behind him and one facing the enemy away from his place of prayer; he prays a rak'a with the row behind, they go to the others' lines, those facing the enemy come and he prays a rak'a with them and gives the salam to them, having prayed two himself and each row one; then those to whom he gave the salam stand in the lines of those facing the enemy, the others come and make up the rak'a, then go and stand in their place and those return and pray a rak'a. Sufyan said: so each person has two rak'as. Report 10359 (Ibn Humayd, from Mihran, and Ali, from Zayd, both from Sufyan: Ibrahim used to say about the prayer of fear) is similar, and report 10360 (al-Harith, from Abd al-Aziz, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Umar ibn al-Khattab) is the same. The editor notes that he took the past-tense verbs from the manuscript instead of the printed present-tense ones, and its wording for "each person has two rak'as"; that al-Harith is Ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Usama and Abd al-Aziz is Ibn Aban al-Umawi, who came earlier at 10315 and 10295; and that he added from the manuscript the formula of mercy that follows the name of Umar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 152}

**Reports 10361-10364: each group makes up a rak'a, and the prayer of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari (p. 153, notes 1-5 on p. 153).** Al-Tabari gives another view: each of the two groups makes up its prayer as it can, without losing any part of it. Report 10361 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Yunus ibn Ubayd, from al-Hasan): Abu Musa al-Ash'ari led his companions in the prayer of fear at Isbahan when he raided it; he prayed a rak'a with one group while another kept watch, those he had led withdrew and the others took their place and he prayed a rak'a with them and gave the salam, and each group stood and prayed a rak'a. Report 10362 (Imran ibn Musa al-Qazzaz, from Abd al-Warith, from Yunus, from al-Hasan, from Abu Musa) is similar. Report 10363 (Ibn Bashshar, from Mu'adh ibn Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, from Abu al-Aliya and Yunus ibn Jubayr): Abu Musa prayed with his companions at "al-Dayr" of Isbahan when they were in no fear that day, but he wished to teach them their prayer; he put them in two rows, one behind him and one facing the enemy, prayed a rak'a with those next to him, they went to their companions' lines and the others came and he prayed a rak'a with them and gave the salam; these made up a rak'a and those a rak'a, so the imam had two rak'as in congregation and each of the others one. Report 10364 (Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from Abu al-Aliya, from Abu Musa) is the same. In the notes the editor Shakir says he could not find a place called "al-Dayr" or, as al-Suyuti's Durr has it, "al-Dar" in the Isbahan region, adding that the gazetteers omit many towns of those parts; that the Durr reads "and they were not much afraid that day"; that he kept the manuscript against the printed text on "two rows" and on "two rak'as"; and that al-Suyuti gives the report from Ibn Abi Shayba in different wording and al-Bayhaqi alludes to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 153}

**Reports 10365-10372: the reports of Ibn Umar and the words of Ibn Abbas (pp. 154-155, notes 1-4 on p. 154 and note 1 on p. 155).** Report 10365 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ayyub, from Nafi', from Ibn Umar) on the prayer of fear: he prays a rak'a with one group while another keeps watch, these go to their companions' place, the others come and he prays a rak'a with them and gives the salam, and each group stands and prays a rak'a. Report 10366 (Nasr ibn Ali, from Abd al-A'la, from Ubayd Allah, from Nafi', from Ibn Umar) is similar; so is 10367 (Imran ibn Bakkar al-Kala'i, from Yahya ibn Salih, from Ibn Ayyash, from Ubayd Allah, from Nafi', from Ibn Umar, from the Prophet), and 10368 (Sa'id ibn Yahya al-Umawi, from his father, from Ibn Jurayj, from al-Zuhri, from Salim, from Ibn Umar, who used to relate that he prayed with the Prophet) and 10369 (Ibn Waki', from Ibn Abd al-A'la, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Salim, from Ibn Umar, from the Prophet). Report 10370 (Ibn Waki', from Jarir, from Abd Allah ibn Nafi', from Nafi', from Ibn Umar): the Prophet said of the prayer of fear that the commander and a group of the people stand and make one prostration while another group stands between them and the enemy, and so on. Report 10371 (Muhammad ibn Harun al-Harbi, from Abu al-Mughira al-Himsi, from al-Awza'i, from Ayyub ibn Musa, from Nafi', from Ibn Umar): the Prophet prayed the prayer of fear with one of the two groups a rak'a, and so on. Report 10372 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) on the passage from the verse's opening clause to the order to pray with the Prophet: one group takes arms and faces the enemy while the other prays a rak'a with the imam, then they take their arms and face the enemy and their companions return and pray a rak'a with the imam, so the imam has two rak'as and the others one, and then they make up another rak'a, and this is the completion of the prayer. The editor notes that reports 10365, 10367, 10366 (the digital text shows 10396, most likely a misreading), 10370 and 10371 are five chains of the hadith of Nafi' from Ibn Umar, a sound hadith given by Ahmad in his Musnad at no. 6159 (which is al-Tabari's chain in 10371) and at no. 6431 through Musa ibn Uqba from Nafi', with a reference to his brother Ahmad Shakir's commentary; that Imran ibn Bakkar al-Kala'i came earlier at 2071; that Salim's report from Ibn Umar is sound and in Ahmad at no. 6351; that Muhammad ibn Harun al-Harbi, known as Abu Nashit, was a Baghdadi whose nisba in the Tahdhib is al-Rab'i (a tribe), "al-Harbi" being from al-Harbiyya, a quarter of Baghdad by Bab Harb, site of the graves of Bishr al-Hafi and Ahmad ibn Hanbal and named for Harb ibn Abd Allah al-Balkhi, a commander of al-Mansur and police chief of Baghdad, and that he found this nisba for Muhammad ibn Harun only in this commentary; and that Abu al-Mughira al-Himsi is Abd al-Quddus ibn al-Hajjaj al-Khawlani, reliable, a teacher of Ahmad, from whom al-Bukhari narrated and for whom the others narrated through intermediaries, who died in 212 with Ahmad ibn Hanbal praying over him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 155}

**The view that the verse came down with the enemy in the direction of the qibla, and reports 10373-10374 (pp. 155-157, note 1 on p. 156).** Al-Tabari gives another view: that the verse came down about the prayer of fear while the enemy was in the direction of the qibla, between the Muslims and the qibla, so the Prophet's prayer that day was the prayer of fear because the enemy lay between the imam and the qibla. Report 10373 (Abu Kurayb, from Yunus ibn Bukayr, from al-Nadr Abu Umar, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas): the Prophet went out on a raid and met the polytheists at Usfan; when he had prayed the noon prayer and they saw him bow and prostrate with his companions, some of them said to the others that this had been an opportunity, for had they attacked they would not have known until the attackers were upon them; one said they have another prayer dearer to them than their families and wealth, so prepare to attack them in it. God sent down on his Prophet the verse on the prayer of fear and told him what the polytheists had plotted. When the Prophet prayed the afternoon prayer with them facing him in the qibla direction, he put the Muslims in two rows behind him; he said the takbir and they all did, he bowed and they all bowed with him; when he prostrated, the row next to him prostrated and the row behind stood facing the enemy; when he finished his prostrations and stood, the second row prostrated and stood, and those next to him withdrew while the others advanced and took their place; when he bowed they all bowed, he rose and they rose, he prostrated and the row next to him prostrated while the second stood facing the enemy; when he finished and those next to him sat, the rear row prostrated, then they sat and all recited the tashahhud with him, and when he gave the salam he gave it to all. When the polytheists saw some of them prostrating while others stood watching them, they said that they had been told what they intended. The editor notes that al-Nadr Abu Umar is Nadr ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Khazzaz, weak in hadith, of whom Abu Nu'aym spoke slightingly when asked; that al-Hakim gives the report in the Mustadrak and calls it sound by al-Bukhari's condition, with al-Dhahabi agreeing, which the editor finds astonishing since al-Bukhari said in al-Nadr's entry that his hadith is rejected, and says this is why scholars pay no attention to al-Hakim's authentications; and that al-Suyuti gives it and adds al-Bazzar. Report 10374 (Ibn Humayd, from al-Hakam ibn Bashir, from Umar ibn Dharr, from Mujahid): the Prophet was at Usfan and the polytheists at Dajnan at the water near Mecca; after he prayed the noon prayer and they saw him bow and the people prostrate, they said that if he prayed another prayer after this they would raid him, and God warned him of it; the Prophet stood in prayer, said the takbir and the people with him, and so on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 157}

**Reports 10375-10378 (pp. 157-159, notes 1-4 on p. 157, notes on p. 158 and notes 1-3 on p. 159).** Report 10375 (Imran ibn Bakkar, from Yahya ibn Salih, from Ibn Ayyash, from Ubayd Allah ibn Amr, from Abu al-Zubayr, from Jabir): I was with the Prophet when we met the polytheists at Nakhl, between us and the qibla; when the noon prayer came he prayed with us all together; when we finished, the polytheists blamed one another and said, if only we had attacked while they prayed, and some said they have a prayer they await, coming now, dearer to them than their sons, so when they pray, attack them. Gabriel came to the Prophet with the news and taught him how to pray. When the afternoon prayer came the Prophet stood on the side nearer the enemy and we stood behind him in two rows; he said the takbir and we all did with him, and so on. Report 10376 (Muhammad ibn Ma'mar, from Hammad ibn Mas'ada, from Hisham ibn Abi Abd Allah, from Abu al-Zubayr, from Jabir, from the Prophet) is similar and so is 10377 (Mu'ammal ibn Hisham, from Isma'il ibn Ibrahim, from Hisham, from Abu al-Zubayr, from Jabir). Report 10378 (Amr ibn Abd al-Hamid, from Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd al-Samad, from Mansur, from Mujahid, from Abu Ayyash al-Zuraqi): we were with the Prophet at Usfan and he led us in the noon prayer, with Khalid ibn al-Walid in command of the polytheists, who said they had caught the Muslims unawares and heedless; God sent down the prayer of fear between noon and afternoon, and he led the afternoon prayer with us divided into two groups, one praying with the Prophet and the other behind them keeping guard; he said the takbir and all said it and bowed together, then those next to him prostrated, he stood, the others advanced and prostrated, he stood and they all bowed, and he prostrated with those next to him until these withdrew to their companions' lines, then the others advanced and prostrated and he gave the salam to all; so all had two rak'as with their imam; and he prayed it once more in the land of Banu Sulaym. In the notes the editor says that the word rendered "blamed one another" means they reproached each other for missing the opportunity, though some take it as urging one another to fight, and he judges the first meaning better; that the Durr has a different word which he does not follow; that Ibn al-Athir and the Lisan give the word in the prayer-of-fear report; that "the two of them" in 10375 means the Messenger of God and Gabriel; that Yahya ibn Salih al-Wuhazi was a reliable Syrian who died in 222, that Ibn Ayyash is Isma'il ibn Ayyash al-Ansi, a reliable memoriser of whom people spoke, that Ubayd Allah ibn Amr al-Raqqi (Abu Wahb) is wrongly given as "Ubayd Allah ibn Umar" in both printed text and manuscript since it is he who narrates from Abu al-Zubayr, and that Abu al-Zubayr is Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn Tadrus; that al-Tabari gives the report by three chains, that Abu al-Zubayr's report from Jabir is in Muslim and al-Nasa'i and alluded to by al-Bukhari, and that Ibn Hajar discussed it at length, the versions in Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi and al-Bayhaqi being abridged and Ibn Jarir's the long one, while al-Suyuti limited himself to Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn Jarir; that Amr ibn Abd al-Hamid al-Amuli, a teacher of al-Tabari who came earlier at 3759, was said by his brother Ahmad Shakir there to be unknown and perhaps corrupted, which the editor says cannot stand because he appears here too and al-Tabari narrates from him in the History, so he is not a corruption; that Abu Ayyash al-Zuraqi's report came earlier by Mansur from Mujahid in three chains (10323, 10324) and that the route of Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd al-Samad is in al-Nasa'i; that the printed text had an awkward phrase "afternoon prayer, meaning two groups" so he added the bracketed words from al-Nasa'i; and that this report is not found in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 159}

**The interpretation on this view, then al-Tabari's choice (pp. 159-162, note 1 on p. 160 and notes 1-3 on p. 161).** Al-Tabari says that on the view of those who gave these reports the sense of the verse is: when you, Muhammad, are among your companions in fear and establish the prayer for them, let a group of those who joined your prayer stand with you; when this group prostrates with your prostration and raises its heads, let those behind you go behind the group that guarded you and them when you prostrated with them; and the second group is the guarding group that prayed with him but did not prostrate with his prostration, so that on their reading the denial that they had prayed means that they had not prostrated with you; let them prostrate with your prostration when you prostrate, with those who prostrated with you in the first rak'a guarding you and them, and the taking of caution and arms is for the guarding group. The editor notes that the manuscript has a conjunction before each of two words which the printed text changed, which he judges correct. Al-Tabari then states his choice: the most fitting interpretation is the view that the sense is: when the group that stood with you in its prayer has prostrated, let them be behind you and behind those who enter your prayer who have not prayed the first rak'a with you facing the enemy, after finishing the rest of its prayer; and let the other group, which was facing the enemy and had not prayed the first rak'a with you, come and pray with you the rak'a that remains for you, taking its caution and arms to fight its enemy after finishing its prayer. He says this matches the report of what the Prophet did on the day of Dhat al-Riqa' and Sahl ibn Abi Hathma's report (the editor notes he means reports 10345 and 10346-10353). His argument is that he has already shown that establishing the prayer is completing it with its bowing and prostration, and that the permission to shorten in the previous verse is only to shorten its bowing and prostration in severe fear; if that is sound, there is no ground for the interpretation that the first group, once it has prostrated with the imam, has finished its prayer, because the word for prostrating can carry the meanings he mentioned, and because nothing in the verse shows that the shortening of the previous verse was a shortening of the number of rak'as. Since that has no ground, the view that the intent is advancing and retiring in prayer as in the Prophet's prayer at Usfan is further still, because both groups had prayed the Prophet's first rak'a at Usfan and it cannot be that those who prayed with him are those described as not having prayed; and if anyone supposes that "not prayed" means "not prostrated", that is not the apparent, understood sense of "prayer", and the meanings of God's words are directed to their most apparent and well-known senses unless something that must be accepted prevents it. Since the verse contains no command for the first group to postpone making up what remains until the imam finishes his prayer, and no harm to the Muslims facing the enemy from its being busy with it, there is no meaning in commanding it to postpone and depart before making up the rest. He adds, nonetheless, that any imam who prays it in a manner matching one of the forms he has reported from the Prophet has prayed validly and completely, because the reports of all of them are sound, and they are among the matters the Prophet taught his community and then allowed them to act on whichever they wished. This is al-Tabari's own choice and argument. The editor notes that the printed text drops the conjunction from "and after" where the manuscript is right, that "further still" is the predicate of "the view of those who said", that the printed text differs from the manuscript in the following sentence, and that the clause on the Muslims facing the enemy is joined to the clause on there being no command in the verse, which he explains grammatically. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 162}

**Explanation of the clause on what the unbelievers wished (p. 162).** Al-Tabari says the clause means that the unbelievers wished the Muslims would be heedless of their arms and baggage, that is, busy with their prayer away from the weapons with which they fight them and the baggage on which they depend on their journeys, and so forget them, so that the unbelievers could fall on them in a single attack while they were occupied, catch them unawares, kill them and take their camp. He says God forbids them to do that after this, all becoming occupied with their prayer when its time comes while facing the enemy and giving the enemy mastery over themselves, their weapons and their baggage; rather they should establish the prayer as God has explained and take from their enemy their caution and arms. The lower part of this page's notes is too damaged in the digital text to read; they appear to refer to earlier explanations of the verbs for wishing and for being heedless in earlier volumes and to correct an error in the printed text in the word for those facing the enemy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 162}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
