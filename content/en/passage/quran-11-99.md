**Commentary on verse 99 (pp. 467-470)**

On [[passage/quran-11-99|11:99]] God says: He made them followed in this, meaning this world, along with the punishment He hastened for them in it, drowning in the sea, by a curse, 'and on the Day of Resurrection', that is, they will be cursed again on the Day of Resurrection with another curse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 467}

Reports 18536 to 18540, from Mujahid: another curse, added to His curse another curse, so they are two curses, a curse after a curse, 'in this' being in the world, 'and on the Day of Resurrection' they were joined with another. As for 'wretched is the gift given', it means wretched is the help helped, the curse to which another like it is added. The root of 'rifd' is help; one says someone gave 'rafd' to another with the governor, with kasra; with fatha it is a large cup. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 468}

Al-Tabari cites a verse of al-A'sha on 'rifd' for a large cup, and the editor explains the occasion in his praise of al-Aswad b. al-Mundhir al-Lakhmi, the captives, and the word 'aqtal'. One says he propped his wall, 'rafada', when he supported it with timber so it should not fall; 'rafd' with fatha is the verbal noun, and 'rifd' the name of what a person is given, which is the 'marfad'. Report 18541, from Ibn Abbas: the curse of this world and the next; 18542, from Qatada: God cursed them in this world and added to it the curse in the next; 18543, from Qatada, the same sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 469}

Report 18544, from Qatada: the two curses from God came one after the other, a curse in this world and a curse in the next; 18545, from al-Dahhak: the two curses struck them in this world, one reinforcing the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 470}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
