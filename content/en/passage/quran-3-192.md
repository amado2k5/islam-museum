## Summary
Verse 192 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 192 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 192 (pp. 477-479).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. The first view: the meaning is that whomever You enter into the Fire from Your servants and make abide in it, You have disgraced; they said a believer whose end is Paradise is not disgraced even if he is punished with some punishment. Anas (8356), through Abu Hafs al-Jubayri (as printed) and Muhammad ibn Bashshar from al-Mu'ammal from Abu Hilal from Qatada: it means one made to abide. The editor's note says he did not find "Abu Hafs al-Jubayri", that the person al-Tabari narrates from is 'Amr ibn 'Ali al-Fallas Abu Hafs al-Sayrafi, that the word is undotted in the manuscript so he cannot tell whether to read al-Jubayri or al-Khaybari, and that 'Amr ibn 'Ali narrates from al-Mu'ammal ibn Isma'il, as in several places such as reports 1885, 1891 and 1898. Ibn al-Musayyab (8357), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from a man: it is specific to those who do not leave it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 477}

**The exchange of al-Ash'ath with al-Hasan (pp. 477-478, report 8358).** Al-Muthanna, through Abu al-Nu'man 'Arim from Hammad ibn Zayd from Qubaysa ibn Marwan from al-Ash'ath al-Humdi (in the text): I said to al-Hasan, Abu Sa'id, what you mention of intercession, is it true? He said yes, true. I said: and what of the verse together with another, [[passage/quran-5-37|5:37]], which says they want to leave the Fire and are not leaving it? He said: by God, you will not overpower me with anything; the Fire has people who do not leave it, as God said. I said: and those who entered and then left? He said: they had committed sins in this world and God seized them for those, entered them, then brought them out for the faith and belief in Him He knows in their hearts. The editor's notes say that the conjunction between the two verses was dropped in the printed text and manuscript and should be restored as the context shows; that the printed text has a useless wording which the publishers changed because they did not understand it, and the manuscript's is right, "you will not overpower me" meaning you will not bring against me an argument that overcomes me, from the root of assault and force; that Qubaysa ibn Marwan ibn al-Muhallab was narrated from by Hammad ibn Zayd and has entries in al-Bukhari's al-Kabir (4/1/177) and Ibn Abi Hatim (3/2/125); and that al-Ash'ath is named after his grandfather and is al-Ash'ath ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Jabir al-Hudani, the blind, also called al-Azdi, who narrated from Anas, al-Hasan and Ibn Sirin, and from whom Shu'ba, Hammad ibn Salama and Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan narrated. Ibn Jurayj (8359), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: it is the one made to abide in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 478}

**The second view (pp. 478-479, report 8360).** Others said the meaning is that whomever He enters into the Fire, whether made to abide or not, has been disgraced by the punishment. Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah (8360), through al-Muthanna from Ishaq from al-Harith ibn Muslim from Bahr from 'Amr ibn Dinar: Jabir came to us on an 'umra, and 'Ata' and I went to him and asked about the verse; he said: what disgrace is greater than his being burned in the Fire! And there is surely disgrace short of that. The editor's note says al-Harith ibn Muslim al-Razi and Bahr al-Saqqa' (Bahr ibn Kaniz al-Bahili) came earlier at report 8097, and that the printed text and manuscript have a plainly mistaken form of the chain; that al-Hakim reports it in the Mustadrak (2: 300) without comment, and al-Dhahabi remarked "Bahr is ruined"; that al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 111) attributes it to al-Hakim and Ibn Jarir in a fuller wording, in which Jabir, asked about their not leaving the Fire, said the Prophet told him they are the unbelievers; and that at the end of the report the manuscript and printed text each have a form which the editor corrected from the other and from the wording of al-Hakim and al-Suyuti, "how disgraced he is" being an exclamation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 479}

**Al-Tabari's choice and the wrongdoers (p. 479).** Al-Tabari says the better of the two views in his judgement is Jabir's, that whoever is entered into the Fire has been disgraced by entering it even if brought out, because disgrace is the tearing of the veil of the disgraced and exposing him, and whoever his Lord punishes in the hereafter for his sins He has exposed by punishing him, and that is disgrace (the editor refers to his explanation at 2: 314, 525). The last clause means, for him, that one who opposes God's command and disobeys has no helper to help him against God, to ward off His punishment or rescue him from His torment. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 479}
