## Summary
Verse 138 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/sacred-livestock-jahiliyya|the sacred livestock of the Jahiliyya (bahira, sa’iba, wasila, ham)]], and [[concept/ibadat-al-asnam-jahiliyya|idol worship in the Jahiliyya and its rites]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 138 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 138, "they said: these cattle and crops are forbidden; none may eat them except whom we will, by their claim", al-Tabari says this is God's report of those ignorant idolaters, that they forbade and permitted from themselves, without God having permitted them anything of it; these who set up equals to their Lord said, out of ignorance, of some cattle and a crop of theirs: these cattle and this crop are forbidden, meaning by cattle and crop what they had assigned to God and to their gods, as mentioned above. It was also said that the cattle are the sa'iba, wasila and bahira that they named; he gives (13914) Mujahid: the cattle are the sa'iba and bahira they named. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 139}

"Hijr" in Arabic usage means forbidden; one says "I made so-and-so hijr for so-and-so", meaning forbade it to him, as in God's "they say: a barrier barred" (25:22), and in the line of al-Mutalammis, in which he tells his she-camel, longing for the farthest palm-grove, that it is forbidden to her. The editor's long note explains the line and its variants, says its author is Jarir ibn Abd al-Masih, who spoke it in his flight from Amr ibn Hind, that "the farthest palm-grove" is, on the evidence of the poem, in the land of Iraq, and that "daharis" means calamities. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 140}

Al-Tabari cites for "hijr" in the sense of forbidden the saying of al-Ajjaj, "and the neighbour woman of the house has my protection" (he ascribes it to Ru'ba, which the editor corrects to Ru'ba's father al-Ajjaj), and another line ascribed to A'sha of Bahila, "I spent the night leaning on my elbow with wakeful eye, as if sleep were forbidden to me at night", meaning forbidden. One says "hijr" and "hujr", with kasra or damma on the ha'; al-Hasan and Qatada read it with damma, and he gives (13915) Qatada reading "wa harthun hujr" with damma. The notes correct the name al-Hasan and discuss a difficulty in the chain of this report, in which the editor suggests dropping the phrase "my uncle told me". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 141}

The readers of the Hijaz, Iraq and Syria read it with kasra, the reading al-Tabari does not allow anyone to depart from, since the authoritative readers agree on it and it is the best dialect of the Arabs. It is also reported that Ibn Abbas read "wa harthun hirj", with the ra' before the jim (13916), a third dialect with the same sense as "hijr", as they say "jadhaba" and "jabadha", "na'a" and "na'a". So "hijr" has three forms: "hijr" with kasra, "hujr" with damma, and "hirj" with kasra and the ra' before the jim. The note explains "judha" as the feminine of "ajwad" and its rare use after al-Tabari. Then the commentators spoke along the lines he gave on the meaning of "hijr". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 142}

He gives the reports: 13917, from Mujahid and Abu Amr, forbidden; 13918, from Ibn Abbas, that "hijr" is what they forbade of the wasila; 13919, from Qatada, forbidden; 13920, from Qatada, a prohibition laid on them by the devils over their property, a hardship and severity, which came from the devils and not from God; 13921, from al-Suddi, they say it is forbidden for us to feed any but whom we will; 13922, from Ibn Zayd, we fence it off from whom we wish, and they fenced it off for their gods, saying we fence it from the women and keep it for the men; 13923, from al-Dahhak, that in pre-Islamic times they did things God had not commanded, forbidding some of their livestock which they did not eat, and setting aside a known part of their crops for their gods, saying what we named for our gods is not lawful to us; and 13924 and 13925, from Mujahid, what they assigned to God and to their partners. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 143}

Then "and cattle whose backs are forbidden, and cattle over which they do not mention God's name, forging a lie against Him; He will repay them for what they used to forge". Al-Tabari says these ignorant idolaters forbade the backs of some of their cattle, so they did not ride them, while benefiting from their milk, their young and everything else from them except their backs for riding (the note explains "rasl" as milk and "nitaj" as what they bear), and forbade other cattle of theirs, so they did not make pilgrimage on them, nor mention God's name over them whether they rode them, milked them or loaded them. He gives (13926) Abu Wa'il: cattle on which they do not make pilgrimage; and (13927) and (13928) the same, the bahira on which they did not make pilgrimage. The notes give biographies of the transmitters in these chains, some of whom the editor could not identify. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 144}

He gives (13929) al-Suddi: "cattle whose backs are forbidden" are the bahira, sa'iba and hami, and "cattle over which they do not mention God's name" are those they would not name God over when they bred them or slaughtered them (the editor thinks the right wording may differ); (13930) Mujahid: some of their camels were ones over which they did not mention God's name, nor in anything about them, not when they rode them, nor when they milked, loaded, lent them, or did any work; and (13931) Ibn Zayd: no one rides them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 145}

As for "forging a lie against Him", he says these idolaters did what they did of forbidding what they forbade and said what they said as a lie against God and a baseless guess about Him, for they attributed what they forbade, as described in His Book, to God as the one who forbade it; God denied that of Himself, called them liars, and told His Prophet and the believers that they lie in what they claim. Then He says "He will repay them": their Lord will repay them for the lie they forged against God what they deserve, and will give them their recompense for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 146}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
