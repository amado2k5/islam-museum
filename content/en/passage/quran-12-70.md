**Commentary on verse 70 (pp. 171-174)**

On "and when he had provided them with their provisions he put the drinking cup in his brother's saddlebag; then a caller cried out, O caravan, you are thieves", al-Tabari says: when Joseph loaded his brothers' camels with the provisions he loaded and met their need, report 19510, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, begins at the end of the page. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "tajhiz" and "jahaz". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 171}

Report 19510, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "when he had provided them with their provisions" means when he had met their need and given them full measure. On "he put the drinking cup in his brother's saddlebag", he says: he put the vessel with which he measured the food into his brother's baggage; "siqaya" is the drinking vessel, the vessel from which the king drank and with which he measured food. The interpreters said the same: report 19511, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Affan, Abd al-Wahid and Yunus: "suwa'" and "siqaya" are the same, the vessel one drinks from. Reports 19512 and 19513, from Mujahid: "siqaya" and "suwa'" are one thing, from which Joseph drank. Reports 19514 and 19515, from Qatada: the king's drinking vessel. Report 19516, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: it is the "siqaya" from which the king drank, meaning his makkuk (a measuring and drinking vessel). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 172}

Report 19517, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they are one thing, from which Joseph drank. Report 19518, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: it is the vessel from which the king drank. Report 19519, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "siqaya" is "suwa'", and it was a cup of gold, as they report. "In his brother's saddlebag" means in the baggage of his brother by his father and mother, Benjamin; report 19520, from Qatada: in his brother's baggage. "Then a caller called" means a crier cried out, and it is also said an announcer announced; "O caravan" is the convoy carrying loads, "you are thieves". The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "rahl" and "adhdhana". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 173}

Report 19521, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: he put the cup in his brother's baggage without the brother knowing; when they set out, a caller cried before the caravan left: you are thieves. Report 19522, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: he then provided them, honoured them, gave them and gave full measure, loaded a camel for each, and loaded a camel for his brother by name as for them; then he ordered the king's cup, which is the "suwa'", reported to have been of silver, and it was put in the baggage of his brother Benjamin. He let them go until they had gone well out of the town, then ordered that they be overtaken and held, and a caller called, O caravan, you are thieves, stop. His messenger reached them and said, as they report: did we not honour your hospitality, give you full measure, treat your lodging well, and do for you what we did not do for others, and bring you into our houses and dwellings? They said: yes, what is it? He said: we have lost the king's cup and suspect none but you. They answered as the next verse gives, [[passage/quran-12-73|12:73]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 174}

Al-Tabari has already explained the meaning of "al-'ir", a plural with no singular of its own form, and the editor refers to the earlier place. It is related from Mujahid that the caravan of Ya'qub's sons was made up of donkeys: report 19523, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, Sufyan and Ibn Jurayj, and report 19524, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Sufyan and a man, from Mujahid: the caravan was donkeys. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 174}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
