## Summary
Verse 128 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[person/sawda-bint-zama|Sawda bint Zam'a]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:nisa-hawl-alrasul}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 128 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 128, the first clause: a wife who fears ill-treatment (pp. 267-268, notes on pp. 267-268).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that if a woman fears from her husband, meaning she knows it of him, a withdrawal, meaning his raising himself above her to another and preferring someone else, whether from dislike or from displeasure at something about her, such as plainness or age, or a turning away from her with his face or with some of the benefits she had from him, there is no blame on the two of them, that is, on the woman who fears this, if they make a settlement between them. That settlement is that she gives up her day to him, or lowers some of what is owed to her, seeking his kindness and to remain in his marriage and in the contract between them; the settlement by giving up part of the right to keep the bond and the marriage, he says, is better than seeking separation and divorce. He says the interpreters said the same. The editor notes the earlier explanations of the words for the husband, the verb of fearing as knowing, the withdrawal, the turning away and blame, that the print had a frightening reading where the manuscript was not understood, and explains that the word for causes originally means a rope and was later used for whatever links to something. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 268}

Under report 10575, through Hannad, Abu al-Ahwas, Simak and Khalid ibn Arara, a man asks Ali about a woman who fears her husband's ill-treatment or aversion. Ali says a wife may find her husband's eyes turn from her because of her plainness, age, bad temper or poverty, so she dislikes parting from him. If she gives him part of her dower, it is lawful for him; if she gives him some of her days, there is no blame. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 268}

Reports 10576 to 10578 give the same answer from Ali through Shu'ba, Hammad ibn Salama, Abu al-Ahwas and Israil. They name the older wife, the plain one, or one her husband does not love; they reconcile, and if she gives him some of her days or wealth, there is no blame on him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 269}

In report 10579, through Ibn Sirin, a man asks Umar about the verse; Umar dislikes the question and strikes him with a whip. A second questioner is told that the verse concerns a husband advanced in years who wants a younger wife for children, and that whatever they agree upon is permitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 269}

Reports 10580 and 10581, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibn Abbas, describe a wife who stays with her husband until she grows old, while he wants to marry another. They reconcile on her having one day and the other wife two or three; a variant gives one night and two, until she bears children or grows old. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 270}

Reports 10582 and 10583 give two more cases from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibn Abbas: a long-married wife grown older whom her husband wants to replace, and a husband who wants to divorce a wife who objects. He proposes to marry another, saying he cannot treat them equally, and she agrees to one day of her own. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 270}

Reports 10584 to 10586 give Aisha's account through Hisham ibn Urwa and his father. One of a man's two wives has grown weak or plain and he does not multiply his visits to her; she tells him not to divorce her and that he is free of her share. Another version adds that she said, 'I make you free of my share,' and the verse was revealed about this. The editor notes that the printed edition altered the wording here, restores the manuscript reading, and compares the parallel versions in al-Bukhari and Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 271}

Report 10587, from Ibn Abbas through Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha, describes a husband who prefers another wife to a wife he does not favour. God commands him to offer her a choice: remain on these terms with his support and provision, or be released. If she chooses to stay after the choice, there is no blame, and this is the meaning of 'reconciliation is better.' Report 10588, from Aisha through Ibn Abi al-Zinad and Hisham, says the verse was revealed about a woman who has grown old and gives her day to another wife. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 272}

Report 10589, from Ubayda through Ibn Sirin, describes a wife whose husband wants to marry another; she reconciles from her day, and they keep what they agree. If the arrangement breaks down, he must be just between them or divorce her. Reports 10590 and 10591 attribute the same view to Ibrahim and Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 273}

Reports 10592 to 10594, from Ubayda through Ayyub, Ibn Sirin, Muhammad and Hisham, say that he may reconcile on what she accepts short of her full right. If she objects or says he has been unfair, he must be just, satisfy her, or divorce her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 273}

Report 10595 is absent from the manuscript, as the editor notes. Ibrahim says that if she wishes she keeps her right, and if she refuses reconciliation the choice is hers: he may divorce her or keep her with her right. Report 10596, from Ali through Mughira and Ibrahim, describes a wife whose husband has been with her a long time and who fears divorce; they agree on arrangements for nights with her and with another wife, and a lower maintenance, and whatever they agree is permitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 274}

Report 10597, from al-Hakam, says that if a wife fears her husband wants to release her, they may reconcile, and she gives up some of her days if he marries. Report 10598, from Ibn Abbas through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, uncle and grandfather, describes a husband with an older wife who marries a younger one, dislikes parting from the mother of his child, and reconciles by giving her some of his wealth and himself, which pleases him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 274}

Report 10599, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid, Sa'id and Qatada: this concerns a man whose wife has passed her youth, and whose regard for her has lessened. He says to her, "If you are content with me in person and wealth with less than you were content with before today," then if they agree on a settlement it is lawful for them; if she refuses, he may not keep her in humiliation. The editor notes that the answer of the conditional is left out, meaning: if you are content with that, so be it; otherwise I will part from you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 274}

Report 10600, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar and al-Zuhri, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Sulayman ibn Yasar, tells the story of Rafi' ibn Khadij. He had a wife who had passed her youth, then married a young woman and favoured her. He divorced his first wife once; near the end of her waiting period he offered to take her back if she would bear the favouring, or to leave her until her term ended. She chose return. When he favoured the younger wife again she would not bear it, so he divorced her a second time and kept favouring the young one. He said that this is the reconciliation which God is reported to have sent down. A variant through Ibn Sirin and Ubayda adds that if he wrongs her a third time he must give her her right or divorce her. The editor notes that al-Hakim gave this in al-Mustadrak as a report traced to Rafi', judged sound by the criteria of al-Bukhari and Muslim; that al-Bayhaqi has another route; and that Malik has a version close to Ma'mar's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 275}

Reports 10601 to 10603 concern an older wife or one her husband does not love. Through Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid, a man says to his wife: you are old, and I want to replace you with a young and beautiful woman; stay with your son and I will not give you any of my time. That is the reconciliation between them, and the editor identifies the speaker as Abu al-Sanabil ibn Ba'kak. Report 10602, through Shibl, gives the same and adds that if she accepts this, there is no blame on him. Report 10603, through Jabir from al-Sha'bi, gives a wife who asks her husband, who wants to divorce her, not to divorce her and to give her one day and his new wife two; al-Sha'bi says there is no harm in it, it is a reconciliation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 276}

Report 10604, from al-Suddi, describes a wife who grows old or does not bear children, whose husband wants to marry another. He comes to her and says he wishes to marry a younger woman, who may bear him children, and he will favour her with days and maintenance; if she agrees, well, otherwise he divorces her; and they settle on what they both wish. The editor notes that both print and manuscript read "more fitting" (anasab) here, a misreading, and the correct word is "younger" (ashabb). Report 10605, from Ibn Zayd through Ibn Wahb, explains nushuz as lifting oneself above the wife and turning away from her in dislike; it refers to a man with two wives, and the reconciliation is either that she appeases him so that he releases her right, or that she wins him back to her side. Report 10606, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha and Mu'awiya ibn Salih, says that the turning away here means dislike. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 277}

Report 10607, from al-Dahhak, applies the verse to a man with an older wife who marries a younger one and leans towards her; he settles with the older wife by giving her some of his wealth and a known share of himself. Report 10608, from Ibn Abbas through Sulayman ibn Mu'adh, Simak and Ikrima, says Sawda feared that the Prophet would divorce her; she said, do not divorce me, keep me, and give my day to A'isha. He did so, and the verse was revealed. The editor notes that the wording of this report differs from the versions in Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Bayhaqi, which agree that whatever they settle on is lawful, and says he does not know where the difference came from. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 277}

The readers differ over the verb of the settlement between the spouses. The majority of the readers of Medina and some of Basra read it with a fatha on the first letter and a shadda on the sad, meaning that they make peace with each other; the t is then assimilated into the s. The majority of the readers of Kufa read it with a damma and without a shadda, meaning that the husband and the wife reconcile between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 278}

Al-Tabari prefers the reading with the fatha and the shadda, because making peace (tasalluh) is in this place better known, clearer in meaning, and more common on Arab tongues than reconciling (islah). He answers those who argue for the damma reading from the word for settlement: settlement is a noun and not a verb, so it cannot be used to decide between the two readings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 279}

**Commentary on verse 128, the second clause: the soul's readiness for avarice (pp. 279-280)**

Reports 10609 to 10614 explain the soul's being present to avarice. Through Ibn Abbas, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, it is her share of it; through Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Sufyan and Ata, it is about days; through Ata, from Ibn Juraij, Ibn Mahdi and Sufyan, it is about days and maintenance, while in other reports from Ata it is about maintenance alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 279}

Reports 10615 to 10617, through Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Abu Bishr, Shu'ba and Ibn al-Mubarak, say that the wife's soul is eager for her share from her husband, of his self and his wealth. Report 10618, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr through a man and Sufyan, says it concerns maintenance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 280}

Reports 10619 to 10621, through Ibn Mahdi, Sufyan, al-Shaybani and Bukayr ibn al-Akhnas from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, say the avarice concerns days and maintenance (10619 and 10620); report 10621, through Shu'ba and Abu Bishr, says the wife is greedy for her husband's wealth and person. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 281}

Report 10622, through Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Sharik and Salim, says that when the verse on a wife who fears ill-treatment was revealed, a woman said, 'I want you to share yourself with me,' though she had accepted that he leave her, neither divorcing her nor visiting her; so God sent down the verse on avarice. Report 10623, from al-Suddi, says the soul reaches out to the husband and his maintenance, and claims the verse was revealed about the Prophet and Sawda bint Zam'a: she had grown old and he wished to divorce her; they agreed that he keep her and give her day to A'isha, and she was stingy of her place with him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 281}

Others say that the soul of each, husband and wife, is stingy with its right before the other. Report 10624, from Ibn Zayd through Ibn Wahb, says his soul is not content to give her anything and so release him, and her soul is not content to give him anything of her wealth and so win him to herself. Report 10625, from Ibn Abbas through Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha, says that avarice is the desire for a thing one is eager for. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 282}

Al-Tabari prefers that the verse means that the souls of women are present with avarice for their shares from their husbands, in days and in maintenance. Avarice (shuh) is excess of eagerness for a thing; here it is the woman's excessive eagerness for her share of his days and her maintenance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 282}

He rejects the view that both husband and wife are meant. A husband's settlement that buys off his wife's turn with a gift from his wealth is not lawful: a reward (ju'l) must be against something of value, a thing or a benefit, and the husband gets neither from forgoing her turn, so it falls under eating wealth falsely. He meets an objection from pre-emption, where a claimant's right can be bought off, by pointing to the agreement that a settlement for a consideration in pre-emption is invalid, which shows that the husband's settlement for a consideration to forgo her turn is invalid too. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 282}

Once that view fails, the sense of the verse is as he states: the souls of women are present with avarice for their rights from their husbands. The report of Rafi' ibn Khadij from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Sulayman ibn Yasar (10600) makes this clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 283}

On the closing words: if you men do good to your wives, when you dislike their plainness, their character or their hurt, by bearing with them, giving them their rights, and living with them honourably; and fear God by not wronging them in what is due to a wife you dislike, in division, maintenance and honourable company. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 283}

Al-Tabari glosses the word for aware (khabir) as the knowing one, from whom nothing is hidden: God knows what you do regarding your wives, good or wrong, and will repay each of you for it, the doer of good for his good and the doer of ill for his ill. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 284}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
