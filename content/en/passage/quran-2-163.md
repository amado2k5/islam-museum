## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and your God is one God; there is no god but Him, the Merciful, the Compassionate" al-Tabari says godhood is the worship of creation, and the one who deserves obedience and worship from people is one object of worship and one Lord, so none other is to be worshipped or associated with Him, since whoever is associated is a creature like them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 265} He reports that the interpreters differed on the meaning of His oneness. Some said it is the negation of likenesses and equals, as in "so-and-so is the one of his people", and argued that "one" is understood in four senses: one of a kind, undivided like the indivisible part, likeness or agreement, and the negation of an equal; since the first three are excluded from Him, the fourth stands. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 265} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 266} Others said it means His being apart from things and things apart from Him, in that He does not enter anything and nothing enters Him, and they denied the four senses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 266} "There is no god but Him" he takes as a report that there is no lord of the worlds but Him and none else deserves worship, that all else is His creation, and that blessings in this world and the next come from Him while what they associate with Him neither harms nor benefits; this is a warning to the polytheists about their error and a call to return. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 266}
