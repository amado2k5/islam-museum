## Summary
Verse 51 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 51 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The report of Ibn Ishaq.** Report 7119 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says this statement is a disavowal of what the Christians say about him and an argument for his Lord against them, and that the straight road means what he carried them on and brought them. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira (2:231) and notes that it belongs to the rest of the reports whose last was number 7115. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 441}

**The readings.** Al-Tabari says most readers of the regions read the word that opens the clause with a kasra on its first letter, as the start of a report, while some read it with a fatha, so that the clause stands in apposition to the proof just mentioned and is attached to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 441} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 442}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says the sound reading in his view is that of the readers of the regions, the kasra as a new start, because the authoritative readers agree on its soundness; what they agree on is proof, what a lone reader departs with is an opinion, and an opinion is not set against a proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 442}

**The verse as an argument against the Najran delegation.** Al-Tabari says the verse is apparently a report, yet it holds God's conclusive proof for His messenger Muhammad against the delegation of the people of Najran who disputed with him, since God informs that Jesus was free of what was ascribed to him by those who ascribed to him something other than what he said of himself, namely that he is a servant of God like all God's servants on earth, apart from the prophethood and the proofs God singled him out with, as a sign of his truthfulness, as the other messengers were given signs and proofs, and a proof of his prophethood. The editor notes that the printed edition has a wording meaning 'their prophethood', that he kept the manuscript's 'his prophethood', and that the phrase is coordinated with the one before it and the pronoun refers to Jesus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 442}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
