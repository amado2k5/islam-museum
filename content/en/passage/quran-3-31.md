## Verse 31 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 322-325)

**The commentators differ on the occasion.** Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-31|3:31]]) says the commentators differed on the occasion. Some said it came down about a people who said in the Prophet's time that they loved their Lord, and the Prophet was told to say to them: if you are truthful, follow me, for that is the sign of your truth. Al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah, from Bakr ibn al-Aswad, from al-Hasan (6845) says that a people said, 'Muhammad, we love our Lord', and the verse was sent down, making the following of His prophet the sign of love of God and the penalty for whoever disagreed. Al-Muthanna, from Ali ibn al-Haytham, from Abd al-Wahhab, from Abu Ubayda, from al-Hasan (6846) is similar; the editor says al-Tabari will shortly state why he finds these two reports weak. Al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj (6847) says a people claimed to love God, and He ordered them to follow Muhammad and made that the sign of their love. Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan (6848) says God wished to make their claim confirmed by deed, so following Muhammad confirmed it; the editor notes that the manuscript has an alternative form and the printed text is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 323}

**The second view: an address to the Najran delegation.** Others said it is God's command to His prophet to say to the Najran delegation of Christians: if what you say about Jesus, great as it is, you say out of honouring God and loving Him, then follow me. Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (6849), glosses: if this is your saying, meaning about Jesus, out of love and honour for God, then follow me and God will love you and forgive what is past of your unbelief. The editor says the words in dashes are al-Tabari's interpolation, that Ibn Hisham's text adds a word 'truly' which he fears al-Tabari's copyist dropped, and that this report is the remainder of those ending at 6824. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 323}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says the better of the two views is that of Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr, because nothing in this sura, before this verse, mentions a people who claimed to love or honour God other than the Najran delegation, so that the saying would be an answer to them, as al-Hasan said. As for al-Hasan's report, he says no sound report establishes it for him, unless al-Hasan meant by the people the Najran delegation, in which case it agrees with his own choice. Where there is neither a report nor an indication in the verse, he finds it best to join its interpretation to what the verses of the sura indicate, since what comes before and after is an account of them and an argument against their saying about the Messiah. So the sense in his view is: say, Muhammad, to the delegation of Najran: if you are as you claim, loving God and honouring the Messiah out of love for your Lord, then make your saying true by following me, for you know I am God's messenger to you as Jesus was to those he was sent to; if you follow me and believe what I bring, He forgives your sins, spares you the punishment and pardons what is past, for He is forgiving to His believing servants and merciful to them and to others. This is al-Tabari's choice, given as his; the editor corrects a reading of the manuscript in one place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 324}
