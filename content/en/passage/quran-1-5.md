## In al-Tabari's commentary
Under "the interpretation of 'You alone we worship'" al-Tabari says it means: to You, O God, we are humble, low and submissive, in acknowledgement that lordship is Yours and no one else's. He reports from Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak that Gabriel told Muhammad to say "You alone we worship", meaning "You alone we declare one, fear and hope for, our Lord, and no other"; the editor notes the chain is weak, as before. He explains that he chose to explain it by humility, lowliness and submission and not by hope and fear, although hope and fear exist only with lowliness, because servitude among the Arabs rests on lowliness: they call a path made smooth by the tread of feet "mu'abbad", as Tarafa ibn al-Abd does in describing his she-camel, a camel made docile by being ridden is "mu'abbad", and the servant is called "abd" for his lowliness before his master. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 160} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 161}
Under "the interpretation of 'and You alone we ask for help'" he says it means: You alone, our Lord, we ask for help in our worship of You and our obedience in all our affairs, and no one else, since whoever disbelieves in You asks the idols he worships for help in his affairs, while we ask You, sincere to You in worship. He gives the same report from Ibn Abbas through the weak chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 161} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 162}
He raises an objection: what sense is there in God commanding His servants to ask Him for help in obeying Him? Could He command them to obey and not help them? And does anyone say "we ask You for help in obeying You" without being helped? His answer is that a believer asking his Lord to help him obey is asking for help in the rest of his life with what he has been charged, not with the good deeds already done; and that God's giving this, together with the ability of the limbs to carry out His charge, is a favour and kindness from Him, and there is neither a fault in management nor injustice in His leaving some servants without success while they are busy with disobedience and in extending His favour to others who strive for His love and hasten to obey Him. He then says that God's command to His servants to ask for help in worship is the clearest proof against those who hold "delegation" among the people of qadar; the editor notes these are the Qadariyya, the Mu'tazila and the Imamiyya, and al-Tabari continues his reply to them on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 162}

We stop here at the foot of p. 162, at the opening of al-Tabari's reply to those who hold "delegation"; the next batch begins at p. 163. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 162}
