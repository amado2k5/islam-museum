## Summary
Verse 245 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 245 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-245|2:245]]) begins on p. 282 with al-Tabari saying the question means: who is it who spends in God's way and so strengthens a weak one or helps one who wants to fight and gives to the needy? This is the loan a servant lends his Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 282} God calls it a loan because a loan in the language is giving another one's property for him to repay its like when it is claimed; as those who gave to the needy in God's way did so seeking the reward He promised, it is called that, and good because the giver gives at God's call and urging, in hope of reward, so it is obedience to God and disobedience to Satan; God has no need of anyone, but it is like the Arabs' saying, I have a loan of good or of evil for you. He cites a verse of Umayya b. Abi al-Salt and the verse of al-Baqara about the grain that grows seven ears ([[passage/quran-2-261|2:261]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 282} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 283} The editor explains how he read the manuscript's wording and the versions of the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 283}

Reports: Ibn Zayd, that this is in God's way and the multiplying is seven hundred for one (5617); Zayd b. Aslam, that when the verse came down Ibn al-Dahdah came to the Prophet and said he had two plots, one in the upland and one in the lowland, and had made the better one a gift; the Prophet said, how many a laden date palm there is for Ibn al-Dahdah in Paradise (5618). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 283} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 284} In the margin the editor says this report is a mursal, weak in its chain since Zayd b. Aslam was a Successor who did not name who told him, and gives its sources and explains the words for palm and laden; that is his judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 284} Qatada (5619): a man in the Prophet's time on hearing the verse gave away his best orchard, and Qatada said God asks His servants for a loan as you hear, He being the Patron and Praised; the editor weakens the report as mursal, and says its end is Qatada's own words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 284} Ibn Mas'ud (5620): when the verse came down Abu al-Dahdah asked the Prophet whether God wants a loan from us; he said yes; he took his hand and said he had lent his Lord an orchard of six hundred palms, then told Umm al-Dahdah, who left it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 284} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 285} In the margin the editor discusses the chain at length, quoting al-Bukhari, Abu Hatim and Ibn Hibban on one transmitter, judges the chain very weak, and gives where the report is found in Ibn Kathir, al-Suyuti and al-Haythami; these are the judgements of those scholars and of the editor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 285}

On the second half of the verse al-Tabari says it is a promise from God to the one who spends in His way of multiplying the reward without limit or end, and al-Suddi said that no one knows what this multiplying is (5621). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 286}

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-245|2:245]]): from a scholar in Ibn 'Uyayna's report (5622), God gave people this world as a loan and asked it of them as a loan; if they give it with willing hearts He multiplies from one good deed to ten to seven hundred and more, and if He takes it from them while they dislike it and they bear it, theirs are the blessings, mercy and guidance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 286} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 287} The editor refers to the al-Baqara verse on patience under affliction. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 287}

Al-Tabari then gives the readers' differences on the verb: some read it with the long vowel and in the raised form, joined to the verb before; others with a doubled letter and no long vowel, in the same sense; others with the long vowel and the subjunctive, as an answer to the question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 287} He chooses the long vowel with the raised form, since the question carries the sense of a condition and when the particle fa enters the answer to a condition it is raised; and he prefers the long vowel over the doubled form because it is the more eloquent and more common among the Arabs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 287} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 288} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument, and the editor refers to al-Farra''s Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 287}

On withholding and expanding, al-Tabari says that God alone has in His hand the withholding and expanding of the servants' provision, not those whom the polytheists claimed to be gods, citing Anas's report that prices rose in the Prophet's time and people asked him to set them, and he said that God is the Expander, the Withholder, the Provider, and that he hoped to meet God with no one claiming a wrong from him in person or property (5623). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 288} In the margin the editor holds the report sound by this chain, names those who transmitted it (Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Abu Dawud) and quotes al-Tirmidhi's grading of good and sound; these are their judgements and his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 288} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 289} Al-Tabari takes the report to mean that dearness, cheapness, ease and hardship are in God's hand, and that the verse urges believers whose provision is expanded to strengthen the needy among them by spending and help them fight the enemy; it is He who withheld provision from some to try them with patience and expanded it for others to test their action, and He repays each according to his obedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 289} The editor explains in the margin the word for pack animals and his view of how the original wording may read. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 289}

Ibn Zayd (5624): God knew that among those who fight in His way are some without strength and among those who do not fight some with wealth, so He called on these, saying: it was expanded for you while you are slow to go out, and withheld from this one who is glad to go, so strengthen him from what is in your hand and you will have a share in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 290}

On the verse's ending, al-Tabari says that the return is to God, so people should fear Him in themselves, not neglect His obligations or exceed His limits; the one with expanded provision should not use it otherwise than permitted, and the straitened should not let want lead him to disobedience, or he earns His punishment on returning to Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 290} Qatada (5625): the return is to the dust from which they were created; the editor notes that the copyist made many slips at this place in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 291}
