## Summary
Verse 7 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 7 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 7 (pp. 597-599, reports 8655-8657).** Abu Jafar says the meaning is that the male children of the dead man have a share of his inheritance and the females have a share of it, from the little or the much that he left behind, a share that is binding, obligatory, known and timed. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on the word for an obligation and explains the word for "timed" as measured and limited, from the word for time and then widened to anything limited, citing a report attributed to Ali that the Messenger of God set no fixed number of lashes for drinking wine, and noting that the grammarians took from it the term for a proper noun whose referent is defined absolutely. Al-Tabari reports that this verse was revealed because the people of the pre-Islamic era gave inheritance to males and not to females. Qatada (8655, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from Abd al-Razzaq from Mamar): they did not give women inheritance, so it was revealed. Ikrima (8656, through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj): it was revealed about Umm Kuhla and the daughter of Kuhla, and Tha'laba and Aws ibn Suwayd, who were of the Ansar; one of them was her husband and the other her child's paternal uncle; she said: O Messenger of God, my husband died and left me and his daughter and we were not given an inheritance; and the child's uncle said: O Messenger of God, she does not ride a horse, does not carry a dependent and does not injure an enemy; wealth is earned for her and she earns none; so the verse was revealed. In the editor's long note (pp. 598-599), Ibn Hajar recorded it in al-Isaba under Umm Kujja, and al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 122) attributes it to Ibn Jarir, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim; Ibn Hajar quotes al-Tabari's report as naming Umm Kujja, the daughter of Umm Kujja, Tha'laba and Aws ibn Thabit, differing from the text of al-Tabari in the woman's name and in Aws; the printing has Umm Kuhha, but the correct form, as Ibn Hajar vocalises it, has a damma on the kaf and a doubled jim with fatha; al-Suyuti writes it differently, which the editor calls a mistake; the manuscript has Umm Kuhla clearly in both places and the editor kept it, since Ibn Hajar himself notes that Abu Musa reported from al-Mustaghfiri that she was Umm Kuhla, and that her kunya may have matched her father's name, so that Ibn Jurayj's report shows she is Umm Kulthum; Umm Kujja will come again at report 8725 as the wife of Abd al-Rahman, brother of Hassan ibn Thabit; Aws ibn Suwayd is the form in the commentary sources, while Ibn Hajar has entries for Aws ibn Thabit al-Ansari, Aws ibn Suwayd, Tha'laba ibn Thabit and Tha'laba ibn Suwayd and mentions the disagreement over their names in this very story, so the editor left al-Tabari's text as it is. He explains the expressions: "does not carry a dependent" means does not take charge of the household and work for them, the dependents being those who need someone to feed them like an orphan; "does not injure an enemy" means does not strike him and wound him, the printing having a form with a hamza and the editor keeping the manuscript's, both being correct. Ibn Zayd (8657, through Yunus from Ibn Wahb): women did not inherit from their fathers in the pre-Islamic era, and the elder inherited but not the young even if male; then the verse was revealed (the editor says the printing changed the form of the verb and the manuscript has what he adopted). On grammar al-Tabari says the last two words of the verse are in the accusative as a description of an indefinite noun because they come in the manner of a verbal noun, as one says "you have a right owed to me", and had a plain noun stood in their place the accusative would not be allowed, since one does not say "you have a right with me, a dirham"; so the sense is a share that is an obligation and a duty, as one says "I have a dirham, a received gift" (the editor refers to al-Farra's Maani al-Quran, 1: 257, whose wording is the same as al-Tabari's). Here ends volume 7; at the foot of p. 599 the print reads that part seven of al-Tabari's commentary is complete and part eight follows, beginning with the commentary on the verse about the kinsfolk, orphans and poor who are present at the division, that is, volume 8 begins with verse 8 of sura 4. After this p. 600 is blank and the indexes begin at p. 601. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 597} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 598} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 599}
