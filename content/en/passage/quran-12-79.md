**Commentary on verse 79 (pp. 202-203)**

On "he said, God forbid that we should take anyone but him with whom we found our property; then we would be wrongdoers", al-Tabari says: Joseph said to his brothers "ma'adh Allah", I seek refuge in God. The Arabs do the same with every verbal noun they put in place of "yaf'alu" and "taf'alu": they put it in the accusative, as in "hamdan lillah" and "shukran lahu", meaning I praise God and thank Him. The Arabs say "ma'adh Allah" and "ma'adhat Allah", adding the feminine ha', as they say "ma ahsana ma'nat hadha al-kalam", and "awdh Allah", "awdhat Allah" and "iyadh Allah", and they say "allahumma 'a'idhan bika", as though it were said I seek refuge in You as one who seeks refuge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 202}

"That we should take anyone but him with whom we found our property", he says: I seek protection in God from taking an innocent for a guilty one. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "'adha". Report 19615, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: if we took anyone other than the one with whom we found our property, we would be doing what we have no right to do and wronging people. Report 19616, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: Joseph said: when you come to your father, greet him and say to him, the king of Egypt prays that you not die before you see your son Joseph, so that he may know that there are truthful ones like him in the land of Egypt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 203}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
