**Commentary on verse 126 (pp. 35-36)**

On "you take vengeance on us only because we believed in the signs of our Lord" he says: what do you find objectionable in us, Pharaoh, and what fault do you find in us, except that we believed, that is, held true, the proofs of our Lord, His signs and evidences, the like of which neither you nor anyone can produce, except God, to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth. Then they took refuge in God by asking Him for patience under Pharaoh's torture and for their souls to be taken in Islam, saying "Our Lord, pour out patience upon us", by which they meant: send down on us a restraint that holds us back from disbelieving in You when Pharaoh tortures us; "and take us to Yourself as Muslims" means take us to You in Islam, the religion of Your friend Abraham, and not in associating partners with You. The editor refers to earlier explanations of "take vengeance", "pour out patience", "patience" and "taking back". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 35}

Report 14957, from al-Suddi through Musa ibn Harun, Amr ibn Hammad and Asbat: Pharaoh killed and crucified them, as Abd Allah ibn Abbas said when they said "pour out patience on us and take us as Muslims": at the beginning of the day they were sorcerers and at its end martyrs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 35}

Report 14958, from Ubayd ibn Umayr through Ibn Waki', his father, Isra'il and Abd al-Aziz ibn Rufay': the sorcerers were sorcerers at the beginning of the day and martyrs at its end. Report 14959, from Qatada through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: we were told that they were sorcerers at the beginning of the day and martyrs at its end. Report 14960, from Mujahid through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they were sorcerers at the start of the day and martyrs at the end. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 36}
