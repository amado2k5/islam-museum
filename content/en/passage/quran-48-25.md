## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "They are the ones who disbelieved and prevented you from the Sacred Mosque and the sacrifice, turning away from reaching its place. And if it were not for believing men and believing women, you did not know them to step upon them, and you would befall you without knowledge of it, so that Allah may enter into His mercy whom He wills. If you remove it, We would surely punish those among them who disbelieved with a painful punishment." These polytheists from Quraysh are the ones who denied the oneness of Allah, prevented you, believers in Allah, from entering the Sacred Mosque, and prevented you. The sacrifice is left behind: it means withheld from reaching its destination. The place of 'an' is accusative because it is attached if you wish with a backburned one, and if you wish, with a repulsion. Some grammarians of Basra used to say about this: They turned away the sacrificial animal with a backward wound, disliking it to reach its place. And by His saying, the Exalted: "to reach its place," he meant reaching the place where he was slaughtered, which is the entrance to the sanctuary, the place where it is lawful to slaughter it. The Messenger of God used to travel with him when he went to Mecca on that journey with seventy camels. In the passage, he quotes from Qatadah.
