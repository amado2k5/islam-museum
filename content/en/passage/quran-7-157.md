**Commentary on verse 157 (pp. 161-165)**

**On "those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet"**

Al-Tabari says: this saying from God makes clear that those for whom He promised His prophet Moses to write the mercy He described by "My mercy encompasses everything" are the community of Muhammad, since God is not known to have described any messenger with this description, meaning "unlettered", other than our Prophet Muhammad, and the reports from the interpreters came to this effect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 161}

Reports 15214, 15215, 15216 and 15217, from Ibn Abbas and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, that "those who fear" are the community of Muhammad, through Ibn Waki', Imran ibn Uyayna, Ata, Zayd ibn Hubab, Hammad ibn Salama, Abu Kurayb, Yahya ibn Yaman, Ash'ath, Ja'far, Ibn Humayd and Jarir; in report 15216 Moses said, would that I had been created in the community of Muhammad, and in 15217 they are those who follow Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 161}

Report 15218, from Nawf al-Himyari, through Ibn Waki', Jarir, Layth and Shahr ibn Hawshab: when Moses chose seventy of his people for his Lord's appointment, God said to Moses, I shall make the earth a place of prostration and purification for you, I shall put the sakina among you in your homes, and I shall make you recite the Torah from memory, the man among you and the woman, the free and the slave, the young and the old. Moses told his people, and they said, we want to pray only in the churches; we want the sakina only as it was, in the Ark; and we want to read the Torah only by sight. Then God said "I shall write it for those who fear" up to "those are the successful". The editor explains that the printed text made "backs" plural and the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 161}

Report 15219, from Nawf al-Bikali, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar and Yahya ibn Abi Kathir: when Moses went with the delegation of the Children of Israel, God spoke to him and said, I have spread the earth for them as a place of purification and prostration where they may pray wherever prayer finds them, except at a privy, a grave or a bath, and I put the sakina in their hearts and make them recite the Torah from memory on their tongues. Moses told the Children of Israel, and they said, we cannot carry the sakina in our hearts, so put it in an Ark for us, and we shall read the Torah only by sight and pray only in a church. God said "I shall write it for those who fear" up to "the successful". Moses said, Lord, make me their prophet; He said, their prophet is from them; he said, make me one of them; He said, you will not reach them; he said, Lord, I came to You with the delegation of the Children of Israel and You made our delegation for others; and God revealed what is at [[passage/quran-7-159|7:159]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 162}

Nawf al-Bikali said: so thank God who kept your absence safe, took your share for you and made the delegation of the Children of Israel yours. Report 15220 is similar from Nawf, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Mu'adh ibn Hisham, his father and Yahya ibn Abi Kathir, and says God sends down the Torah on them to be recited on their tongues, their men, women and children. Report 15221, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the community of Muhammad. Report 15222, from al-Suddi: these are the community of Muhammad. Report 15223, from Qatada, like 15205: when it was said what was said, the Jews and Christians wished for it, and God sent down a clear, firm condition, following the unlettered Messenger-Prophet, who is your Prophet, who was unlettered and did not write. The editor says Ishaq ibn Isma'il is Habawayh Abu Yazid al-Razi. Al-Tabari says he explained "unlettered" earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 163}

**On "whom they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel"**

Al-Tabari says the pronoun in "they find him" refers to the Messenger, who is Muhammad. Report 15224, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: this is Muhammad. Report 15225, from Ata ibn Yasar, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Uthman ibn Umar, Fulayh and Hilal ibn Ali: I met Abd Allah ibn Amr and said, tell me of the Messenger of God's description in the Torah; he said, yes, by God, he is described in the Torah as in the Qur'an, as a witness, a bearer of good news and a warner and a safeguard for the unlettered, God's servant and messenger, whom God named the one who relies on Him; not harsh or coarse or clamorous in the markets, and he does not repay evil with evil but pardons and overlooks; God will not take him until He straightens the crooked community through him, by their saying "there is no god but God", opening through him sealed hearts, deaf ears and blind eyes. Ata said: then I met Ka'b and asked him about it and they did not differ by a letter, except that Ka'b gave the words for the sealed hearts, deaf ears and blind eyes in his own language. The editor gives biographies of Uthman ibn Umar, Fulayh ibn Sulayman and Hilal ibn Ali ibn Usama al-Madani, and says they are reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 164}

Report 15226, from Ata, through Abu Kurayb, Musa ibn Dawud, Fulayh and Hilal: I met Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As, and he related the like, except that for Ka'b's words he gave words in his language. Report 15227, from Abd Allah, through Musa, Abd al-Aziz ibn Salama, Hilal and Ata ibn Yasar, is similar and has no words of Ka'b. Report 15228, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they find his description, his command and his prophethood written with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 165}

**On "he enjoins them what is right and forbids them what is wrong"**

Al-Tabari says: this unlettered Prophet enjoins his followers the right, which is belief in God and keeping to obedience to Him in what He commanded and forbade, and forbids them the wrong, which is shirk and refraining from what God forbade them. "He makes lawful for them the good things" means what the Jahiliyya had forbidden of the bahira, sa'iba, wasila and ham animals, "and forbids them the foul things" means the flesh of swine and usury and what they treated as lawful of foods and drinks that God had forbidden. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "right", "wrong", "good things" and "foul things". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 165}

We stop here at the foot of p. 165, after the explanation of "forbids them the foul things" in verse 157 and before the reports on it; the next batch begins at p. 166. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 165}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
