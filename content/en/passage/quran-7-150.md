## Summary
Verse 150 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 150 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 150 (pp. 120-130)**

Al-Tabari says: when Moses returned to his people of the Children of Israel he returned angry and grieved, since God had told him that his people were tried and the Samaritan had misled them, so he returned angry and grieved for that. "Asaf" is intense anger and rage against whoever angered one. Report 15124, from Abu al-Darda, through Imran ibn Bakkar al-Kala'i, Abd al-Salam ibn Muhammad al-Hadrami, Shurayh ibn Yazid and Nasr ibn Alqama: "asaf" is a station beyond anger, stronger than it. The editor gives biographies of Abd al-Salam al-Hadrami, Shurayh ibn Yazid and Nasr ibn Alqama, and notes that Nasr's report from Abu al-Darda is mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 120}

Others said "asaf" means grieved. Report 15125, from al-Suddi, through Musa ibn Harun, Amr and Asbat: grieved. Report 15126, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, uncle, father and grandfather: "asaf" is grieved, and in sura al-Zukhruf, [[passage/quran-43-55|43:55]], "they angered Us" means made Us angry; "asaf" has two senses, anger and grief. Report 15127, from al-Hasan, through Nasr ibn Ali, Sulayman ibn Sulayman and Malik ibn Dinar: angry and grieved. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 121}

On "evil is what you did in my place after me" he says: evil is the deed you did after I left you, in what you owed me regarding my people I left behind among you, and my religion which your Lord commanded you. One says "he succeeded him with good" or "with evil" when he treated his family or people, or whoever was connected with him, well or badly after he left them. On "did you hasten your Lord's command?" he says: did you get ahead of your Lord's command within yourselves and go away from it? One says that someone "hastened" a matter if he got ahead of it, and "I hastened him" meaning I urged him on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 122}

On "and he threw down the tablets", al-Tabari says Moses threw down the tablets, and the scholars differed on why. Some said he threw them in anger at his people who had worshipped the calf. Report 15128, from Ibn Abbas, through Tamim ibn al-Muntasir, Yazid, al-Asbagh ibn Zayd, al-Qasim ibn Abi Ayyub and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: when Moses returned angry and grieved, he took his brother by the head, dragging him to him, and threw down the tablets in anger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 122}

Report 15129, from Ibn Abbas, through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar, Ibn Uyayna, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: when Moses returned to his people he was near them and heard their voices, and said, I hear the voices of a people at play; when he saw them clinging to the calf he threw down the tablets and broke them, and took his brother by the head, dragging him. Report 15130, from al-Suddi: he came back angry and grieved and addressed his people as in [[passage/quran-20-86|20:86]] and [[passage/quran-20-87|20:87]], then threw down the tablets and took his brother by the head, and Aaron answered as in [[passage/quran-20-94|20:94]]. Report 15131, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when he saw their worship of the calf he threw the tablets from his hand, then seized his brother's head and beard, saying what is at [[passage/quran-20-92|20:92]] and [[passage/quran-20-93|20:93]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 123}

Others said he threw the tablets only because of virtues he found in them for people other than his own, which weighed heavily on him. Report 15132, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: Moses said, Lord, I find in the tablets a community that is the best community brought out for mankind, who enjoin the right and forbid the wrong, make them my community; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. He said, Lord, I find in the tablets a community who are the last in creation yet the first to enter Paradise, make them my community; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 123}

The rest of report 15132 from Qatada: Moses said, Lord, I find in the tablets a community whose scriptures are in their chests, which they recite, while those before them read their book by sight, and once it was taken away they remembered nothing; Qatada said, God gave you, community, a power of memory He gave to no other community; make them my community; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. Then he said, I find a community who believe in the first Book and the last and fight the remnants of error until they fight the one-eyed liar; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 124}

Then, a community who eat their alms in their bellies and are rewarded for it, whereas earlier nations, when one gave alms and it was accepted, God sent a fire to consume it, and if it was rejected it was left to be eaten by birds and beasts, and God takes your alms from your rich for your poor; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. Then a community among whom, if one intends a good deed and does not do it, a good deed is written for him, and if he does it, ten like it up to seven hundred; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. Then a community among whom, if one intends an evil deed, nothing is written against him until he does it, and then one evil deed is written; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. Then a community who answer and are answered; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. Then a community who intercede and are interceded for; He said, that is the community of Ahmad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 124}

He said: it is told to us that God's prophet Moses cast away the tablets and said, O God, make me of the community of Ahmad; God's prophet Moses was given two things no prophet was given: God said to him what is at [[passage/quran-7-144|7:144]], and the prophet of God was satisfied, then he was given the second, which is at [[passage/quran-7-159|7:159]], and he was fully satisfied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 124}

Report 15133, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: when Moses took the tablets he said, Lord, I find in the tablets a community who are the best of nations, who enjoin the right and forbid the wrong; He said, that is the community of Ahmad; and a community who are the last and the first on the Day of Resurrection; then he gave something like the report from Bishr ibn Mu'adh, except that he said in it that Moses threw down the tablets and said, O God, make me of the community of Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 125}

Al-Tabari then says that the better view is that Moses threw down the tablets in anger at his people for worshipping the calf, because God reported so in His book, at [[passage/quran-7-150|7:150]], that Moses came back angry and grieved, threw down the tablets and took his brother by the head, dragging him to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 125}

**The writing and the material of the tablets**

It is said that when God wrote the Torah for Moses in the tablets He drew him so near that he heard the scratching of the pen. Report 15134, from Ali, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Isra'il, al-Suddi and Abu Umara: God wrote the tablets for Moses while he heard the scratching of the pens on the tablets. Report 15135, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Isra'il and Ata ibn al-Sa'ib: He drew him near until he heard the scratching of the pens. The editor explains that the dots at the head of this report show that its chain is attached to the previous one, and records a difference between the printed text and the manuscript in an introductory phrase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 125}

It is also said that the Torah was in seven sevenths, and when Moses threw down the tablets they broke and six sevenths were taken up, among them the detailing of everything, leaving the guidance and mercy in the remaining seventh, which is what is at [[passage/quran-7-154|7:154]]. The Torah was, it is reported, seventy camel-loads, a part read in a year. Report 15136, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Khalid al-Makfuf, Abd al-Rahman and Abu Ja'far: the Torah was sent down as seventy camel-loads, of which one part is read in a year, and only four people read it: Moses son of Imran, Jesus, Ezra and Joshua son of Nun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 126}

They differed on what the tablets were made of. Some said green emerald, some said ruby, some said hail. Report 15137, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Dawraqi, Hajjaj ibn Muhammad, Ibn Jurayj, Ya'la ibn Muslim and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: Moses threw down the tablets and they broke and were taken up except a sixth; Ibn Jurayj said he was told the tablets were of chrysolite and emerald from Paradise. Report 15138, from Abu al-Aliya, through several transmitters, Adam al-Asqalani, Abu Ja'far and al-Rabi': Moses' tablets were of hail. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 127}

Report 15139, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Humayd, Hukkam, Abu al-Junayd and Ja'far ibn Abi al-Mughira: they were of a ruby, written in gold, and the Merciful wrote them with His hand so that the people of the heavens heard the scratching of the pen as it wrote. Report 15140, from Mujahid or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Harith, al-Qasim, Abd al-Rahman, Muhammad ibn Abi al-Wadda' and Khusayf: they were emerald, and when Moses threw them down the guidance and mercy remained and the detailing went. Report 15141, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Ashja'i, Muhammad ibn Muslim and Khusayf: of green emerald. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 127}

Some claimed the tablets were two tablets; if so, "in the tablets" was said of two, as in the verse of sura al-Nisa' "if he has brothers" said of two brothers, and the editor refers to the earlier discussion of a plural meant for two and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 127}

**Taking his brother by the head, and the reading of "son of my mother"**

As for "and took his brother by the head, dragging him to him", this was an act of God's prophet out of displeasure with his brother Aaron for leaving his followers and staying with the Children of Israel where he had left them, as God relates of Moses' words to him at [[passage/quran-20-92|20:92]] and [[passage/quran-20-93|20:93]], when Aaron told him his excuse and he accepted it, that is Aaron's words to Moses at [[passage/quran-20-94|20:94]]; and he said "son of my mother, the people judged me weak". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 128}

The readers differed over the reading of "son of my mother". Most readers of Medina and some of Basra read it with fatha on the mim of "umm", and most readers of Kufa with kasra. The scholars of Arabic differed over fatha and kasra, all agreeing that both are dialects in use among the Arabs. Some grammarians of Basra said fatha was used because the two nouns were made into one noun, as in "son of my paternal uncle", and said this is irregular and not to be used as a model; whoever reads kasra follows the dialect of those who say "here is a boy who has come", making it one noun ending in kasra like "khazi bazi". The editor explains that "al-khazibaz" is a kind of fly, "khaz" and "baz" being two sounds of the fly made into one and built on kasra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 128}

Some grammarians of Kufa said "son of my mother" and "son of my paternal uncle" are put in the accusative as a declinable word is in some cases, as one says "O my sorrow" and "O my woe"; it is as if they said "O mother!" and "O uncle!"; they did not say that of "brother", though it would have been correct. Those who used kasra did so because it was used so often that they dropped the ya; the Arabs hardly drop the ya except from a vocative noun the caller annexes to himself, apart from these two phrases, which are used so much; and when what is less used comes, they keep the ya and say "son of my father", "son of my sister", "my brother", "son of my maternal aunt" and "son of my maternal uncle". The editor notes that all this is the statement of al-Farra in Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 129}

Al-Tabari says the correct view is that when the mim of "son of my mother" has fatha, a lament is meant, "O son of my mother!", and likewise in "son of my uncle"; and when it has kasra, annexation is meant, with the ya, which stands for the speaker himself, then dropped. Some who denied the comparison of this kasra with the kasra on the za in "khazi bazi" reasoned that in "khaz baz" the second is known only through the first and the first only through the second, so it became like sounds. Yunus al-Jarmi is reported to have made "umm" and "amm" feminine and said that it is made one noun only with the masculine "ibn". Some say the good dialect and sound analogy is that of those who say "son of my mother" keeping the ya, and they cited a line by Abu Zubayd, speaking to his nephew or his brother in lament, and then a line by another poet, Ghalfa' ibn al-Harith of Kinda, from a poem mourning his brother Shurahbil, killed at the first Day of al-Kulab. The editor explains their words in long notes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 129}
