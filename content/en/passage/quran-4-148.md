**Commentary on verse 148: publicly speaking evil, and the one who is wronged (pp. 343-349)**

The readers differ on the vowel of the word in the verse: most read it with a damma on the letter, and some with a fatha. Those who read the damma differ on its meaning. Some say God does not love any of us to call out evil against someone, except one who is wronged, who may pray against his wrongdoer, since God has permitted him that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 343}

Report 10749, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha and Mu'awiya, says God does not love anyone to pray against another unless he is wronged, for he is allowed to pray against the one who wronged him; but if he is patient it is better for him. Report 10750 gives the same chain in a shorter form of the same sense. Report 10751, from Qatada, says God excuses the wronged man for calling out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 344}

Report 10752, from al-Hasan: it is the man who wrongs another and who does not curse him, but says: O God, help me against him; O God, win back my right for me; O God, stand between him and what he wants, and the like of such prayers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 344}

Al-Tabari notes that on Ibn Abbas's reading the word 'man' is in the nominative, because he takes 'public evil' as meaning a prayer and excepts the wronged. The grammarians consider that wrong, since such a 'man' cannot be nominative after a negation that does not reach it; it may be in the accusative as an exception, as in similar cases, like the phrase 'I dislike contention, except, O God, a man who seeks God through it'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 345}

Others say the sense is that God does not love public evil speech, except from one who is wronged, who may report what was done to him. Report 10753, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih: a man lands at a man's home and is not well hosted; he leaves and says his host did a poor job of hospitality. Report 10754, from Mujahid through Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: except one whom what was said to him has affected. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 346}

Report 10755, from Mujahid through Hammad: it is the guest whose baggage was turned, who speaks publicly ill of his host. Report 10756 and 10757, from Mujahid: except one who was wronged and defends himself, speaking ill. Report 10758, from Mujahid through Ibn Uyayna: a man lands at another and is not treated well, so God permitted him to speak about him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 346}

Report 10759, from Mujahid through Sufyan and Ibrahim ibn Abi Bakr: it concerns hospitality, when a man comes to a people and stays with them, and they do not host him, so God permitted him to speak of them. Report 10760, from Mujahid through Abd al-Razzaq: a man hosted another and the host did not give him his right of hospitality; when he left he told the people. Report 10761, from Mujahid through Ibn Jurayj: it was revealed about a man who was a guest in a desert place and was not hosted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 347}

Others say the sense is: except one who is wronged and avenges himself on his wrongdoer, for God has permitted him to do so. Report 10762, from al-Suddi, says God does not love public evil from any of the creatures, but the one who is wronged and avenges himself by the like of what he suffered is not blameworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 348}

Others read the vowel as a fatha, and explain it as: except one who was wronged, there is no harm in speaking ill of him publicly. Report 10763, from Ibn Zayd through his father, who read it with a fatha and said: except one who persists in that hypocrisy, and so is spoken of publicly until he gives it up. Report 10764, from Ibn Zayd, says the whole verse concerns the hypocrites, so that one who has repented is not told: are you not the hypocrite who did such and such? unless he persists in hypocrisy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 349}

Al-Tabari prefers the damma, since the scholars of recitation and interpretation agree on its soundness and the fatha is an outlier. The sense then is that God does not love anyone to speak publicly ill of another, except the one who is wronged, who has no blame in reporting what was done to him; this covers one who was not hosted, or whose hospitality was poor, or who was wronged by an injustice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 349}
