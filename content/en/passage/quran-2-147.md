## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "the truth is from your Lord, so do not be among the doubters" al-Tabari says: know, Muhammad, that the truth is what your Lord made known to you and brought from Him, not what the Jews and Christians tell you; it is God's report to His Prophet that the qibla He directed him to is the true qibla that Abraham and the prophets after him were on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 190} "Al-mumtarin" are the doubters; from al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd the meaning is do not be in doubt, for it is your qibla and the qibla of the prophets before you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 190} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 191} He explains that "mumtari" is a form from "miriya", which is doubt, and cites a line of al-A'sha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 191}

He puts a question: was the Prophet in doubt that the qibla was true, that he was forbidden doubt? He answers that this is speech the Arabs frame as a command or prohibition to the addressee while meaning another, as in "O Prophet, fear God and do not obey the unbelievers and hypocrites", where those meant are his companions who believed in him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 191} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 192}
