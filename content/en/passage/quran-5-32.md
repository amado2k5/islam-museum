**Commentary on verse 32: "because of that We decreed for the Children of Israel", pp. 231-233**

Al-Tabari says "because of that" means from the pulling-in of that, its consequence and offence: from the offence the killer of his brother among Adam's two sons drew down, We decreed for the Children of Israel. One says "I drew this matter on" for pulling and gaining it, and he cites a line of verse about people at war, meaning that he is the one who drew it on them and the offender; the editor gives its attribution and variants. So the sense is: because of the crime of Adam's son who unjustly killed his brother, We ruled for the Children of Israel that whoever of them kills a person unjustly, not for a person killed and so himself put to death in retaliation, "or for corruption in the land", meaning killing one who had not committed such corruption as made her deserve killing; corruption in the land is waging war on God and His messenger and terrorising the road. Report 11770, from al-Dahhak, says it means because of Adam's son who unjustly killed his brother.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 231}

The interpreters then differed over "whoever kills a soul, unless for a soul or corruption in the land, it is as if he had killed all people, and whoever gives it life, as if he had given life to all people". Some said: whoever kills a prophet or just leader is as if he killed all people, and whoever strengthens the arm of a prophet or just leader is as if he gave life to all people, as report 11771 from Ibn 'Abbas says; the editor identifies its narrators.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 232}

Report 11772, from Ibn 'Abbas, says that whoever kills one soul that God made inviolable is like one who kills all people, and whoever refrains from killing one soul out of fear of God is like one who spares all people, meaning the prophets. Others said that whoever kills a soul has, in the killer's sin, killed all people to the slain man, and whoever gives it life by saving it from destruction has, to the one saved, given life to all people, which report 11773, from al-Suddi, from several Companions, begins to give.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 233}

We stop here at the foot of p. 233, at the start of report 11773 from al-Suddi; the rest of verse 32 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
