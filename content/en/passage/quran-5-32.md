## Summary
Verse 32 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 32 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 32: "because of that We decreed for the Children of Israel", pp. 231-233**

Al-Tabari says "because of that" means from the pulling-in of that, its consequence and offence: from the offence the killer of his brother among Adam's two sons drew down, We decreed for the Children of Israel. One says "I drew this matter on" for pulling and gaining it, and he cites a line of verse about people at war, meaning that he is the one who drew it on them and the offender; the editor gives its attribution and variants. So the sense is: because of the crime of Adam's son who unjustly killed his brother, We ruled for the Children of Israel that whoever of them kills a person unjustly, not for a person killed and so himself put to death in retaliation, "or for corruption in the land", meaning killing one who had not committed such corruption as made her deserve killing; corruption in the land is waging war on God and His messenger and terrorising the road. Report 11770, from al-Dahhak, says it means because of Adam's son who unjustly killed his brother.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 231}

The interpreters then differed over "whoever kills a soul, unless for a soul or corruption in the land, it is as if he had killed all people, and whoever gives it life, as if he had given life to all people". Some said: whoever kills a prophet or just leader is as if he killed all people, and whoever strengthens the arm of a prophet or just leader is as if he gave life to all people, as report 11771 from Ibn 'Abbas says; the editor identifies its narrators.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 232}

Report 11772, from Ibn 'Abbas, says that whoever kills one soul that God made inviolable is like one who kills all people, and whoever refrains from killing one soul out of fear of God is like one who spares all people, meaning the prophets. Others said that whoever kills a soul has, in the killer's sin, killed all people to the slain man, and whoever gives it life by saving it from destruction has, to the one saved, given life to all people, which report 11773, from al-Suddi, from several Companions, begins to give.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 233}

The rest of report 11773, from al-Suddi, says that "whoever kills a soul, it is as if he had killed all people" is so in sin to the slain person, and "whoever gives it life" by saving it from destruction has, to the one saved, given life to all people.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 234}

Others said it means that the killer of a soul whose killing is forbidden will burn in the Fire as he would if he had killed all people, and whoever gave it life, that is was safe from killing it, has been safe from killing all people. Reports 11774, 11775 and 11776, from Mujahid and Ibn 'Abbas, say that whoever held back from killing it gave it life, whoever destroyed a soul is as if he had killed all people, and whoever killed no one, all people were safe from him. Report 11777, from 'Abda ibn Abi Lubaba, says he asked Mujahid, who said that had he killed all people his recompense would be Hell for ever, God's anger and curse and a great punishment, which echoes the verse of [[passage/quran-4-93|4:93]].  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 234}

Report 11778, from Mujahid, says God made the recompense of one who deliberately kills a believing soul Hell, His anger and curse, and that had he killed all people the punishment would be no more than that, and that whoever killed no one, people had rest from him. Reports 11779 and 11780, from Mujahid, say he destroyed himself and that it is in sin; report 11780m says he goes to Hell for killing a believer as if he had killed all people. Report 11781, from Ibn 'Abbas, says that giving it life is not killing a soul God made inviolable, so people lived from the one who held its killing forbidden except by right.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 235}

Reports 11782 to 11786, from Mujahid, say that whoever held it inviolable and did not kill it gave it life, that this is like what is in the surah of the women regarding the recompense, and that he turned to his companions and said it is this and this; the editor takes this to mean that killing one inviolable soul is killing all people and sparing it is sparing all people.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 236}

Others said it means that the retaliation due on the killer is what would be due if he had killed all people, as report 11787, from Ibn Zayd, says. Others said "whoever gave it life" means whoever pardoned one against whom retaliation was due and did not kill him; report 11788, from Ibn Zayd, says God gives him a reward as if he had given life to all people, whether the slain man's guardian or the victim himself pardoning before he dies. Reports 11789, 11790 and 11791, from al-Hasan, say it is whoever pardons, and that it is pardon after having power.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 237}

Others said "gave it life" means saved it from drowning, burning or destruction, as reports 11792, 11793 and 11794, from Mujahid, give, with the editor explaining the words for burning and collapse. Report 11795, from al-Dahhak, says that for one who kills without right, whether scrupulous or not, and report 11796 says that had he not killed him it would be as if he had given life to the people, not having held lawful what is forbidden.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 238}

Qatada and al-Hasan said as follows: report 11797, from al-Hasan, says that this is grave; report 11798, from Qatada, says that whoever kills a soul without a soul or corruption, it is as if he killed all people, its reward is great and its burden great, so give it life with your wealth and with your pardon if you can; and that he knew of no lawful shedding of a Muslim's blood from this direction of prayer except for one of three: disbelief after Islam, which calls for death; adultery after marriage, which calls for stoning; or deliberate killing, which calls for retaliation. Report 11799, from Qatada, is similar. Report 11800, from Sulayman ibn 'Ali al-Rab'i, says he asked al-Hasan whether it was for us as it was for the Children of Israel, and he said: yes, by Him other than whom there is no god, and what made the blood of the Children of Israel more precious to God than ours?  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 239}

Report 11801, from Khalid Abu al-Fadl, says al-Hasan recited the verses from "his soul led him on" to "as if he had given life to all people" and said God made the burden great and the reward attractive as you hear, and that if you think, son of Adam, that were you to kill all people you still have deeds to save you from the Fire, your soul has lied to you by God and the devil has lied to you. Report 11802, from al-Hasan, says that "as if he killed all people" is a burden and "as if he gave life" is a reward. The editor examines the identity of the narrator and judges him to be Khalid ibn Rabah al-Hudhali.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 240}

We stop here at the foot of p. 240, where al-Tabari's choice among the views on killing and giving life begins; the rest of verse 32 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
