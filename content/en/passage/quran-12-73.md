**Commentary on verse 73 (pp. 180-182)**

On "they said, by God, you know we did not come to spread corruption in the land, and we were not thieves", al-Tabari says: Joseph's brothers said "ta-llahi", meaning "wa-llahi". The ta' in "ta-llahi" is a waw turned into ta', as was done in "tawrat", "turath" and "tukhama", which come from "warraytu", "waritha" and "wakhama"; in all those words the waw is part of the noun, which is not so in "ta-llahi", where it is the waw of the oath. It was turned into ta' because of how often the Arabs swore by saying "wa-llahi", so this word was singled out by having the waw turned into ta'; and whoever says this with God's name does not say "ta-l-Rahman" or use it with any other of God's names or with anything else sworn by. The editor sets out the difference between the Basrans and Kufans on the origin of "tawrat" and corrects the printed wording from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 180}

On "you know we did not come to spread corruption in the land", he says: you know we did not come to disobey God in your land, as a group of interpreters said; report 19555, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far and his father: we did not come to disobey in the land. If someone asks: what knowledge did those addressed have that they had not come for that, such that those speakers allowed themselves to say it? It is said: they allowed themselves to say it because, as reported, they had returned the goods they found in their saddlebags, and said, had we been thieves we would not have returned to you the goods we found in our baggage. It is also said that they were known along their route and journey not to wrong anyone or take what was not theirs, and they said this when told "you are thieves". The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "corruption in the land". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 181}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
