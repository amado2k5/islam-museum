## Verse 12 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 226-227)

For [[passage/quran-3-12|3:12]] al-Tabari records that the reciters differed over the reading of the two verbs for 'will be overcome' and 'will be gathered'.

Some read them with the letter ta, as an address to the disbelievers that they will be overcome, and argued for their choice from the address to them in the next verse ([[passage/quran-3-13|3:13]]); they said that this shows the verb here is likewise an address to them. This is the reading of most of the reciters of the Hijaz and Basra and some of Kufa. Al-Tabari says that for one who holds that those promised defeat are the ones the Prophet was told to speak to, either ya or ta is permissible, since the address of the revelation when it came down was to others, which is like someone saying 'I said to the people: you are overcome' and 'I said to them: they are overcome'. He notes that the reading of Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud is reported to give the address form in the verse of al-Anfal ([[passage/quran-8-38|8:38]]), where the common reading is in the third person. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 226}

A group of Kufan reciters read them with ya, in the sense: say to the Jews that the Arab polytheists will be overcome and gathered to Hell; and for one who reads it so, in that sense, nothing but ya is allowed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 226} The editor refers for all this to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 191-192). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 227}

Al-Tabari then says, as his own choice, that he prefers the reading with ta, in the sense: say, Muhammad, to those who disbelieved among the Jews of the Banu Israel, who follow what is resembling in the verses of the Book to seek discord and to seek its interpretation, that they will be overcome and gathered to Hell. His reason is that the address in the next verse shows these people are addressed in this one too, and that joining an address to a like address is better than to a report about someone absent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 227}

He adds a further reason, report 6666 from Ibn Abbas (through Abu Kurayb, Yunus ibn Bukayr, Muhammad ibn Ishaq, Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, the freedman of Zayd, and Said ibn Jubayr or Ikrima): when the Messenger had struck Quraysh at Badr and came to Madina, he gathered the Jews in the market of the Banu Qaynuqa and said: O assembly of Jews, accept Islam before there befalls you what befell Quraysh. They said: Muhammad, do not let yourself deceive you that you killed some men of Quraysh who were inexperienced and did not know fighting; if you fought us, by God, you would know that we are the men and that you have not met the like of us. So God sent down about their words the verses from this one to the end of the verse after the next ([[passage/quran-3-13|3:13]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 227} The editor says Ibn Hisham's Sira has two phrases that differ a little from al-Tabari's wording, and that the plural for 'inexperienced' is of a word meaning the ignorant, unseasoned man who has not been tried in affairs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 227}

We stop at the end of p. 227, after report 6666 from Ibn Abbas on the Jews of the Banu Qaynuqa; Ibn Ishaq's reports 6667 and 6668 follow it on the next page. These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices and arguments are his, and the editor's notes and references are his, with no museum verdict.
