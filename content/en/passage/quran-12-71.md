**Commentary on verses 71 and 72 (pp. 175-178)**

On "they said, turning to them, what is it you are missing? They said, we miss the king's cup, and for whoever brings it is a camel's load, and I guarantee it" (verses 71 and 72, which al-Tabari explains together and which are therefore gathered here under verse 71), al-Tabari says: Ya'qub's sons, when it was called out to them "O caravan, you are thieves", turned to the caller and those with him and said "what is it you are missing?"; the men said "we miss the king's cup". The readers differed on the word. It is reported that Abu Hurayra read "sa' al-malik", without a waw, as though taking it as the sa', the measure by which food is measured; Abu Raja' is reported to have read "saw' al-malik"; and Yahya ibn Ya'mar is reported to have read "sawgh al-malik" with a ghayn, as though taking it as the verbal noun of "saagha yasughu". What the readers of the great cities follow is "suwa' al-malik", and al-Tabari does not allow reading against it because the authorities agree on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 175}

"Suwa'" is the vessel with which Joseph measured the food, and the interpreters said the same. Report 19525, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba, Abu Bishr and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the king's cup was shaped like a makkuk, and al-Abbas had one like it in the pre-Islamic period to drink from. Report 19526, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki', Shu'ba, Abu Bishr and Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Ibn Abbas: it was of silver like a makkuk, and al-Abbas had one of them in the pre-Islamic period. Report 19527, from Ikrima, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki', Sharik and Simak: it was of silver. Report 19528, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ya'qub, Hushaym and Abu Bishr: it was his drinking vessel and it was rather tall. Reports 19529 and 19530, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Waki', Suwayd ibn Amr and Abu Awana, and through al-Muthanna and al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal: it is the Persian makkuk whose two ends meet, from which the non-Arabs drank. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 176}

Report 19531, from al-Dahhak, through Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra' and Juwaybir: the king's vessel from which he drank. Report 19532, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Yahya ibn Abbad, Shu'ba, Abu Bishr and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: a silver makkuk they drank from, and al-Abbas had one in the pre-Islamic period. Report 19533, from Qatada, through Ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the king's vessel from which he drank. Report 19534, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Sa'id ibn Mansur and Abu Awana: the Persian makkuk whose ends meet. Report 19535, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: Joseph drank from it. Report 19536, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Ma'mar al-Bahrani, Abd al-Samad ibn Abd al-Warith, Sadaqa ibn Abbad and his father: it was of copper. "And for whoever brings it a camel's load" means for whoever brings the cup, a camel-load of food; report 19537, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: a camel's burden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 177}

Report 19538, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "a camel's load" is a donkey's load, and that is a dialect; reports 19539 to 19542, from Mujahid, are similar, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Shababa; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj. The editor corrects "a load of food" in the printed text and the manuscript to "a donkey's load" because of the words "that is a dialect", since they claim it is a dialect word for the donkey. On "and I guarantee it" he says: I am guarantor that I will give him a camel-load of food if he brings me the king's cup. Report 19543, from Ibn Abbas, through Ali, Abd Allah and Mu'awiya: guarantor. Report 19544, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: the "za'im" is the caller who said "O caravan". Report 19545 begins at the end of the page, through Muhammad ibn Amr and Abu Asim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 178}
