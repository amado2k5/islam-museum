## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari reads "if you are in doubt about what We have sent down upon Our servant, then bring a surah like it" as God's argument for His Prophet against the polytheists of his own people among the Arabs, their hypocrites, and the unbelieving People of the Book, whose stories open the passage beginning "those who disbelieve", and these, with their like, are those the verses address. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 372}

Al-Tabari spells out the challenge as God saying to the Arab polytheists and the unbelieving People of the Book: if you doubt what He sent down on His servant of light and proof, bring an argument that answers his, since the proof of anyone who claims prophethood is a sign that all creation cannot match. He holds that the failure of people so skilled in eloquence, and of whoever they call on for help, is itself proof of his truthfulness, as the proof of earlier messengers was what creation could not match; had the Qur'an been his invention they would not have failed, since he was a human like them in body and sharpness of tongue. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 373}
On "like it" he reports from Qatada that it means like this Qur'an, true and without falsehood, and from Mujahid that it is like the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 373} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 374}
He explains the Mujahid and Qatada reading as: bring a surah like this Qur'an from your own speech, you Arabs, as Muhammad brought it in your languages and the sense of your speech. Others held that it means a surah from a human like Muhammad. Al-Tabari chooses the first as sound, because in another surah God says "bring a surah like it", and a surah is plainly not a likeness of Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 374}

Al-Tabari answers the question whether the Qur'an has any "like" to bring: he says the sense is a likeness in Arabic expression, not in the composition and meanings by which the Qur'an stands apart from all other speech, for which it has no equal. God did not ask them for a surah in a tongue other than theirs, which would give them an excuse; He asked for one in their own language, and if Muhammad invented it, they together should be better able to match it than he alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 375} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 376}
On "and call your witnesses besides God" he reports from Ibn Abbas that these are their helpers in their position, and from Mujahid and Ibn Jurayj that they are people who would testify for them, Ibn Jurayj adding that they would testify, once the surah was produced, that it matched the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 376} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 377}
He says "call" means seek help, and "shuhada'" is the plural of "shahid", a witness or one who is present with another. He prefers Ibn Abbas: summon your helpers who support you in denial, to test whether you can match it. He rejects the Mujahid and Ibn Jurayj reading because people in the Prophet's time were three kinds, believers, hypocrites and unbelievers, and believers could not be imagined testifying to such a falsehood. This is his own choice. He points to a verse of surat al-Isra that humans and jinn together could not produce its like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 378}
