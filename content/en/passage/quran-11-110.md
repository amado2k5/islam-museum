**Commentary on verse 110 (p. 493)**

On [[passage/quran-11-110|11:110]] God consoles His Prophet for his polytheist people's denial of him by what the children of Israel did to Moses: do not grieve, Muhammad, at these polytheists' denying you, but go on with what your Lord commanded of conveying His message; what they do to you in rejecting your counsel is what their like among earlier nations did, one of their customs. Then He told what Moses's people did, saying 'We gave Moses the Book', meaning the Torah, as We gave you the Furqan, and Moses's people differed over that Book, some denying it and some believing it, as your people did with the Furqan; 'and had it not been for a word that went forth from your Lord', that He does not hasten punishment on His creatures but waits until the book reaches its term, 'it would have been decided between them', that is, between denier and believer by destroying the denier and saving the believer, 'and they are in doubt about it, suspicious', that is, those who deny it are in doubt of its truth that it is from God, and it troubles them so they do not know whether it is true or false; but they are in dispute over it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 493}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
