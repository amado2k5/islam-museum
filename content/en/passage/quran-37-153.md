## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 153 to 157 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Choose daughters over sons": God reproaches those who say daughters are among the polytheists of Quraysh: "Choose Allah, O people, 'Daughters over sons'?" When the Arabs direct the interrogative to reproach, they sometimes affirm the interrogative alif and sometimes raise it, as it is said: 'Idhafatam' with a shortened letter meaning 'your good things in your worldly life,' which is used to interrogate but not to be questioned. The meaning in both cases is the same. If it is not questioned in the phrase 'I chose the girls,' the alif is chosen in the connected, beginning with a kasra, and when it is questioned, it is opened and cut. It has been mentioned from some people of Medina that he read this by leaving out interrogative and connecting. As for the reciters of Kufa and Basra, they read it with interrogative and fatha in all cases, which is the reading we choose for the consensus of the argument among the readers. In the passage, he quotes Qatadah.
