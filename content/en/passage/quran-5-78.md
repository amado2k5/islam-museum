**Commentary on verse 78: "Those of the Children of Israel who disbelieved were cursed by David and Jesus", pp. 489-494**

Al-Tabari says: God tells His prophet to say to these Christians: do not exaggerate by saying of the Messiah what is not true, and do not say of him what the Jews said, whom God cursed through His prophets and messengers David and Jesus son of Mary. Report 12298, from Ibn Abbas, says they were cursed in every tongue: in the days of Moses in the Torah, of David in the Psalms, of Jesus in the Gospel and of Muhammad in the Qur'an; report 12299 gives the like for the Gospel and the Psalms.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 489}

Report 12300, from Said ibn Jubayr on Ibn Abbas, says they mixed with the wrongdoers in their trade after the prohibition, so God struck their hearts against one another and they were cursed by David and Jesus. Report 12301, from Mujahid, says they were cursed through David and became apes, and through Jesus and became swine. Report 12302 gives Ibn Abbas on cursing in every tongue from Ibn Jurayj, then another view that David passed a group of them in a house, asked who was inside, was told swine, and prayed, so they became swine; later Jesus prayed that God curse whoever slandered him and his mother and make them apes.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 490}

Report 12303, from Qatada, says God made them apes through David in his time and swine through Jesus in the Gospel, and reports 12304 and 12305, from Abu Malik, say they were transformed into apes through David and swine through Jesus. In a note on one narrator the editor says he was reliable but hostile to Ali, and that al-Hakim did not find him strong.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 490}

Report 12306, from Ibn Mas'ud on the Prophet, says that a man of the Children of Israel who saw his brother in sin would forbid him half-heartedly, and the next day what he had seen would not stop him from eating, drinking and mixing with him; so God struck their hearts against one another and cursed them through David and Jesus, "for they disobeyed and transgressed". The Prophet swore that they must enjoin the right, forbid the wrong, take the wrongdoer by the hand and bend him to the truth, or God would strike their hearts together and curse them as He cursed those. The editor explains the word for half-heartedness as falling short in forbidding and flattering the disobedient, and sets out how he reads the word for bending in the manuscript.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 491}

Report 12307, from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, says that when wrongdoing spread among the Children of Israel a man would meet another and say "fear God", then not refrain from eating and drinking with him; when God saw that He struck their hearts together and sent down these verses. The Prophet, who had been reclining, sat up and said "No, by Him in whose hand is my soul, until you bend the wrongdoer to the truth." The editor discusses the chains and judges this one weak because of a break.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 492}

Reports 12308 to 12311, from Sufyan and others on Ali ibn Budhayma from Abu Ubayda, some through Ibn Mas'ud and some without him, carry one sense: when wrongdoing appeared among the Children of Israel a man would see his brother or neighbour at it and forbid him yet remain his eating and drinking companion, so God struck their hearts together; and the Prophet sat up angry and said no, by God, until you take the wrongdoer by the hand and bend him to the truth. The editor traces the report to al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Ahmad and Abu Dawud, notes that most chains are mursal or broken, and that al-Tirmidhi called it good but rare.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 494}

We stop here at the foot of p. 494, after report 12311 from Ali ibn Budhayma on bending the wrongdoer to the truth; the rest of verse 78 follows in the next batch. The pages of commentary end at p. 585, before the indexes beginning at p. 587.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
