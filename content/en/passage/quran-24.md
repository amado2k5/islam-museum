## Summary
Surat an-Nur: surah 24 in the order of the Mushaf, Medinan, 64 verses, and revelation order 102 among the surahs. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Content
Its verses are recorded in the museum one by one under the records passage/quran-24-‹verse number›.

## In Tantawi's al-Tafsir al-Wasit
In the "introduction and preface" to the sura in [[work/al-tafsir-al-wasit|al-Tafsir al-Wasit]], [[person/muhammad-sayyid-tantawi|Tantawi]] says that it is Medinan, has 64 verses and came down after Surat al-Nasr, and that it contains the rules of chastity and modesty which he sees as the mainstay of a sound society. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4392} He quotes from al-Alusi a reported hadith, "Teach your men Surat al-Ma'ida and teach your women Surat al-Nur", and a report from Haritha ibn Mudarrib that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] wrote to them to learn Surat al-Nisa', al-Ahzab and al-Nur. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4392}

He then sets out its themes: it begins by declaring the duty to submit to its rulings, then gives the penalty for fornication, then the ruling on those who accuse chaste women, and on a husband who accuses his wife with no witness but himself. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4392} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4393} Sixteen of its verses tell the story of the slander against [[person/aisha|Aisha]]; in Tantawi's reading they warn of the punishment awaiting those who spread the slander, urge verification of reports and good opinion of fellow believers, and close the story with her vindication; see [[event/incident-of-the-slander|the incident of the slander]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4393} Then come the etiquette of asking permission to enter, the lowering of the gaze by men and women, the encouragement to marry off the unmarried, and the declaration that God is the light of the heavens and the earth. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.4393}
