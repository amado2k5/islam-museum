**Commentary on verse 149 (pp. 118-120)**

Al-Tabari says: "when it fell upon their hands" means when those who worshipped the calf regretted, at Moses' return to them, and submitted to Moses and his judgement about them. The Arabs say this of anyone remorseful over something that has passed and helpless in it: "it fell in his hands" or "was made to fall", two eloquent forms. Its origin is in taking captive: a man strikes or throws another, hurling him from his hands to the ground to take him prisoner and bind him, so the one thrown is fallen into the hands of the one who threw him; so it was said of anyone helpless, submissive in his helplessness and regretting what he said. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, and says al-Tabari's detail is good and an account of the word's origin that one rarely finds in lexicons. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 118}

By "and they saw they had gone astray" he means they saw that they had swerved from the straight path, left God's religion and disbelieved in their Lord, and said, repenting to God and returning to Him from their unbelief, that if our Lord does not have mercy on us and forgive us we shall be among the losers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 119}

The readers then differed over its reading. Some readers of Medina, Mecca, Kufa and Basra read "if our Lord does not show mercy" with the third person and "our Lord" in the nominative, as a statement. Most readers of Kufa read "if You do not show us mercy, our Lord" with the second person and "our Lord" in the vocative, as their address to their Lord. Those who read so argued that in one of the two readings it is "they said, our Lord, if You do not show us mercy and forgive us", which indicates address. The editor explains that this is the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b and is also in the codex of Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, as al-Farra mentions, and that the printed text's editor rearranged the words and spoiled the evidence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 119}

Al-Tabari prefers the reading as a statement, with the third person and "our Lord" in the nominative, because nothing precedes it that requires it to be an address; as for the reading "they said, our Lord", its soundness is not known from a route to which one must defer. The meaning of "if our Lord does not have mercy on us and forgive us" is: if our Lord does not turn to us with repentance by His mercy and cover our sins with it, we shall be among the destroyed whose deeds are void. The editor refers to the explanations of "mercy", "forgiveness" and "loss". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 120}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
