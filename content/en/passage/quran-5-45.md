## Summary
Verse 45 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 45 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 45: "We prescribed for them in it a life for a life, an eye for an eye", pp. 358-359**

Al-Tabari says: We prescribed for these Jews who make you judge, Muhammad, while they have the Torah with God's ruling in it, meaning We made it binding on them in it to judge in a life: when a person kills another without right the killer is killed for the one killed; and "an eye for an eye", so the eye that blinded another's is blinded in return, the nose cut off for the nose, the ear cut for the ear, the tooth pulled for the tooth, and retaliation taken from one who wounded another unjustly.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 358}

This is God's report to His prophet about the Jews and a consolation for the unbelief of those of them who disbelieved in him after acknowledging his prophethood, and a notice of their boldness old and new against their Lord and His messengers and their going ahead of God's book with distortion and change. God says to him: how can these Jews accept your ruling, when they come asking you to judge and they have the Torah, which they confess is My book and revelation to My messenger Moses, in which is My ruling of stoning for married adulterers and My decree among them that whoever kills a person unjustly is killed for it, whoever blinds an eye without right has his eye blinded in retaliation, whoever cuts off a nose has his nose cut, whoever pulls a tooth has his pulled, and whoever wounds another is retaliated against with a like wound; yet with the ruling they have in the Torah they turn away from it and leave off acting by it, so they are the more likely to leave your ruling and resent your judgement among them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 359}

The interpreters spoke likewise. Report 12064, from Ibn Jurayj, says that when Qurayza saw the Prophet had ruled stoning, which they had been hiding in their book, Qurayza rose and said: Muhammad, judge between us and our brothers.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 359}

The rest of report 12064, from Ibn Jurayj, has Qurayza speak of a feud with Banu al-Nadir from before the Prophet's coming, al-Nadir having treated Qurayza as inferior and their blood-money at half that of al-Nadir; the Prophet said the blood of the Qurayzi is the equal of the blood of the Nadiri; al-Nadir were angry and said we will not obey you in stoning but will keep our own penalties, so the verse of [[passage/quran-5-50|5:50]] was revealed, and "We prescribed for them in it a life for a life".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 360}

Report 12065, from Ibn 'Abbas, asks what is the matter with them that they differ, killing two for one and putting out two eyes for one. Report 12066, from Abu Malik, says two tribes of the Ansar fought and there were dead, one tribe having the upper hand, and the Prophet made free for free, slave for slave and woman for woman, so [[passage/quran-2-178|2:178]] was revealed; Sufyan was told from Ibn 'Abbas that the verse of a life for a life abrogated it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 360}

Report 12067, from Mujahid, from Ibn 'Abbas, says the Children of Israel had retaliation for the slain and no blood-money for life or wound, and God lightened this for Muhammad's community by making blood-money for life and wounds, a lightening and mercy from your Lord. Report 12068, from Ibn 'Abbas, says the Children of Israel had no blood-money for what God prescribed for Moses in the Torah, whether a life, wound, tooth, eye or nose, only retaliation or pardon. Reports 12069, 12070 and 12071, from Qatada and Ibn Zayd, say "We prescribed for them in it" means in the Torah, and wounds are taken one for another; report 12072, from Ibn 'Abbas, says a life is taken for a life, an eye for an eye, a nose cut for a nose, a tooth pulled for a tooth, and wounds retaliated.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 361}

Al-Tabari says that in this, free Muslims are equal among themselves, men and women, in a life and what is short of a life, and slaves, men and women, are equal among themselves when it is deliberate, in a life and what is short of a life.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 362}

**Commentary on verse 45, continued: "whoever gives it as charity, it is an expiation for him", pp. 362-366**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over who is meant. Some said the wounded and the guardian of the slain. Reports 12073, 12074 and 12075, from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr, say it removes from him, the wounded, as much of his sins; reports 12076 to 12079, from Ibrahim and Jabir ibn Zayd, say it is for the wounded. Report 12080, from Abu al-Safar, says a Qurayshi pushed an Ansari and broke his front tooth, and he took it to Mu'awiya; Abu al-Darda' said he heard the Prophet say that no Muslim is hurt in his body and forgives it but God raises him a degree and removes a sin from him, and the Qurayshi was let go. The editor notes a break in its chain. Report 12081, from 'Ubada ibn al-Samit, from the Prophet, says whoever is wounded in his body and gives it as charity, his sins are expiated by as much as he gave.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 363}

Reports 12082, 12083 and 12084, from al-Hasan, 'Amir and Qatada, say it is an expiation for the wounded and for the slain man's guardian who pardoned, and report 12085, from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr, is like report 12075.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 365}

Others said the wounder is meant, and the sense of the verse is that whoever gives as charity what is due him of retaliation against one who owes it and pardons him, that pardon of the offender is an expiation for the offender's sin, as retaliation from him would be, while the reward of the pardoner is with God. Report 12086, from Ibn 'Abbas, says it is an expiation for the wounder and the reward of the one hurt is with God; reports 12087, 12088 and 12089, from Mujahid, say it is for the wounder who sinned.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 366}

We stop here at the foot of p. 366, at report 12090 from Ibrahim and Mujahid on charity toward the wounder; the rest of verse 45 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
