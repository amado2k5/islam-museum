**Commentary on verse 110 (pp. 494-498)**

Al-Tabari says: the building of those who took a mosque for harm and unbelief will not cease to be "a doubt", meaning their mosque which they built will not cease to be a doubt in their hearts, meaning uncertainty and hypocrisy in their hearts, supposing they were doing good in building it; "until their hearts are cut" means until their hearts break apart and they die; "and God is knowing" of the state of these hypocrites who built the mosque of harm, of their doubt in their religion, of what they aimed at and wished in building it, of what their affair is coming to in the hereafter and in life as long as they live and of other things of their affair and the affair of others, "wise" in His management of them and of all His creatures. The editor refers to earlier explanations of doubt, knowledge and wisdom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 495}

What the interpreters said agrees with what we said. Report 17251, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdallah, Mu'awiya and Ali: "their building which they built will not cease to be a doubt in their hearts", meaning uncertainty, "until their hearts are cut", meaning death. Report 17252, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "a doubt in their hearts", he said, uncertainty in their hearts, "until their hearts are cut", until they die; report 17253, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: until they die. Reports 17254, 17255, 17256 and 17257, from Mujahid, by the routes of Matar ibn Muhammad al-Dabbi, Abu Qutayba, Shu'ba, al-Hakam, Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Abdallah and Warqa': "until their hearts are cut", he said, unless they die. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 495}

Report 17258, from Qatada and al-Hasan, through Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak and Ma'mar: "their building which they built will not cease to be a doubt in their hearts", they said, uncertainty in their hearts. Report 17259, from Habib, through Ibn Waki', Ishaq al-Razi and Abu Sinan: "a doubt in their hearts", he said, rage in their hearts; reports 17260 and 17261, from Mujahid and Habib, say the same as above about death. Report 17262, from al-Suddi, through Qabisa and Sufyan: "a doubt in their hearts", he said, unbelief; I said "did Mujammi' ibn Jariya disbelieve?", and he said "no, but a stinging irritation"; report 17263, from him, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Sufyan: a stinging irritation in their hearts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 496}

Report 17264, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "their building which they built will not cease to be a doubt in their hearts", it will not cease to be a doubt in their hearts, content with what they did, as the love of the calf was in the hearts of the companions of Moses, and he recited "they were made to drink the calf into their hearts for their unbelief", which is [[passage/quran-2-93|2:93]], meaning its love; "until their hearts are cut", he said, it will not cease to be in their hearts until they die, meaning the hypocrites. Report 17265, from Ibrahim, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Qays and al-Suddi: "a doubt in their hearts", he said, uncertainty; I said "Abu Imran, you say this having read the Qur'an?", and he said "it is only a stinging irritation". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 497}

The readers differed in reading His words "until their hearts are cut". Some of the readers of the Hijaz, Medina, Basra and Kufa read it with a damma on the first letter, as a passive, with the sense unless God cuts their hearts. Some of the readers of Medina and Kufa read it with a fatha, as the verb belonging to the hearts, meaning unless their hearts are cut to pieces, one of the two "t" letters being dropped. It is reported that al-Hasan used to read "up to when their hearts are cut", meaning until their hearts are cut. It is reported that in the reading of Abdallah it is "even if their hearts were cut", and on the strength of that those who read the passive read it so. The editor refers to al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 497}

Al-Tabari holds that the fatha and the damma are close in meaning, since hearts are not cut to pieces when they are cut except by God's cutting them, and God does not cut them unless they are cut up; they are two well-known readings, each read by a group of readers, so whichever the reader reads he is right in his reading. As for reading it "up to when", it is a reading contrary to the codices of the Muslims, and he does not consider reading against their codices permissible. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 498}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
