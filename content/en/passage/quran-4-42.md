## Summary
Verse 42 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 42 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the verse (p. 371, note 1).** Al-Tabari says: on the day when a witness is brought from each community and Muhammad as witness over his community, those who denied God's oneness and disobeyed his messenger wish to become one with the ground. Note 1 refers to the earlier discussion of the verb of wishing (the volume and page numbers are unclear in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 371}

**The readings (pp. 372-373).** The reciters differed. Most reciters of the Hijaz, Mecca and Medina read it with the doubled sin and waw and a vowel a on the first letter, the verb being, in their view, a form that lost one of two identical letters through assimilation; it means they wish to become dust and so equal the earth. Most reciters of Kufa read it with a on the first letter and a light sin, with the same meaning, and gave up the doubling on the ground that the Arabs scarcely combine two doubled letters in one word. Others read it passive, with u on the first letter, meaning that God makes them even with the earth so that they become dust like it by his making them so, as is done, it is reported, with beasts. Abu Ja'far says these readings are close in meaning and a reciter is right whichever he follows, since whoever wishes to be dust wishes it through God's making him so, and whoever wishes God had made him so has wished to become dust. Still, the reading he finds most admirable is the second, with the light sin, to avoid two doublings in one word and to harmonise it with [[passage/quran-78-40|78:40]], where the unbeliever wishes to have been dust (the verse number is smudged in the scan and read from the sense): God reports that they wish to have been dust and does not report that they said so, and likewise here they wish to be made even with it and so become dust; he prefers it for agreeing with the meaning God reported of them there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 373}

**What they conceal and do not (p. 373, reports 9520-9521).** As for the last clause, about concealing speech, the interpreters took it to mean that their limbs will not conceal anything from God even if their mouths deny it. Ibn Humayd related from Hakkam, from Amr, from Mutarrif, from al-Minhal b. Amr, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9520) that a man came to Ibn Abbas, citing the polytheists' denial of polytheism in [[passage/quran-6-23|6:23]] and the verse here that says nothing is concealed. Ibn Abbas said that when they saw that only the people of Islam enter paradise they said to one another: come, let us deny; so they denied, and God sealed their mouths and their hands and feet spoke, so nothing of theirs stayed hidden. Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from a man, from al-Minhal b. Amr, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9521) that a man came to Ibn Abbas saying things in the Qur'an conflicted for him; Ibn Abbas asked whether he doubted it, and he said no, it was a conflict, citing the two places and saying they had concealed. Ibn Abbas said that when they saw on the day of resurrection that God forgives the people of Islam, forgives sins but not polytheism, and no sin is too great for him to forgive, the polytheists denied in hope of forgiveness; their mouths were sealed and their hands and feet spoke of what they had done, and then they wished what the verse says. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 373}

**Nafi b. al-Azraq and Ibn Abbas (p. 374, reports 9522-9523, notes 1-3).** Al-Muthanna related from Muslim b. Ibrahim, from al-Qasim, from al-Zubayr, from al-Dahhak (9522) that Nafi b. al-Azraq came to Ibn Abbas and asked about the two places. Ibn Abbas said he supposed Nafi had risen from among his companions saying he would put to Ibn Abbas what is obscure in the Qur'an, and told him to say to them, on return, that God gathers the people on the day of resurrection in one open ground (note 1: a wide stretch of land with some trees); the polytheists say God accepts nothing from anyone but those who affirmed his oneness, and so say: come, let us say (note 2). He asks them and they deny polytheism; their mouths are sealed, their limbs are made to speak and testify that they were polytheists, and then they wish what the verse says. Note 2: the editor kept the manuscript wording, where the printed edition had a different word whose sense its editor could not find; he holds it right, though the dictionaries fall short in recording this sense, since the verb here comes from speaking and means lying or hinting at it, and he cites the Sira of Ibn Hisham (3:58), in the account of the killing of Ka'b b. al-Ashraf, where Muhammad b. Maslama tells the Prophet they must say things and the Prophet answers that they may say what they see fit and are free of blame, which is close to lying. Muhammad b. Sa'd related from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas (9523) that the meaning is that the earth be made even with the mountains and the earth be over them (note 3: the printed edition drops the second word for earth; the editor says the manuscript is right). Then al-Tabari begins to give the sense of the verse on this view reported from Ibn Abbas, and the sentence breaks off at the end of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 374}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
