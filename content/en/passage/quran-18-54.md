## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "And indeed, We have directed in this Qur'an for mankind every parable, and man is the most contentious of things": He, the Exalted, says: Indeed, We have set forth in this Qur'an for mankind every example, and We admonished them therein of every admonition, and We argued against them with every argument so that they might remember, so they may respond, take heed, take heed, and turn away from what they are established in, such as associating partners with Allah and idol worship. "And man was the most contentious of things." He says: Man was the most bitter and quarrelsome thing, neither rejecting the truth nor being deterred by admonition, as Ibn Zayd said regarding the phrase: "Man was the most contentious of arguments," he said: Dispute means the dispute, the quarrels of people against their prophets, and their refutation of what they brought. He recited: "This is nothing but a human like you who eats from what you eat," from which he says: "and drinks from what you drink," and he reads: "He wants to bestow favor upon you," and he recited: "until he died..." In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Zayd.
