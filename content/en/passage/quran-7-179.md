**Commentary on verse 179 (pp. 276-281)**

Al-Tabari says: We created for Gehenna many of the jinn and men; one says "God dhara'a His creation, yadhra'uhum dhar'an", and the editor refers to the earlier explanation of the verb. The interpreters said much the same: reports 15443 and 15444, from al-Hasan: We created; report 15445, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: children of fornication are among those God created for Gehenna; report 15446, from Abd Allah ibn Amr from the Prophet: when God created for Gehenna those He created, the child of fornication was among those He created for Gehenna, the editor noting that its chain is weak because of the unknown companion in al-Ta'if of Mu'awiya ibn Ishaq, identifying its men, and that al-Suyuti added Ibn Abi Hatim, Abu al-Shaykh and Ibn Mardawayh. Reports 15447, from al-Suddi, 15448, from Mujahid, and 15449, from Ibn Abbas: We created. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 276} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 277} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 278}

Al-Tabari says: God said "We have created for Gehenna many of the jinn and men" because His knowledge had gone out over them that they would go to it by their unbelief in their Lord. As for "they have hearts with which they do not understand", the sense is that those whom God created for Gehenna have hearts with which they do not reflect on God's signs, do not ponder His evidence for His oneness and do not take lesson from His proofs for His messengers, so that they might know their Lord's oneness and the truth of their prophets' prophethood; He describes them as not understanding with them because they turn from the truth and leave off pondering the soundness of the messengers' prophethood. Likewise "and they have eyes with which they do not see", meaning they do not look with them at God's signs and evidences to consider and reflect on them; He describes them as not seeing with them for their not using them for the truth. Likewise "and they have ears with which they do not hear" the verses of God's book to take lesson from them, but they turn from them and say what is in [[passage/quran-41-26|41:26]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 278}

Al-Tabari says this is like His description of them elsewhere as deaf, dumb and blind, [[passage/quran-2-171|2:171]], and the Arabs say it of one who does not use some of his faculties for what they are fit for. He cites the verse of Miskin al-Darimi, who describes himself as blind when his neighbour goes out until the curtain covers her and deaf to what passed between them, describing himself as blind and deaf for leaving off looking and listening; and the verse of Abd Allah ibn Murra al-Ijli, on a vile word from base people that he turned away from and restrained himself in anger that almost split his ribs. The editor gives the sources of both and the right wording of the lines, explains "awra" as the ugly word, and adds lines by Hatim al-Ta'i or al-A'war al-Shanni on returning the ugly word with restraint. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 278} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 279}

Al-Tabari says this is common in the speech and poetry of the Arabs. The interpreters said much the same, and report 15450, from Mujahid: "they have hearts with which they do not understand", understanding with them nothing of the matter of the hereafter, "and they have eyes with which they do not see" guidance, "and they have ears with which they do not hear" the truth; then He made them like cattle, equally, then made them worse than cattle, saying "nay, they are further astray", then He told that they are the heedless. The editor notes that the manuscript has "equally worse" and that he keeps the word "equally" where it stands. In a note on the preceding verses he explains "badira" as the slips and lapses that run ahead of a man when he is angry, "to restrain the self" as holding it back, and "to be filled" as being full of rage and anger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 279} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 280}
On "those are like cattle; nay, they are further astray; those are the heedless", al-Tabari says: those whom He created for Gehenna are like cattle, the beasts that do not understand what is said to them and do not grasp what they see of what is fit and unfit and do not tell with their hearts good from evil so as to distinguish between them; God likened them to cattle since they do not recall what they see with their eyes of His proofs and do not reflect on what they hear of the verses of His book. Then He said "nay, they are further astray", meaning further gone from the truth and clinging more to the way of falsehood than the beasts, because beasts have no choice or discernment but are subjected, and even so flee from harms and seek for themselves the more suitable food, whereas those whom God describes, with the understanding and the reason that tells benefit from harm they were given, leave what is good for their world and hereafter and seek what harms them, so the beasts are more rightly guided than they and they are further astray. And "those are the heedless" means those I have described are the ones who were heedless, meaning inattentive, of My signs and proofs, who left pondering them, taking lesson from them and inferring from them what they point to of their Lord's oneness, not the beasts, whom their Lord has taught what He made them subject to. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "cattle", "straying" and "heedless". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 280} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 281}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
