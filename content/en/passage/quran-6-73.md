## Summary
Verse 73 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 73 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "it is he who created the heavens and the earth in truth; and on the day he says 'be' and it is, his word is the truth; his is the dominion on the day the trumpet is blown, knower of the unseen and the witnessed, and he is the wise, the aware" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to those who hold rivals equal to their Lord and call you to worship idols: we are commanded to submit to the Lord of the worlds, who created the heavens and the earth in truth, not one who neither benefits nor harms, neither hears nor sees. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 458}

The interpreters differed on "in truth". Some said it means he created the heavens and the earth rightly and soundly, not in vanity and error, as [[passage/quran-38-27|38:27]], "we did not create the heaven and the earth and what is between them in vain"; they said the "ba" and the article are put in as the Arabs do in similar cases, saying "so-and-so speaks in truth" meaning he speaks the truth, and there is nothing in "his speaking in truth" but his hitting on the right in it, not that truth is a meaning other than the speech but an attribute of it; and likewise the creation of the heavens and the earth is a wisdom among God's wisdoms, God being described by wisdom in creating them and what else he created, not that there is a truth, other than their creation, by which he created them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 458}

The editor says the printed text spoiled the wording by omitting, changing and corrupting, vowelling "siwa" as a doubled verb and making "created them by it" a verbal noun; the right form is the manuscript's, "siwa" with kasra meaning "other than", the first "creation of them" a verbal noun in the genitive and "created them by it" a past verb, and he calls the printed version the meddling of publishers and correctors.

Others said it means he created the heavens and the earth by his speech and his word to them, [[passage/quran-41-11|41:11]], "come willingly or unwillingly"; they said truth here means his speech, citing "on the day he says 'be' and it is, his word is the truth", truth being his word and speech; and they said God created things by his speech and utterance, and what he created things by is other than the things created; so, this being so, God's speech by which he created the creation must be uncreated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 459}

As for "on the day he says 'be' and it is", the grammarians differed over what governs "the day he says" and its sense. A Basran grammarian said the day is annexed to "he says 'be' and it is", is in the accusative and has no apparent predicate, meaning that it is accusative as "remember the day he says 'be' and it is", and likewise "the day the trumpet is blown"; another said "he says 'be' and it is" is for the trumpet alone, so that the sense on their reading is: the day he says to the trumpet "be", and it is, his word is the truth the day it is blown, knower of the unseen and the witnessed; the word is then nominative by "the truth" and "the truth" is the word, and "the day he says be" and "the day the trumpet is blown" are connected with the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 459}

The editor says the first passage in the manuscript has a gap and repetition and the printed text is nearer the right; that the printed wording "as he created by it the uncreated things" is empty and corrupt in sense, the manuscript's wording being garbled, and he inferred the right reading from the sentence that follows: what he created things by is other than the created things, and being other it is uncreated; that the sentence "and some said the day the trumpet is blown..." he could not place here and fears something has fallen out, the saying after it being al-Farra's in Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

Others said "be and it is" means everything God will restore in the next world after its perishing and bring back after its non-being, so that on their view the speech ends at "be and it is", and "his word is the truth" is the predicate of a subject, the sense being: it is he who created the heavens and the earth in truth, and the day he says to things "be" they are, their creation in truth after their perishing; then the report begins of his word and his promise to his creatures that he will restore them after perishing as a truth, saying: this word of his is the truth, in which there is no doubt, and he reported that his is the dominion the day the trumpet is blown, so that "the day the trumpet is blown" on this reading belongs with "the dominion", and it may also belong with "the truth". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 460}

Others said the sense is: on the day he says to what has perished "be", his word is the truth, making the word nominative by "the day he says be and it is", with "be and it is" a place for the word and "the day the trumpet is blown" connected with the truth, as though the sense turned to "on that day his word is the truth, the day the trumpet is blown"; if on this reading "the day the trumpet is blown" is made a clarification of the first day, it is a sound way, and if "his word is the truth" were made nominative by "the day the trumpet is blown", with "the day he says be and it is" attached to it, it would be allowable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 460}

Al-Tabari holds that the right view is that God reported that he alone created the heavens and the earth apart from everything else, making known to those who associate others with him their ignorance in worshipping idols and images and the error of what they persist in, worshipping what neither harms nor benefits nor can bring benefit to itself or ward off harm from itself, and arguing against their denial of resurrection after death, reward and punishment, by his power to originate it in the first place, since one who originated it will find it no hardship to destroy it and bring it back after destroying it. He says: "it is he who created", you who hold equal to your Lord what neither benefits nor harms, "the heavens and the earth in truth", a proof against his creation that they may know their maker by it and take it as evidence of his great power and authority, and make worship pure for him; "and on the day he says 'be' and it is" says: the day he says, when the earth is changed into other than the earth and the heavens likewise, "be", and it is as he wills, so that the earth is other than the earth, and the speech ends at "be and it is". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 461}

The editor says the printed wording is faulty, that the copyist dropped what he put in brackets, which makes the sentence right, and that the bracketed words are needed and "tubaddal" is sounder than the manuscript's "tubaddilu".

Since this is the sense there must be an omitted word the apparent speech shows, the sense being: on the day he says likewise "be and it is", the heavens and the earth are changed into other than the heavens and the earth; and "it is he who created the heavens and the earth in truth" shows this; then the report begins about the word: "his word is the truth", meaning this promise he promised, of changing the heavens and the earth into other than the earth and heavens, is the truth with no doubt; "his is the dominion the day the trumpet is blown", so "the day the trumpet is blown" belongs with "the dominion", the sense being: God's is the dominion on that day, because the second blast in the trumpet is the time when God changes the heavens and the earth into others. It is also allowable that "his word is the truth" be nominative by "on the day he says be and it is", with "be and it is" a place for the word, so that the sense is: it is he who created the heavens and the earth in truth, and on the day he changes them into other than the heavens and the earth and says to that "be" and it is, his word is the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 462}

As for "his is the dominion the day the trumpet is blown", he singled out the report of his dominion on that day though the dominion is his alone at every time in this world and the next, because he meant that no one disputes it with him that day and no one claims it, and that he is alone in it apart from all who disputed it with him in this world among the tyrants, all of whom submit to him in it that day and know that their claim in this world was false. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 462}

There was disagreement over the sense of "the trumpet" here. Some said it is a horn blown twice, once for the perishing of all living on earth and once for the raising of every dead one, and they reasoned from [[passage/quran-39-68|39:68]], "the trumpet is blown and all in the heavens and the earth swoon but those God wills; then it is blown again and they stand looking on", and from the report that when God's messenger was asked about the trumpet he said it is a horn that is blown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 462}

The editor says Ahmad related it in the Musnad of Abdullah ibn Amr, Abu Dawud in his Sunan, al-Tirmidhi in the chapter on the trumpet, calling it good and sound, and al-Hakim in the Mustadrak, calling its chain sound, al-Dhahabi agreeing; and explains "qarn" as a bugle made from horns.

Others said "the trumpet" here is the plural of "sura", form, into which its spirit is blown and it lives, as they say "sur" for the wall of a city, the plural of "sura"; al-Tabari adduces a line of Jarir for this; the Arabs say both "he blew into the trumpet" and "he blew the trumpet", and for the second he cites a line by a poet the editor did not identify, saying that but for Ibn Jam'da Quhandiz and Khurasan would not have been conquered until the trumpet is blown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 463}

The editor says the printed text and manuscript have a particle that should be "like", that the line is in al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, Nasab Quraysh, al-Jawaliqi's al-Mu'arrab and Lisan al-Arab, that Ibn Jam'da is Abdullah ibn Ja'da ibn Hubayra al-Makhzumi, whose father Ja'da was governor of Khurasan for Ali ibn Abi Talib, and that "quhandiz" is a word of the people of Khurasan meaning a fort or citadel.

Abu Jafar said: the right view in this for us is what the reports from God's messenger agree on, that he said Israfil has put the trumpet to his mouth and bent his brow, waiting for the command to blow, and that he said the trumpet is a horn that is blown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 463}

The editor says al-Tirmidhi related it in the chapter on the trumpet and at the opening of the commentary on Surat al-Zumar, and that Ibn Kathir mentioned it and ascribed it to Muslim, but he could not find its place in Sahih Muslim.

It is reported from Ibn Abbas that he said of "the day the trumpet is blown, knower of the unseen and the witnessed" that it means the knower of the unseen and the witnessed is the one who blows the trumpet (13432). Ibn Abbas seems to have taken "knower of the unseen and the witnessed" as the unnamed agent in "is blown", the sense being the day God blows the trumpet, knower of the unseen and the witnessed, as the Arabs say "ate your food, Abdullah", naming the eater after the report has run without naming him; though this is a view not to be rejected, it is better that "knower of the unseen and the witnessed" be nominative as an attribute of "who" in "it is he who created the heavens and the earth in truth". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 464}

It is also reported from him that "the trumpet" here is the first blast (13433), and he argues from [[passage/quran-39-68|39:68]], where "then it is blown again" is the second. By "knower of the unseen and the witnessed" he means knower of what you perceive, people, and witness, and what is hidden from your senses and sight so that you neither sense nor see it; "he is the wise" in his ordering and turning his creation from being to non-being and then from non-being and perishing to being, and then in repaying them with the reward or punishment he repays them; "the aware" of all they do and earn, good and bad, keeping it against them to repay them for all of it. He says: so beware his punishment, you who hold others equal to your Lord, for he knows all you do and leave, and he stands behind the repayment for what you do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 465}

The editor refers for "blowing", "witnessing", "the unseen", "the wise" and "the aware" to earlier passages.

The editor's supplement (Ahmad Shakir, from the "completion of the tracing"; not al-Tabari's words). On the report of the Horn on p. 462, that it is a horn to be blown: his brother Mahmud traced it to the Musnad, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim, and the editor adds that Ahmad gives it once more (no. 6805) and that al-Hakim has it twice more in the Mustadrak (vol. 2, pp. 436 and 506), grading it sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 590}

On the report on p. 463 that Israfil has put the Horn to his mouth and bent his brow, waiting for the command to blow: Mahmud quoted Ibn Kathir as saying Muslim transmitted it, then added that he could not find it in Sahih Muslim. The editor agrees and says Ibn Kathir erred: al-Nabulusi (Dhakha'ir al-Mawarith, no. 7960) credits Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, not Muslim. Al-Suyuti, in his additions to al-Jami' al-Saghir, has it from Abu Sa'id (crediting Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim) and from Ibn Abbas (crediting Ahmad and al-Hakim); Abu Sa'id's is in the Musnad and in the Mustadrak by two chains, Ibn Abbas's in the Musnad (no. 3010) and the Mustadrak. In the versions the editor saw, Israfil is not named, only "the one of the Horn". He judges both reports weak because their chains are weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 591}

Verse 73 closes here on p. 465, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-74|6:74]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
