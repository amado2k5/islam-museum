## Summary
Verse 167 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 167 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 167: Ibn Ubayy and those who turned back from Uhud (8193-8198, pp. 377-381).** The verse begins with the clause joined to the preceding, that God willed to know those who dissembled (see [[passage/quran-3-166|3:166]] and report 8192, which closes on this sense, p. 378). Abu Ja'far then says that what is meant is 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul the hypocrite and his companions, who turned back from the Messenger of God and his companions when the Prophet marched to the idolaters at Uhud. The Muslims said to them: come and fight with us, or repel for us by swelling our numbers; and they said: if we knew you were going to fight we would go with you, but we do not see that there will be fighting between you and the people; and they disclosed with their tongues something other than the enmity they were hiding towards the Messenger and the people of faith in him (p. 378). Ibn Ishaq (8193), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama, who said: Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Hibban, 'Asim ibn 'Umar ibn Qatada, al-Husayn ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Amr ibn Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and others of our scholars told me, all of them having related: the Messenger of God set out for Uhud with a thousand of his companions, and when they were at al-Shawt between Uhud and Medina, 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul withdrew from them with a third of the people and said: he obeyed them and went out and disobeyed me; by God, we do not know why we should kill ourselves here, people! So he went back with those of his people who followed him from the hypocrites and the doubters, and 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr ibn Haram, brother of Banu Salima, followed them saying: my people, I remind you of God, do not forsake your prophet and your people when their enemy is present. They said: if we knew you would fight we would not hand you over, but we do not see that there will be fighting. When they stubbornly refused him and would only go back, he said: God keep you far, enemies of God; God will do without you! And the Messenger of God went on. The editor notes that the printed text has "so he said" where the manuscript and Sira are right, and that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 68), following the earlier report (7715 as printed), with some differences of wording between al-Tabari's and Ibn Hisham's versions (pp. 378-379). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 378} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 379}

Ibn Ishaq (8194), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: it means 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy and his companions who went back from the Messenger of God when he marched to his idolater enemy at Uhud; their saying means that had they known you would fight they would have accompanied you and defended you, but they did not think there would be fighting; what they were hiding within themselves showed, and God says that they were that day nearer to disbelief than to belief, showing you faith that was not in their hearts, and God knows best what they conceal. The editor notes that the printed text and manuscript are disturbed and defective through the copyist's slip, that he completed them from the Sira and completed the verse and its interpretation after it, and that the report is in the Sira (3: 125), a continuation of reports ending with 8192. Al-Suddi (8195), through Muhammad, from Ahmad, from Asbat: the Messenger of God went out on the day of Uhud with a thousand men, having promised them victory if they were steadfast; when they set out, 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul went back with three hundred, and Abu Jabir al-Sulami followed them calling them; when they overpowered him and said, we know of no fighting, and if you obey us you will go back with us, God then mentioned Ibn Ubayy's companions and the saying of Abu Jabir (so in the printed text, where the man's name is disturbed) when he called them, and sent down about them what is in [[passage/quran-3-168|3:168]]. The editor says this report is damagingly abridged and its completion came earlier as 7723, that the answer to "when they overpowered him" lies in the rest, namely that they were minded to go back, meaning Banu Salima, the clan of Abu Jabir; and that part of it came as 7723 and is in the Tarikh (3: 12) (pp. 379-380). 'Ikrima (8196), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj: it was sent down about 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul; and Ibn Jurayj, from 'Abdallah ibn Kathir, from Mujahid: if we knew we would find fighting with you, if we knew a place of fighting, we would follow you (p. 380). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 379} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 380}

**The disagreement over "repel" (pp. 380-381).** Some said it means: increase our numbers, for if you do you repel the enemy: al-Suddi (8197), through Muhammad, from Ahmad, from Asbat, and Ibn Jurayj (8198), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj: by your numbers against the enemy even if there is no fighting. Others said it means: keep watch at the frontier (ribat) if you will not fight: Abu 'Awn al-Ansari (8198 repeated, which the editor marks with the letter mim), through Isma'il ibn Hafs al-Ayli and 'Ali ibn Sahl al-Ramli, from al-Walid ibn Muslim, from 'Utba ibn Damra: he said, keep the frontier. In the note on p. 381 the editor says Isma'il ibn Hafs al-Ayli was given a biography at 7581 (the printed text has another form in error and the manuscript an undotted one; he printed the correct reading), al-Walid ibn Muslim al-Qurashi at 4610, and that 'Utba ibn Damra ibn Habib ibn Suhayb al-Zubaydi al-Himsi related from his father, his uncle al-Muhajir, Muhammad ibn Ziyad al-Alhani and Abu 'Awn al-Shami, is listed by Ibn Hibban among the reliable narrators and has an entry in the Tahdhib, and that Abu 'Awn al-Ansari al-Shami al-A'war related from Abu Idris al-Khawlani, is reliable and has an entry in the Tahdhib. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 380} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 381}

**End of the verse (p. 381).** Al-Tabari then says the meaning is that God knows best what these hypocrites hold within themselves against the believers of enmity and hatred, and that had they known of fighting they would not have followed them nor defended them; He encompasses what they hide, is aware of it and counts it against them until He tears their veils in this world and exposes them, and casts them into the lowest depth of the Fire in the next. The editor notes that the printed text has a wording differing from the manuscript for no reason and comments on this with sarcasm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 381}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 381}
