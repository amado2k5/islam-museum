## Summary
Verse 32 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 32 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Reports on the occasion, about Umm Salama (pp. 261-263, reports 9236-9244).** Al-Tabari gives reports for what he has said about the women's longing. Muhammad b. Bashshar (9236, from Mu'ammal, from Sufyan, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) reports that Umm Salama said: Messenger of God, we are not given inheritance and we do not fight in God's way and get killed? So the verse came down. Abu Kurayb (9237, from Mu'awiya b. Hisham, from Sufyan al-Thawri, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) gives her words that the men fight and we do not, and we have only half of the inheritance; the verse came down with its sentence about men and women, and [[passage/quran-33-35|33:35]], on Muslim men and women, came down as well. Al-Muthanna (9238, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) reports that a man should not long by saying he wishes he had the property and family of so-and-so; God forbade this and told him to ask for His gift. Muhammad b. Amr (9239, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says it is the women's saying that they wish they were men so they could fight and reach what men reach; al-Muthanna (9240, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) gives the same. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9241, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) gives Umm Salama's words that men fight and women do not and women have half the inheritance. He also gives (9242, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from an old man of Mecca) that the women used to wish they were men, to strive as men strive. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9243, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from al-Hasan) reports al-Hasan's reproach of wishing for someone's property: how do you know that his ruin is not in that very property? Al-Qasim (9244, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ikrima and Mujahid) says it came down about Umm Salama, daughter of Abu Umayya b. al-Mughira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 263}

**The editor's notes on these reports (pp. 261-263).** On 9236 he says the Sufyan in the chain may be al-Thawri or Ibn Uyayna, since Mu'ammal narrates from both and both narrated this report: al-Thawri at 9237, Ibn Uyayna at 9241. In another note he says the print has the nominative form of the women's wish while the manuscript has the accusative, carefully vowelled, so he kept the manuscript; most hold that the particle used there puts the first noun in the accusative and the predicate in the nominative, though some grammarians put both in the accusative and cite a line of verse. On p. 262 he reports that some Arabs treat that particle like the verb 'find', giving it two objects, so the accusative narration is sound in his view, not to be changed and not a copyist's slip. In his note on 9241 he says the report is in the Tafsir of Abd al-Razzaq (photographed manuscript) with this chain, and that Ahmad gave it in the Musnad from Sufyan, meaning Ibn Uyayna; al-Tirmidhi from Ibn Abi Umar from Sufyan, with the words 'from Mujahid, from Umm Salama, that she said'; al-Hakim through Qabisa b. Uqba from al-Thawri; and al-Wahidi in his Asbab al-nuzul through Qutayba from Ibn Uyayna. The form differs: 'Mujahid said, Umm Salama said', which looks like a mursal report, and 'from Mujahid, from Umm Salama', which looks connected. He records that al-Tirmidhi judged it mursal, and that al-Hakim said it meets the condition of the two sheikhs if Mujahid heard from Umm Salama, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. On p. 263 the editor himself holds that the two forms mean the same and differ only in the narrators' wording, that both occur in Ibn Uyayna's and al-Thawri's transmissions, and he cites Ibn Kathir quoting Ibn Abi Hatim that Yahya al-Qattan and Waki narrated it from al-Thawri 'from Mujahid, from Umm Salama'. He calls al-Tirmidhi's verdict of mursal a judgement without proof: Mujahid certainly met Umm Salama, being born in 21 AH while she died after 60 AH; contemporaneity in a trustworthy narrator is taken as connection unless he is a concealer of defects, and nobody claimed that of Mujahid except a remark by al-Qutb al-Halabi, which al-Hafiz reported in the Tahdhib and then said he had not seen anyone ascribe it to him. The editor concludes that the report is connected and sound. He adds that Ibn Kathir cited it from the Musnad and pointed to al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Mardawayh, Ibn Jarir and al-Hakim, and that al-Suyuti cited it adding Abd b. Humayd, Sa'id b. Mansur and Ibn al-Mundhir. For 9244 he refers to Ibn Kathir and the Durr, noting that al-Suyuti ascribed it to Ibn Jarir alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 263}

**Report 9245 and its note (p. 264).** Hajjaj (9245, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ata) says it means the person who says he wishes he had so-and-so's property, told to ask God for His gift, and likewise the women's saying that they wish they were men to fight. The editor says the print has the nominative, that he kept the manuscript's accusative, correct for the grammarians because some Arabs let that particle put both nouns in the accusative; Abu Ubayd al-Qasim b. Sallam, al-Farra', Ibn al-Sayyid and Ibn al-Tarawa said so, citing a line of verse on travelling at night. He refers back to his note on p. 261. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 264}

**The second view: let none long for ranks of excellence (pp. 264-265, reports 9246-9248).** Others said the meaning is that none of you should long for the ranks of excellence by which God distinguished some. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9246, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) reports that the men said they wanted double the women's reward, as they have two portions of inheritance, and the women said they wanted a reward like the men's since they cannot fight and would have fought had it been written for them; the verse came down, and God told them to ask Him for His gift so that He would provide the deeds, which is better for them. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9247, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ayyub, from Muhammad) says they were forbidden wishes and guided to something better. Al-Muthanna (9248, from Arim, from Hammad b. Zayd, from Ayyub) says that when Muhammad heard a man wishing for worldly things he would remind him that God had forbidden it and pointed to the better. Al-Tabari says that on this view the meaning is: men and women, do not long for the ranks and degrees of good by which God distinguished some of you; let each be content with what God apportioned to him, and ask God for His gift. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 265}

**The dispute over the portion of men and women (pp. 265-267).** Al-Tabari says the people of interpretation differed over the second part of the [[passage/quran-4-32|verse]]. Some said men have a portion of the reward for obedience and the punishment for disobedience that they acquired, and women the same. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9249, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) reports that the people of the Jahiliyya gave nothing to women or children, but gave inheritance to whoever worked, benefited and defended; when a portion was fixed for woman and child, and the male received the portion of two females, the women said they wished their portions were like men's, and the men said they hoped to be preferred over women in the next life by their good deeds as they were in inheritance; the verse came down, and a woman is rewarded for a good deed tenfold as a man is. The editor explains that to 'seek a livelihood for one's family' is to strive to earn for them, and that the print, the manuscript and the Durr have a word he finds meaningless here, so he conjectures a verb meaning to hasten and fulfil a need, giving 'when her portion was hastened and settled for the woman'. Al-Muthanna (9250, from Abd al-Rahman b. Abi Hammad, from Abu Layla, from Abu Hariz) reports that when the rule of the male's double portion came down, the women said that then they should bear two portions of sins as they have two of inheritance; the verse came down, meaning sins, and the women were told to ask God for His gift. The note refers to Abd al-Rahman b. Abi Hammad at reports 3109, 8431, 6691 and 4077, with al-Muthanna narrating from him; Abu Layla is Abd Allah b. Maysara al-Kufi, called Abu Ishaq, treated at 6920; Abu Hariz is Abd Allah b. al-Husayn al-Azdi, judge of Sijistan, whom Ibn Hibban in al-Thiqat called truthful, Ibn Adi said most of what he narrates no one corroborates, and Sa'id b. Abi Maryam said he was a man of analogy with nothing in hadith; the print had Abu Jarir, an error, and the manuscript is undotted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 267}

Others said the meaning is that men have a portion of what they inherit from their dead, and women a portion of it. Al-Muthanna (9251, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) says it means what parents and relatives left, the male having twice the female portion. Ibn Humayd (9252, from Jarir, from Abu Ishaq, from Ikrima or another) says it concerns inheritance, since they did not give women any.

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 267).** Al-Tabari says the better of the two views is that men have a portion of God's reward and punishment for what they did of good or evil, and women the same. His reason is that God made for each group of men and women a portion of what they acquired, whereas inheritance is not something the heir acquires but wealth God had the deceased leave without earning; acquiring means working, and the one who acquires is the one who works for a livelihood. The meaning therefore cannot be a portion of what they inherited, for then the wording would have been a portion of what they did not acquire. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on earning and acquiring. This is al-Tabari's choice and argument; the earlier views are as transmitted.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 267 in the commentary on verse 32 of sura 4, in al-Tabari's choice for the meaning of the portion from what men and women acquired, and continue from p. 268. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 267}
