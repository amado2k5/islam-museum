**Commentary on verse 148 (pp. 117-118)**

Al-Tabari says: the Children of Israel, Moses' people, took, after Moses left them to go to his Lord for communion and to fulfil the appointment, a calf from their jewellery, that is, the young of a cow, and worshipped it. He then explains what that calf was: "a body that lowed", the "khuwar" being the lowing of cattle. He reports that they went astray in a way people of reason do not, since the Lord, to whom the dominion of the heavens and the earth belongs and who governs them, cannot be a body that lows, speaks to no one and guides to no good. These people, because of it, said, this is our god and Moses' god, and clung to it in worship out of ignorance, turning away from God and error. He has explained the cause of their worship and how the calf was made earlier, which spares repeating, and the editor refers to places in the second volume and notes that the "calf" was explained only here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 117}

In "hulliyy" (jewellery) there are two forms: damma on the ha, which is the original, and kasra, as in similar words, and whichever the reader reads is right, since both readings are well attested and agree in meaning. The editor notes the printed text altered the manuscript's wording here and spoiled the sentence, and that the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 117}

On "did they not see that it did not speak to them or guide them to a way?" he says: did those who clung to the calf they made from their jewellery to worship it not see that the calf neither speaks to them nor guides them to any road? That is not the quality of their Lord, to whom worship is truly due; His quality is that He speaks to His prophets and messengers, guides His creatures to the way of good and forbids them the ways of ruin and destruction. "They took it" means they took the calf as a god, and in taking it as a worshipped lord they wronged themselves, by worshipping someone other than the one to whom worship belongs and ascribing divinity to one who does not have it. He has explained the meaning of "wrong" earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 118}
