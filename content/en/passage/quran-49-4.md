## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 4 to 5 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Indeed, those who call you behind the Hujurat most do not understand": those who call out to you, O Muhammad, are behind your Hujurat. Al-Hujurat is the plural of Hujurat, and the three forms are Hujrat, then the plural of Hijr is called Hujrat and Hujurat. Some Arabs have pluralized Al-Hijr: Hujrat with a fatha on the jim. Likewise, every plural from three to ten is pluralized on a verb that is pluralized as 'Fa'alat' with a second fatha. The nominative is more clear and better, as exemplified by the poet's saying: But if 'Abbad was sufficient to dwell on 'Bala', and to verses in which the chambers are based, he would say: 'Yes, and for Banu Hashim.' And His saying: "And most of them do not understand" means: most of them are ignorant of the religion of Allah, and what is necessary for them is your right and your reverence. He mentioned that this verse, which came after it, was revealed about a group of Bedouins who came calling the Messenger of Allah from behind his chambers: O Muhammad, come out to us. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid.
