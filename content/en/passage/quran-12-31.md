**Commentary on verse 31 (pp. 68-71)**

Al-Tabari says: when the Aziz's wife heard of the scheming of the women who said in the city what God mentioned of them; their scheming was, per report 19164 from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat, "their talk". Report 19165, from Ibn Ishaq: when the women showed that, saying "she solicits her slave", as a scheme against her so she would show them Joseph, who was described to them for his beauty and good looks. Report 19166, from Qatada: "their scheming" means their talk; "she sent to them" means to the women who talked of her affair and Joseph's. The editor refers to the explanation of "scheming". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 69}

"A'tadat" is the form "af'alat" from "'itad", meaning provision, and means she prepared for them; "muttaka'" is a place for a meal and what they lean on of cushions and pillows, a form "muftal" from "ittaka'tu", and one says "lay down a leaning-place for him", meaning what he leans on. Report 19167, from Sa'id: food, drink and a place to lean; report 19168, from al-Suddi: what they lean on; report 19169, from Ibn Abbas: a sitting place; report 19170, from al-Hasan, who read "muttaka'" with a long vowel and hamza and said it is the place and the food; report 19171: whoever reads "mutka'" lightly means food, and whoever reads "muttaka'" means the leaning-place. In a footnote the editor says that al-Hasan's reading with the long vowel is recorded by Ibn Khalawayh in Shawadhdh al-Qira'at and by Abu Hayyan, who ascribed it to al-Hasan and Ibn Hurmuz, as a form "muftal" from leaning with the fatha lengthened so an alif arises, and refers to the earlier explanation of "a'tada". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 70}

This is what we mentioned from those we mentioned as the interpretation of the word: that she prepared for the women a sitting place with a place to lean, food, drink and citron. Some then explained "muttaka'" as the food, as a report of what the place was prepared for; some as citron, since the text says "and she gave each of them a knife", for a knife is prepared for citron and the like, which is cut with it; some as "buzmawarid"; report 19172, from al-Dahhak. Abu Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna said: "muttaka'" is the cushion leaned on, and some claim it is citron, which is the most false of falsehoods on earth, but perhaps there was citron with the leaning-place, which they ate. The editor refers to the Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 70}

Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam related Abu Ubayda's statement and then said: the jurists know interpretation better than he; then he said: perhaps it is part of what has gone from the Arabs' speech, for al-Kisa'i used to say that much of the Arabs' speech has gone and its people died out. Al-Tabari said: that the jurists know interpretation better than Abu Ubayda, as Abu Ubayd says, is beyond doubt, yet Abu Ubayda was not far from the right in this statement, for whoever said the "muttaka'" is citron only explained what was prepared in the sitting place that had the leaning-place and for which they were given knives, since it is known that knives are not prepared for a leaning-place except to slash it, and they were not given the knives for that. What makes the point clear is what we mentioned from Ibn Abbas, that the "muttaka'" is the sitting place, then it was reported from Mujahid from him, report 19173, through Sulayman ibn Abd al-Jabbar, Muhammad ibn al-Salt, Abu Kudayna and Husayn: she gave them citron and gave each of them a knife. In this report of Mujahid Ibn Abbas made clear what she gave the women and passed over explaining the sense of "muttaka'", as its meaning was known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 71}

Report 19174, from Ibn Abbas, through Yahya ibn Talha al-Yarbu'i, Fudayl ibn Iyad, Husayn and Mujahid: the "muttaka'" is the turunj (citron). Report 19175, from al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn and Hushaym. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 71}

Report 19175 continues, from Awf, from Ibn Abbas, that he used to read it "mutkan" in the light form and say it is citron. Others said: the "muttaka'" is food; reports 19176 to 19180, from Atiyya, al-Hasan and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: food, through Ibn Waki' and Ibn Idris; Ya'qub, al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Ibn Ulayya and Abu Raja'; and Ibn Bashshar, Ghundar, Shu'ba and Abu Bishr. Report 19181, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan and Mansur: whoever reads "muttaka'" it is food, and whoever reads "mutkan" lightly it is citron. Reports 19182 to 19187, from Mujahid: food, and whoever reads it lightly, citron. Report 19188, from Layth: I heard some of them say citron. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 72}

Reports 19189 to 19191, from Qatada and Ikrima: food. Report 19192, from Ibn Abbas: "mutkan" meaning citron, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father. Report 19193, from Ibn Ishaq: the "muttaka'" is food; report 19194, from Mujahid: food. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 73}

Report 19195, from Ibn Zayd: food; report 19196, from al-Dahhak: anything cut with a knife. Al-Tabari said: God, reporting on the Aziz's wife and the women who talked about her in the city, said "and she gave each of them a knife", meaning she gave each of the women present a knife to cut with whatever they cut, whether citron, buzmawarid or anything else cut with a knife. Report 19197, from al-Suddi: a knife and citron to eat; report 19198, from Ibn Abbas through Mujahid: she gave them citron and gave each a knife; report 19199, from Ibn Ishaq: to cut their food; report 19200, from Ibn Zayd: she gave them turunj and honey and they cut the turunj with the knife and ate it with honey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 74}

Al-Tabari said: in this phrase is the confirmation of what we said and chose on "she prepared for them a muttaka'". God reported that the Aziz's wife gave the women knives and left unsaid what she gave them knives for, since it is known that knives are given to one invited to a gathering only to cut what is eaten; the hearer's understanding from mention of her giving her companions the knives dispensed with mentioning what she gave them for; likewise mention of her preparing the "muttaka'" dispensed with mentioning what it was prepared for, the foods, drinks, fruits and kinds of diversion that attend gatherings, the hearers understanding it. As for the "muttaka'" itself, it is what we described, specifically and no other. On "and she said, come out to them; when they saw him they magnified him" he says: the Aziz's wife said to Joseph "come out to them", and Joseph came out; when they saw Joseph they thought him great and revered him. Reports 19201 to 19203, from Mujahid: they magnified him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 75}

Reports 19204 to 19210, from Qatada, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd, Ibn Abbas and Mujahid: they magnified him, and Ibn Zayd added: and were struck dumb. Report 19208, from Abd al-Samad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Abbas, from his father, from his grandfather, on "they magnified him": they menstruated. The editor notes that Isma'il ibn Sayf al-Ijli, al-Tabari's teacher, has no biography he could find, that Ali ibn Abis al-Asadi is weak, and that Abd al-Samad ibn Ali al-Hashimi was an emir at Mecca and is listed by al-Uqayli among the weak. Al-Tabari said: this statement, I mean what is reported from Abd al-Samad on the sense of "akbarnahu", that they menstruated, unless it means that they menstruated out of their awe of Joseph and magnifying what God allotted him of radiance and beauty and what women feel at such sights, is a saying without sense, for the meaning is "when they saw Joseph they magnified him", the pronoun referring to Joseph, and it is impossible that they menstruated Joseph; but if the report from Ibn Abbas is sound as related, it may well mean that they menstruated from what they felt of Joseph's beauty and loveliness and what women feel at such things. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 76}

Some reporters claimed that someone recited to them a line for "akbarna" in the sense of "they menstruated", which al-Tabari thinks has no basis because it is not known among the reporters; it speaks of one who comes to women in their purity and not when they "akbarna". The editor's footnote relates that al-Azhari quoted Abu al-Haytham, who asked a man of Tayy about his wife and he said he had been promised a cousin and that she had "akbarat", meaning had menstruated; al-Azhari said that if this word is sound in the language in the sense of menstruation it has a fine explanation, for a girl at her first menstruation has left the limit of childhood for adulthood, in which commands and prohibitions bind her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 77}

On "and they cut their hands" the people of interpretation differed. Some said it means they slashed their hands with the knife thinking they were cutting the citron; reports 19211 to 19214, from Mujahid: slash after slash with the knife. Report 19215, from al-Suddi: the women began slashing their hands thinking they were cutting the citron. Report 19216, from al-Suddi: they had knives in their hands with the citron, so they cut their hands and the blood ran, and they said: we blame you for loving this man and we have cut our hands and the blood runs! Report 19217, from Ibn Zayd: they went on slashing their hands with the knife, thinking only that they were slashing the turunj, their minds gone from what they saw. Report 19218, from Qatada: they slashed their hands. Report 19219, from Ibn Abbas: they went on cutting their hands thinking they were cutting the citron. Report 19220, from Qatada: they went on slashing their hands and did not feel it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 78}

Report 19221, from Ibn Ishaq: she said to Joseph "come out to them" and he came out; when they saw him they magnified him and their minds were overcome with wonder at seeing him, and they began cutting their hands with the knives they had, not knowing what they were doing, and said "God forbid, this is not a man". Others said: the meaning is that they cut their hands until they severed them, without knowing; reports 19222 and 19223, from Mujahid and Qatada: they cut their hands until they dropped them. Al-Tabari said: the right view is that God reported they cut their hands without noticing, out of awe of Joseph; it may be a cutting that severed, or a slash and scratch, and no statement is sounder than to accept the plain sense of the revelation. Report 19224, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: Joseph and his mother were given a third of beauty. The editor notes that Ibn Mas'ud's report is a sound halted-at-the-Companion chain, recorded by al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur and by al-Haythami in Majma' al-Zawa'id, who said al-Tabarani recorded it as halted and its men are the men of the Sahih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 79}

We stop here at the foot of p. 79, in the middle of the commentary on verse 31 (at report 19224 from Ibn Mas'ud, "Joseph and his mother were given a third of beauty"); the next batch begins at p. 80. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 79}
