## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And Solomon has a storm that blows by his command to the land which We have blessed, and We were of all things Knowing": "And We subjected the wind to Solomon son of David, a storm and its storm: the intensity of its blowing: 'It moves by his command to the land which We have blessed.' He means: the wind blows by Solomon's command 'to the land We blessed,' meaning to the Levant, because it used to carry Solomon and his companions wherever Solomon wished, then return with him to his home in the Levant. For this reason, it is said: "To the land in which we have been blessed." Also: from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from some scholars, from Wahb ibn Munabbih who said: When Solomon went out to his gathering, birds would gather around him, and jinn and humans would stand up for him until he sat on his bed. He was a fierce warrior, rarely staying away from battle, and never heard of any king in any part of the earth except that he would come to humiliate him. In the passage, he quotes from Qatadah and Ibn Zayd.
