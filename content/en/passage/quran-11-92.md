**Commentary on verse 92 (pp. 459-462)**

On [[passage/quran-11-92|11:92]] al-Tabari says: Shu'ayb said to his people, my people, have you honoured your kin so that they were more honoured by you than God, and made light of your Lord, putting Him behind your backs, not obeying His command, not fearing His punishment, not magnifying Him as He should be magnified? One says of a man who does not meet another's need that he cast his need behind his back, that is, left it without turning to it, and if he met it, that he set it before him and before his eyes; and one says 'I put my need behind me' and 'I made it a back thing'. He cites a verse of Artat b. Suhayya al-Murri, from his lampoon against Shabib b. al-Barsa', and the editor gives its sources. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 459}

Report 18515, from Ibn Abbas: Shu'ayb's people and kin were more honoured by them than God, and God's standing was small with them; 18516, from Ibn Abbas: 'behind your backs' means at the nape; 18517, 18518 and 18519, from Qatada: you honoured your people and made light of your Lord, you did not watch for Him in anything, you watch for my people, you do not fear Him; 18520, from Qatada: you honoured your people and were misled about your Lord, and Sufyan says the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 460}

Sufyan said: as one man says to another, you left my need behind your back, you made light of His command; and when a man means to meet his companion's need he puts it before him and does not make light of it. Report 18521, from Ibn Zayd: the 'back thing' is the spare, like a camel driver who sets out with spare camels carrying nothing unless needed, and he says, your Lord with you is like this, if you need Him, and if you do not, He is nothing. Others said the sense is that they put what Shu'ayb brought behind their backs, so the pronoun refers to what he brought and not to God; report 18523, from Mujahid: they cast aside His command, and 18524: you cast aside His command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 461}

Reports 18525, 18526 and 18527, from Mujahid: they are Shu'ayb's kin, by leaving what he brought behind their backs. Al-Tabari says: we chose the view we chose in the interpretation because 'you put Him behind your backs' is close to 'are my kin more honoured by you than God', so it is likelier and more fitting that the pronoun refers to God, being so near Him. As for 'my Lord encompasses what you do', it means my Lord's knowledge encompasses your deeds so none is hidden from Him, and He will repay you for all of it, now and later. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 462}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
