## Summary
Verse 103 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/sacred-livestock-jahiliyya|the sacred livestock of the Jahiliyya (bahira, sa’iba, wasila, ham)]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 103 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "God did not appoint any bahira, or sa'iba, or wasila, or ham": God did not slit the ear of any bahira, nor let any sa'iba loose, nor join any wasila, nor protect any ham, but you, the unbelievers, did that and forbade it, inventing a lie against your Lord. Report 12819, from Abu Hurayra: I heard the messenger of God say: I saw Amr ibn Amir al-Khuza'i dragging his entrails in the fire; he was the first to set the sa'iba loose (the editor traces the report in Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim and Ibn Kathir, explains the words for "entrails" and "setting loose", and cites a line from Ibn Hisham's Sira). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 116}

Report 12820, from Abu Hurayra: I heard the messenger of God say to Aktham ibn al-Jawn: Aktham, I saw Amr ibn Luhayy ibn Qam'a ibn Khindif dragging his entrails in the fire, and I have seen no man more like another than you are like him, nor he like you! Aktham said: may his likeness harm me, messenger of God? He said: no, you are a believer and he an unbeliever; he was the first to change the religion of Ishmael, to slit the ear of the bahira, to let the sa'iba loose and to protect the ham. In his note the editor describes the transmitters of the report and where it appears in Ibn Hisham's Sira, al-Istiab, al-Isaba and al-Durr al-manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 117}

Report 12821, from Zayd ibn Aslam: the messenger of God said: I know who first slit the ears of the bahiras, a man of Mudlij who had two she-camels; he cut their ears and forbade their milk and their backs and said, these two are for God; then he needed them, drank their milk and rode their backs. He said: I have seen him in the fire, with the people of the fire harmed by the smell of his entrails (the editor notes that this is a mursal report). Report 12822, from Abu Hurayra: the messenger of God said: the fire was shown to me, and I saw in it Amr son of so-and-so son of so-and-so son of Khindif dragging his entrails in the fire; he was the first to change the religion of Abraham and to let the sa'iba loose, and the man most like him that I have seen is Aktham ibn al-Jawn! Aktham said: messenger of God, will his likeness harm me? He said: no, because you are a Muslim and he an unbeliever (the editor says al-Hakim declared it sound on Muslim's condition and Ibn Hazm judged it sound). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 119}

Report 12823, from Abd al-Razzaq: I saw Amr ibn Amir al-Khuza'i dragging his entrails in the fire, and he was the first to set the sa'ibas loose (mursal). Report 12824, from Zayd ibn Aslam: the messenger of God said: I know the first to set the sa'ibas loose and the first to change the covenant of Abraham! They said, who is he, messenger of God? He said: Amr ibn Luhayy, the brother of Banu Ka'b; I have seen him dragging his entrails in the fire, his smell harming the people of the fire; and I know the first to slit the bahiras! They said, who is he? He said: a man of Banu Mudlij who had two she-camels; he cut their ears and forbade their milk and then drank their milk afterwards, and I saw him in the fire, with them biting him with their mouths and trampling him with their hooves (the editor notes Ibn Hajar judged the first account sounder and that the correct name is Amr ibn Luhayy). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 120}

Al-Tabari explains the words: "bahira" is a form "fa'ila" from the saying "I slit the ear of this she-camel", I slit it, "abhuruhu bahran", the camel being "mabhura", which then becomes "fa'ila" so she is called bahira; the "bahir" among camels is one struck by a sickness from drinking too much water (he quotes a line by a poet whom the editor could not trace). A report from the messenger of God has come in line with what he says about the bahira: report 12825, from Abu al-Ahwas from his father: I went to the Prophet and he said to me: do you not see your camels, that you breed them with whole ears, then take the razor and cut them and say "this is a bahira", and you slit their ears and say "this is sarm"? I said yes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 121}

The Prophet said: God's forearm is stronger and God's razor sharper; all your property is lawful, none of it is forbidden to you. Report 12826, from Abu al-Ahwas from his father: I came to the messenger of God and he said: do the camels of your people give birth with sound ears, so you take the razor and cut their ears and say "these are buhr", and slit them or slit their skins and say "these are surum", and forbid them to you and your family? I said yes. He said: what God has given you is lawful to you, God's forearm is stronger and God's razor sharper (the editor traces the report in Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi, Ahmad, al-Bayhaqi, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti and explains its words). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 122}

As for the "sa'iba", it is the one let loose and left free. In the pre-Islamic time one of them would do this to some of his livestock, forbidding himself any benefit from it, as some Muslims would free a slave as a sa'iba and not benefit from him or from his wala'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 123}

"Musayyaba" was brought out in the form "sa'iba", as one says "radiya" for a "life" in the sense of "pleasing". As for the "wasila", a female of their livestock in the pre-Islamic time which bore twins in one birth, a male and a female, they said she had joined her brother, warding off his slaughter, and called her wasila. As for the "ham", it is the stud animal whose back is protected from riding and use because of the succession of offspring from his stock. The interpreters differ on the descriptions of what these names denote and the reason they did this. Report 12827, from Abu Hurayra: I heard the messenger of God say to Aktham ibn al-Jawn al-Khuza'i: Aktham, I saw Amr ibn Luhayy ibn Qam'a ibn Khindif dragging his entrails in the fire, and I have seen no man more like another than you are like him, nor he like you! Aktham said: will his likeness harm me, prophet of God? He said: no, you are a believer and he an unbeliever, and he was the first to change the religion of Ishmael, to set up idols and to let the sa'iba loose among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 124}

Al-Tabari then adds Ibn Ishaq's description of these classes (which, as the editor explains, is Ibn Ishaq's own wording): when a she-camel bore ten females in succession with no male among them, she was set loose; her back was not ridden, her fleece not shorn and her milk not drunk except by a guest; and any female she bore after that had her ear slit and was let go with her mother among the camels, her back not ridden, her fleece not shorn and her milk drunk only by a guest, as was done with her mother; she is the "bahira", the daughter of the "sa'iba". The "wasila" is a ewe which bore ten females in succession in five births with no male among them; she was made a wasila, they said she had joined, and what she bore afterwards belonged to their males and not their females, unless one of them died, in which case they shared in eating it, males and females. The "ham" is a stud animal who has sired ten females in succession with no male among them; his back was protected, not ridden, his hair not shorn, and he was let loose among his camels to cover them, being used for nothing else. God says "God did not appoint any bahira, or sa'iba, or wasila, or ham" to the words "and they are not guided". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 125}

Report 12828, from Masruq on this verse: I asked Alqama, who said: what do you want with something the people of the pre-Islamic time did? (al-Tabari says some of the report's text was lost to him). Report 12829, from Muslim: I went to Alqama and asked him about "God did not appoint any bahira..." and he said: what will you do with this? This is just something that the people of the pre-Islamic time did! Then I went to Masruq, who said: the bahira was a she-camel which, when she had borne five or seven litters, they slit her ear and said, this is a bahira; the sa'iba was where a man took some of his property and said, this is a sa'iba; the wasila: when a she-camel bore a male the males ate it and not the females, and when she bore a male and a female in one birth they said she had joined her brother and did not eat either of them, and when the male died the males ate it and not the females; the ham was the camel that had sired and whose offspring had sired, and they said he had done what was on him, so they took no benefit from his back and said, this is a protected one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 126}

Report 12830, from Alqama, is similar. Report 12831, from Abu al-Ahwas: the bahira is the one that has borne five litters and then been left. Report 12832, from al-Sha'bi: the bahira is the one with the ear cut; the sa'iba is what is set loose for those who come asking (guests); the wasila: when she bore after four litters and then bore the fifth a male and a female, she joined her brother; the ham is the one whose grandsons have covered among the camels (the editor explains "mukhadrama" as with half the ear cut off and corrects the wording "for those who come" from the manuscript). Report 12833, from al-Sha'bi, is the same in outline. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 127}

Report 12834, from al-Sha'bi: asked about the bahira he said it is the one whose ears are cut off; asked about the sa'iba he said they used to give camels and sheep to their gods and leave them with their gods, and they went off and mixed with other people's flocks, and only men drank their milk, and if any of them died, men and women all ate it. Report 12835, from Mujahid: the bahira among camels, the people of the pre-Islamic time forbade her wool, back, meat and milk except to men; what she bore, male or female, was in her condition, and if she died men and women shared in eating her meat; when a camel covers among the offspring of the bahira he is the ham; the sa'iba among sheep was similar, except that what she bore up to six young was in her condition, and when she bore in the seventh a male or a female or two males, they slaughtered it and their men ate it without their women, and if she bore twins, a female and a male, she was a wasila, from leaving the male unslaughtered because of the female, and if both were female they were left. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 128}

Report 12836, from Ibn Abbas: the bahira is the she-camel: when she had borne five litters a man set out to the fifth, if it was not a male, and slit its ears, did not shear its wool or taste its milk, and that is the bahira; the sa'iba: a man would set loose from his property what he wished; the wasila is the ewe: when she bore seven, he set out to the seventh, and if it was male it was slaughtered, if female left, and if there were two in her womb, a male and female, and she bore them, they said she joined her brother and both were left unslaughtered, and that is the wasila; the ham: a man had a stud, and when he had sired ten they said "ham, leave him". Report 12837, from him: "that they should set them loose for their idols"; the wasila is the ewe; the ham is the male camel. Report 12838, from Qatada: a strictness Satan imposed on the people of the pre-Islamic time over their property and a harshness on them: the bahira among camels: when a man bred five litters he looked at the fifth birth, and if it was a male he slaughtered it and the men ate it without the women, if it was dead both males and females shared in it, and if female she was left with her ear slit, her wool not shorn, her milk not drunk, her back not ridden, and God's name not mentioned over her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 129}

The sa'iba: they set loose whatever of their property they liked, and it was not kept from a pool to drink at or from a preserve to graze in; the wasila among sheep: from the seventh birth, if a kid it was slaughtered and the men ate it without the women, and if it was dead both shared, and if she brought forth a male and a female they said she joined her brother and so prevented its slaughter; the ham: a stud animal who had mounted ten of his grandsons' line, or his offspring's offspring, was called a ham, his back protected, so he was not haltered, bridled or ridden. Report 12839, from al-Suddi: the bahira among camels: when a she-camel bore five litters, if the fifth was a male they slaughtered it and offered it to their gods, and its mother was an ordinary camel; if it was a female they spared it, slit its mother's ear, sheared her fleece and left her in the valley, so she was not used in blood-money, not milked, not shorn and not loaded; the sa'iba is a man who sets free what he wishes of his property out of thanks if his property increased, or he recovered from an illness, or he rode a she-camel and succeeded; he sends her off and no Arab touches her without being struck by punishment in this world; the wasila among sheep: a ewe which bore three or five litters, and the last was a kid, they slaughtered it and offered it to the house of the gods; if a she-kid they spared it; if a kid and a she-kid they spared the kid for the sake of the she-kid, for she is a wasila who joined her brother. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 130}

The ham is the stud who covers among the camels for ten years; it is said that when his grandson had covered he was said to have protected his back, and they leave him, never touched or slaughtered, and not kept from any grazing he wants. Report 12840, from Ibn al-Musayyab: the bahira among camels is the one whose milk is given to the idols; the sa'iba among camels they used to set loose for their idols; the wasila among camels was the she-camel which first bore a female and then a second female, and they called her wasila, saying she joined two females with no male between them, and they cut her ears for their idols or slaughtered her (al-Tabari doubts which wording); the ham is the male camel who covered a counted number, and when he reached it they said, this is a ham who has protected his back, so he was left; Qatada says, when he covered ten. Report 12841, from Qatada: the bahira among camels: when a she-camel had borne five litters, if the fifth was a male it was for the men and not the women, and if a female they slit her ear and let her go, and did not slaughter a young of hers, drink her milk or ride her; the sa'iba: they set loose some of their camels and they were not kept from a pool or grazing; the wasila is the ewe which, when she had {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 131}

borne seven litters, if the seventh was a male it was slaughtered and the men ate it and not the women, and if female she was left. Report 12842, from al-Dahhak: the bahira: when a she-camel had borne five litters they slaughtered the fifth if it was a male, and if a young female they slit its ear and spared it and she is a bahira; their women did not eat of the male, it was exclusively for their men, and if the camel died or bore dead young, their men and women were equal in it and ate of it; the sa'iba: a man set loose some of his livestock and it was left in the preserve, with no use made of its back, its young, its milk, its hair or its wool; the wasila: when a ewe had borne seven litters they slaughtered the seventh if a kid, and spared it if a she-kid, and if a kid and a she-kid they spared both and said, the kid was joined by his sister so she forbade him to us; the ham: the stud whose offspring's offspring were ridden, and they said, he has protected his back and his grandsons have secured him, so they did not ride him or keep him from a preserve of trees or a pool. And a group of their camels had God's name mentioned over them in none of their affairs, neither if they rode, loaded, milked, bred nor sold; about that God revealed "God did not appoint any bahira or sa'iba" to the words "and most of them do not understand". Report 12843, from Ibn Zayd: this is something the people of the pre-Islamic time did and it has gone; the bahira, a man (it continues on p. 133). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 132}

We stop here at the foot of p. 132, in the middle of the commentary on verse 103, at the start of report 12843 from Ibn Zayd.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
