## Summary
Verse 120 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 120 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says: God was pleased with these truthful ones who... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 244}

The rest: God was pleased with these truthful ones who were true in keeping faith with him in what they promised, obedience to him and avoiding disobedience; "and they with him" means they were pleased with God in his keeping faith with them in the great reward he promised for their obeying his commands and prohibitions; "that is the great triumph" means what God gave them, gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding in them, well-pleasing and well-pleased, is the great success in the quest and the reaching of the need they sought in the world and worked for in it, so they got what they sought and reached what they hoped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 245}

The editor refers for "pleasure", "triumph" and "dominion" to earlier volumes 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10.

He then explains "to God belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and what is in them, and he is able to do all things": Christians, to God belongs the rule of the heavens and the earth and what is in them, and not to Jesus whom you claim as your god, nor to his mother, nor to anyone in the heavens or on earth; the heavens, the earth and what is in them are his creation, and Jesus and his mother are part of it, by being in a place and moving, which show by their dwelling in the place where they are that they are two servants owned by him who owns the heavens, the earth and what is in them. He thus points them and all his creatures to where his argument against them lies, so that they reflect, take heed and understand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 245}

"And he is able to do all things" means: God, to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and what is in them, is able to destroy them and to destroy Jesus, his mother and all on earth as he first created them; neither this nor anything he wills is beyond him, since his power is power that no power resembles and his rule rule that no rule or kingdom resembles. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 246}

On p. 246 the editor notes that the part of the old division from which his manuscript was copied ends here, with its closing words: the end of the commentary on Surat al-Ma'ida, blessing on our lord Muhammad and his family, there is no power or might but in God the high, the great, and, God willing, the commentary on Surat al-An'am follows; praise be to God, Lord of the worlds.

Surat al-Ma'ida ends here at p. 246, and al-Tabari begins the commentary on [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]] at p. 249, that is [[passage/quran-6-1|6:1]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
