## Summary
Verse 6 of [[passage/quran-1|Surat al-Fatiha]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 6 of 7 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
Under "the interpretation of 'guide us'" al-Tabari says the sense of "Guide us on the straight path" here is "grant us success in keeping to it", as reported from Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak, that Gabriel told Muhammad to say it, meaning "inspire us with the guiding way". God's inspiring it is His granting success, like "we ask for help": the servant asks his Lord for success in staying firm in obedience and reaching the right in what remains of his life, not for deeds already done. So the sense of the speech is: O God, You alone we worship, sincere to You; help us in worshipping You, and grant us success on the way and path of those You have favoured among Your prophets and people of obedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 166} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 167}
Asked where "guidance" is found in Arabic with the sense of "success", he says it is more than can be counted, and cites a line the editor cannot attribute and another in which a poet prays for his addressee's guidance in the sense of success, and the many verses saying that God does not guide the wrongdoing people: that does not mean He does not explain their duties, since explanation covers all those charged, but that He does not grant them success or open their hearts to truth and faith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 167}
He answers those who say "guide us" means "increase our guidance": this means one of two things. Either they think the Prophet was told to ask for more explanation, which makes no sense, since God lays no duty on a servant without first making it clear, and it would be a command to ask God to explain what He has made a duty or to impose what He has not imposed, a poor prayer; or they think he was told to ask for more help and success, which is either for deeds already done or for what is to come, and since the need for help with past deeds is gone, it returns to what he said: asking for success in what remains of life. In this he sees the fault of the view of the people of qadar that anyone commanded has been given help that ends his need of his Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 167} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 168}
He mentions another view, that "guide us on the straight path" means "make us walk the road to Paradise in the next life", lead and carry us to it, as in [[passage/quran-37-23|37:23]], "guide them to the path of Hell", meaning bring them into the Fire, as a bride is led to her husband, a gift to a man and a foot carries the leg; he refers to a line of Tarafa ibn al-Abd. He answers that "You alone we ask for help" shows the error of this reading, and that the commentators among the Companions and Followers agree that "the path" here does not bear that sense and that "guide us" is a request to hold firm in guidance for the rest of life. The Arabs say "I guided him the road", "to the road" and "toward the road" when pointing it out and steering him, and the Qur'an uses all of these, as in [[passage/quran-7-43|7:43]] and [[passage/quran-16-121|16:121]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 168} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 169}

We stop here at the foot of p. 169, at the three ways the Arabs say "I guided him the road"; the next batch begins at p. 170. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 169}
