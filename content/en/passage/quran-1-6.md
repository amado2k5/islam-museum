## Summary
Verse 6 of [[passage/quran-1|Surat al-Fatiha]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 6 of 7 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
Under "the interpretation of 'guide us'" al-Tabari says the sense of "Guide us on the straight path" here is "grant us success in keeping to it", as reported from Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak, that Gabriel told Muhammad to say it, meaning "inspire us with the guiding way". God's inspiring it is His granting success, like "we ask for help": the servant asks his Lord for success in staying firm in obedience and reaching the right in what remains of his life, not for deeds already done. So the sense of the speech is: O God, You alone we worship, sincere to You; help us in worshipping You, and grant us success on the way and path of those You have favoured among Your prophets and people of obedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 166} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 167}
Asked where "guidance" is found in Arabic with the sense of "success", he says it is more than can be counted, and cites a line the editor cannot attribute and another in which a poet prays for his addressee's guidance in the sense of success, and the many verses saying that God does not guide the wrongdoing people: that does not mean He does not explain their duties, since explanation covers all those charged, but that He does not grant them success or open their hearts to truth and faith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 167}
He answers those who say "guide us" means "increase our guidance": this means one of two things. Either they think the Prophet was told to ask for more explanation, which makes no sense, since God lays no duty on a servant without first making it clear, and it would be a command to ask God to explain what He has made a duty or to impose what He has not imposed, a poor prayer; or they think he was told to ask for more help and success, which is either for deeds already done or for what is to come, and since the need for help with past deeds is gone, it returns to what he said: asking for success in what remains of life. In this he sees the fault of the view of the people of qadar that anyone commanded has been given help that ends his need of his Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 167} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 168}
He mentions another view, that "guide us on the straight path" means "make us walk the road to Paradise in the next life", lead and carry us to it, as in [[passage/quran-37-23|37:23]], "guide them to the path of Hell", meaning bring them into the Fire, as a bride is led to her husband, a gift to a man and a foot carries the leg; he refers to a line of Tarafa ibn al-Abd. He answers that "You alone we ask for help" shows the error of this reading, and that the commentators among the Companions and Followers agree that "the path" here does not bear that sense and that "guide us" is a request to hold firm in guidance for the rest of life. The Arabs say "I guided him the road", "to the road" and "toward the road" when pointing it out and steering him, and the Qur'an uses all of these, as in [[passage/quran-7-43|7:43]] and [[passage/quran-16-121|16:121]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 168} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 169}
Al-Tabari completes his evidence for the Arabs' use of "hada" with the object directly, with "to" and with "for", citing a line in which a preposition is dropped so the verb takes its object directly and one by al-Nabigha, and says such usage is common in their verse and speech. He begins "the interpretation of the straight path": all interpreters agree it means the clear road with no crookedness, and so it is in the language of all the Arabs; he cites lines of Jarir praising the caliph Hisham, of Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali and of a rajaz poet, and the editor notes where they are found and whom they are credited to. The Arabs borrow "path" for any saying or action described as straight or crooked. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}
His own choice is that the soundest interpretation of "Guide us on the straight path" is: grant us success in keeping to what You have approved and what You granted to those of Your servants You favoured, in word and deed; that is the straight path, since whoever is given what the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs were given is given success in Islam, in believing the messengers, holding to the Book, acting on what God commanded, refraining from what He forbade, and following the way of the Prophet and of Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali and every righteous servant of God, and all of that belongs to the straight path. He says the interpreters differed over what is meant by the straight path, and his choice takes in all their meanings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171}
He then gives the reports. From Ali ibn Abi Talib from the Prophet, who mentioned the Qur'an and said it is the straight path, by two chains containing al-Harith al-A'war and an unknown narrator; the editor says the chain is very weak, that al-Tirmidhi called the hadith strange, with an unknown narrator and some criticism of al-Harith, and that Ibn Kathir treated it as the words of Ali, halting at him, which he calls good. From Ali halting at him: the Book of God. From Abdullah ibn Mas'ud: the Book of God; the editor says this chain is sound and that al-Hakim graded it sound. From Jabir ibn Abdullah: Islam, wider than between the heavens and the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 171} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 173}
From Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak: the guiding road, the religion of God that has no crookedness, and by another chain: Islam, which the editor calls very weak because of al-Furat ibn al-Sa'ib. From Ibn al-Hanafiyya: the religion of God which He accepts from no one else; from al-Suddi: Islam; from Ibn Jurayj through Ibn Abbas: the road; from Abu al-Aliya: the Messenger of God and his two companions after him, Abu Bakr and Umar, and he told al-Hasan, who said Abu al-Aliya spoke truly and gave good counsel; from Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn Aslam: Islam; and from al-Nawwas ibn Sam'an from the Prophet: God gave a parable of a straight path and the path is Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 173} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 174} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 175} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 176}
On the straight path being so called, al-Tabari adds that God described it as straight because it is right, with no error in it. He answers a person he calls weak-minded who said God named it straight because it goes straight with its people to Paradise: this departs from all interpreters, and their agreement against it is proof enough of its mistake. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 177}
