## Summary
Verse 43 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 43 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the opening (p. 375, note 3).** Abu Ja'far says the call to those who believe is to those who affirmed God and his messenger; the prohibition on approaching prayer means they should not pray; the word for drunk is a plural of the word for a drunken man; and knowing what they say means that in their prayer they recognise what God commanded, or recommended them to say in it, as against what he forbade and warned against. Note 3 says al-Tabari's phrase stands in both the printed edition and the manuscript in a form the context does not suit and the editor put what he judged right. Then the interpreters differed over the intoxication God meant, and some said it is intoxication from drink. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 375}

**Those who said drink (pp. 376-377, reports 9524-9532, note 1).** Muhammad b. Bashshar related from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Abu Abd al-Rahman, from Ali (9524) that he, Abd al-Rahman and another man drank wine, and Abd al-Rahman led them in prayer (as the scan reads) and recited the sura of the unbelievers but muddled it, and the verse came down. Al-Muthanna related from al-Hajjaj b. al-Minhal, from Hammad, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Abdullah b. Habib (9525) that Abd al-Rahman b. Awf made food and drink and invited some companions of the Prophet; they ate and drank until they were tipsy and put Ali forward to lead the sunset prayer, and he recited the sura of the unbelievers, and God sent down this verse. Muhammad b. Sa'd related from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas (9526) that it was sent down before wine was prohibited. Ibn Humayd related from Jarir, from Mughira, from Abu Razin (9527) that it came down while they were drinking, before the prohibition came; and by the same chain (9528) that they drank after the verse of sura 2 ([[passage/quran-2-219|2:219]]) and after the verse of sura 4, and gave it up when the verse of sura 5 ([[passage/quran-5-90|5:90]] and [[passage/quran-5-91|5:91]], as the notes give them) came down. Muhammad b. Amr related from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (9529) that they were forbidden to pray drunk and then the prohibition of wine abrogated it, and al-Muthanna related the same from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (9530). Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada (9531) that they avoided intoxication when the prayers were due, then it was abrogated by the prohibition of wine. Ibn Humayd related from Jarir, from Mughira, from Abu Wa'il, Abu Razin and Ibrahim (9532) that they mentioned this verse with [[passage/quran-2-219|2:219]] (the sura 2 number prints as 90 and is read as 219 from the content) and [[passage/quran-16-67|16:67]] and said that was before the prohibition of wine came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 377}

**Those who said sleep (pp. 377-378, reports 9533-9534, note 1).** Others said sleep is what is meant. Ibn Waki related from his father, from Salama b. Nubayt, from al-Dahhak (9533) that the verse is not about one who approaches prayer drunk from drink; sleep is meant. Ahmad b. Hazim (the family name is unclear in the scan) related from Abu Nu'aym, from Salama, from al-Dahhak (9534) that it did not mean intoxication from wine, only from sleep. Note 1 says the printed wording of report 9533 differs from the manuscript, which has a gap breaking the sense and a marginal letter marking an error, and that he tried to read it and gave it as he read it (the lines are garbled in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 378}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 378-379, notes 2-4).** Abu Ja'far says the better of the two views is that the verse is God's prohibition for believers to come to prayer under the influence of drink before wine was prohibited, because of the many reports from the Prophet's companions to that effect, and that it was revealed about those named. He then raises an objection: how can that be, when a drunk person, while his mind is gone, is like a madman, and he holds that obligating the mad is impossible for their lack of understanding what they are commanded and forbidden? He answers that if the drunk were like the madman his command and prohibition would be invalid; but the drunk is one who understands what he does and leaves, though drink has made his tongue and body parts heavy and numb until he cannot perform his recitation in prayer and its obligatory limits, without his mind having gone, so he knows and understands what he was commanded and forbidden and cannot do some of it because of the numbness of his body. One who reaches a point where he no longer understands what he does and leaves has passed from drunkenness to the state of the mad, and he is not the one addressed by the prohibition, since he is mad; the one addressed is the drunk as described. Notes 2-4 mark places where the printed edition and manuscript differ, some of them the editor's uncertain readings, and their lines are garbled in the scan, so only the legible parts are given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 379}

**Janaba and the traveller (pp. 379-380, reports 9535-9541, notes 2-3).** Abu Ja'far says the interpreters differed over the second clause. Some said the meaning is: do not pray under the influence of drink until you understand your own words, and do not pray in a state of major impurity (janaba) unless you are crossing a road, that is, travelling, up to the point of bathing (note 1 refers to the earlier discussion of the word for the impure, shortly before, at p. 340). Among those who said it: Ibn Abbas, from Abu Mijlaz (9535, related by Muhammad b. Bashshar and Muhammad b. al-Muthanna from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Qatada; Ibn al-Muthanna said "in travel"; the same chain through Hisham, 9539) said the traveller, and from Muhammad b. Sa'd through his father, uncle, father and grandfather (9536): do not pray in janaba when you find water, and if you find none I have allowed you to wipe with the earth. Ali (9537, from Ibn Waki, from his father, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal, from Abbad b. Abdullah or from Zirr; and 9540, from Ibn Humayd, from Harun b. al-Mughira, from Anbasa, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal b. Amr, from Abbad b. Abdullah) said: unless you are travelling and find no water, then perform tayammum, and it came down about travel. Note 2 says the printed and manuscript wording of one phrase in 9537 is a slip and the editor put the right form. Note 3 (which runs onto p. 380) says Abbad b. Abdullah al-Asadi related from Ali and al-Minhal b. Amr from him; al-Bukhari said of him "there is something to consider," Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy, Ahmad spoke against him, and he is in al-Tahdhib. Sa'id b. Jubayr (9538, related by Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Salim al-Afṭas) said: the traveller. Mujahid (9541, from Ibn al-Muthanna, from Harun, from Ibn Mujahid, from his father) said: the traveller who finds no water performs tayammum, enters it (the mosque, as the editor says in note 1 on p. 380, adding that the printed edition dropped the word because its editor did not understand it, which he calls a bad piece of work) and prays. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 380}

Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada, from Ibn Abi Najih) said it is a man on a journey who becomes impure, performs tayammum and prays; he said (9543, from al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih; note 2 says the printed edition and manuscript differ in one phrase and the editor put the right reading) that they are travellers who find no water and perform tayammum on clean earth; and the same (9544, from Muhammad b. Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih). Al-Hasan b. Muslim (9545, from Ibn Waki, from his father, from Mis'ar, from Bukayr b. al-Akhnas; note 1 on p. 381 says the printed wording differs from the manuscript and he kept the manuscript's except for one word) said: unless they are travelling and find no water, then perform tayammum. Al-Hakam (9546, from Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam, from Amr, from Mansur) said: the traveller who becomes impure and finds no water performs tayammum; al-Muthanna related from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan, from Salim al-Afṭas, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, and from Mansur, from al-Hakam (9547) that both said the impure traveller who finds no water performs tayammum and prays; likewise 9548 (Abu Nu'aym, from Sufyan, from Salim, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) and 9549 (from Sufyan, from Mansur, from al-Hakam). Al-Qasim related from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah b. Kathir (9550): we used to hear that it is about travel. Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd (9551) that it is the traveller who finds no water and must perform tayammum and pray, and Ibn Zayd said his father used to say this (the report ends on p. 382). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 382}

**Those who said the mosque (p. 382, reports 9552-9554, notes 1-3).** Others said the meaning is: keep away from the place of prayer when drunk until you grasp your own words, and when in janaba until you have bathed, unless crossing it to leave. The holders of this view said the word for prayer stands in for the place of prayer and the mosque, since the Muslims prayed in their mosques then and did not stay away from the congregation there (note 1 explains the word for congregating as attending or holding the congregational prayer, and the printed edition's different word), so the prohibition on approaching prayer sufficed without naming mosques and the place of prayer. Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Abd al-Karim al-Jazari, from Abu Ubayda b. Abdullah, from his father (9552) that it is passing through the mosque; note 2 says Abu Ubayda is the son of Abdullah b. Mas'ud, whose father is the Companion, and that his biography was given earlier. Ahmad b. Hazim related from Ubaydullah b. Musa, from Abu Ja'far al-Razi, from Zayd b. Aslam, from Ibn Yasar, from Ibn Abbas (9553) that he should not approach the mosque unless his road runs through it, so that he passes through and does not sit (note 3: the printed edition has a different word because its editor could not read the manuscript properly, written as it is). Ibn Bashshar related from Mu'adh b. Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, from Sa'id (9554), about the impure person, that he passes through the mosque, standing, not sitting, even without ablution, and recited this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 382}

**The rest of the view that the mosque is meant (pp. 383-384, reports 9555-9569, note 1).** Ibn Humayd related from Harun, from (a name unclear), from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Abbas (9555; the number prints as 9000 and is taken from the sequence) that there is no harm in a menstruating woman or a person in janaba passing through the mosque so long as they do not sit in it. Ya'qub b. Ibrahim related from Hushaym, from Abu al-Zubayr (9556; printed as 1007, taken from the sequence) that any of them would pass through the mosque in janaba, crossing. Al-Hasan said (9557, from Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada) that the person in janaba passes through and does not sit. Ibrahim said (9558 from Ibn Bashshar from Abu Ahmad, and from al-Muthanna from Abu Nu'aym, both from Sufyan, from Mansur; 9559 from al-Muthanna, from Abu Ghassan Malik b. Isma'il, from Isra'il, from Mansur; 9560 from Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mansur) that if he finds no road but the mosque he passes through, and there is no harm in it when he has no other way. Sa'id b. Jubayr said (9561, from al-Muthanna, from [al-Himmani], from Sharik, from Salim) that he passes through and does not sit, and recited the verse; note 1 says the manuscript has an incomplete chain, the printed edition made it "Sharik related to us," and the editor judged the dropped name to be al-Himmani, which he put in brackets from the reports that follow and from al-Muthanna's chains of this kind earlier. The same, through Sharik, from Abd al-Karim, from Abu Ubayda (9562), from Simak, from Ikrima (9563), from al-Hasan b. Ubaydullah, from Abu al-Duha (9564); al-Hasan said (9565, Ibn Humayd, Harun, Isma'il) no harm for the menstruating woman and the person in janaba to pass and not sit; al-Zuhri said (9566, printed 9569, taken from the sequence) permission was given for the person in janaba to pass through the mosque. Al-Muthanna related from Abu Salih, from al-Layth, from Yazid b. Abi Habib (9567) that some men of the Ansar had doors opening into the mosque; they would become impure with no water to hand, want water and find no way but through the mosque, and God sent down the verse. Against this, al-Muthanna related from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Shu'ba, from Hammad, from Ibrahim (9568) that he may not cross the mosque unless he finds no other road, and Ibn Humayd related from Harun, from Ibn Mujahid, from his father (9569) that the person in janaba should not pass through the mosque taking it as a road. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 384}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 384-385, notes 1-2).** Abu Ja'far says the better of the two views is that of those who took it to mean passers through a road in the mosque, because God made clear the ruling for the traveller without water while in janaba in the clause that follows, on the sick, the traveller and the one coming from relieving himself, so that if the traveller were meant in the earlier clause, mentioning him again would have no intelligible sense, his ruling having already been given. So the verse means: believers, do not approach the mosques to pray in them while drunk until you grasp your own words, nor approach them in janaba up to the point of bathing, unless merely crossing. The word for the passer is the one who crosses by passing and cutting across; one says "I crossed this road," "so-and-so crossed the river" when he cut across it, and of a she-camel strong for journeys "she is a 'ubr of journeys" and "an 'abr of journeys," for her strength for them. Note 1: the printed edition and manuscript read a word in the wrong form and the editor corrected it. Note 2: the printed edition dropped the second 'abr asfar, taking it for a repetition, whereas two forms are meant, one with u on the first letter and a rest on the second, the other with a, and a third with i. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 385}

**The sick (pp. 385-388, reports 9570-9579, the editor's notes).** Abu Ja'far says the sick means those with a wound or smallpox who are in janaba. Ibn Humayd related from Yahya b. Wadih, from Abu al-Munbih al-Fadl b. Sulaym, from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Mas'ud (9570) that the sick person who is permitted tayammum is the one with a broken limb and the wounded; if the one with a fracture becomes impure he bathes and does not undo his splints, and the wounded does not undo his wound unless it is one not feared for. In the notes the editor says he did not find "Abu al-Munbih al-Fadl b. Sulaym" but found al-Fadl b. Sulaym al-Abdi, who related from al-Qasim b. Khalid and from whom Muslim b. Ibrahim related (Ibn Abi Hatim 2/3/63); that the printed edition dropped the phrase on the splints as though its editor did not know its sense, which is better known than that; and that the manuscript has a different word for the wounded and the printed one is right. Tamim b. al-Muntasir related from Ishaq b. Yusuf al-Azraq, from Sharik, from Isma'il al-Suddi, from Abu Malik (9571) that it is for the sick person with a wound that he fears to wash, who is allowed tayammum. Al-Suddi said (9572, from Muhammad b. al-Husayn, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) that sickness is wound, a wound whose owner fears for it from water, which would harm him if it touched him, and he performs tayammum (note 4: the manuscript and printed wording differ and the editor chose a reading between them). Sa'id b. Jubayr said (9573, from Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from Azra) that when there are wounds or sores he performs tayammum (note 5: Azra b. Abd al-Rahman al-Khuza'i; the printed edition had Urwa, the manuscript is right though unpointed). Ibrahim said (9574 and 9575): sores on the forearms. Al-Dahhak said (9576, from Ibn Humayd, from Harun, from Amr, from Juwaybir): one with a wound he fears for performs tayammum, then recited. Mujahid said (9577): sickness is a wound, a sore or smallpox that makes a man fear the cold and harm of water for himself; he performs tayammum with earth as the traveller without water does (note 1: the printed edition words it differently and the editor kept the manuscript). Ibn Bashshar related from Mu'adh b. Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, from Asim al-Ahwal, from al-Sha'bi (9578) that he was asked about a smallpox sufferer who becomes impure and said: the horsemen of this verse have gone. Note 2: the manuscript reads "about the saying: al-majdud," which the editor put in brackets for doubt; the phrase about the horsemen puzzled him, and he leaned to the view that the speaker meant the verse came down about the Ansar among the Prophet's companions whose doors were in the mosque (as in 9567), so the phrase refers to that part of the verse and means they have gone and none is left; he calls this the limit of his effort. Others said: Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd (9579) that the sick one is he who finds no one to bring him water and cannot reach it, with no servant or helper; if he cannot take the water and has no one to bring it and cannot crawl to it, he performs tayammum and prays when the prayer is due; Ibn Zayd said all this is his father's saying, and that he does not leave the prayer and is more excused than the traveller (pp. 387-388). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 388}

**Al-Tabari on the rest of the clause (p. 388, note 1, report 9580).** Abu Ja'far says the verse therefore means: if you are wounded, or have sores, a fracture or an ailment because of which you cannot wash from janaba, and you are resident, not travelling, perform tayammum with pure soil. The mention of travel means: if you are travelling, healthy and in janaba, perform tayammum. Likewise for one who has come from relieving himself while a healthy traveller: he also performs tayammum. The word for the low ground is a wide valley that dips, used as a euphemism for relieving oneself because the Arabs preferred to do so in the low grounds until it became the common usage, and anyone who relieves himself wherever it is on the earth is called one who has gone to the low ground, and "so-and-so came from the low ground" means he relieved himself. Note 1 refers to the earlier discussion of the word (the page number is unclear in the scan). It is reported from Mujahid that the low ground is the valley (9580, from al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 388}

**Touching (pp. 389-390, reports 9581-9589, notes).** Abu Ja'far says it means that you have been in hand-to-hand contact with women. Then the interpreters differed over the touching God meant, and some said it means intercourse. Humayd b. Mas'ada related from Yazid b. Zuray', from Shu'ba, from Abu Bishr, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9581): they mentioned touching, and some of the clients (mawali) said it is not intercourse and some Arabs said it is; I went to Ibn Abbas, who asked which party I was from, and when I said the clients he said the clients' party was beaten: touch, contact and intimacy all mean intercourse, but God alludes by what he wills to what he wills. A note says al-Bayhaqi gave it in the Sunan (1:125) through Ibrahim b. Marzuq, from Wahb b. Jarir, from Shu'ba. The same from Ibn Abbas through Abu Qays (9582, Ibn Bashshar from Muhammad b. Ja'far from Shu'ba) and Abu Ishaq (9583, Ibn al-Muthanna), that it is intercourse. Ibn Bashshar related from Wahb b. Jarir, from his father, from Qatada, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9584): I, Ata and Ubayd b. Umayr disagreed, Ubayd saying intercourse and Ata and I saying touching; we asked Ibn Abbas, who said the clients' party was beaten and the Arabs were right: it is intercourse, but God veils and alludes. Ibn al-Muthanna related from Abd al-A'la, from Sa'id, from Qatada (9585) that Ikrima, Sa'id b. Jubayr, Ata b. Abi Rabah and Ubayd b. Umayr disagreed over mutual touching, Sa'id and Ata saying something short of intercourse and Ubayd saying marriage; Ibn Abbas came out and said the two clients erred and the Arab was right: mutual touching is marriage, but God alludes and veils; and the same through Ibn Waki, from Muhammad b. Bishr, from Sa'id, from Qatada (9586). Ibn al-Muthanna related from Muhammad b. Uthma, from Sa'id b. Bashir, from Qatada (9587) that Sa'id and Ata took the word as pressing with the hand and Ubayd as intercourse, and Ibn Abbas said the two clients erred and the Arab was right (note 1: the word stands so in the printed edition and manuscript and perhaps should be the verbal noun; note 2: Muhammad b. Uthma is Muhammad b. Khalid b. Uthma). Abu Kurayb and Ya'qub b. Ibrahim related from Ibn Abbas (9588) that touching is intercourse; note 3: the editor fears a gap in this chain. Ibn Waki related from Ibn Ulayya and Abd al-Wahhab, from Khalid, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas the same (9589). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 390}

**The rest of the reports that intercourse is meant, from Ibn Abbas and others (pp. 391-392, reports 9590-9605).** Ya'qub b. Ibrahim related from Hushaym, from Abu Bishr, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas (9590) that the words for touching, contact and meeting all mean intercourse, but God alludes to whatever He wills. Abd al-Hamid b. Bayan related from Ishaq al-Azraq, from Sufyan, from Asim al-Ahwal, from Bakr b. Abdullah, from Ibn Abbas (9591) that the contact is intercourse and that God is generous and alludes to what He wills; the same through Muhammad b. Abdullah b. Abd al-Hakam, from Ayyub b. Suwayd, from Sufyan (9592). Ibn al-Muthanna related from Ibn Abi Adi, from Dawud, from Ja'far b. Abi Wahshiyya, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9593) that the Arabs and the mawali differed at Ibn Abbas's door, the Arabs saying it is intercourse and the mawali saying by the hand, and Ibn Abbas came out and said the party of the mawali had been defeated and the contact is intercourse; similar through a man not named, from Sa'id (9594), and through Yazid b. Harun, from Dawud (9595). Al-Muthanna related from Abdullah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas (9596) that the contact is the marital union. Ibn Waki' related from Ibn Numayr, from al-A'mash, from Abd al-Malik b. Maysara, from Sa'id (9597) that the mawali and the Arabs gathered in the mosque with Ibn Abbas in the portico; the mawali agreed that it is less than intercourse and the Arabs that it is intercourse; Ibn Abbas asked Sa'id which party he belonged to, he said the mawali, and Ibn Abbas said: you were defeated. Ibn Waki' also related, from his father, from Sufyan, from Abu Ishaq, from Sa'id, from Ibn Abbas (9598), by the same chain from Sufyan, from Asim, from Bakr (9598m), from Hafs, from al-A'mash, from Habib, from Sa'id (9599), from Malik, from Zuhayr, from Khusayf, from Ikrima (9600), and from Hafs, from Dawud, from Ja'far b. Iyas, from Sa'id (9601), all from Ibn Abbas, that it is intercourse. From Ali b. Abi Talib that it is intercourse, through Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from Ash'ath, from al-Sha'bi (9602); from al-Hasan (9603, Ibn Waki', from Abd al-A'la, from Yunus); from Mujahid (9604, Khusayf said he asked him and he said so); and from Qatada and al-Hasan, who said it is going in to women (9605, Bishr b. Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 392}

**The view that every kind of touch is meant, and ablution required (pp. 392-396, reports 9606-9628, notes 1-3).** Others said God meant every touch, by the hand or any other part of the body, and they made ablution obligatory on anyone whose body touches hers in contact. They relayed from Abdullah b. Mas'ud, in words to this effect, that the contact is what is less than intercourse: Ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Mukhariq, from Tariq b. Shihab (9606); Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Mukhariq (9608); Ya'qub b. Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Shu'ba, from al-Mughira, from Ibrahim (9609); and that the kiss is part of touching: Ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Mansur, from Hilal, from Abu Ubayda (9607; Mansur was unsure whether it was from Abdullah or from Abu Ubayda), Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from al-A'mash, from Ibrahim, from Abu Ubayda (9610), Abu al-Sa'ib, from Abu Mu'awiya, and Ibn Waki', from Ibn Fudayl, both from al-A'mash, adding that ablution is required for it (9611), and Tamim b. al-Muntasir, from Ishaq, from Sharik, from al-A'mash, the same (9612). Reports 9613-9616m say that Muhammad (Ibn Sirin) asked Ubayda al-Salmani about the clause and he answered with a gesture of his hand, closing his fingers, so that Muhammad understood what he meant: Ahmad b. Abda al-Dabbi, from Sulaym b. Akhdar, from Ibn Awn (9613); Ya'qub and Ibn Waki', from Ibn Ulayya, from Salama b. Alqama (9614), from Ibn Awn (9615), from Khalid (9616) and from Hisham (9616m); and Ibn Waki', from Jarir, from Hisham, from Ibn Sirin (9626). In the editor's note 1 to 9613 he identifies Sulaym b. Akhdar of Basra and cites Ibn Sa'd that he was the most constant companion of Ibn Awn and trustworthy, and says Abu Abdullah in the chain is Ahmad b. Abda al-Dabbi; in the note to 9614 he explains that he corrected the printed reading, which has a word for supposing, to a word for understanding on the evidence of the manuscript and of 9616m, that the phrase "he said by his hand" means he gestured, and that the word lamas, with a kasra, is a verbal noun of the verb for touching. Yunus b. Abd al-A'la related from Ibn Wahb, from Ubaydullah b. Umar, from Nafi (9617) that Ibn Umar did ablution after kissing a woman, held that it was required, and said it belongs to touching. From Amir al-Sha'bi that contact is less than intercourse (9618, Abd al-Hamid b. Bayan, from Muhammad b. Yazid, from Ismail); from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i that touching with desire breaks ablution (9619, Ibn Humayd, from Yahya b. Wadih, from Mihall b. Mihriz), with the editor noting that al-Bukhari listed Mihall among the weak, that Yahya al-Qattan called him middling, and that the editor holds him reliable; from al-Hakam and Hammad (9620) and Ata' (9621) that it is less than intercourse; from Abdullah b. Mas'ud by further routes (9622-9625, in 9625 through Ibn Waki' he is said to have recited the verse after saying it); and from Abu Ubayda that the kiss is part of touching (9627), while 9628 gives the kiss and "the thing", where the editor notes that the manuscript is undotted and he fears the right reading is "the touch". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 396}

**Al-Tabari's choice and the reports of the kiss without ablution (pp. 396-398, reports 9629-9632, notes).** Abu Ja'far al-Tabari says the better of the two views is that of those who held that intercourse is meant and no other sense of touching, because of the sound report that the Prophet kissed one of his wives and then prayed without doing ablution (this is his choice and argument). He then gives reports: from Aisha that the Prophet would do ablution, kiss, and pray without further ablution, related by Isma'il b. Musa al-Suddi, from Abu Bakr b. Ayyash, from al-A'mash, from Habib b. Abi Thabit, from Urwa (9629), and by Abu Kurayb from Waki' (9630) in the form that he kissed one of his wives and went out to prayer without ablution, with Urwa saying to her "who is she but you?" and she laughed. From Zaynab al-Sahmiyya from the Prophet to the same effect (9631, Abu Kurayb, from Hafs b. Ghiyath, from Hajjaj, from Amr b. Shu'ayb). From Aisha (9632, Umar b. Shabba, from Shihab b. Abbad, from Mindal, from Layth, from Ata', and from Abu Ruq, from Ibrahim al-Taymi) that he would give her a kiss after ablution and not repeat the ablution. In the editor's notes, which are his: Urwa in 9629-9630 is Urwa b. al-Zubayr, Aisha's nephew, with certainty, against those who said he was Urwa al-Muzani on the strength of a remark attributed to al-Thawri; the versions of Ahmad and Ibn Majah state that he is Ibn al-Zubayr, and Abu Dawud himself overturned the remark; the editor says he treated this hadith at length in his commentary on al-Tirmidhi, where he held that the contact in the verse is intercourse and that touching a woman does not break ablution, and saw no need to repeat it here. Note 1 to 9631 says Hajjaj is Ibn Arta'a and that Zaynab al-Sahmiyya, daughter of Muhammad b. Abdullah b. Amr b. al-As, is listed among the reliable by Ibn Hibban while al-Daraqutni claimed she was unknown; that in this form the report is mursal since she is a Follower, but Ahmad and Ibn Majah give it connected to Aisha through Ibn Fudayl, from al-Hajjaj, from Amr, from Zaynab; and that some memorisers faulted it because Hajjaj was a mudallis. Note 2 to 9632 says al-Tabari gives it by two routes, that the name Shihab b. Abbad al-Abdi (reliable, among the teachers of al-Bukhari and Muslim) is corrupted in both the manuscript and the print, that Mindal is disputed between authentication and weakening and the editor judges his hadith hasan, that Abu Ruq is Atiyya b. al-Harith al-Hamdani, that the first route was given by al-Bazzar and al-Daraqutni with a sound chain in the editor's view, who calls al-Daraqutni's objection, from a saying of al-Thawri that it is Ata's own remark, a feeble one, that the second route was given by Ahmad, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Daraqutni and al-Bayhaqi through al-Thawri from Abu Ruq, that Abu Dawud called it mursal because Ibrahim al-Taymi did not hear from Aisha, that al-Nasa'i said nothing in the chapter is better though mursal, that al-Tirmidhi alluded to it without confirming that al-Taymi heard from her, and that the editor adds it is also related as connected through al-Taymi from his father from Aisha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 398}

**The report from Umm Salama, al-Tabari's argument and the sense of the cited verse line (pp. 398-399, report 9633, notes).** Al-Tabari gives (9633) from Sa'id b. Yahya al-Umawi, from his father (the start of the chain is not legible in the scan), from Yazid b. Sinan, from al-Awza'i, from Yahya b. Abi Kathir, from Abu Salama, from Umm Salama that the Prophet used to kiss her while fasting and then neither broke his fast nor renewed ablution. In the editor's notes, which are his: this report is not in the six books and he did not find it in Ahmad's Musnad; Ibn Kathir relays it from al-Tabari and ascribes it to no one else; al-Haythami in Majma' al-Zawa'id says it is with al-Tabarani in al-Awsat and that Yazid b. Sinan al-Ruhawi in it is disputed between those who weakened and those who authenticated him, the editor preferring authentication; and he adds that al-Daraqutni related another report to the same effect through Sa'id b. Bashir, from Mansur, from al-Zuhri, from Abu Salama, from Aisha. Then al-Tabari says that in the sound report from the Prophet is clear indication that the touching here is the touching of intercourse and not all senses of touching. He cites a line of verse in which intercourse is alluded to, and glosses a word in it as a verbal noun meaning contact (that gloss is al-Tabari's; in note 3 the editor calls it a strange view he found in no one else, most saying the word is the name of a woman; in note 2 he says the line was traced in volume 4). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 399}

**What was said about the occasion: the people with wounds (p. 400, report 9634, notes).** It is mentioned that the verse was revealed about a group of the Prophet's Companions who were in janaba and had wounds. Al-Muthanna related from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Muhammad b. Jabir, from Hammad, from Ibrahim (9634), speaking of the sick person unable to wash after janaba, or the menstruating woman, that tayammum suffices for them, and that wounds spread among the Companions, who were then tried with janaba and complained to the Prophet, and the verse came down. In the editor's notes, which are his, he says he preferred a reading of "they" in place of "among them" and adopted it; that the print misread the manuscript at the words "and he said: it befell"; and that the verb for "lost" means went astray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 400}

**What was said about the occasion: lack of water on a journey, the story of Aisha's necklace (pp. 400-401, reports 9635-9636, notes).** Others said it was revealed about some Companions who lacked water on a journey. Ibn Abd al-A'la related from al-Mu'tamir b. Sulayman, from Ubaydullah b. Umar, from Abd al-Rahman b. al-Qasim, from Aisha (9635) that she was on a journey with the Prophet and, at Dhat al-Jaysh, her necklace was lost; the Prophet ordered a search, it was not found, and he halted and the people halted and spent the night, and people said Aisha had detained the Prophet. She said: Abu Bakr came to me while the Prophet's head was in my lap asleep, and he began to nudge and pinch me, saying, because of your necklace you have detained the Prophet. I did not move for fear of waking him, though it hurt me, and when he saw I would not answer him he left. When the Prophet woke and wanted to pray and found no water, God revealed the verse of tayammum, and Usayd b. Hudayr said: this is not the first of your blessings, house of Abu Bakr. Ya'qub b. Ibrahim related from Ibn Ulayya, from Ayyub, from Ibn Abi Mulayka (9636) that the Prophet was on a journey and Aisha lost a necklace, so he ordered the people to halt without water; Abu Bakr came to Aisha and said: you have burdened the people (Ayyub showed with his hand that he pinched her); the verse of tayammum came down, the necklace was found where the camel had knelt, and the people said they had never seen a woman of greater blessing. In the editor's notes, which are his: the verb translated "nudge" means to prod or jab, and the phrase "I would not answer him" is the manuscript's reading while the print has a wrong letter; Ubaydullah b. Umar is al-Umari, one of the seven jurists; 9635 looks mursal because Abd al-Rahman b. al-Qasim did not hear from Aisha, and it comes connected at 9641; 9636 is also mursal because Ibn Abi Mulayka, a Follower, told the story without naming who told him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 401}

**The story of al-Asla' on janaba and tayammum (pp. 402-403, reports 9637-9638, notes).** Muhammad b. Abdullah al-Hilali related from Imran b. Muhammad al-Jadad, from al-Rabi' b. Badr, from his father, from a man of theirs of the Banu al-A'raj called al-Asla' (9637) who said: I served the Prophet and saddled for him, and one night he said to me, get up and saddle for me. I said, I am in janaba. He was silent a while, then Gabriel came to him with the verse of the earth, and he described to us two strikes. Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Amr b. Khalid, from al-Rabi' b. Badr, from his father, from his grandfather, from al-Asla' (9638) something similar, in which the Prophet was silent for a little (or an hour; Amr was unsure) and then said: get up, Asla', and perform tayammum. He said: I did and then saddled for him; we travelled until we passed water and he said: Asla', wash your skin with this. He showed me tayammum as his father had been shown: one strike for the face and one for the hands and the elbows. In the editor's notes, which are his: he found no biography of Imran b. Muhammad; al-Rabi' b. Badr is weak by agreement and his father and grandfather are unknown, since only al-Rabi' narrated from them; the report was given by Ibn Sa'd, al-Daraqutni, al-Tahawi and al-Bayhaqi, all through al-Rabi', and al-Bayhaqi said al-Rabi' is weak but did not stand alone in it, which al-Zayla'i and Ibn al-Turkumani answered by saying that is not enough to rely on until the other route and its grade are known; that other route is quoted by Ibn Kathir from Ibn Mardawayh through al-Abbas b. Abi Sariyya (whom the editor could not identify), from al-Haytham b. Rizq (listed by Ibn Abi Hatim with no disparagement), from his father, from al-Asla'; al-Haythami ascribed it to al-Tabarani and noted that some said al-Haytham's report is not corroborated; and Ibn Hajar listed these reports in al-Isaba. A further note says the word for "a little" is explained by "an hour" and that the print reads "up to the elbows" where the editor kept the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 403}

**The necklace story as told by Ibn Abbas, Aisha and Urwa (pp. 403-406, reports 9639-9642, notes).** Abu Kurayb related from Hafs b. Bughayl, from Zuhayr b. Mu'awiya, from Abdullah b. Uthman b. Khuthaym, from Abdullah b. Ubaydullah b. Abi Mulayka (9639) that Dhakwan Abu Amr, Aisha's doorkeeper, told him that Ibn Abbas came in to see her in her illness and said: rejoice, you were the dearest of the Prophet's wives to him and he loved only what is good; your necklace fell on the night at al-Abwa', the Prophet spent until morning picking it up at the halting-place, the people were without water, and God revealed the verse of tayammum, so it was through you and through the concession God permitted this community. Sufyan b. Waki' related from Ibn Numayr, from Hisham, from his father, from Aisha (9640) that she borrowed a necklace from Asma' and it was lost; the Prophet sent men to look for it and they found it, prayer-time came upon them without water and they prayed without ablution, they complained of this to the Prophet, and God revealed the verse of tayammum; Usayd b. Hudayr said to Aisha: may God reward you well, by God nothing befell you that you disliked but God made good come of it for you and the Muslims. Ahmad b. Abd al-Rahman b. Wahb related from his uncle Abdullah b. Wahb, from Amr b. al-Harith, from Abd al-Rahman b. al-Qasim, from his father, from Aisha (9641) that her necklace fell at al-Bayda' as they were entering Medina; the Prophet halted and dismounted, and while he slept in her lap Abu Bakr came and jabbed her, saying: you have detained the people. Then the Prophet woke at dawn, water was sought and not found, and the verse about rising to prayer was revealed ([[passage/quran-5-6|5:6]]); Usayd said: God has blessed the people through you, house of Abu Bakr, you are only a blessing. Al-Hasan b. Shabib related from Ibn Uyayna, from Abdullah b. Uthman b. Khuthaym, from Ibn Abi Mulayka (9642) that Ibn Abbas went in to Aisha and said: you were the greatest blessing to the Muslims; your necklace fell at al-Abwa' and God revealed about you the verse of tayammum. In the editor's notes, which are his: Hafs b. Bughayl is reliable and the print's "Nufayl" is a corruption; the print made "Abdullah b. Ubayd" and "Ibn Abi Mulayka" two narrators, which is a plain error; Dhakwan, Aisha's freedman, is a reliable Follower; 9639 is part of a long report in Ahmad's Musnad; the manuscript has "this verse" where the correct reading is "the concession"; 9640 was given by Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah and al-Bayhaqi through Hisham b. Urwa; and 9641 is the connected form of 9635, given by Malik in the Muwatta' and by Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim and al-Nasa'i through Malik. In note 3 on 9642 the editor cites Ibn Adi that al-Hasan b. Shabib, al-Tabari's teacher, narrated falsehoods from reliable authorities and connected mursal reports, and al-Daraqutni that he may be noted but is not strong; the editor holds this report from him is certainly mistaken because he narrates from Ibn Uyayna directly from Ibn Khuthaym while Ahmad has Ibn Uyayna from Ma'mar from Ibn Khuthaym, and he will not weigh the shaykh against Ahmad; he adds that al-Bukhari and Ibn Sa'd give it through Umar b. Sa'id b. Abi Husayn from Ibn Abi Mulayka and, like al-Tabari here, without naming Dhakwan, that he leans to these being mursal since Ibn Abi Mulayka did not witness Aisha's death-bed, and that Ibn Hajar's suggestion that he did is a far-fetched stretch. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 406}

**The readers' differing readings of the clause on touching (p. 406).** The readers differed over the reading of that clause: most readers of Medina and some of Basra and Kufa read it with the form of mutual action, meaning that you touched your women and they touched you, and most readers of Kufa read the plain form, meaning that you men touched your women. Al-Tabari says the two readings are close in meaning, since a man is never touching his wife without her touching him, so each form signifies the other, and whichever the reader uses he is right because the meanings agree (this is al-Tabari's view). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 406}

**The next clause begins: the sense of intending (p. 407, report 9643, notes).** Abu Ja'far says that the next clause means: if you had contact with women and sought water to purify yourselves and did not find it, for a price or without one, then aim for earth, the verb being from the phrase "I aimed at such a thing" when one intends it, with its derived forms; he says the light form is not heard, which is his linguistic account, and the editor's note 1 says al-Lihyani did report it. He cites a line of verse by al-A'sha of Banu Tha'laba in which the verb means to head for something across a great distance (described, not reproduced; the editor refers to the note on its source in volume 5). He adds that in the reading of Abdullah (Ibn Mas'ud) there is reported a different verb of the same sense. Then he says the interpreters said likewise, and Abdullah b. Muhammad related from Abdan, from Ibn al-Mubarak, that he heard Sufyan explain the verb as seeking out and intending (9643); in note 4 the editor identifies Abdullah b. Muhammad (Abu Muhammad al-Hanafi) and Abdan (Abdullah b. Uthman b. Jabala al-Azdi) and says the same chain occurs in earlier and later reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 407}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 407 in the commentary on verse 43 of sura 4, after report 9643 on Sufyan's gloss of the verb of intending in the next clause, and continue from p. 408. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 407}
