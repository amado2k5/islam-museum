## Summary
Verse 43 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 43 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the opening (p. 375, note 3).** Abu Ja'far says the call to those who believe is to those who affirmed God and his messenger; the prohibition on approaching prayer means they should not pray; the word for drunk is a plural of the word for a drunken man; and knowing what they say means that in their prayer they recognise what God commanded, or recommended them to say in it, as against what he forbade and warned against. Note 3 says al-Tabari's phrase stands in both the printed edition and the manuscript in a form the context does not suit and the editor put what he judged right. Then the interpreters differed over the intoxication God meant, and some said it is intoxication from drink. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 375}

**Those who said drink (pp. 376-377, reports 9524-9532, note 1).** Muhammad b. Bashshar related from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Abu Abd al-Rahman, from Ali (9524) that he, Abd al-Rahman and another man drank wine, and Abd al-Rahman led them in prayer (as the scan reads) and recited the sura of the unbelievers but muddled it, and the verse came down. Al-Muthanna related from al-Hajjaj b. al-Minhal, from Hammad, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Abdullah b. Habib (9525) that Abd al-Rahman b. Awf made food and drink and invited some companions of the Prophet; they ate and drank until they were tipsy and put Ali forward to lead the sunset prayer, and he recited the sura of the unbelievers, and God sent down this verse. Muhammad b. Sa'd related from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas (9526) that it was sent down before wine was prohibited. Ibn Humayd related from Jarir, from Mughira, from Abu Razin (9527) that it came down while they were drinking, before the prohibition came; and by the same chain (9528) that they drank after the verse of sura 2 ([[passage/quran-2-219|2:219]]) and after the verse of sura 4, and gave it up when the verse of sura 5 ([[passage/quran-5-90|5:90]] and [[passage/quran-5-91|5:91]], as the notes give them) came down. Muhammad b. Amr related from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (9529) that they were forbidden to pray drunk and then the prohibition of wine abrogated it, and al-Muthanna related the same from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (9530). Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada (9531) that they avoided intoxication when the prayers were due, then it was abrogated by the prohibition of wine. Ibn Humayd related from Jarir, from Mughira, from Abu Wa'il, Abu Razin and Ibrahim (9532) that they mentioned this verse with [[passage/quran-2-219|2:219]] (the sura 2 number prints as 90 and is read as 219 from the content) and [[passage/quran-16-67|16:67]] and said that was before the prohibition of wine came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 377}

**Those who said sleep (pp. 377-378, reports 9533-9534, note 1).** Others said sleep is what is meant. Ibn Waki related from his father, from Salama b. Nubayt, from al-Dahhak (9533) that the verse is not about one who approaches prayer drunk from drink; sleep is meant. Ahmad b. Hazim (the family name is unclear in the scan) related from Abu Nu'aym, from Salama, from al-Dahhak (9534) that it did not mean intoxication from wine, only from sleep. Note 1 says the printed wording of report 9533 differs from the manuscript, which has a gap breaking the sense and a marginal letter marking an error, and that he tried to read it and gave it as he read it (the lines are garbled in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 378}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 378-379, notes 2-4).** Abu Ja'far says the better of the two views is that the verse is God's prohibition for believers to come to prayer under the influence of drink before wine was prohibited, because of the many reports from the Prophet's companions to that effect, and that it was revealed about those named. He then raises an objection: how can that be, when a drunk person, while his mind is gone, is like a madman, and he holds that obligating the mad is impossible for their lack of understanding what they are commanded and forbidden? He answers that if the drunk were like the madman his command and prohibition would be invalid; but the drunk is one who understands what he does and leaves, though drink has made his tongue and body parts heavy and numb until he cannot perform his recitation in prayer and its obligatory limits, without his mind having gone, so he knows and understands what he was commanded and forbidden and cannot do some of it because of the numbness of his body. One who reaches a point where he no longer understands what he does and leaves has passed from drunkenness to the state of the mad, and he is not the one addressed by the prohibition, since he is mad; the one addressed is the drunk as described. Notes 2-4 mark places where the printed edition and manuscript differ, some of them the editor's uncertain readings, and their lines are garbled in the scan, so only the legible parts are given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 379}

**Janaba and the traveller (pp. 379-380, reports 9535-9541, notes 2-3).** Abu Ja'far says the interpreters differed over the second clause. Some said the meaning is: do not pray under the influence of drink until you understand your own words, and do not pray in a state of major impurity (janaba) unless you are crossing a road, that is, travelling, up to the point of bathing (note 1 refers to the earlier discussion of the word for the impure, shortly before, at p. 340). Among those who said it: Ibn Abbas, from Abu Mijlaz (9535, related by Muhammad b. Bashshar and Muhammad b. al-Muthanna from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Qatada; Ibn al-Muthanna said "in travel"; the same chain through Hisham, 9539) said the traveller, and from Muhammad b. Sa'd through his father, uncle, father and grandfather (9536): do not pray in janaba when you find water, and if you find none I have allowed you to wipe with the earth. Ali (9537, from Ibn Waki, from his father, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal, from Abbad b. Abdullah or from Zirr; and 9540, from Ibn Humayd, from Harun b. al-Mughira, from Anbasa, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal b. Amr, from Abbad b. Abdullah) said: unless you are travelling and find no water, then perform tayammum, and it came down about travel. Note 2 says the printed and manuscript wording of one phrase in 9537 is a slip and the editor put the right form. Note 3 (which runs onto p. 380) says Abbad b. Abdullah al-Asadi related from Ali and al-Minhal b. Amr from him; al-Bukhari said of him "there is something to consider," Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy, Ahmad spoke against him, and he is in al-Tahdhib. Sa'id b. Jubayr (9538, related by Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Salim al-Afṭas) said: the traveller. Mujahid (9541, from Ibn al-Muthanna, from Harun, from Ibn Mujahid, from his father) said: the traveller who finds no water performs tayammum, enters it (the mosque, as the editor says in note 1 on p. 380, adding that the printed edition dropped the word because its editor did not understand it, which he calls a bad piece of work) and prays. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 380}

**More reports on the traveller (pp. 380-382, reports 9542-9551, notes).** Mujahid (9542, from al-Hasan b. Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada, from Ibn Abi Najih) said it is a man on a journey who becomes impure, performs tayammum and prays; he said (9543, from al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih; note 2 says the printed edition and manuscript differ in one phrase and the editor put the right reading) that they are travellers who find no water and perform tayammum on clean earth; and the same (9544, from Muhammad b. Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih). Al-Hasan b. Muslim (9545, from Ibn Waki, from his father, from Mis'ar, from Bukayr b. al-Akhnas; note 1 on p. 381 says the printed wording differs from the manuscript and he kept the manuscript's except for one word) said: unless they are travelling and find no water, then perform tayammum. Al-Hakam (9546, from Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam, from Amr, from Mansur) said: the traveller who becomes impure and finds no water performs tayammum; al-Muthanna related from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan, from Salim al-Afṭas, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, and from Mansur, from al-Hakam (9547) that both said the impure traveller who finds no water performs tayammum and prays; likewise 9548 (Abu Nu'aym, from Sufyan, from Salim, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) and 9549 (from Sufyan, from Mansur, from al-Hakam). Al-Qasim related from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah b. Kathir (9550): we used to hear that it is about travel. Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd (9551) that it is the traveller who finds no water and must perform tayammum and pray, and Ibn Zayd said his father used to say this (the report ends on p. 382). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 382}

**Those who said the mosque (p. 382, reports 9552-9554, notes 1-3).** Others said the meaning is: keep away from the place of prayer when drunk until you grasp your own words, and when in janaba until you have bathed, unless crossing it to leave. The holders of this view said the word for prayer stands in for the place of prayer and the mosque, since the Muslims prayed in their mosques then and did not stay away from the congregation there (note 1 explains the word for congregating as attending or holding the congregational prayer, and the printed edition's different word), so the prohibition on approaching prayer sufficed without naming mosques and the place of prayer. Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Abd al-Karim al-Jazari, from Abu Ubayda b. Abdullah, from his father (9552) that it is passing through the mosque; note 2 says Abu Ubayda is the son of Abdullah b. Mas'ud, whose father is the Companion, and that his biography was given earlier. Ahmad b. Hazim related from Ubaydullah b. Musa, from Abu Ja'far al-Razi, from Zayd b. Aslam, from Ibn Yasar, from Ibn Abbas (9553) that he should not approach the mosque unless his road runs through it, so that he passes through and does not sit (note 3: the printed edition has a different word because its editor could not read the manuscript properly, written as it is). Ibn Bashshar related from Mu'adh b. Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, from Sa'id (9554), about the impure person, that he passes through the mosque, standing, not sitting, even without ablution, and recited this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 382}

**The rest of the view that the mosque is meant (pp. 383-384, reports 9555-9569, note 1).** Ibn Humayd related from Harun, from (a name unclear), from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Abbas (9555; the number prints as 9000 and is taken from the sequence) that there is no harm in a menstruating woman or a person in janaba passing through the mosque so long as they do not sit in it. Ya'qub b. Ibrahim related from Hushaym, from Abu al-Zubayr (9556; printed as 1007, taken from the sequence) that any of them would pass through the mosque in janaba, crossing. Al-Hasan said (9557, from Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada) that the person in janaba passes through and does not sit. Ibrahim said (9558 from Ibn Bashshar from Abu Ahmad, and from al-Muthanna from Abu Nu'aym, both from Sufyan, from Mansur; 9559 from al-Muthanna, from Abu Ghassan Malik b. Isma'il, from Isra'il, from Mansur; 9560 from Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mansur) that if he finds no road but the mosque he passes through, and there is no harm in it when he has no other way. Sa'id b. Jubayr said (9561, from al-Muthanna, from [al-Himmani], from Sharik, from Salim) that he passes through and does not sit, and recited the verse; note 1 says the manuscript has an incomplete chain, the printed edition made it "Sharik related to us," and the editor judged the dropped name to be al-Himmani, which he put in brackets from the reports that follow and from al-Muthanna's chains of this kind earlier. The same, through Sharik, from Abd al-Karim, from Abu Ubayda (9562), from Simak, from Ikrima (9563), from al-Hasan b. Ubaydullah, from Abu al-Duha (9564); al-Hasan said (9565, Ibn Humayd, Harun, Isma'il) no harm for the menstruating woman and the person in janaba to pass and not sit; al-Zuhri said (9566, printed 9569, taken from the sequence) permission was given for the person in janaba to pass through the mosque. Al-Muthanna related from Abu Salih, from al-Layth, from Yazid b. Abi Habib (9567) that some men of the Ansar had doors opening into the mosque; they would become impure with no water to hand, want water and find no way but through the mosque, and God sent down the verse. Against this, al-Muthanna related from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Shu'ba, from Hammad, from Ibrahim (9568) that he may not cross the mosque unless he finds no other road, and Ibn Humayd related from Harun, from Ibn Mujahid, from his father (9569) that the person in janaba should not pass through the mosque taking it as a road. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 384}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 384-385, notes 1-2).** Abu Ja'far says the better of the two views is that of those who took it to mean passers through a road in the mosque, because God made clear the ruling for the traveller without water while in janaba in the clause that follows, on the sick, the traveller and the one coming from relieving himself, so that if the traveller were meant in the earlier clause, mentioning him again would have no intelligible sense, his ruling having already been given. So the verse means: believers, do not approach the mosques to pray in them while drunk until you grasp your own words, nor approach them in janaba up to the point of bathing, unless merely crossing. The word for the passer is the one who crosses by passing and cutting across; one says "I crossed this road," "so-and-so crossed the river" when he cut across it, and of a she-camel strong for journeys "she is a 'ubr of journeys" and "an 'abr of journeys," for her strength for them. Note 1: the printed edition and manuscript read a word in the wrong form and the editor corrected it. Note 2: the printed edition dropped the second 'abr asfar, taking it for a repetition, whereas two forms are meant, one with u on the first letter and a rest on the second, the other with a, and a third with i. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 385}

**The sick (pp. 385-388, reports 9570-9579, the editor's notes).** Abu Ja'far says the sick means those with a wound or smallpox who are in janaba. Ibn Humayd related from Yahya b. Wadih, from Abu al-Munbih al-Fadl b. Sulaym, from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Mas'ud (9570) that the sick person who is permitted tayammum is the one with a broken limb and the wounded; if the one with a fracture becomes impure he bathes and does not undo his splints, and the wounded does not undo his wound unless it is one not feared for. In the notes the editor says he did not find "Abu al-Munbih al-Fadl b. Sulaym" but found al-Fadl b. Sulaym al-Abdi, who related from al-Qasim b. Khalid and from whom Muslim b. Ibrahim related (Ibn Abi Hatim 2/3/63); that the printed edition dropped the phrase on the splints as though its editor did not know its sense, which is better known than that; and that the manuscript has a different word for the wounded and the printed one is right. Tamim b. al-Muntasir related from Ishaq b. Yusuf al-Azraq, from Sharik, from Isma'il al-Suddi, from Abu Malik (9571) that it is for the sick person with a wound that he fears to wash, who is allowed tayammum. Al-Suddi said (9572, from Muhammad b. al-Husayn, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) that sickness is wound, a wound whose owner fears for it from water, which would harm him if it touched him, and he performs tayammum (note 4: the manuscript and printed wording differ and the editor chose a reading between them). Sa'id b. Jubayr said (9573, from Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from Azra) that when there are wounds or sores he performs tayammum (note 5: Azra b. Abd al-Rahman al-Khuza'i; the printed edition had Urwa, the manuscript is right though unpointed). Ibrahim said (9574 and 9575): sores on the forearms. Al-Dahhak said (9576, from Ibn Humayd, from Harun, from Amr, from Juwaybir): one with a wound he fears for performs tayammum, then recited. Mujahid said (9577): sickness is a wound, a sore or smallpox that makes a man fear the cold and harm of water for himself; he performs tayammum with earth as the traveller without water does (note 1: the printed edition words it differently and the editor kept the manuscript). Ibn Bashshar related from Mu'adh b. Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, from Asim al-Ahwal, from al-Sha'bi (9578) that he was asked about a smallpox sufferer who becomes impure and said: the horsemen of this verse have gone. Note 2: the manuscript reads "about the saying: al-majdud," which the editor put in brackets for doubt; the phrase about the horsemen puzzled him, and he leaned to the view that the speaker meant the verse came down about the Ansar among the Prophet's companions whose doors were in the mosque (as in 9567), so the phrase refers to that part of the verse and means they have gone and none is left; he calls this the limit of his effort. Others said: Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd (9579) that the sick one is he who finds no one to bring him water and cannot reach it, with no servant or helper; if he cannot take the water and has no one to bring it and cannot crawl to it, he performs tayammum and prays when the prayer is due; Ibn Zayd said all this is his father's saying, and that he does not leave the prayer and is more excused than the traveller (pp. 387-388). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 388}

**Al-Tabari on the rest of the clause (p. 388, note 1, report 9580).** Abu Ja'far says the verse therefore means: if you are wounded, or have sores, a fracture or an ailment because of which you cannot wash from janaba, and you are resident, not travelling, perform tayammum with pure soil. The mention of travel means: if you are travelling, healthy and in janaba, perform tayammum. Likewise for one who has come from relieving himself while a healthy traveller: he also performs tayammum. The word for the low ground is a wide valley that dips, used as a euphemism for relieving oneself because the Arabs preferred to do so in the low grounds until it became the common usage, and anyone who relieves himself wherever it is on the earth is called one who has gone to the low ground, and "so-and-so came from the low ground" means he relieved himself. Note 1 refers to the earlier discussion of the word (the page number is unclear in the scan). It is reported from Mujahid that the low ground is the valley (9580, from al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 388}

**Touching (pp. 389-390, reports 9581-9589, notes).** Abu Ja'far says it means that you have been in hand-to-hand contact with women. Then the interpreters differed over the touching God meant, and some said it means intercourse. Humayd b. Mas'ada related from Yazid b. Zuray', from Shu'ba, from Abu Bishr, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9581): they mentioned touching, and some of the clients (mawali) said it is not intercourse and some Arabs said it is; I went to Ibn Abbas, who asked which party I was from, and when I said the clients he said the clients' party was beaten: touch, contact and intimacy all mean intercourse, but God alludes by what he wills to what he wills. A note says al-Bayhaqi gave it in the Sunan (1:125) through Ibrahim b. Marzuq, from Wahb b. Jarir, from Shu'ba. The same from Ibn Abbas through Abu Qays (9582, Ibn Bashshar from Muhammad b. Ja'far from Shu'ba) and Abu Ishaq (9583, Ibn al-Muthanna), that it is intercourse. Ibn Bashshar related from Wahb b. Jarir, from his father, from Qatada, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9584): I, Ata and Ubayd b. Umayr disagreed, Ubayd saying intercourse and Ata and I saying touching; we asked Ibn Abbas, who said the clients' party was beaten and the Arabs were right: it is intercourse, but God veils and alludes. Ibn al-Muthanna related from Abd al-A'la, from Sa'id, from Qatada (9585) that Ikrima, Sa'id b. Jubayr, Ata b. Abi Rabah and Ubayd b. Umayr disagreed over mutual touching, Sa'id and Ata saying something short of intercourse and Ubayd saying marriage; Ibn Abbas came out and said the two clients erred and the Arab was right: mutual touching is marriage, but God alludes and veils; and the same through Ibn Waki, from Muhammad b. Bishr, from Sa'id, from Qatada (9586). Ibn al-Muthanna related from Muhammad b. Uthma, from Sa'id b. Bashir, from Qatada (9587) that Sa'id and Ata took the word as pressing with the hand and Ubayd as intercourse, and Ibn Abbas said the two clients erred and the Arab was right (note 1: the word stands so in the printed edition and manuscript and perhaps should be the verbal noun; note 2: Muhammad b. Uthma is Muhammad b. Khalid b. Uthma). Abu Kurayb and Ya'qub b. Ibrahim related from Ibn Abbas (9588) that touching is intercourse; note 3: the editor fears a gap in this chain. Ibn Waki related from Ibn Ulayya and Abd al-Wahhab, from Khalid, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas the same (9589). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 390}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
