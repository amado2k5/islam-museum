## Summary
Verse 43 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 43 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the opening (p. 375, note 3).** Abu Ja'far says the call to those who believe is to those who affirmed God and his messenger; the prohibition on approaching prayer means they should not pray; the word for drunk is a plural of the word for a drunken man; and knowing what they say means that in their prayer they recognise what God commanded, or recommended them to say in it, as against what he forbade and warned against. Note 3 says al-Tabari's phrase stands in both the printed edition and the manuscript in a form the context does not suit and the editor put what he judged right. Then the interpreters differed over the intoxication God meant, and some said it is intoxication from drink. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 375}

**Those who said drink (pp. 376-377, reports 9524-9532, note 1).** Muhammad b. Bashshar related from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Abu Abd al-Rahman, from Ali (9524) that he, Abd al-Rahman and another man drank wine, and Abd al-Rahman led them in prayer (as the scan reads) and recited the sura of the unbelievers but muddled it, and the verse came down. Al-Muthanna related from al-Hajjaj b. al-Minhal, from Hammad, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Abdullah b. Habib (9525) that Abd al-Rahman b. Awf made food and drink and invited some companions of the Prophet; they ate and drank until they were tipsy and put Ali forward to lead the sunset prayer, and he recited the sura of the unbelievers, and God sent down this verse. Muhammad b. Sa'd related from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas (9526) that it was sent down before wine was prohibited. Ibn Humayd related from Jarir, from Mughira, from Abu Razin (9527) that it came down while they were drinking, before the prohibition came; and by the same chain (9528) that they drank after the verse of sura 2 ([[passage/quran-2-219|2:219]]) and after the verse of sura 4, and gave it up when the verse of sura 5 ([[passage/quran-5-90|5:90]] and [[passage/quran-5-91|5:91]], as the notes give them) came down. Muhammad b. Amr related from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (9529) that they were forbidden to pray drunk and then the prohibition of wine abrogated it, and al-Muthanna related the same from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (9530). Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada (9531) that they avoided intoxication when the prayers were due, then it was abrogated by the prohibition of wine. Ibn Humayd related from Jarir, from Mughira, from Abu Wa'il, Abu Razin and Ibrahim (9532) that they mentioned this verse with [[passage/quran-2-219|2:219]] (the sura 2 number prints as 90 and is read as 219 from the content) and [[passage/quran-16-67|16:67]] and said that was before the prohibition of wine came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 377}

**Those who said sleep (pp. 377-378, reports 9533-9534, note 1).** Others said sleep is what is meant. Ibn Waki related from his father, from Salama b. Nubayt, from al-Dahhak (9533) that the verse is not about one who approaches prayer drunk from drink; sleep is meant. Ahmad b. Hazim (the family name is unclear in the scan) related from Abu Nu'aym, from Salama, from al-Dahhak (9534) that it did not mean intoxication from wine, only from sleep. Note 1 says the printed wording of report 9533 differs from the manuscript, which has a gap breaking the sense and a marginal letter marking an error, and that he tried to read it and gave it as he read it (the lines are garbled in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 378}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 378-379, notes 2-4).** Abu Ja'far says the better of the two views is that the verse is God's prohibition for believers to come to prayer under the influence of drink before wine was prohibited, because of the many reports from the Prophet's companions to that effect, and that it was revealed about those named. He then raises an objection: how can that be, when a drunk person, while his mind is gone, is like a madman, and he holds that obligating the mad is impossible for their lack of understanding what they are commanded and forbidden? He answers that if the drunk were like the madman his command and prohibition would be invalid; but the drunk is one who understands what he does and leaves, though drink has made his tongue and body parts heavy and numb until he cannot perform his recitation in prayer and its obligatory limits, without his mind having gone, so he knows and understands what he was commanded and forbidden and cannot do some of it because of the numbness of his body. One who reaches a point where he no longer understands what he does and leaves has passed from drunkenness to the state of the mad, and he is not the one addressed by the prohibition, since he is mad; the one addressed is the drunk as described. Notes 2-4 mark places where the printed edition and manuscript differ, some of them the editor's uncertain readings, and their lines are garbled in the scan, so only the legible parts are given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 379}

**Janaba and the traveller (pp. 379-380, reports 9535-9541, notes 2-3).** Abu Ja'far says the interpreters differed over the second clause. Some said the meaning is: do not pray under the influence of drink until you understand your own words, and do not pray in a state of major impurity (janaba) unless you are crossing a road, that is, travelling, up to the point of bathing (note 1 refers to the earlier discussion of the word for the impure, shortly before, at p. 340). Among those who said it: Ibn Abbas, from Abu Mijlaz (9535, related by Muhammad b. Bashshar and Muhammad b. al-Muthanna from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Qatada; Ibn al-Muthanna said "in travel"; the same chain through Hisham, 9539) said the traveller, and from Muhammad b. Sa'd through his father, uncle, father and grandfather (9536): do not pray in janaba when you find water, and if you find none I have allowed you to wipe with the earth. Ali (9537, from Ibn Waki, from his father, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal, from Abbad b. Abdullah or from Zirr; and 9540, from Ibn Humayd, from Harun b. al-Mughira, from Anbasa, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal b. Amr, from Abbad b. Abdullah) said: unless you are travelling and find no water, then perform tayammum, and it came down about travel. Note 2 says the printed and manuscript wording of one phrase in 9537 is a slip and the editor put the right form. Note 3 (which runs onto p. 380) says Abbad b. Abdullah al-Asadi related from Ali and al-Minhal b. Amr from him; al-Bukhari said of him "there is something to consider," Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy, Ahmad spoke against him, and he is in al-Tahdhib. Sa'id b. Jubayr (9538, related by Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Salim al-Afṭas) said: the traveller. Mujahid (9541, from Ibn al-Muthanna, from Harun, from Ibn Mujahid, from his father) said: the traveller who finds no water performs tayammum, enters it (the mosque, as the editor says in note 1 on p. 380, adding that the printed edition dropped the word because its editor did not understand it, which he calls a bad piece of work) and prays. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 380}

**More reports on the traveller (pp. 380-382, reports 9542-9551, notes).** Mujahid (9542, from al-Hasan b. Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada, from Ibn Abi Najih) said it is a man on a journey who becomes impure, performs tayammum and prays; he said (9543, from al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih; note 2 says the printed edition and manuscript differ in one phrase and the editor put the right reading) that they are travellers who find no water and perform tayammum on clean earth; and the same (9544, from Muhammad b. Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih). Al-Hasan b. Muslim (9545, from Ibn Waki, from his father, from Mis'ar, from Bukayr b. al-Akhnas; note 1 on p. 381 says the printed wording differs from the manuscript and he kept the manuscript's except for one word) said: unless they are travelling and find no water, then perform tayammum. Al-Hakam (9546, from Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam, from Amr, from Mansur) said: the traveller who becomes impure and finds no water performs tayammum; al-Muthanna related from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan, from Salim al-Afṭas, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, and from Mansur, from al-Hakam (9547) that both said the impure traveller who finds no water performs tayammum and prays; likewise 9548 (Abu Nu'aym, from Sufyan, from Salim, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) and 9549 (from Sufyan, from Mansur, from al-Hakam). Al-Qasim related from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah b. Kathir (9550): we used to hear that it is about travel. Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd (9551) that it is the traveller who finds no water and must perform tayammum and pray, and Ibn Zayd said his father used to say this (the report ends on p. 382). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 382}

**Those who said the mosque (p. 382, reports 9552-9554, notes 1-3).** Others said the meaning is: keep away from the place of prayer when drunk until you grasp your own words, and when in janaba until you have bathed, unless crossing it to leave. The holders of this view said the word for prayer stands in for the place of prayer and the mosque, since the Muslims prayed in their mosques then and did not stay away from the congregation there (note 1 explains the word for congregating as attending or holding the congregational prayer, and the printed edition's different word), so the prohibition on approaching prayer sufficed without naming mosques and the place of prayer. Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Abd al-Karim al-Jazari, from Abu Ubayda b. Abdullah, from his father (9552) that it is passing through the mosque; note 2 says Abu Ubayda is the son of Abdullah b. Mas'ud, whose father is the Companion, and that his biography was given earlier. Ahmad b. Hazim related from Ubaydullah b. Musa, from Abu Ja'far al-Razi, from Zayd b. Aslam, from Ibn Yasar, from Ibn Abbas (9553) that he should not approach the mosque unless his road runs through it, so that he passes through and does not sit (note 3: the printed edition has a different word because its editor could not read the manuscript properly, written as it is). Ibn Bashshar related from Mu'adh b. Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, from Sa'id (9554), about the impure person, that he passes through the mosque, standing, not sitting, even without ablution, and recited this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 382}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 382 in the commentary on verse 43 of sura 4, after report 9554 on a person in major ritual impurity (janaba) passing through the mosque, and continue from p. 383. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 382}
