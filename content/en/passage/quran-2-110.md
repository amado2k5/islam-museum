## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "establish prayer and give zakat, and whatever good you send ahead for yourselves you will find with God", al-Tabari refers to what came earlier on the meaning of establishing prayer and giving zakat. On "whatever good you send ahead for yourselves" he says the sense is: whatever righteous deed you do in your lifetime, send it ahead before your death as a store for yourselves for the return, and you will find its reward with your Lord on the Day of Resurrection. "Khayr" is the deed God is pleased with, and "you will find it" means you will find its reward, from al-Rabi', the sense being enough from context, as in the verse of Umar ibn Laja', "the city glorified", meaning its people. He says God commanded them this so that they be cleansed of the error that had gone before in taking the Jews' advice, leaning on them, and the discourtesy in addressing the Prophet with "ra'ina", since establishing prayers is an expiation of sins, giving zakat purifies souls and bodies of the stains of sin, and sending good ahead gains the winning of God's pleasure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 505} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 506}

On "God sees what you do" he says it is a report to the believers that whatever they do of good and evil, secretly and openly, God sees and repays them good for good and the like for wrong, and though the speech takes the form of a report it contains a promise and a warning, a command and a deterrent, so that they strive in obeying Him and beware disobeying Him. "Basir" is a turned form of "mubsir", as "badi'" of "mubdi'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 506}
