**Commentary on verse 86 (pp. 225-232)**

On "he said, I only complain of my distress and my grief to God, and I know from God what you do not know", al-Tabari says: Ya'qub said to those of his sons who said "by God, you will not cease to remember Joseph until you become harad or are among the perishing": it is not to you that I complain of my distress and grief, but to God; by "I only complain of my distress" he means that he complains of his care and grief to no one but God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 225}

Report 19709, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: Ibn Abbas said "bathth" is my care. Report 19710, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Ya'qub spoke out of knowledge of God, "I only complain of my distress and my grief to God, and I know from God what you do not know", having seen their harshness, coarseness and bad speech to him: I did not complain of that to you, "and I know from God what you do not know". The editor notes that he corrected the wording "their speech to him", which means their rudeness in how they addressed him. Report 19711, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', Abu Usama and Awf: "I only complain of my distress and my grief to God", he said, my need and my grief are with God. Report 19712, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Hawdha ibn Khalifa and Awf, from al-Hasan, is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 226}

It is also said that "bathth" is the most intense grief, which is Abu Ubayda's wording in Majaz al-Qur'an; in al-Tabari's view it comes from "bathth al-hadith", pouring out a report, and what is meant is: I only complain of my news, in which I am in care, and pour out my talk and my grief to God. Report 19713, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Yahya ibn Sa'id and Awf: "I only complain of my distress", he said, my grief. Report 19714, through Ibn Bashshar, Yahya ibn Sa'id and Awf, from al-Hasan: my need. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 226}

As for "and I know from God what you do not know", Ibn Abbas used to say of it, as reported from him: report 19715, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: I know that Joseph's dream is true and that I shall prostrate to him. Report 19716, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: when they told him of the king's prayer, Ya'qub's soul sensed something and he said, there is no righteous one on earth but a prophet! and so he hoped, saying: perhaps it is Joseph. The editor says the wording of this report is disturbed and that he fears something has dropped out or been altered. Report 19717, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: we were told that God's prophet Ya'qub never had an affliction come to him without his good thought of God coming after it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 227}

Report 19718, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Humayd, Hakkam and Isa ibn Yazid: it was asked, how great was Ya'qub's grief for his son? He said: the grief of seventy bereaved mothers! He was asked, what was his reward? He said: the reward of a hundred martyrs, and he did not think ill of God for an hour of night or day. Report 19719, from al-Hasan, from the Prophet, the same, through Ibn Humayd, Hakkam, Abu Mu'adh and Yunus. Report 19720, from Talha ibn Musarrif al-Iyami, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, al-Mubarak ibn Mujahid and a man of the Azd: three things, do not mention them and avoid mentioning them: do not complain of your illness, do not complain of your misfortune, and do not praise yourself; the rest of the saying is on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 227}

Report 19720 from Talha ibn Musarrif continues: and I was told that a neighbour of Ya'qub ibn Ishaq came in to him and said: O Ya'qub, why do I see you broken and spent, when you have not reached the age your father reached? He said: what broke and spent me is the distress God tried me with in Joseph's care and remembrance! Then God revealed to him: O Ya'qub, do you complain of Me to My creatures! He said: Lord, it is a fault I made, so forgive it! He said: I have forgiven you. After that, when he was asked, he would say "I only complain of my distress and my grief to God, and I know from God what you do not know". Report 19721, from Habib ibn Abi Thabit, through Amr ibn Ali, Mu'ammal ibn Isma'il and Sufyan: it reached me that Ya'qub grew so old that his eyebrows fell over his cheeks and he would lift them with a rag; a man said to him, what has brought you to what I see? He said: length of time and many sorrows! Then God revealed to him: O Ya'qub, do you complain of Me? He said: a fault, forgive it. Report 19722, from Thawr ibn Yazid, through Ibn Humayd and Yahya ibn Wadih: Ya'qub went in to Pharaoh with his eyebrows fallen over his eyes, and Pharaoh said, what has brought you to this, Ibrahim! They said, he is Ya'qub, and he said, what has brought you to this, Ya'qub? He said: length of time and many sorrows. Then God said: O Ya'qub, do you complain of Me! He said: Lord, a fault I made, forgive it. Report 19723, from Layth ibn Abi Sulaym, through Amr ibn Ali, Abd al-Wahhab and Hisham: Gabriel went in to Joseph in the prison and he knew him, and said: O angel of fair face and sweet scent, honoured by his Lord, will you not tell me of Ya'qub, is he alive? He said: yes. He said: how great is his grief? He said: the grief of seventy bereaved mothers. He said: is there reward in that? He said: the reward of a hundred martyrs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 228}

Report 19724, from Mujahid, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq and Layth ibn Abi Sulaym: I was told that Gabriel came to Joseph in Egypt in the form of a man, and when Joseph saw him he knew him, stood and said: O angel of sweet scent and clean clothes, honoured by his Lord, have you any knowledge of Ya'qub? He said: yes. He said: how is he? He said: his sight has gone. He said: what took his sight? He said: grief for you. He said: what is he given for that? He said: the reward of seventy martyrs. Report 19725, from Abu Shurayh, through Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la and Ibn Wahb: I heard someone relate that Joseph asked Gabriel how great Ya'qub's grief was; he said the grief of seventy bereaved mothers; he asked how great his reward was; he said the reward of seventy martyrs. Report 19726, from Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far, through Ibn Wahb and Nafi' ibn Yazid: Gabriel went in to Joseph in the well or the prison, and Joseph asked about his father's grief; he said the grief of seventy bereaved mothers, and its reward the reward of a hundred martyrs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 229}

Report 19727, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Isma'il ibn Abd al-Karim and Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil: Gabriel came to Joseph with good news while he was in prison and said: do you know me, O truthful one? He said: I see a pure form and a good spirit unlike the spirits of the sinful. He said: I am the messenger of the Lord of the worlds, the trustworthy Spirit. He said: what brought you in to me at the entrance of sinners, when you are the best of the good, the chief of the near ones and the trustee of the Lord of the worlds? He said: do you not know, Joseph, that (the text here is unclear in the printed source) the purity of the prophets purifies the houses, and that the land they enter is the purest of lands, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 229}

and that God has purified the prison and what is around it through you, O purest of the pure and son of the purified? Only by the overflow of your purity and the purity of your righteous, sincere fathers is it purified! He said: how can I have the name of the truthful, and you count me among the sincere, when I have been put into the entrance of sinners and named among the astray and corrupt? He said: your heart was not tempted and you did not obey your mistress in disobeying your Lord, and for that God named you among the truthful, counted you among the sincere and joined you with your righteous fathers. He said: have you knowledge of Ya'qub, O trustworthy Spirit? He said: yes, God gave him fine patience and tried him with grief for you, so he suppresses it. He said: how great is his grief? He said: the grief of seventy bereaved mothers. He said: what reward does he have, Gabriel? He said: the measure of a hundred martyrs. Report 19728, from Thabit al-Bunani, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Layth: Gabriel went in to Joseph in the prison and Joseph knew him; he came and greeted him, and Joseph said: O angel of sweet scent and clean clothes, honoured by his Lord, have you knowledge of Ya'qub? He said yes. He said: do you know what he has done? He said: his eyes have turned white. He said: from what? He said: from grief for you. He said: how great is his grief? He said: the grief of seventy bereaved mothers. He said: has he a reward for that? He said: yes, the reward of a hundred martyrs. The editor notes differences between the printed text and the manuscript in some phrases of this report and keeps what he judged correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 230}

Report 19729, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: Gabriel came to Joseph in prison, greeted him, and came in the form of a man of fair face, sweet scent and clean clothes; Joseph said to him: tell me how Ya'qub is. He said: he grieved for you intensely. He said: how great was his grief? He said: the grief of seventy bereaved mothers. He said: how great is his reward? He said: the reward of seventy or a hundred martyrs. Joseph said: to whom did he turn after me? He said: to your brother Benjamin. He said: do you think I shall ever meet him? He said: yes. Joseph wept for what his father met after him, then said: I do not mind what I met if God shows him to me. Report 19730, from Ikrima, through Amr ibn Muhammad, Ibrahim ibn Yazid and Amr ibn Dinar: Gabriel came to Joseph in the prison and greeted him; Joseph asked about Ya'qub and he said: how severe is his grief! and his reward is the reward of seventy martyrs. He said: do you think I shall meet him? He said: yes. And Joseph's soul was at ease. Report 19731, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Layth: when Ya'qub went in to the king with his eyebrows fallen over his eyes, the king said: what is this? He said: the years and the sorrows. His Lord said: O Ya'qub, why do you complain of Me to My creatures, did I not do for you and do for you? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 231}

Report 19732, from Muslim ibn Yasar, raising it to the Prophet, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri and Abd al-Rahman ibn Ziyad: whoever pours out his grief has not been patient; then he recited "I only complain of my distress and my grief to God". The editor notes a difference between the manuscript and the printed text in the wording. Report 19733, from al-Hasan, through Amr ibn Abd al-Hamid al-Amuli, Abu Usama and Hisham: from the time Joseph left Ya'qub until the day he returned was eighty years, and grief never left his heart; he wept until he lost his sight. Al-Hasan said: by God, there was then no creature on earth more honoured to God than Ya'qub. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 231}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
