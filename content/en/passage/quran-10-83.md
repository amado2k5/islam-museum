**Commentary on verse 83 (pp. 163-167)**

On [[passage/quran-10-83|10:83]] al-Tabari says: none believed Moses, despite the proofs and evidences he brought them, except offspring of his people, fearful of Pharaoh and their chiefs. The interpreters then differed on the meaning of 'offspring' here. Some said it is the few. Report 17774, from Qatada, that Ibn Abbas used to say 'offspring' is the few; report 17775, from al-Dahhak: offspring is the few, as in [[passage/quran-6-133|6:133]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 163}

Others said the meaning is: none believed Moses except the offspring of those to whom Moses was sent among the Children of Israel, because of the long time, the fathers having died and the sons remained, so they were called offspring because they were the descendants of those who perished among the people Moses was sent to. Report 17776, from Mujahid: the sons of those he was sent to, through the length of time, their fathers having died; two reports from Mujahid follow, to the same effect; report 17779, from Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid, likewise; report 17780, from al-A'mash: the sons of those he was sent to, over whom time had grown long, their fathers having died. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on 'offspring' and to al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 164}

Others said the meaning is: none believed Moses except offspring of Pharaoh's people. Report 17781, from Ibn Abbas: the offspring who believed Moses were few people from other than the Children of Israel, from Pharaoh's people, among them Pharaoh's wife, the believer of Pharaoh's house, Pharaoh's treasurer and the treasurer's wife. A report has also come from Ibn Abbas that points the other way: report 17782, from him on 'offspring of his people': the Children of Israel, which shows that he took the offspring here to be the Children of Israel and not others of Pharaoh's people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 165}

Al-Tabari says that the soundest of these sayings for him on the meaning of the verse is the one he cited from Mujahid, that 'offspring' here means the descendants of those among the Children of Israel to whom Moses was sent, who perished before acknowledging his prophethood because of the long time, so that their descendants lived to see him and those God mentions believed in Moses. He says so because nothing but Moses is mentioned in this verse, so that the pronoun in 'of his people' should go back to Moses, who is near, is better than that it go back to Pharaoh, who is far, as there is no evidence from report or reasoning to the contrary. And in 'in fear of Pharaoh and their chiefs' there is clear evidence that the pronoun goes back to Moses and not to Pharaoh, for if it were Pharaoh the speech would be 'in fear of him'. By 'in fear of Pharaoh' he means in a state of fear on the part of those of Moses' people's descendants who believed in Moses; the sense is that none believed Moses except offspring of his people, of the Children of Israel, while afraid of Pharaoh and his chiefs lest they persecute them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 165}

One of the people of Arabic, who the editor says is al-Farra', claimed it was said 'offspring of his people' because those who believed him had mothers from the Children of Israel and fathers from the Copts, so were called offspring for that reason, as the sons of Persian men whose mothers are Arab are called 'abna''. Al-Tabari says the known meaning of 'offspring' in Arab speech is the descendants of the one they are attributed to through men and women, as in [[passage/quran-17-3|17:3]], and as in [[passage/quran-6-84|6:84]] and [[passage/quran-6-85|6:85]], where those descended through men and women are counted among Abraham's offspring. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 166}

As for 'and their chiefs', the 'mala'' are the nobles, and the sense is in fear of Pharaoh and of their nobles. The people of Arabic differed on whom the pronoun in 'their chiefs' refers to: some of the Basran grammarians said it refers to the offspring, so that he read it as in fear of Pharaoh and of the chiefs of the offspring of the Children of Israel. Some of the Kufan grammarians, al-Farra' the editor says, said it refers to Pharaoh, and that this is allowed though he is one man because when a king is mentioned with fear, travel or arriving from a journey the mind goes to him and those with him; you say 'the caliph arrived and the people grew many', meaning those with him, and 'he arrived and prices rose', because by his arrival you mean the arrival of those with him. The editor gives the readings of the printed text and manuscript and al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 166}

Al-Tabari goes on quoting al-Farra' that 'Pharaoh' may mean the house of Pharaoh with 'house' dropped, which is allowed, as in [[passage/quran-12-82|12:82]] where the people of the town are meant, and likewise [[passage/quran-65-1|65:1]]. Al-Tabari says the soundest of the sayings for him is that the pronoun in 'their chiefs' goes back to the offspring, and the sense is: in fear of Pharaoh and of the chiefs of the offspring, since among the offspring of the generation to whom Moses was sent were those whose father was a Copt and whose mother was an Israelite, and whoever was so stood with Pharaoh against Moses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 167}

His words 'lest he persecute them' mean that the belief of those among Moses' people's offspring who believed was in fear of Pharaoh persecuting them with punishment, so as to bar them from their religion and make them return from their belief to disbelief in God. He says 'persecute them' in the singular and not the plural because the report about Pharaoh shows it, his people being as he was, from what came before in 'in fear of Pharaoh and their chiefs'. His words 'Pharaoh was exalted in the land' mean a tyrant, arrogant toward God in His earth, 'and he was among the transgressors' means among those who pass beyond the truth to falsehood, that is, his disbelief in God, his abandoning faith in Him, his denial of God's oneness, his claim of godhead for himself and his shedding of blood unlawfully. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'persecution' and 'transgression'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 167}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
