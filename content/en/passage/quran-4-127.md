## Summary
Verse 127 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 127 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 127, first clause: the ruling on women and what is recited (pp. 253-256, notes 1-3 on p. 254, note 1 on p. 255 and notes 1-3 on p. 256).** Al-Tabari says the opening means that the Prophet's companions ask him for a ruling on the affairs of women and what is due to them and from them, the mention of women standing for their affair because the words make this clear; he is to tell them that God gives the ruling on them, and also on what is recited to them in the Book about orphan women to whom they do not give what is written for them while desiring to marry them. The interpreters differed on what is recited in the Book. One view is that it means: God gives you the ruling on them and on what is recited to you, and what is recited is the verses of the shares of inheritance at the start of this sura. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 256}

Reports for that view: 10539 (Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam ibn Salm, from Amr ibn Abi Qays, from Ata, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) says that the people of the Jahiliyya did not give the child an inheritance until he grew up and did not give inheritance to women, and that when Islam came God revealed the answer with reference to the shares at the start of the sura, to those who do not give women what was written for them. 10540 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father, from Aisha) says it concerns the orphan girl in a man's care who may share his property, and whom he, as the nearest of kin, is reluctant to marry, and keeps from marriage for her property and from marrying another, not wishing anyone to share her wealth. 10541 (Ibn Waki' and Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) says that in the Jahiliyya they did not give women or the young man inheritance until he reached puberty, and God revealed the verse in reference to the shares at the start of the sura. 10542 (Ibn Waki', from Jarir, from Ash'ath, from Ja'far, from Shu'ba, as the text has it) says they did not give the orphan girl an inheritance, did not marry her and kept her from marriage, until God revealed it. 10543 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdallah ibn Kathir, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) says that only the grown man inherited, not the small man or the woman; when the inheritance verses came it was hard on the people, who said that the small one who does not work and the woman would inherit like the man who works; they hoped for a development from heaven, waited, and when none came said that if this was settled it was binding, and asked the Prophet, and God revealed the clause; Sa'id adds that the guardian, if the woman was beautiful and wealthy, desired her, married her and kept her for himself, and if she had neither beauty nor wealth he married her to another and did not marry her himself. 10544 (Ibn Humayd and Ibn Waki', from Jarir, from Mughira, from Ibrahim) and 10545 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Mughira, from Ibrahim) say that when an orphan girl was plain they gave her no inheritance and kept her from marriage until she died and they inherited from her, and God forbade this. 10546 (Sufyan ibn Waki', from Abdallah, from Isra'il, from al-Suddi, from Abu Malik) says that a woman with a guardian who did not want her was kept by him if he did not marry her, and he let no one else marry her. 10547 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says that the people of the Jahiliyya gave neither women nor boys anything, saying that they neither raid nor take booty, and that God made the inheritance of women a binding duty so that a man would be reluctant to take for himself the property of his orphan girl if she was not good-looking; 10548 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is similar. 10549 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, through his father, uncle and grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) says that what is recited means the shares God laid down for women; the orphan girl in a man's care, whom he wished to marry or lie with, was denied her property in the hope that she would die and he would inherit; and if a near kinsman of hers died she was given none of the inheritance; this was in the Jahiliyya, and God made it clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 256}

The editor Shakir gives notes on these pages. On p. 254 and following he identifies Amr ibn Abi Qays al-Razi (called al-Azraq) and Ata as Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, notes that 10541 is another route of 10539 and that al-Hakim records a form of it from Ibn Abbas in the Mustadrak, calling the chain sound though the two sheikhs did not include it, and that the print has "the child" where the manuscript has "the young man"; that the report of Hisham ibn Urwa from his father from Aisha is given in several forms, including in al-Bukhari (Fath al-Bari 8:179 and 199) and earlier in this commentary at 8457; that al-Suyuti attributes 10543 also to Ibn al-Mundhir in al-Durr al-Manthur; that in 10547 the print omits the word "not", which spoils the sense, and he explains the verbs of rivalry and of begrudging; that in 10549 the verb for desiring is used with an elided preposition ("desires not to marry her"), a form he calls permissible, and that the word for near kinsman means a close relative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 256}

**Commentary on verse 127, first clause continued: the orphan girls and the second to fourth views on what is recited (pp. 257-262, notes on pp. 257-262).** Report 10550 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says that a man might have in his care an orphan girl who was plain and had property; he was reluctant to marry her and kept her back for her property, and God sent down about this what is heard. 10551 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says much the same. 10552 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) tells of Jabir ibn Abdallah al-Ansari, of the Banu Salima, who had a blind, plain cousin who had inherited from her father: he did not wish to marry her, nor to let another do so lest the husband take her property, and he asked the Messenger about it, as did others with girls in their care; Jabir kept asking whether such a girl inherits, and the Messenger kept answering yes, and God sent down the verse about them. The editor Shakir refers to another form of the Jabir account at report 4939. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 262}

The second view, which al-Tabari states as that of others, is that the meaning is: God gives you the ruling on them and on what is recited to you in the Book at the end of the sura, which they take to be the verse on the one with no ascendants or descendants ([[passage/quran-4-176|4:176]]). For it, 10553 (al-Harith, from Abd al-Aziz, from Sallam ibn Sulaym, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) says the people of the Jahiliyya did not give children inheritance until they reached puberty, and God sent down this verse, and also the verse on a man who dies childless. The editor identifies al-Harith as Ibn Abi Usama and Abd al-Aziz as Ibn Aban al-Umawi, and notes that the manuscript is faulty here and the first printer mended it well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 267}

The third view is that what is recited is the verse near the start of the sura on fearing injustice to orphans ([[passage/quran-4-3|4:3]]). 10554 (Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Wahb, from Yunus ibn Yazid, from Ibn Shihab, from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr) has Urwa ask Aisha, who explains that it is the orphan girl in her guardian's care, sharing his property, whose wealth and looks please him, so that he wishes to marry her without being fair in her dowry; they were forbidden to marry such girls unless they dealt fairly and gave them the highest dowry of their like, and were told to marry other women. Urwa adds that Aisha said people afterwards asked the Messenger for a ruling on women, and this verse came down, the recited text being the earlier verse. 10555 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from al-Layth, from Yunus, from Ibn Shihab, from Urwa, from Aisha) gives the same. Al-Tabari remarks that on these three views the word for what is recited is joined in the genitive to the pronoun in the ruling on them, so that the sense is: God gives you the ruling on women and on what is recited to you in the Book. The editor notes that 10554 and 10555 were given earlier (8457 and 8459), that the print has "in the care of her guardian" against the manuscript, and cites al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an for the grammar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 267}

The fourth view is that the verse came down about some Companions who asked the Messenger about some matters of women and left unasked other things they were doing, so God ruled on what they asked and on what they left. 10556 (Ibn al-Muthanna and Sufyan ibn Waki', from Abd al-A'la, from Dawud, from Muhammad ibn Abi Musa; the wording is Ibn al-Muthanna's) says they did not marry an orphan girl if she was plain, nor handed her property over, and inherited the elders and not the younger ones; God then ruled on what they had kept silent about, by the verse on a wife who fears her husband's ill-treatment ([[passage/quran-4-128|4:128]]). Al-Tabari adds that on this view the text recited is that verse. The editor notes that al-Bukhari lists a man of this name in his great history, that Ibn Hajar in Tahdhib al-Tahdhib seems to count two men of this name, and that the one here appears to be the one Dawud ibn Abi Hind narrated from. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 267}

**Al-Tabari's choice on what is recited (pp. 260-262).** Al-Tabari says the soundest of these views, and closest to the apparent wording, is that what is recited means the verses of the shares of inheritance at the start and end of the sura. His reason, in his own words as summarised here: a dowry is not something written for a woman except through marriage, so before a marriage nothing is owed to her by anyone; hence the view that the fairness in orphans' dowries is meant has no ground, since the verse speaks of orphan women who are kept from what was written for them, and that is the inheritance God made obligatory. He judges the report of Muhammad ibn Abi Musa to depart from the interpreters' sayings and from the apparent wording, with nothing in the text or in a report to support it, and holds that joining the parts of the speech together is preferable where possible, so that the phrase about orphan women is a continuation of what is recited rather than an explanation of the ruling on them, because of its nearness. The sense of the verse, then, is that God gives the ruling on women and on what is recited in His Book about orphan women to whom they do not give what He decreed for them of inheritance. 10557 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) glosses the phrase as not giving them inheritance, and 10558 (al-Muthanna, from Amr ibn Awn, from Hushaym, from Mughira, from Ibrahim) says it means inheritance, adding that they did not let women inherit. The editor notes that the manuscript drops words between two sentences, explains the sentence construction, records readings corrected from the manuscript, and glosses the grammatical term for an explanatory substitute. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 262}

**The desire to marry or to avoid marrying them (pp. 262-264, notes on pp. 262-264).** The interpreters differed on the next phrase. Some said it means turning away from marrying them: 10559 (Humayd ibn Mas'ada al-Sami, from Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, from Abdallah ibn Awn, from al-Hasan al-Basri) and 10560 (Ya'qub and Ibn Waki', from Ibn Ulayya, from Ibn Awn, from al-Hasan) give it so; 10561 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Yunus ibn Yazid, from Ibn Shihab, from Urwa, from Aisha) says it is a man's aversion to the orphan in his care when she has little wealth or beauty, and that they were forbidden to marry the orphans whose wealth and looks attracted them except with fairness, because of their aversion to the others; 10562 (al-Muthanna, from Abdallah ibn Salih, from al-Layth, from Yunus, from Ibn Shihab, from Urwa, from Aisha) repeats it. Others said it means desiring them: 10563 (Humayd ibn Mas'ada, from Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, from Ibn Awn, from Muhammad ibn Sirin, from Ubayda) and 10564 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim and Ibn Waki', from Ibn Ulayya, from Ibn Awn, who asked Ubayda). 10565 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas) says that in the Jahiliyya a man with an orphan girl would throw his garment over her, after which no one could marry her; if she was beautiful and he loved her he married her and ate her property, and if plain he kept her back until she died and then inherited from her, and God forbade this. Al-Tabari prefers the first reading, turning away from marrying them: their holding back the girls' property while hindering them was only so that they would inherit it, with no husband to interfere; had they desired to marry them there would be no recognised reason to withhold the property, since as guardians nothing prevented the marriage. The editor notes that the Aisha reports are continuations of 8457 and 8459, that the Ibn Abbas chain recurs at 8882, that the print had "al-Shami" for al-Sami (the manuscript has none of the attribution) and "Ubayd Allah" for Abdallah ibn Awn, and that the printed readings were corrected from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 264}

**The weak among the children and standing for orphans in fairness (pp. 264-267, notes on pp. 265-267).** Al-Tabari says the next clauses mean that God also gives the ruling on the weak among the children and on standing for orphans with justice, the earlier reports on this having been given; the ruling was that the young be given their rights in inheritance, since they used not to inherit, and that justice be done in giving each the share God assigned. 10566 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says they did not let a girl or small boy inherit, and God told them to deal fairly: each rightful person gets his right, male or female, the small as the grown. 10567 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says women, small and grown entered the inheritances, and the inheritance verses abrogated the earlier practice. 10568 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) and 10569 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) gloss fairness as justice. 10570 (Ibn Waki', from Abdallah, from Isra'il, from al-Suddi, from Abu Malik) says only the eldest, then the next eldest, inherited. 10571 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas) says that in the Jahiliyya neither the small nor daughters inherited, and God forbade this and made known each share, the male the like of two females' share ([[passage/quran-4-11|4:11]], [[passage/quran-4-176|4:176]]), small or grown. 10572 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) says the small and weak were given nothing until God ordered they be given their share. 10573 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hushaym, from Mughira, from Ibrahim) says that when the guardian of an orphan girl came to Umar ibn al-Khattab, he would tell him, if she was pretty and rich, to marry her to another and seek someone better than himself, and if she was plain and without property, to marry her himself as he was the one most entitled. 10574 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hushaym, from Yunus ibn Ubayd, from al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj) has a man ask Ali ibn Abi Talib about his orphan; Ali asks whether he would marry her if she were rich and beautiful, the man says yes, and Ali says to marry her then, plain and without money, and to choose the better for her, and if another is better for her to join her to that better one. Al-Tabari says that standing for orphans in fairness is the justice God commanded in their affairs. The editor notes that the print had a corrupt word for the weak ones where the manuscript is right, explains the word for state or concern with the saying that any weighty matter not begun with God's praise is cut off, explains the phrase for choosing the better for her, and observes that he cannot find a narrator named al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj in that generation; the print has al-Hasan (al-Basri), but he suspects a distortion, perhaps of al-Husayn ibn Malik al-Anbari, from whom Yunus ibn Ubayd narrated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 267}

Al-Tabari then explains the clause on whatever good they do: whatever of justice they show in the property of orphans, in what God ordered them, and in obedience to Him, God has always known what comes from them, counts it all against them and keeps it until He repays them on the Day of Resurrection. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of the word for whatever, of the verbs for being and knowing, and gives references in the language indexes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 267}
