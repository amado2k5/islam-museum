## Summary
Verse 86 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 86 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "and Ishmael, Elisha, Jonah and Lot; each we preferred over the worlds" al-Tabari says: and we guided also of Noah's offspring Ishmael, who is Ishmael ibn Abraham, and Elisha, who is Elisha ibn Akhtub ibn al-Ajuz. The readers differed in the reading of his name. Most readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read "al-Yasa'" with one lam, light; some claimed it is a "yaf'alu" form from the saying "wasi'a yasa'u", and the Arabs hardly ever put the article on a noun of the "yaf'al" shape, not saying "I saw al-Yazid", "al-Yahya came to me" or "I passed al-Yashkur" except in verse of necessity, and then only when praise is sought; he cites a line of Ibn Mayyada praising al-Walid ibn Yazid as found blessed, strong in the regions of the caliphate, and complete, putting the article on "Yazid" because he put it on "al-Walid" and made it follow the same form. A group of Kufan readers read "al-Layasa'" with two lams and doubling, saying that read so it is more like foreign names, and they denied the light reading, saying we do not know in Arab speech a noun on the "yaf'al" shape with the article. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 510}

The editor says the printed text has "al-Tajib came to me", a plain error, while the manuscript has an unpointed word whose right reading is as he set.

Abu Jafar said: the right reading for me is that of those who read it with one lam, light, for the consensus of the people of reports that this is the known form of his name rather than the doubled one, and since it is a foreign name it is pronounced as it is; the entering of... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 511}

The editor says of Ibn Mayyada's line that it is in al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, Amali Ibn al-Shajari, al-Khizana and Sharh Shawahid al-Mughni, from a poem in which he praised al-Walid ibn Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, that "ahna' al-khilafa" are its regions and sides, a figure for bearing the burdens of the caliphate, ordering the realm and governing the subjects, and that print has "a'ba' al-khilafa", one of the two transmissions, while he kept the manuscript's.

We stop here at the foot of p. 511, in the middle of al-Tabari's sentence preferring the reading "al-Yasa'" with one lam, and he continues on p. 512.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
