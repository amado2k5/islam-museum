## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "Have you not seen that Allah has subjected to you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth and has bestowed upon you His blessings, apparent and hidden, and among the people is he who disputes about Allah without knowledge, guidance, or a luminous Book": "Have you not seen," O people, "that Allah has subjected to you whatever is in the heavens"—the sun, the moon, the star, the clouds, and whatever is on the earth—animals, trees, water, sea, astronomy, and other benefits—all of this happens for your own benefit, your interests, your food, your sustenance, your provisions, and your refuge. You enjoy some of that. All of it, and you benefit from it all, "and bestow upon you His apparent and hidden blessings." Reciters differed in their interpretation; some Meccan scholars and the general Kufan read it as: "And bestow upon you a favor" in the singular form, and they interpreted it as Islam, or as testimony that there is no god but Allah. In the passage, statements from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, and Sufyan are also quoted.
