**Commentary on verse 84 (pp. 443-445)**

On [[passage/quran-11-84|11:84]] al-Tabari says: We sent to the children of Midian their brother Shu'ayb, and when he came he said 'my people, worship God, you have no god but Him', that is, obey Him and humble yourselves to Him in obedience to what He commands and forbids; you have no object of worship but Him who deserves your worship; 'and do not give short measure and weight', that is, do not cheat people of their rights in your measure and scale; 'I see you in good'. The interpreters differ on this good: some said it was cheap prices, and he warned them of dearness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 443}

Report 18467, from Ibn Abbas: 'I see you in good', he said cheap prices, 'and I fear for you the punishment of an all-encompassing day', he said dear prices; 18468, from al-Hasan, speaking of Shu'ayb's people: cheap prices; 18469, from al-Hasan: wealth and cheap prices. Others said it means I see you with wealth and the adornments of this world; 18470, from Qatada: the good of this world and its adornment. The editor comments on a narrator's name in report 18467 and on the name of al-Tabari's teacher in 18468. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 444}

Report 18471, from Qatada: he saw upon them a shell of the shells of this world and its adornment, the editor explaining that 'shell' is first the bark of a tree, then lent to what is worn as adornment of life; 18472, from Ibn Zayd: in your world, as God said 'if he leaves good', calling wealth good because people call it so. Al-Tabari says: the best view is what God relates of Shu'ayb, that he said to his people 'I see you in good', meaning the good of this world, which takes in wealth, the adornment of worldly life and cheap prices, with no sign that he meant some and not others. Shu'ayb said it because his people lived in ease, with cheap prices and much wealth, so he told them not to cheat people of their rights in their measures and scales, since God had given them wide provision, 'and I fear for you', for going against God's command and cheating people of their goods in measure and scale, 'the punishment of an all-encompassing day', that is, that a punishment come upon you from a day that surrounds you with its punishment; 'encompassing' describes the day though it belongs to the punishment, the sense being clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 445}
