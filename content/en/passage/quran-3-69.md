## Summary
Verse 69 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 69 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 69.** Al-Tabari says the first verb means "wished", and that the group means a company of the People of the Book, the people of the Torah among the Jews and the people of the Gospel among the Christians, who wish to turn the believers away from Islam and send them back to the disbelief they hold, destroying them by it. He notes he explained the sense of wishing earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 500}

**Leading astray as destruction.** Al-Tabari says leading astray here means destroying, as in [[passage/quran-32-10|32:10]], where the sense is when we are lost in the earth, meaning perished. He cites in support a line of al-Akhtal in his satire of Jarir, describing his rival as a speck in a murky wave which the flood threw out so that he perished, and a line of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani in his lament for the king al-Numan, which he takes to mean that those who destroyed him returned with a clear eye. The editor refers to earlier places for the verb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 500}

**The editor on the line of al-Nabigha.** The editor says the line is in al-Nabigha's diwan and in Lisan al-Arab, from his poem lamenting Abu Hujr al-Numan ibn al-Harith ibn Abi Shamir al-Ghassani, and gives lines before and after it to the effect that death is an appointment and life after the one lamented has no profit. He says al-Asmai and Abu Ubayda transmit the word with the unpointed letter sad; that al-Asmai explained it as: the first arrival came with news of his death and was not believed, and then those who followed him came with continuous, true report confirming his death, the word being taken from the leading horse and the one that runs second in a race; and that Abu Ubayda explained it as the people of prayer, the monks and religious. The editor rejects al-Asmai's account for its remoteness and strangeness and thinks Abu Ubayda's, weak as it is, better. He prefers the version al-Tabari transmitted, which has two explanations: the first is al-Tabari's, destroyers, which would require al-Numan to have died killed, and the editor found no report supporting that; the other is "those who buried him", who hid him in the earth, which is the common usage among the Arabs, and he cites in support a line of al-Muhabbal about a people who lost their festival and their horseman. He explains that people first denied the news of al-Numan's death and believed it when those who had buried him came with what they had seen. He adds that al-Jawlan is a mountain in the region of Damascus, in the district of Hawran, and that al-Nabigha's verse shows that al-Numan's dwellings and palaces were there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 500} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 501}

**They destroy only themselves.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: by trying to turn you from your religion they destroy no one but themselves, "themselves" meaning their followers and partisans in their creed; they destroyed themselves and their followers by what they attempted, since they thereby deserved God's anger, wrath and curse for their disbelief and for breaking the covenant He took from them in their scripture to follow and affirm Muhammad and acknowledge his prophethood. Then he says they do this in ignorance of the punishment God is bringing on them and the painful torment stored up for them, and that the closing clause means they do not perceive or know that they lead astray only themselves. He says he explained the sense of perceiving with evidence elsewhere, and the editor refers to earlier places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 501} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 502}
