## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "or do you say that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and the tribes were Jews or Christians? Say: are you more knowing, or God?" al-Tabari says there are two ways of reading it: "am taqulun" with ta', joined to "do you dispute with us about God", so it means say to them, do you argue with us about God, or do you say that Abraham; and "am yaqulun" with ya', as a question beginning anew, like "or do they say he invented it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 122}

Al-Tabari mentions that some grammarians claimed that "am yaqulun" with ya', if what follows "am" is a complete sentence, is joined to the first question; he himself chooses "am taqulun" with ta', joined to "say: do you dispute with us", meaning: which of these two will you do? Dispute with us about God's religion, claiming you are nearer to Him and better guided, or claim that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and those God named were Jews or Christians of your creed, so that people see your falsehood, since Judaism and Christianity arose after these prophets. He holds the reading with ya' impermissible for being irregular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 122} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 123}

He says the verse is God's argument for His Prophet against the Jews and Christians: say to them, do you dispute with us about God and claim your religion is better than ours, then bring your proof and we shall follow you; or say that Abraham and those with him were Jews or Christians, then bring proof of your claim, for God made them leaders to be followed; then say to them, are you more knowing of them and what religions they followed, or God? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 123} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 124}

On "and who is more wrong than he who conceals a testimony he has from God" he says: if the Jews and Christians claim that Abraham and those named were Jews or Christians, who is more wrong than they, when they concealed a testimony they have from God that Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and the tribes were Muslims, and ascribed Judaism and Christianity to them. The interpreters differ on it. From Mujahid, it concerns the Jews' saying of Abraham and those named that they were Jews or Christians, and God says do not hide from Me a testimony you have about them, while He knew they were lying; from al-Hasan, the people had from God a testimony that His prophets were free of Judaism and Christianity, as they had one that your wealth and blood are forbidden among you, yet they treated it as lawful; from al-Rabi', the People of the Book concealed Islam while knowing it is God's religion and finding written in the Torah and Gospel that they were not Jews or Christians, Judaism and Christianity coming a good while after them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 124} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 125}

For al-Tabari the point is that if the Jews and Christians claim Abraham and those named were Jews or Christians, the falsehood of their claim about God's prophets becomes plain to the polytheists who are their allies, and if they deny that they were Jews or Christians they are told come to the religion those prophets were on, for you and we both acknowledge they were right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 125}

Others said God meant the Jews in concealing the matter of Muhammad and his prophethood, which they knew and found in their books, from Qatada, al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd. Al-Tabari says he chose the first view because the verse comes after the account of the prophets God named, so it is likelier to belong to their account. He asks what testimony the Jews and Christians have from God about Abraham and those with him, and says it is the testimony in what God sent down to them in the Torah and Gospel, commanding them to follow their practice and their religion and telling that they were upright and Muslim, which they concealed when the Prophet called them to Islam and they said no one will enter the Garden unless a Jew or a Christian. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 126} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 127}

On "and God is not unaware of what you do" he says: say to those who dispute with you that God is not unaware of what you do in concealing the truth that He obliged you to make clear to people about Abraham and those with him regarding Islam, nor does He overlook your punishment for it; He counts it against you to repay you what you deserve in this world and the next, and He repaid them soon in this world by killing some of them and expelling them from their land, and will repay them in the Hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 127}
