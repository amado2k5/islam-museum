## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "those who disbelieved and died as disbelievers, on them is the curse of God, the angels and all people" al-Tabari says: those who denied Muhammad's prophethood and rejected him from the Jews and Christians, other communities and the polytheists, and died so, God put them far from His mercy and the angels and all people curse them, the curse of the angels and people being their saying "on them is the curse of God". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 261} He asks how the curse of all people can be on one who dies an unbeliever when most of them do not believe, and gives the interpreters' differing answers: some said only the believers are meant, from Qatada and al-Rabi'; others said it is on the Day of Resurrection, when the unbeliever is made to stand before the witnesses and all people curse him, from Abu al-'Aliya; others said it is the saying of whoever it may be, "may God curse the wrongdoer", which reaches every unbeliever since he is a wrongdoer, from al-Suddi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 261} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 262} Al-Tabari chooses that all people are meant in the sense of their cursing them by saying "may God curse the wrongdoer", since no son of Adam refrains from saying it, whatever his community, so every unbeliever comes under his curse; he cites the verse of Hud, "behold, the curse of God is on the wrongdoers". As for what Qatada said, he holds the apparent text is against it and there is no proof for it, since God told that the unbelievers curse the wrongdoers in the Hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 263}
