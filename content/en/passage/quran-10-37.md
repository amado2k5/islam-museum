**Commentary on verse 37 (pp. 90-91)**

On [[passage/quran-10-37|10:37]] al-Tabari says: it is not fitting for this Qur'an to be invented apart from God, meaning that no one should fabricate it from other than God, which is like the sense of [[passage/quran-3-161|3:161]] on what is fitting for a prophet. It is a report from God that this Qur'an is from Him, that He sent it down to His servant Muhammad, and a denial of the polytheists who said it was poetry and soothsaying and of those who said that Muhammad was only learning it from Yuhannis the Roman. He says to them that no one among creation could invent it apart from God, "but a confirmation of what came before it", meaning He sent it confirming earlier scriptures sent to God's prophets, such as the Torah and the Gospel, "and an explanation of the Book", the exposition of the Book that God wrote for the community of Muhammad and the obligations He laid on them. The editor refers to the gloss on "invention" and notes the passive reading of the Medinans and Kufans; he also notes that the printed text gives another name in place of "Yuhannis" and that he keeps the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 90}

He then says the Book's obligations and rulings were in His prior knowledge, "there is no doubt in it": there is no doubt that it is the confirmation of the scripture before it and the explanation of the Book, from the Lord of the worlds, not an invention or fabrication from another. The editor refers to the glosses on "explanation", "doubt" and "the worlds". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 91}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
