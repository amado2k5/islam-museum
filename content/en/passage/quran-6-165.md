## Summary
Verse 165 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 165 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "then to your Lord is your return and He will tell you of that in which you differed", God tells His Prophet to say to those who equate idols with their Lord: each doer among us and you has the reward of his deed and bears his own burden, so do what you do; then to your Lord, people, is your return, that is, your destination and turning, and He will tell you what you differed over in the world of religions and creeds, since some of you follow Judaism, some Christianity, some Magianism and some the worship of images and the claiming of partners and rivals with God; then He will repay all of you for what he did in the world of good or evil, and you will then know who among us is the doer of good and who the doer of ill. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "return" and "telling". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 287}

Then, on "it is He who made you successors on the earth and raised some of you above others in rank, to test you in what He gave you", God tells His Prophet and his community: God made you, people, successors of the earth by destroying the earlier generations and peoples and making you their successors on it, succeeding them and inhabiting it after them. "Khala'if" is the plural of "khalifa", as "wasa'if" is of "wasifa", from the saying "so-and-so succeeded so-and-so in his house"; as in a line of al-Shammakh describing how death strikes others and misses him, and how he succeeds people after people in the dwellings. The note gives the setting of the poem, said to his wife Aisha when she blamed him for his long care of his wealth, and says the line sits loosely in the poem. He gives (14308) from al-Suddi that "successors of the earth" means He destroyed the generations and made us successors after them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 288}

As for "raised some of you above others in rank", he says: He made your states differ, setting some above others, raising one above another by the provision He spread for him, favouring him with the wealth and ease He gave over this poor man in what he was granted of worldly means, and another above another by the strength and power He gave over this weak and feeble one, so He made them differ by raising one in rank above another and lowering one from another; and he gives (14309) from al-Suddi that this is in provision. "To test you in what He gave you" means to try you in what He bestowed of His favour and granted of His provision, so He knows who obeys Him in what He commanded and forbade and who disobeys, and who pays out of what He gave the due He commanded him to pay and who falls short. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "rank", "testing" and "giving". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 289}

Then, on "your Lord is swift in punishment, and He is forgiving and merciful", he says: your Lord, Muhammad, is swift in punishment to whoever angers Him by committing sins and going against His command in what He commanded and forbade, and to whoever is tested with His favour and bounty and turns his back on Him despite His favour and establishing him on earth, as He did with earlier generations; "forgiving" means He covers the sins of whoever is tested by His blessing and turns to Him in obedience when tried with His command and prohibition, so He veils him in them and does not expose him at the reckoning; "merciful" in that He forgoes punishment for past sins between him and Him if he repents and turns back to Him before meeting Him and returning to Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 290}

Here ends the commentary on Surat al-An'am in this volume. The note records that at this point a part of the old division from which the copy was taken ended, with the words "end of the commentary on Surat al-An'am; praise be to God as He deserves, and blessings on our master Muhammad and his family", followed by the commentary on the surah in which al-A'raf is mentioned and then by the basmala. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 290}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
