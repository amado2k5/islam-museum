## Summary
Verse 95 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 95 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "God is the splitter of the grain and the date-stone" al-Tabari says: this is God's alerting of these who set gods and idols equal to him to the place of his argument against them and making known to them the error of what they persist in, associating images in their worship of him. He says: the one who has worship, people, apart from all you worship of gods and idols, is God who split the grain, meaning split the seed of everything that grows of plants and brought out the crop from it, "and the stone" of everything planted that has a stone, bringing out the tree from it. "Habb" is the plural of "habba", "nawa" the plural of "nawat". 13581, from al-Suddi: splitting the grain from the ear, splitting the stone from the palm; 13582, from Qatada: he splits the grain and the stone from the plant; 13583, from Ibn Zayd: God is the splitter of it, split it and grew from it what he grew, split the stone and brought out the growth of a palm, split the seed and brought out the growth of what he created. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 551}

Others said "faliq" means creator. 13584 and 13585, from al-Dahhak: creator of the grain and the stone; 13586, from Ibn Abbas: creator of the grain and the stone. Others said the sense is that he split the cleft in the seed and stone. 13587 and 13588, from Mujahid: the two clefts in them; 13589, from Abu Malik: the cleft that is in the stone and in wheat; 13590, from Mujahid: the two clefts in them; 13591, from al-Dahhak: creator of the grain and the stone, meaning every seed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 552}

Abu Jafar said: the best of the views on this for being right is what we put forward, because God followed it with his report of bringing out the living from the dead and the dead from the living, so it was known by that that he meant by his report of himself that he splits the grain from the plant and the stone from the shoots and trees, as he brings out the living from the dead and the dead from the living. As for what is related from al-Dahhak on the sense of "splitter", that it is creator, it is a saying, unless he meant that he is the creator of the plants and shoots from it by splitting it, for which I know no sense, since it is not known in Arab speech that "God split the thing" means created. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 552}

On "he brings out the living from the dead and brings out the dead from the living; that is God, so how are you turned away?" he says: he brings the living ear out of the dead grain and the dead grain out of the living ear, the living tree out of the dead stone and the dead stone out of the living tree. The tree while standing on its roots has not dried and the plant on its stalk has not withered, the Arabs call it living, and when it withers and dries or is cut from its root they call it dead. 13592, from al-Suddi: he brings out the living ear from the dead grain and the dead grain from the living ear, the living palm from the dead stone and the dead stone from the living palm; 13593, from Abu Malik: the palm from the stone and the stone from the palm, the grain from the ear and the ear from the grain. Others said, and 13594, from Ibn Abbas: "God is the splitter of the grain and the stone, bringing out the living from the dead and the dead from the living", he brings out the dead sperm-drop from the living, and then brings out from the sperm-drop a living human. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 554}

Abu Jafar said: we chose the interpretation we chose because it follows "God is the splitter of the grain and the stone", given that "he brings out the living from the dead and the dead from the living", though it reports God's bringing out of the grain the ear and out of the ear the grain, still includes in its generality what is related from Ibn Abbas in its interpretation, every dead thing God brought out of a living body and every living thing God brought out of a dead body. As for "that is God", he says: the doer of all that is God, majestic is his majesty; "so how are you turned away?" says: by what turns from the truth, ignorant ones, are you turned from the right and averted? Will you not reflect and know that it does not befit that a partner be set in the worship of him who favoured you by splitting the grain and the stone, bringing out for you from the dry grain and stone crops, tillage and fruits, some of which you eat and some of which you enjoy, one who neither harms nor benefits, neither hears nor sees? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 554}

The editor refers for "turning" to an earlier passage.

Verse 95 closes here on p. 554, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-96|6:96]].
