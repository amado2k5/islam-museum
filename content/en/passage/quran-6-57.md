## Summary
Verse 57 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 57 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: I am on a clear proof from my Lord, and you have denied it; I have not what you hasten; judgement is only God's, he relates the truth and is the best of judges" al-Tabari says to his prophet: say, Muhammad, to these who hold others equal to their Lord and call you to join others with your Lord: I am on a clear proof from my Lord, that is, a plain exposition I have understood and a proof made clear to me from my Lord, namely my affirming oneness and my devotion in serving him with nothing joined to him. So the Arabs say "so-and-so is on a clear proof about this matter" when he has a clear grasp of it, and from that is a poet's line "do you seek proof after his admission". "And you have denied it" says: and you have denied your Lord, the pronoun in "it" referring to the Lord; "I have not what you hasten" says: what you hasten of God's vengeance and punishment is not in my hand, nor am I able to do it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 398}

That is because, when God sent his prophet with his oneness, he called them to God and told them he was his messenger, they said, [[passage/quran-21-3|21:3]], "is this other than a man like you? Will you come to magic with your eyes open?", and said of the Qur'an that it is muddled dreams, some said no, it is a thing he invented, and others said no, Muhammad is a poet, let him bring us a sign as the earlier ones were sent; God said to his prophet: answer them that signs are in God's hand, not yours, you are only a messenger and it is for you only to convey what you were sent with, and God will decree the truth between them and you and part you from them by it so that the right among you and the false is made plain; "and he is the best of judges" means the best who makes plain and distinguishes between the rightful and the false and tells them, since no partiality enters his judgement and decree toward anyone because of a means to him, kinship or relation, nor injustice in his decree, as he takes no bribe in judgements that he would be unjust; so he is the most just of judges and the best of those who decide. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 398}

The editor refers for "proof" to the language indexes, says he did not know the poet of the line, refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, and for "decision" to p. 338 of volume 5.

It has been reported to us in Abdullah's reading, "he is the swiftest of those who decide". Report 13302, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he said of Abdullah's reading... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 398}

We stop here at the foot of p. 398, at the start of report 13302 from Sa'id ibn Jubayr on Abdullah's reading "he is the swiftest of those who decide", which continues on p. 399.
