## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "And those who disbelieve say, 'Do not bring us the Hour.' Say, 'Yes, by my Lord, You will surely bring to you the Knower of the unseen, from which not the weight of an atom in the heavens or on the earth, nor smaller or greater than that, except in a clear Book.'" And those who denied Allah's ability to restore His creation after their destruction hasten to you, in the form they were in before their destruction, from among your people by the Hour, mock Your promise to them and deny Your news. Say to them: Yes, it will come to you, by my Lord. I swear by it that the Hour will come upon you. Then after mentioning the Hour and glorifying it, His Majesty returned to Himself and said: "Knower of the unseen." Reciters differed in their reading; the general reciters of Medina read it as 'Knower of the unseen' as an active participle, using the nominative as a nominative for resumption, as there is a barrier between the phrase 'and the unseen' and 'Knower of the unseen.' Some reciters of Kufa and Basra read this as ''alam' as a subject example, but they reduced the 'alam' in response to the phrase 'and my Lord,' as it was an attribute. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Qatadah.
