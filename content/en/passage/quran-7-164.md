**Commentary on verse 164 (pp. 184-193)**

Al-Tabari says God is addressing His Prophet Muhammad: remember also when a community of them, a group that was preaching to those who broke the Sabbath and forbidding them to disobey God in it, said "why do you preach to a people whom God will destroy" in this world for disobeying Him, opposing His command and treating as lawful what He forbade them, "or punish with a severe punishment" in the hereafter. Those who had been forbidding them answered their words: our preaching is an excuse before your Lord, with which we discharge the duty He laid on us of commanding the right and forbidding the wrong, "and perhaps they will fear God", that is, they may be conscious of God and return to obedience and repent of their disobedience and transgression. Ibn Abbas is reported as explaining "an excuse before your Lord" as "we were angry at their deeds", and "that they may fear" as "that they desist from what they are doing", and Ibn Zayd as "that they leave this work". The editor notes that the verse is vowelled in the printed mushaf with an accusative on "excuse" while al-Tabari's choice is the nominative, refers to the earlier explanations of "community" and "fearing God", and notes that Ibn Abbas's report is given at length in the second volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 184} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 185}

He then gives the differences among the readers over "an excuse": most readers of the Hijaz, Kufa and Basra read it in the nominative, in the sense he has set out, and some Kufans read it in the accusative, in the sense of "making an excuse": we preached and did so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 185}

The scholars differed over this group that said "why do you preach to a people whom God will destroy": was it among the saved or the destroyed? Some said it was among the saved, because it was the group that forbade the doomed group from transgressing in the Sabbath. Ibn Abbas is reported as saying that the town was on the sea shore and called Ayla; that God forbade them fish on their Sabbath, so the fish came to them openly on the shore and, once the Sabbath had passed, they could not catch them; that after a time one party of them took the fish on their Sabbath and another party forbade them, but they only increased in error and defiance, while a further party went on forbidding them; and that when this had lasted a long time some of the forbidders said: know that these people are due the punishment, why preach to a people God will destroy? They were angrier for God than the other party, and said "an excuse before your Lord, and perhaps they will fear God", though every one of them had been forbidding. When God's anger fell, the two parties, those who said "why do you preach" and those who said "an excuse before your Lord", were saved, and God destroyed the people of disobedience who took the fish and made them apes and pigs. The editor notes what he corrected in al-Tabari's wording between the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 186}

Al-Tabari completes Ibn Abbas's report: the people of a town by the sea had their fish come to them openly on their Sabbath, from every direction, and not on other days. They said that if they took of these fish on the day they came it would suffice them for the rest of the days. Believers preached to them and forbade it, and a party of the believers said these were people set on something they would not abandon, and God would disgrace and punish them; so the believers said to each other "an excuse before your Lord": if destruction came perhaps they would be saved, and if the wrongdoers desisted they would have a reward. It adds that God had assigned the Children of Israel a day to devote to His worship, which was Monday; the wicked among them passed from it to Saturday and called that the day, Moses forbade them, they differed over it, and the Sabbath was laid on them with a ban on working or transgressing in it. A man of theirs went to gather firewood; Moses seized him and asked whether anyone had told him to do it, and when no one had, his companions stoned him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 186} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 187}

Report 15268, from al-Suddi: some of the forbidders said to others why preach to people when you have preached and they have not obeyed, and others answered "an excuse before your Lord". Reports 15269 and 15270, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas: he said, "I do not know whether those who said 'why do you preach to a people whom God will destroy' were saved or not"; Ikrima said he kept at him until he made him recognize that they were saved, and Ibn Abbas gave him a robe. The editor notes that Mu'adh ibn Hani' al-Qaysi is trustworthy. Report 15271, from Ikrima: he entered on Ibn Abbas with the Qur'an in his lap, weeping, and Ibn Abbas recited the verses to "for their wickedness" and said "I do not hear the third party mentioned; we fear we may be like them." Ikrima replied: do you not hear "when they exceeded what they were forbidden"? His distress lifted and he gave him a robe. The editor points out that the opening of this report was given earlier as 15254. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 187} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 188}

Report 15272, from Ikrima through a man: he came to Ibn Abbas one day as he wept with the Qur'an in his lap, open at sura al-A'raf, and asked him why. Ibn Abbas asked: do you know Ayla? He said yes. He said: a clan of the Jews lived there; the fish were driven to them on the Sabbath and then dived beyond reach, so that they could be taken only with great toil. On the Sabbath they came openly, white and fat, like ewes near to giving birth, flattening their backs on the thresholds and buildings. They lived thus for a time, then Satan whispered to them that they had been forbidden only to eat the fish on the Sabbath, so they might take them then and eat them on other days. One party said so; another said that on the contrary they were forbidden to eat, take or hunt them on the Sabbath. When the coming Friday arrived one party went out with themselves, their sons and their women; one party withdrew to the right and another to the left. Those on the right said: woe to you, fear God, do not expose yourselves to His punishment. Those on the left said "why do you preach to a people whom God will destroy". Those on the right said "an excuse before your Lord", for they preferred that the offenders not be struck or destroyed, and if they did not desist the excuse stood. The offenders went on with their sin, so those on the right said: we will not spend the night with you in your city, and we think you will not see the morning before God strikes you with a swallowing of the earth, a hail of stones, or some other punishment of His. In the morning they knocked at the gate and received no answer, so they set a ladder and raised a man to the top of the wall, who looked and said: servants of God, apes, howling, with tails! They opened the gate and went in. The apes knew their kin among the people, but the people did not know their kin among the apes; the apes came to their relatives, smelling their clothes and weeping, and the people said to them: did we not forbid you? and they nodded. Ibn Abbas then recited [[passage/quran-7-165|7:165]] and said: I see that the Jews who forbade were saved, and I do not see the others mentioned, while we see things we dislike and do not speak about them. Ikrima answered: do you not see that they hated what the others were doing, opposed them and said "why do you preach"? So he ordered two thick cloaks for him. The editor lists the manuscript and printed variants in words of the report and his corrections, explains the word for ewes near to giving birth, and notes that al-Suyuti omitted a word he found difficult. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 188} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 189} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 190}

Report 15273, from Qatada: it was told to us that on the Sabbath the fish came and lay flat on their shores and courtyards because of what had reached them of God's command in the water, and on other days they stayed far off until someone sought them. Satan came to them and said that only eating them on the Sabbath was forbidden, so they should catch them then and eat them afterwards. The people became three kinds: one held back from God's sacred limit and forbade disobedience to Him, one held back out of awe of God, and one violated the limit and fell into sin. The editor says that the report has a certain gap, marked with dots, explains the word "inna" in the sense of "yes" in the previous note, and cites a line of verse for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 190}

Report 15274, from Ibn Abbas through Mujahid: fish were forbidden them on the Sabbath and came to them openly on that day, a trial they were put to, and did not come on other days unless sought, which was also a trial; they took them on the Sabbath treating it as lawful and in disobedience, and God said to them "be despised apes", except a party who did not transgress and forbade them, and said to one another "why do you preach to a people". Report 15275, from Ibn Zayd: "perhaps they will fear" means perhaps they will abandon what they are doing. They had been tried by the fish being held back from them: they kept the Sabbath, doing no work in it, and on the Sabbath the fish came openly, while on other days not one fish came. They craved the flesh of fish, and one of them took a fish, tied a thread to its tail and the thread to a stake, and left it in the water; when the sun set on Sunday he pulled it in by the thread and grilled it. His neighbour smelled the fish and asked him, and he denied it, then the neighbour saw the fish in his oven, and the man told him; the neighbour said he thought God would punish him. When no quick punishment came, on the next Sabbath he took two fish and tied them, and his neighbour, who saw that no punishment hurried upon him, joined in, and they began to hunt. The people of the town noticed, and those who forbade the wrong forbade them. They were two parties: one forbade and held back, the other forbade but did not hold back. Those who had forbidden and held back said to those who forbade without holding back "why do you preach to a people whom God will destroy", and the latter said "an excuse before your Lord". The people of the town said: you have done an evil deed, whoever wishes to keep apart and be clean should keep apart from these. So they separated within the city, put up a wall between them and made doors in it. When night came God sent a punishment on them, and in the morning the believers saw none of them; entering, they found them apes, the man, his wives and his children. They began to say: so-and-so, did we not warn you of God's assaults and punishments? and there was only weeping. Ibn Zayd adds that God punished only the wrongdoers who persisted, and that the forbidders had all forbidden, though some were better than others. The editor explains the words for intense craving, for a kind of rock and for fish, and notes a gap in the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 191} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 192}

Report 15276, from Ikrima: Ibn Abbas read the verse and said "I do not know whether the people were saved or destroyed"; Ikrima kept at him until he recognized that they were saved, and he gave him a robe. Report 15277, from Ibn Ruman as told by Malik: the fish came to them on the Sabbath and when evening came they went, so that none was seen until the next Sabbath. One of them took a thread and a peg and tied a fish in the water on the Sabbath, and when it was the night of Sunday he took it and grilled it. People smelled it and asked, and he denied it until he said it was fish skin they had found. The next Sabbath he did the same and then told them, "if you wished you could do as I do," and he informed them and they did the same until it was widespread. They had a city with an outskirts, which they locked, and the transformation struck them. Their neighbours came in the morning, found the city locked, called, got no answer and climbed over; they were apes, and an ape would come near and rub against someone it had known before. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 192} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 193}

Al-Tabari then gives the other view, that the group that said "why do you preach to a people whom God will destroy" was among the destroyed party, and begins with report 15278 from Ibn Abbas that they invented the Sabbath, were tried in it, and the fish were forbidden them on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 193}

We stop here at the foot of p. 193, at the start of report 15278 from Ibn Abbas, in the view that the group that said "why do you preach" was among the destroyed; the next batch begins at p. 194. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 193}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
