**Commentary on verse 109 (pp. 18-19)**

Al-Tabari says the body of men among Pharaoh's people, the nobles, said: this man, meaning Moses, is "a knowing sorcerer", one who seizes people's eyes by deceiving them until the staff seems a snake, the dark-skinned man seems white, and a thing seems other than it is. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "chiefs". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 18}

He explains the root of "sorcery" by the saying that the rain "bewitched" the land when it poured on it, cutting its plants at the roots and turning the soil over, so that the land is "bewitched"; the sorcerer's sorcery is likened to this because he makes the one he bewitches see a thing other than it is. He cites a line of Dhu al-Rumma describing the mirage (not reproduced here). The editor praises this explanation as clearer than what the dictionaries give and says it should be noted there, and in his footnote he sets out the transmissions of the line, the meaning of its words and Abu Amr ibn al-Ala's version. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 19}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
