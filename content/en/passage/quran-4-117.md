## Summary
Verse 117 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 117 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 117, first clause: the differing views on the word for females (pp. 207-209, note 1 on p. 207, notes 1-3 on p. 208 and notes 1-3 on p. 209).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said the sense is that they call upon, besides God, only al-Lat, al-Uzza and Manat, whom God called females because the polytheists named them with female names. Reports 10430 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Husayn, from Abu Malik: al-Lat, al-Uzza and Manat, all feminine) and 10431 (al-Muthanna, from Amr ibn Awn, from Hushaym, from Husayn, from Abu Malik, to the same effect) give this, as does 10432 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): they name them females, Lat, Manat and Uzza; and 10433 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd): their gods, al-Lat, al-Uzza, Isaf and Na'ila, are females whom they call besides God, and he recited the following clause on the rebellious devil. The editor's note says Isaf, also spelled Yasaf, and Na'ila are said to be Isaf ibn Amr and Na'ila bint Sahl of Jurhum, who found themselves alone in the Kaaba and committed fornication there, so God turned them to two stones which Quraysh later worshipped; it is also said that Amr ibn Luhayy set them up on al-Safa and al-Marwa, where he slaughtered before them facing the Kaaba. Others said the sense is that they call only on lifeless matter in which there is no spirit: report 10434 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya ibn Salih, from Ali ibn Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) says dead; report 10435 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says dead, with no spirit, the manuscript having the plural "spirits"; report 10436 (al-Muthanna, from al-Hajjaj, from Mubarak ibn Fadala, from al-Hasan) says the females are everything dead with no spirit in it, a dry log or a dry stone, and links the verse with the following verses on the rebellious devil and the slitting of cattle's ears. Others said the polytheists used to say that the angels are daughters of God: report 10437 (Yahya ibn Abi Talib, from Yazid, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) says the angels, whom they claim are daughters of God. The editor notes that the manuscript lacks the text from the beginning of 10437 to this point, and that the printed text has an added particle there which he removed. Others said the idol-worshippers used to name their idols females, and God sent the verse down accordingly: report 10438 (Sufyan ibn Waki', from Yazid ibn Harun, from Nuh ibn Qays, from Abu Raja', from al-Hasan) says every clan of the Arabs had an idol they called the female of such-and-such a clan, so God sent the verse down; report 10439 (al-Muthanna, from Muslim ibn Ibrahim, from Nuh ibn Qays, from Muhammad ibn Sayf Abu Raja' al-Hudani, from al-Hasan) is the same. The editor notes that Ibn Jinni's al-Muhtasab (question 1430, a manuscript he holds) reports al-Hasan as saying that it was the name of an idol of a clan of the Arabs whom they worshipped and called by that name, so he fears the words "they worshipped them" dropped out of the copyist's text; and that Abu Raja' Muhammad ibn Sayf al-Hudani met Anas, narrated from al-Hasan, Ibn Sirin, Matar al-Warraq and Ikrima and others, is reliable and is in the Tahdhib, the printed text having a wrong form of his nisba. Others said that the females here are the idols: report 10440 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says idols; and 10441 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 209} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 209}

**The readings of the word, and al-Tabari's choice (pp. 210-211, notes 1-5 on p. 210 and note 1-2 on p. 211).** Report 10442 (Sufyan, from Abu Usama, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father) says the codex of Aisha had a different word here, meaning idols. Al-Tabari says it is reported from Ibn Abbas that he read the word as the plural of the word for an idol, a plural form which then changed its first letter into a hamza, as people say a form meaning faces for the plural of face, and as in [[passage/quran-77-11|77:11]], where a form meaning appointed is read with a hamza; and that some read it as the plural of the word for a female, as the plural of fruit is formed. Al-Tabari's choice, his own, is that the reading which he does not allow reading otherwise than by is the one read as the plural of the word for a female, because it is so in the codices of the Muslims and because the authorities are agreed on reading it so. Of the interpretations, given that this reading is right in his view, the most fitting is that of those who said it means the gods the polytheists of the Arabs worshipped besides God and named with female names, such as al-Lat, al-Uzza, Na'ila, Manat and the like, because the most apparent sense of the word in Arab speech is what is known as feminine, and interpretation should be directed to its best-known sense. So the sense of the verse is that those who break away from the Messenger and follow other than the believers' way call upon nothing besides God except what they named with female names such as al-Lat and al-Uzza; and God says that it suffices these associators, as an argument against them in their error, disbelief and departure from the right course, that they worship females and call them gods and lords, the female of anything being its lowest, so they acknowledge servitude to the lowliest of things, knowing its lowliness, while refusing to give pure servitude to the One who owns everything and in whose hand is creation and command. The editor's notes: the forms of the plural of idol and the changes from the radical letter (with reference to al-Farra' 1:288, 1:289); that the printed text has "I do not allow" where the manuscript has "we do not allow"; that the printed text has a form with a preposition where the manuscript's Arabic is better; and, on p. 211, a reference to the earlier explanation of "besides" and a note that al-Tabari uses a particular word for worship, in a form the printed text changed each time to another, with a list of earlier places where he used it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 211} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 211}

**The second clause of verse 117: the rebellious devil, and report 10443 (pp. 211-212).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that those who call upon these female idols besides God call, by their calling upon them, only a rebellious devil, meaning one who has defied God in opposing what He ordered and forbade. Report 10443 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says: he rebelled against disobedience to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 212} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 212}
