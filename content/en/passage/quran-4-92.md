## Summary
Verse 92 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 92 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the opening clause (pp. 30-31, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is that God has not allowed or made lawful for a believer to kill a believer. Report 10088 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: it was not for him, in the charge he received from his Lord, which is God's covenant with him. As for the exception for mistake, it means that a believer may kill a believer unintentionally, which God did not make lawful, and it is the kind of exception grammarians call disconnected; he cites a line of Jarir about women who trod on nothing but the hem of a garment. The editor says the line is in Jarir's collection, the Naqa'id and Abu Ubayda's Majaz, from a poem satirising al-Farazdaq and the family of al-Zibriqan, notes that the collection reads a phrase differently, explains the cloth terms, and corrects a word in manuscript and print. Al-Tabari then sets out the ruling for a mistaken killing: the freeing of a slave from the killer's property and a compensation payment (diya) paid by his aqila to the victim's family, unless the family waive it for the one who owes it, in which case it lapses. The editor explains that the aqila are the paternal kin who pay for a mistaken killing. Al-Tabari says the particle in the exception is in the accusative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 31}

**The occasion: Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a (pp. 32-34, notes).** It is reported that the verse concerns Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a al-Makhzumi, who killed a man after the man's conversion without knowing he had become Muslim. Report 10089 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid: he killed a believing man who had tortured him with Abu Jahl. Ayyash had emigrated to the Prophet as a believer; Abu Jahl, his half-brother through their mother, came and said his mother begged him, in the name of kinship, to return to her; Ayyash went back with him and Abu Jahl bound him until they reached Mecca. When the unbelievers saw him it increased their disbelief and trial, and they said Abu Jahl could do what he pleased with Muhammad's Companions. Later Ayyash followed that man, thinking him still an unbeliever, and killed him. The editor says the printed edition gave the name of Asma's father in a form he corrects from the manuscript, and notes that she was of the Tamim line. Report 10090 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid is similar, saying Ayyash emigrated to Medina as a believer. Report 10091 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Mujahid is similar, and Ibn Jurayj adds from Ikrima that al-Harith ibn Yazid ibn Unaysa of the Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy had tortured Ayyash with Abu Jahl and then set out to emigrate; Ayyash met him at al-Harra, struck him with the sword until he fell still, thinking him an unbeliever, then told the Prophet; the verse came down, the Prophet recited it to him and told him to get up and free a slave. The editor says the printed edition wrote the name of al-Harith's grandfather in a form he corrects from al-Isaba and Usd al-Ghaba, and that the verb used for "fell still" is a figurative sense the dictionaries do not give. Report 10092 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: Ayyash was Abu Jahl's half-brother through their mother, became Muslim and emigrated with the first emigrants before the Prophet arrived. Abu Jahl and al-Harith ibn Hisham, with a man of the Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy, came to him in Medina; he was his mother's favourite, and they said she had sworn that no roof would shelter her until she saw him. They gave him a pledge by God not to disturb him until he returned to Medina, and a Companion gave him a fine camel to sit on if he feared anything. Once out of Medina they seized and bound him and the Amiri flogged him, and he swore to kill the Amiri. He stayed imprisoned in Mecca until the day of the conquest, when the Amiri, who had meanwhile become Muslim without Ayyash knowing, met him and Ayyash struck and killed him. The verse came down on his not knowing the man was a believer, with the family's option to forgo the compensation. The editor says the printed edition altered two phrases in this report and that the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 34}

**Second view: Abu al-Darda (p. 34, notes 1-2).** Report 10093 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd: it came down about a man killed by Abu al-Darda. They were on a raiding party; Abu al-Darda turned into a ravine on an errand and found a man of the enemy people with his sheep, charged him with the sword, and the man pronounced the profession of God's oneness; Abu al-Darda struck him and brought the sheep to the others. Then something troubled him, and he told the Prophet, who said, "Why did you not split open his heart?" He answered that all he could have found was blood or water. The Prophet said, "He told you with his tongue and you did not believe him?" and kept repeating the question about the profession of oneness while Abu al-Darda asked what would become of him, until he wished that it had been the beginning of his own Islam. The Qur'an came down up to the point of forgoing the compensation. The editor says the printed edition left out the words "all of this came down about him" and he does not know why, and he explains the phrase "what could I find". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 34}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 34-35, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the right view is that God taught His servants through this verse what is due from one who kills a believer unintentionally, expiation and compensation. It may have come down about Ayyash and his victim and about Abu al-Darda and his; whichever it was, the aim is to teach this, and those who understand God's revelation know it, and ignorance of whom it concerned does not harm them (his view). The editor says the manuscript wording here is not sound and the printed one is good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 35}

**The freed slave: first view (pp. 35-36).** Al-Tabari reports that the scholars differed on what makes a freed slave count as a believer. Some said not until he has chosen faith after puberty, prayed and fasted, so a child does not qualify. Report 10094 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Abu Hayyan) from al-Sha'bi: one who has prayed and knows the faith. Report 10095 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas: one who understood faith, fasted and prayed. Report 10096 (Abu Kurayb, from Waki', from al-A'mash) from Ibrahim: wherever the Qur'an specifies a slave who is a believer only one who fasted and prayed suffices; where it does not, a boy suffices. Report 10097 (told to al-Tabari from Yazid ibn Harun, from Hisham ibn Hassan) from al-Hasan: the same. Report 10098 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from al-A'mash) from Ibrahim: where specified, one who has prayed; where not, one who has not prayed. Report 10099 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: one who has prayed; he disliked freeing a child for this. Report 10100 (Yahya ibn Talha al-Yarbu'i, from Fudayl ibn Iyad, from Mughira) from Ibrahim: when he understands his religion. Report 10101 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from Qatada: a child does not suffice. Report 10102 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas: one who understood faith, fasted and prayed; if he finds none, two consecutive months of fasting, and the compensation is due to the family unless they waive it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 36}

**Second view (p. 36).** Others said that one born to two Muslim parents is a believer even as a child. Report 10103 (Abu Kurayb, from Waki', from Sufyan, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ata': any slave born in Islam suffices. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 36}

**Al-Tabari's choice between the two (pp. 36-37, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari prefers, as his own view, that for a mistaken killing only a slave who has believed and understands faith, among adult men and women, suffices where his parents were of some religion other than Islam and neither became Muslim before he was freed. For one born to two Muslim parents, he says, all scholars agree he is treated as a believer even before the age of discernment in inheritance, in the funeral prayer, in what is due from and to him for injuries, and in marriage; so he should have the same status for expiation, and whoever refuses is asked for the difference by principle or analogy and is bound to the same elsewhere. The editor says the printed edition dropped the word "adult" and that the manuscript has a corruption he reads as printed, and he corrects a meaningless phrase in both. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 37}

**The compensation handed over, and waiving it (pp. 37-38, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the compensation "handed over" means paid to the victim's family in full, undiminished. This is reported from Ibn Abbas as "made complete" (10104: al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj). The waiver means that they give the compensation as a charity to the killer or his aqila, the "ta" being assimilated into the "sad"; a reading of Ubayy uses the longer form (10105: al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Bakr ibn al-Shurud, from Ubayy's reading). The editor says Ishaq ibn Ibrahim ibn al-Dayf and Bakr ibn al-Shurud occurred earlier in report 8562 and refers to the earlier treatment of the word for "handed over". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 38}

**A believer killed from among a hostile people (p. 38, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari explains: if the man killed unintentionally was of a people hostile to you in religion, idolaters who declared war on you, and was a believer, and the killer thought he was still an unbeliever, then freeing a slave is owed. He says the commentators differed: some said that if the slain man was among them and had not emigrated and a believer killed him, no compensation is owed and only the freeing. The editor says the printed edition and the manuscript differ in one phrase and the right reading is "had declared war on you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 38}
