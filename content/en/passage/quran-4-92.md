## Summary
Verse 92 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 92 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the opening clause (pp. 30-31, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is that God has not allowed or made lawful for a believer to kill a believer. Report 10088 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: it was not for him, in the charge he received from his Lord, which is God's covenant with him. As for the exception for mistake, it means that a believer may kill a believer unintentionally, which God did not make lawful, and it is the kind of exception grammarians call disconnected; he cites a line of Jarir about women who trod on nothing but the hem of a garment. The editor says the line is in Jarir's collection, the Naqa'id and Abu Ubayda's Majaz, from a poem satirising al-Farazdaq and the family of al-Zibriqan, notes that the collection reads a phrase differently, explains the cloth terms, and corrects a word in manuscript and print. Al-Tabari then sets out the ruling for a mistaken killing: the freeing of a slave from the killer's property and a compensation payment (diya) paid by his aqila to the victim's family, unless the family waive it for the one who owes it, in which case it lapses. The editor explains that the aqila are the paternal kin who pay for a mistaken killing. Al-Tabari says the particle in the exception is in the accusative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 31}

**The occasion: Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a (pp. 32-34, notes).** It is reported that the verse concerns Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a al-Makhzumi, who killed a man after the man's conversion without knowing he had become Muslim. Report 10089 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid: he killed a believing man who had tortured him with Abu Jahl. Ayyash had emigrated to the Prophet as a believer; Abu Jahl, his half-brother through their mother, came and said his mother begged him, in the name of kinship, to return to her; Ayyash went back with him and Abu Jahl bound him until they reached Mecca. When the unbelievers saw him it increased their disbelief and trial, and they said Abu Jahl could do what he pleased with Muhammad's Companions. Later Ayyash followed that man, thinking him still an unbeliever, and killed him. The editor says the printed edition gave the name of Asma's father in a form he corrects from the manuscript, and notes that she was of the Tamim line. Report 10090 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid is similar, saying Ayyash emigrated to Medina as a believer. Report 10091 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Mujahid is similar, and Ibn Jurayj adds from Ikrima that al-Harith ibn Yazid ibn Unaysa of the Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy had tortured Ayyash with Abu Jahl and then set out to emigrate; Ayyash met him at al-Harra, struck him with the sword until he fell still, thinking him an unbeliever, then told the Prophet; the verse came down, the Prophet recited it to him and told him to get up and free a slave. The editor says the printed edition wrote the name of al-Harith's grandfather in a form he corrects from al-Isaba and Usd al-Ghaba, and that the verb used for "fell still" is a figurative sense the dictionaries do not give. Report 10092 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: Ayyash was Abu Jahl's half-brother through their mother, became Muslim and emigrated with the first emigrants before the Prophet arrived. Abu Jahl and al-Harith ibn Hisham, with a man of the Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy, came to him in Medina; he was his mother's favourite, and they said she had sworn that no roof would shelter her until she saw him. They gave him a pledge by God not to disturb him until he returned to Medina, and a Companion gave him a fine camel to sit on if he feared anything. Once out of Medina they seized and bound him and the Amiri flogged him, and he swore to kill the Amiri. He stayed imprisoned in Mecca until the day of the conquest, when the Amiri, who had meanwhile become Muslim without Ayyash knowing, met him and Ayyash struck and killed him. The verse came down on his not knowing the man was a believer, with the family's option to forgo the compensation. The editor says the printed edition altered two phrases in this report and that the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 34}

**Second view: Abu al-Darda (p. 34, notes 1-2).** Report 10093 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd: it came down about a man killed by Abu al-Darda. They were on a raiding party; Abu al-Darda turned into a ravine on an errand and found a man of the enemy people with his sheep, charged him with the sword, and the man pronounced the profession of God's oneness; Abu al-Darda struck him and brought the sheep to the others. Then something troubled him, and he told the Prophet, who said, "Why did you not split open his heart?" He answered that all he could have found was blood or water. The Prophet said, "He told you with his tongue and you did not believe him?" and kept repeating the question about the profession of oneness while Abu al-Darda asked what would become of him, until he wished that it had been the beginning of his own Islam. The Qur'an came down up to the point of forgoing the compensation. The editor says the printed edition left out the words "all of this came down about him" and he does not know why, and he explains the phrase "what could I find". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 34}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 34-35, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the right view is that God taught His servants through this verse what is due from one who kills a believer unintentionally, expiation and compensation. It may have come down about Ayyash and his victim and about Abu al-Darda and his; whichever it was, the aim is to teach this, and those who understand God's revelation know it, and ignorance of whom it concerned does not harm them (his view). The editor says the manuscript wording here is not sound and the printed one is good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 35}

**The freed slave: first view (pp. 35-36).** Al-Tabari reports that the scholars differed on what makes a freed slave count as a believer. Some said not until he has chosen faith after puberty, prayed and fasted, so a child does not qualify. Report 10094 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Abu Hayyan) from al-Sha'bi: one who has prayed and knows the faith. Report 10095 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas: one who understood faith, fasted and prayed. Report 10096 (Abu Kurayb, from Waki', from al-A'mash) from Ibrahim: wherever the Qur'an specifies a slave who is a believer only one who fasted and prayed suffices; where it does not, a boy suffices. Report 10097 (told to al-Tabari from Yazid ibn Harun, from Hisham ibn Hassan) from al-Hasan: the same. Report 10098 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from al-A'mash) from Ibrahim: where specified, one who has prayed; where not, one who has not prayed. Report 10099 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: one who has prayed; he disliked freeing a child for this. Report 10100 (Yahya ibn Talha al-Yarbu'i, from Fudayl ibn Iyad, from Mughira) from Ibrahim: when he understands his religion. Report 10101 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from Qatada: a child does not suffice. Report 10102 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas: one who understood faith, fasted and prayed; if he finds none, two consecutive months of fasting, and the compensation is due to the family unless they waive it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 36}

**Second view (p. 36).** Others said that one born to two Muslim parents is a believer even as a child. Report 10103 (Abu Kurayb, from Waki', from Sufyan, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ata': any slave born in Islam suffices. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 36}

**Al-Tabari's choice between the two (pp. 36-37, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari prefers, as his own view, that for a mistaken killing only a slave who has believed and understands faith, among adult men and women, suffices where his parents were of some religion other than Islam and neither became Muslim before he was freed. For one born to two Muslim parents, he says, all scholars agree he is treated as a believer even before the age of discernment in inheritance, in the funeral prayer, in what is due from and to him for injuries, and in marriage; so he should have the same status for expiation, and whoever refuses is asked for the difference by principle or analogy and is bound to the same elsewhere. The editor says the printed edition dropped the word "adult" and that the manuscript has a corruption he reads as printed, and he corrects a meaningless phrase in both. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 37}

**The compensation handed over, and waiving it (pp. 37-38, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the compensation "handed over" means paid to the victim's family in full, undiminished. This is reported from Ibn Abbas as "made complete" (10104: al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj). The waiver means that they give the compensation as a charity to the killer or his aqila, the "ta" being assimilated into the "sad"; a reading of Ubayy uses the longer form (10105: al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Bakr ibn al-Shurud, from Ubayy's reading). The editor says Ishaq ibn Ibrahim ibn al-Dayf and Bakr ibn al-Shurud occurred earlier in report 8562 and refers to the earlier treatment of the word for "handed over". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 38}

**A believer killed from among a hostile people (p. 38, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari explains: if the man killed unintentionally was of a people hostile to you in religion, idolaters who declared war on you, and was a believer, and the killer thought he was still an unbeliever, then freeing a slave is owed. He says the commentators differed: some said that if the slain man was among them and had not emigrated and a believer killed him, no compensation is owed and only the freeing. The editor says the printed edition and the manuscript differ in one phrase and the right reading is "had declared war on you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 38}

**First view on a man killed from a hostile people (pp. 39-40).** Al-Tabari gives the reports for the view that the slain man was a believer living among unbelieving kin. Report 10106 (Muhammad ibn Bashshar, from Yahya ibn Sa'id, from Sufyan, from Simak, from Ikrima and al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: he is the man who becomes Muslim in the abode of war and is killed; no blood-money is owed, only the expiation. Reports 10107 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Isra'il, from Simak) and 10108 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Ghassan, from Isra'il, from Simak, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas) say the same: the man is a believer and his people unbelievers, so no blood-money, only the freeing of a slave. Report 10109 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: in the land of unbelief, freeing and no blood-money. Report 10110 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: his kin receive nothing because they are unbelievers with no covenant or protection from God. Report 10111 (al-Muthanna, from al-Hajjaj, from Hammad, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib) from Ibn Abbas: a man became Muslim, then went back to his people and stayed among them while they were idolaters; a Muslim army passed, he was killed among the others, and his killer frees a slave with no blood-money. Report 10112 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: a Muslim among a people with no pact, killed by mistake, and the killer owes the freeing. Report 10113 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali) from Ibn Abbas: the killer makes expiation by freeing a slave or, in its place, the fast the verse names, with no blood-money. Report 10114 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd: the blood-money is not paid to them lest they use it to grow strong against the Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 40}

**Second view (p. 40).** Others said the verse means a man of the people of war who came to the abode of Islam and converted, then went back; when a Muslim army passed, his people fled and he stayed, and the Muslims killed him thinking him an unbeliever. Report 10115 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas): he is the believer among the idolaters who hear of a Muslim detachment; they flee, he stays and is killed, and the freeing is owed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 40}

**The clause on a people bound to the Muslims by a pact (p. 41, note 1).** Al-Tabari explains that the continuation of [[passage/quran-4-92|4:92]] speaks of a victim from a people who have a pact and protection with the Muslims and are not at war with them: the killer owes blood-money, borne by his aqila, to the victim's kin, and the freeing of a slave who is a believer as expiation. The editor refers back to the meaning of "mithaq" (p. 19). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 41}

**First view on the status of this victim: an unbeliever (pp. 41-42).** Some said the victim is an unbeliever, yet the blood-money is owed because he and his people hold a pact; it is their property, and nothing of theirs is lawful to the Muslims without their consent. Report 10116 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali) from Ibn Abbas: if he was an unbeliever under Muslim protection, the killer owes blood-money and the freeing or the fast. Report 10117 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ayyub) from al-Zuhri: the blood-money of a protected person equals a Muslim's, and he read the verse that way. Report 10118 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd Allah ibn Idris, from Isa ibn Abi al-Mughira) from al-Sha'bi: he is of the people of the pact and not a believer. Report 10119 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Mahdi, from Hushaym, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: not a believer. Report 10120 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: the freeing is expiation for what the killer did to people under his protection and pact, then the fast for one who cannot free. Report 10121 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd: pay them the blood-money by the pact, and protected peoples are included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 42}

**Second view: he is a believer (pp. 42-43, note 1).** Others said he is a believer, and the killer owes blood-money paid to his idolater kin because they are a protected people. Report 10122 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: a Muslim whose people are idolaters with a pact; his blood-money goes to his kin and his inheritance to the Muslims (the end of the report is unclear in the printed text). Report 10123 (al-Muthanna, from Suwayd, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Hushaym, from Abu Ishaq al-Kufi) from Jabir ibn Zayd: that he is a believer. Report 10124 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Mahdi, from Hammad ibn Salama, from Yunus) from al-Hasan: all of them are believers. The editor says the printed edition has a different wording from the manuscript's "all of them are believers", and he thinks the likeliest sense is every victim mentioned in the earlier verses, not the victim's people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 43}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 43-44, notes 2-3).** Al-Tabari says the better of the two views is that the victim is of the people of the pact and not a believer, because God left his description open and did not state belief as He did for the two earlier victims; that omission is, to him, clear evidence (his view). He answers those who take the mention of blood-money to his kin as proof of belief: in his account the blood-money of protected persons and Muslims is the same, since all agree that the compensation for their slaves is equal, and even if it were half or a third it would still be blood-money, just as a believing woman's is half by agreement and remains blood-money (others differ on the amount, which al-Tabari mentions and the museum does not weigh). The editor says a phrase is faulty in both the printed text and the manuscript and that he corrected one of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 44}

**The meaning of mithaq (p. 44, note 1).** Al-Tabari says it is the pact and protection, as he explained elsewhere. Report 10125 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: a pact. Report 10126 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from al-Zuhri: the mutual agreement. Report 10127 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Ghassan, from Isra'il, from Simak, from Ikrima) from Ibn Abbas: a pact. Report 10128 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Isra'il) from Ikrima likewise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 44}

**What counts as mistake (pp. 44-45).** Al-Tabari asks what mistake makes blood-money and expiation due, and answers with al-Nakha'i. Report 10129 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, from Sufyan, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: to aim at one thing and hit another. Report 10130 (Abu Kurayb and Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: to throw at something and hit a person one did not intend, which falls on the aqila. In note 1 the editor explains that the aqila are the paternal kin who pay the blood-money for one of their number. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 45}

**The amount and the ages of the camels: first view (pp. 45-46, notes 2-3).** Al-Tabari says the blood-money for killing a believer is a hundred camels among camel owners, on the killer's aqila; there is no disagreement on that, only on the ages. Some said in quarters: twenty-five each of four age classes (hiqqa, jadha'a, bint makhad, bint labun). Report 10131 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Ibrahim) from Ali: for the near-intentional killing, thirty-three hiqqa, thirty-three jadha'a and thirty-four thaniyya up to the bazil of its year; for a mistaken killing, the quarters. Reports 10132 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Firas and al-Shaybani, from al-Sha'bi), 10133 (from Abu Ishaq, from Asim ibn Damra) and 10134 (Wasil ibn Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Fudayl, from Ash'ath ibn Sawwar, from al-Sha'bi) are from Ali in the same sense, the last saying a hundred in quarters. The editor explains the camel age names by the year a camel has completed and the next it enters (hiqqa, jadha'a, bint makhad, bint labun, then thaniyya and bazil, with Ibn al-A'rabi's remark that no name precedes the thaniyya or follows the bazil); his last note continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 46}

**Second view: fifths (p. 46, note 1).** Others said fifths: twenty each of hiqqa, jadha'a, bint labun, ibn labun and bint makhad. Report 10135 (Muhammad ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from Abu Mijlaz, from Abu Ubayda, from his father) from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud. Report 10136 (Wasil ibn Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Fudayl, from Ash'ath, from Amir) from Ibn Mas'ud: for a mistaken killing, a hundred camels in fifths (it continues on the next page). The editor says grammarians objected to one plural of "ibn labun" and that these sound reports show it correct in Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 46}

**The rest of the fifths and the Ibn Mas'ud report (pp. 47-48, notes 1-4).** The end of report 10136: the last fifth is of banu makhad. Report 10137 (Mujahid ibn Musa, from Yazid, from Sulayman al-Taymi, from Abu Mijlaz, from Abu Ubayda) from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud: the blood-money is in fifths. The editor notes that lexicographers rejected the form "banu makhad" and that this report and the next show it sound. Those who hold this view argued from report 10138 (Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, from Yahya ibn Abi Za'ida and Abu Khalid al-Ahmar, from Hajjaj, from Zayd ibn Jubayr, from al-Khashaf ibn Malik) from Ibn Mas'ud: the Prophet judged the blood-money for mistake in fifths, which Ibn Abi Za'ida explained as twenty of each class. The editor says the printed text has "al-Riba'i" and the right form "al-Rifa'i" is from the manuscript; he records that Zayd ibn Jubayr narrated from al-Khashaf ibn Malik al-Ta'i, that al-Nasa'i is quoted as calling him reliable, al-Daraqutni in his Sunan as unknown, and al-Azdi as not so strong. He traces the report to al-Bayhaqi's al-Sunan al-Kubra (8: 75-76) by two chains, quotes Abu Dawud that it is Abd Allah's own statement, not raised to the Prophet, quotes al-Daraqutni that al-Khashaf is unknown, that Hajjaj was known for passing off reports and that reliable narrators differed on him, and quotes al-Bayhaqi that Hajjaj ibn Artat is not relied on, al-Khashaf is unknown, the sound form is a statement of Ibn Mas'ud, and that those who set aside his view gave two excuses: the weakness of al-Khashaf's report and breaks in the chains of the stopped versions (these assessments are the editor's quotations; the museum gives no verdict). Report 10139 (Abu Hisham, from Yahya, from his father, from Abu Ishaq, from Alqama) from Abd Allah: he judged so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 48}

**A third view: unequal quarters (pp. 48-49, notes).** Others said quarters, but thirty hiqqa, thirty bint labun, twenty bint makhad and twenty male ibn labun. Report 10140 (Ibn Bashshar, from Muhammad ibn Bakr, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from Abd Rabbih, from Abu Iyad) from Uthman and Zayd ibn Thabit: for the near-intentional killing, forty pregnant jadha'a (khalifa), thirty hiqqa and thirty bint makhad; for mistake, thirty hiqqa, thirty jadha'a, twenty bint makhad and twenty male ibn labun (the second class is jadha'a in this report and bint labun in the heading and in 10141, as printed). Report 10141 (Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab) from Zayd ibn Thabit: thirty hiqqa, thirty bint labun, twenty bint makhad, twenty ibn labun. Report 10142 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abu Uthma, from Sa'id ibn Bashir, from Qatada, from Abd Rabbih, from Abu Iyad) from Uthman, and from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab from Zayd likewise. The editor explains "khalifa" as a pregnant she-camel with its plural, gives a hadith and a report on the rebuilding of the Ka'ba as usage (pp. 48-49), says Abu Uthma is Muhammad ibn Khalid ibn Uthma and that he preferred the manuscript's reading, quotes Ali ibn al-Madini that Abd Rabbih ibn Abi Yazid is unknown and only Qatada narrated from him, and notes that Abu Iyad al-Madani is disputed in name and narration and this report shows he also narrated from Uthman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 49}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the ages (pp. 49-50, notes 2-3).** Al-Tabari says all agree that for a purely mistaken killing among camel owners the blood-money is a hundred camels. They differ on the ages, but agree it may not fall below the lowest age named nor rise above the highest; so, he holds, whichever of those ages the one liable pays suffices, because neither God nor His messenger fixed a limit except what they agreed on, and the ruler may choose within it as he sees best for both sides (his choice). The editor says he repaired a sentence in both printed text and manuscript by adding two letters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 50}

**Gold and silver (pp. 50-51, notes).** Al-Tabari says that if the killer's aqila are people of gold, the victim's heirs are owed a thousand dinars in his view, as the scholars of the regions hold. Some said this was Umar's valuation of the camels for gold people in his time and must be revalued in each age when camels are lacking; they cite report 10143 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Ayyub ibn Musa) from Makhul: the blood-money rose and fell, and at the Prophet's death it stood at eight hundred dinars; Umar feared what came later and made it twelve thousand dirhams and a thousand dinars. Those who made it a thousand dinars in all ages said it is a duty laid down through the Messenger as the camels are for camel owners, and that the consensus of the scholars of the regions in every age, apart from a few, that it is neither raised above nor lowered below a thousand is the clearest proof; had it been a valuation of a hundred camels it would vary with prices. Al-Tabari says this view is right because of that consensus (his choice). For people of silver he gives twelve thousand dirhams and refers to his own book on the rulings of Islam's laws for his reasons; others said ten thousand dirhams. The editor explains the words for silver and coined dirhams (note 1, p. 51), records the printed and manuscript readings of a phrase on p. 50, and refers to al-Bayhaqi (8: 76-80). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 51}

**Blood-money of a covenanted person: first view, the same as a free Muslim's (pp. 51-53).** Al-Tabari says scholars differed on the amount; some said it equals a free Muslim's. Report 10144 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Bishr ibn al-Sari, from Ibrahim ibn Sa'd) from al-Zuhri: Abu Bakr and Uthman made the blood-money of a Jew or Christian under covenant like a Muslim's. 10145 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Bishr ibn al-Sari, from al-Dastuwa'i, from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir) from al-Hakam ibn Utayba: Ibn Mas'ud made it so for People of the Book under protection. 10146 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Hammad) from Ibrahim: theirs and ours are equal. 10147 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Abu al-Walid, from Hammad) from Ibrahim and Dawud, from al-Sha'bi: Jew, Christian and Magian like a free Muslim. 10148 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: it used to be said so where he has protection. 10149 from Mujahid and Ata (Ya'qub, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ibn Abi Najih); 10150 from Ibrahim (Sawwar ibn Abd Allah, from Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, from al-Mas'udi, from Hammad); 10151 from al-Zuhri (Ya'qub, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ayyub); 10152 from Amir al-Sha'bi (Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Abi Za'ida, from Ash'ath); 10153 (Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Abi Za'ida, from Sa'id ibn Abi Aruba, from Abu Ma'shar) and 10154 (Abu al-Sa'ib, from Abu Mu'awiya, from al-A'mash) from Ibrahim likewise. 10155 (Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayan, from Muhammad ibn Yazid, from Isma'il) from Amir: told that al-Hasan said the Magian's is eight hundred and the Jew's and Christian's four thousand, he said theirs is one. 10156 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Qays ibn Muslim) from al-Sha'bi: the covenanted and the Muslim are equal in their expiation (as printed). 10157 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Mansur) from Ibrahim. The editor's note on 10154 says the chain ran on into the next in both printed text and manuscript; he restored it, since Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayan is al-Sukkari al-Qannad, al-Tabari's teacher. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 53}

**Second and third views: a half and a third (pp. 53-54, note 1).** Others said half a Muslim's. Report 10158 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Abd al-A'la, from Dawud) from Amr ibn Shu'ayb: Umar made it half and the Magian's eight hundred; told that al-Hasan says four thousand, he said that was before "al-ghilma" (its sense is unclear; the editor kept the manuscript reading and says he did not know what it meant), and that Umar set the Magian's at the level of a slave. 10159 (Abu Kurayb, from Abd Allah al-Ashja'i, from Sufyan, from Abu al-Zinad) from Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz: half. Others said a third. 10160 (Wasil ibn Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Fudayl, from Mutarrif, from Abu Uthman, judge of Marw): Umar made the Jew's and Christian's four thousand. 10161 (Ammar ibn Khalid al-Wasiti, from Yahya ibn Sa'id, from al-A'mash, from Thabit), 10162 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Abd al-Samad, from Shu'ba, from Thabit) and 10163 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Thabit) from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, from Umar: the Christian's or People of the Book's four thousand, the Magian's eight hundred. 10164 (Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Qatada) from Abu al-Malih: a man of his people shot a Jew or Christian with an arrow and killed him, it was brought to Umar, who made him pay four thousand. 10165 (Qatada, from Ibn al-Musayyab), 10166 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from some companions, from Ibn al-Musayyab) and 10167 (Hushaym, from Ibn Abi Layla, from Ata) from Umar likewise. 10168 (Hushaym, from Yahya ibn Sa'id) from Sulayman ibn Yasar: four thousand for Jew and Christian, eight hundred for the Magian; 10169 (Sawwar ibn Abd Allah, from Khalid ibn al-Harith, from Abd al-Malik) from Ata likewise. Then 10170 (Husayn ibn al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd) from al-Dahhak: the fast is for one who cannot find a slave to free, while the blood-money is obligatory and nothing cancels it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 54}

**The close of the discussion of the substitute for freeing (pp. 55-56, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says that one who cannot find a believing slave to free as expiation for his mistaken killing of a believer or covenanted person, because the price is beyond his means, owes the unbroken fast the verse names. Report 10171 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid: one who cannot find a freeing (or "itaqa", Abu Asim being unsure of the word) for killing a believer by mistake; it came down about Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a. The editor explains "itaqa" as a verbal noun and refers to the note on report 9265. Others said the fast stands in place of both blood-money and the slave, so that the verse means one who can find neither a slave nor blood-money to hand over. Report 10172 (al-Muthanna, from Suwayd ibn Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Zakariyya, from al-Sha'bi) from Masruq: asked whether the fast is for the slave alone or for blood-money and slave, he said that for one who cannot find, it is for both. Report 10173 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Zakariyya, from Amir) from Masruq is similar. Al-Tabari prefers that the fast replaces the slave and not the blood-money, because the blood-money for a mistake falls on the killer's aqila and the expiation on the killer, by the consensus of the authority reported from their Prophet, and one person's fasting cannot discharge what falls on another's property (his choice). By unbroken he means that the person does not cut the fast by breaking some days without an obstructing excuse. On the clause that presents this as an easing, he explains it as a passing-over by God into ease, lightening the duty of freeing for those in difficulty by requiring the fast, and says God has always known what is good for His servants in what He charges them with and been wise in what He decrees for them. The editor says the printed text has "from our Prophet" and the manuscript "from their Prophet", which he adopted, and likewise took "and he does not cut it" from the manuscript; for the words for repentance, being, knowing and wise he refers to the language indexes of earlier volumes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 56}
