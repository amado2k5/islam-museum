**Commentary on verse 37 (pp. 100-101)**

On "he said: no food by which you are provided will come to you but I will tell you its interpretation before it comes" al-Tabari says Joseph told the two youths who asked him to interpret: no food you are provided will come to you, young men, in your sleep but I will tell you its interpretation when you are awake, before it comes. Reports 19284 and 19285, from al-Suddi and Ibn Ishaq, say the same, through Ibn Waki', Amr, Asbat, Ibn Humayd and Salama. By "its interpretation" he means what the food they saw coming to them in their dream comes to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 100}

On "that is of what my Lord taught me" he says: what I mention of knowing how to interpret dreams is of what my Lord taught me. "I have left the creed of a people who do not believe in God" comes as the opening of a new statement, meaning I have left the creed, in sense I have not inclined to it, and he began so because the beginning shows the meaning. It means: I have disowned the creed of those who do not believe in God and affirm His oneness, "and they are deniers of the hereafter": besides leaving belief in God's oneness they do not affirm the return, the raising, reward or punishment. The "they" is repeated since "of the hereafter" falls between the two, making the first as if dropped and resting the weight on the second, as in [[passage/quran-27-3|27:3]] and [[passage/quran-31-4|31:4]] and as in [[passage/quran-23-35|23:35]]. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "milla". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 101}

If it is asked what the sense of this report from Joseph is, and where in these words is his answer to the youths about interpreting their dreams, the reply is that Joseph disliked to interpret because he knew the disagreeable outcome for one of them, so he turned from it and took up another matter so that they would stop asking. Report 19286, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: he disliked the interpretation for them and told them something they had not asked, to show them he had knowledge; when the king meant to kill someone he made a known food and sent it to him; Joseph said, no food will come to you, up to "you give thanks"; they did not leave him, so he turned them aside and said, "are many lords better than God the One, the Overpowering", up to "they know"; they did not leave him until he interpreted: as for one of you, he will serve his lord wine, and the other will be crucified and the birds eat from his head. They said, we saw nothing, we were only playing; he said, the matter is decreed. Al-Tabari says that on this reading of Ibn Jurayj the words "no food will come to you" refer to waking, not sleep: he told them he knew what the food coming to them from the king or from others leads to, since he knew the kind that, when it came, was a sign that its recipient would be killed and the kind that was a sign of something else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 102}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
