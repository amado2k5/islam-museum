## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 90 to 92 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, exalted above what they associate with Him": He is the Knower of all things absent from His creation, neither seeing nor seeing Him, nor seeing Him and seeing Him. This is only news from God about those polytheists who said: "Allah has taken a son and worshipped gods besides Him," that in what they say and do they are false and mistaken. They say what they say about it without knowledge, even out of ignorance of it. The knower of ancient matters, their news, their witnesses, and their absence from them, is God, to whom nothing is hidden, so His knowledge is the truth without their knowledge. He said: 'Knower of the unseen,' so it is raised as a beginning, meaning: He is the knower of the unseen. Therefore, the fa' was included in His saying: 'Fa Ta'la', as it is said: 'I passed by your brother al-Muhsin and you did good to him.' The 'al-Muhshan' is raised if you made 'ta'ahs' to him with a 'fa', because if the meaning of the speech is 'I passed by your brother,' he is 'al-Muhsin', so he did good to him.
