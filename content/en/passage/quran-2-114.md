## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "who is more unjust than one who prevents the mosques of God from having His name mentioned in them and strives to ruin them", al-Tabari refers to what came earlier on injustice being putting a thing in the wrong place, and says the sense is: what person is more transgressing and bold against God than one who prevents God's mosques from being worshipped in. "Masajid" is the plural of "masjid", the place where one prostrates to God, like "majlis" and "manzil". On "that His name be mentioned in them" there are two ways: that the sense is preventing God's mosques from His name being mentioned in them ("an" being accusative through loss of the preposition), or preventing God's name being mentioned in His mosques ("an" being accusative as a substitute for "mosques"); "and strove" is coordinated with "prevented". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 519} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 520}

The interpreters differed over who is meant. Some said the Christians, and the mosque is Jerusalem, from Ibn Abbas and Mujahid (that they threw filth in Jerusalem and prevented people from praying in it). Others said Nebuchadnezzar and his troops and those of the Christians who helped them, from Qatada (that hatred of the Jews led them to help Nebuchadnezzar the Babylonian ruin Jerusalem) and by another route from him, and al-Suddi (that the Romans backed Nebuchadnezzar in ruining it and he ordered carcasses to be thrown in it, because the Children of Israel had killed Yahya ibn Zakariyya). Others said God meant the polytheists of Quraysh, who barred the Messenger of God from the Sacred Mosque, from Ibn Zayd (at al-Hudaybiya, when they stood between him and entering Mecca until he slaughtered his offering at Dhu Tuwa and made a truce, saying whoever killed our fathers at Badr will not enter among us; and on "strove to ruin them" that they cut off those who would tend it by remembering Him and who would come for pilgrimage and umra). Al-Tabari then says that the best of these readings, in his view, is that of those who said the Christians are meant, because they were those who strove for the ruin of Jerusalem and helped Nebuchadnezzar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 520} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 521}
