## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari notes that the interpreters differ on the occasion for "God is not shy to strike a parable of even a gnat or what is above it". He reports from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that when God struck the two parables for the hypocrites, that of the one who kindled a fire and that of the rainstorm from the sky, the hypocrites said God is too high and exalted to strike such parables, and this verse was sent down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 398}

Al-Tabari reports from al-Rabi' ibn Anas that this is a parable God struck for this world: the gnat lives while hungry and dies when it is fat, and so these people, when they are filled with the world, God seizes them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 398} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 399}
He reports from Qatada that the meaning is that God is not shy of the truth, to mention of it little or much, and that when He mentioned the fly and the spider the misguided said what did God mean by mentioning this, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 399} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 400}
Al-Tabari prefers the report from Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas, since the verse follows parables struck for the hypocrites in this surah and so answers their objection to those, not to parables in other surahs. He answers the view that the spider and fly parables of other surahs are meant by saying the verse says God is not shy to strike parables in the truth, small or great, to test His servants so that people of faith are told apart from those who go astray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 400} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 401}
He gives Mujahid that the parables, small and great, are believed by the believers, who know them as truth from their Lord, by which God guides, while He misleads the transgressors by them. He cites Qatada and Ibn Jurayj that the gnat is the weakest of God's creatures, which is why it is named as the lowest and humblest of parables, while the highest has no limit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 401} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 402}
He finds the hypocrites' objection in "as for those who disbelieve they say what did God mean by this as a parable", and says God showed the error of their words and that the right is what the believers said. He also records that some reputed experts in Arabic took "is not shy" to mean "does not fear", citing a verse in surat al-Ahzab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 402}

Al-Tabari explains "to strike a parable" as to make clear and describe, citing a verse of surat al-Rum and a line by al-Kumayt; "mathal" is a likeness. So "God is not shy to strike a parable" means He does not fear to describe a likeness for what is likened. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 402} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 403}
He takes "ma" to mean "that which", so "that which is a gnat in smallness and what is above it". Asked why "gnat" is in the accusative, he gives two answers: that the word after "ma" takes the case of "ma", as in a line attributed to Hassan ibn Thabit, or that the sense is "from a gnat to what is above it", with "between" and "to" dropped and indicated by the accusative and the particle "fa", and he gives Arabic idioms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 404} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 405}
He records that some Arabic grammarians took "ma" as a redundant filler, with "gnat" the object of "strike", and explains what follows from that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 405}
On "and what is above it" he says it means what is greater than it, as Qatada and Ibn Jurayj call the gnat the weakest of God's creatures so anything above it is stronger. Another view, "above it in smallness", he regards as against the interpretation of those whose knowledge of the Qur'an's meaning is trusted. He concludes that God is not shy to describe a likeness for what is likened, of whatever lies from a gnat up to what is above it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 405} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 406}
On "as for those who believe, they know that it is the truth from their Lord" he says these are those who affirmed God and His Messenger, and they recognize that the parable God struck is a true parable for what He struck it for. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 406}
