## Summary
Verse 111 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 111 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Hurt that is not harm (p. 108).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that these defiant ones of the People of the Book will not harm the believers by their disbelief and denial of their prophet Muhammad, except for hurt: they hurt them by their polytheism, by making them hear their disbelief, by what they say of Jesus, his mother and Ezra, and by calling them to error, and they will not harm them by that. This is a disconnected exception, differing in sense from what precedes, as in the Arabs' saying "he complained of nothing but good", a phrase reported from them by hearing. He says the interpreters spoke in this way. The editor keeps the manuscript's reading in the note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 108}

**Qatada, al-Rabi, Ibn Jurayj and al-Hasan (7626-7629, pp. 108-109).** From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7626): hurt you hear from them. From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7627): the same. From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj (7628): their associating Ezra, Jesus and the cross. From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7629): you hear falsehood about God from them, calling you by it to error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 108} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 109}

**The second half: turning their backs and no help (p. 109).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: if the People of the Book, Jews and Christians, fight you, they will be routed before you and turn their backs in flight. The phrase about the backs is figurative for their rout, since the routed man turns his back to the pursuer, fleeing to a refuge for fear for himself with the pursuer behind him, so his back faces the pursuer's face. Then God will not help them against you, believers, for their disbelief in God and His messenger and your belief in what your prophet Muhammad brought you, because God cast terror into their hearts and supported you with His help. The editor says the printed edition altered the manuscript and that the sentence is sound on the manuscript's text once the word for hearts is completed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 109}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 109}

**A promise of help and the raised verb (p. 110).** Al-Tabari says this is a promise from God to His prophet Muhammad and the believers of help against those of the People of the Book who disbelieve in him. The final verb is raised although the one before it is jussive in the conditional reply, as a fresh start to the sentence, because the verse-ends before it end in nun and he attached it to them, as the raised form occurs in [[passage/quran-77-36|77:36]], whereas the other form occurs after a negative in a place that is not a verse-end, [[passage/quran-35-36|35:36]]; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1:229). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 110}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 110}
