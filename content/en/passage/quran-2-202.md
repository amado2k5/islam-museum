## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "those will have a share of what they earned, and God is swift in reckoning": al-Tabari says "those" are the ones who say after completing their rites "Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the hereafter and protect us from the punishment of the Fire", in longing for what is with God and knowing that all good is from Him; God informed us that they have a share and portion of their hajj and rites, and a great reward for the work whose hardship they bore with their wealth and persons, particular to them and not to the other party, who bore the toil of their deeds and the burden of their journeys without longing for the reward with their Lord, but with a base hope for the goods of this world and its quick spoils. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 206} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 207} From Qatada: this is a servant who intended this world and worked for it and has his share in it, while the others have a share of what they earned, that is a portion of their deeds; from Ibn Zayd: they made hajj for this world and asking, not wanting the hereafter or believing in it, while these are the Prophet and the believers, who have the reward for what they did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 207} As for "God is swift in reckoning", it means that He encompasses the deeds of both parties and repays each for his work; He describes Himself as swift in reckoning because He counts the deeds of His servants without tallying on fingers, thought or deliberation, as weak creatures do, nothing in earth or heaven being hidden from Him, so He praised Himself for swift reckoning and told His creatures that He is not like them, needing the tally of a hand or the memory of a heart. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 207} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 208} The editor notes that he chose "so He praised Himself" over the printed wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 208}
