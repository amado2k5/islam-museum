**Commentary on verse 74 (pp. 400-406)**

On [[passage/quran-11-74|11:74]] al-Tabari says: when the fear that Abraham felt in himself of Our messengers, on seeing that their hands did not reach his food, left him, and he was safe from any harm intended against him and his family, 'and the glad tidings came to him' of Isaac, he began to 'plead with Us for the people of Lot'. Report 18331, from Qatada: the fear left him, and the glad tidings of Isaac came; report 18332, from Ibn Ishaq: glad tidings of Isaac, and Jacob was born from the loins of Isaac, and he was safe from what he had feared and said, praise be to God who gave me, in old age, Ishmael and Isaac, as in [[passage/quran-14-39|14:39]]. It has also been said that the glad tidings that came to him were that it was not he whom they wanted; report 18333, from Qatada: when they told him that they were sent to the people of Lot and that it was not he whom they intended. Others said he was given the tidings of Isaac. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 401}

'Rau'' is fear: one says 'it frightened me, yaru'uni, rau'an' when he feared it, and of this is the Prophet's saying to a man, how will it be with you at the fright of the believer; and a line of Antara, 'what startled me but the camel-train of her people amid the dwellings, picking up the black seed of the khamkham', meaning what frightened me. Reports 18334, 18335 and 18336, from Mujahid: 'rau'' is dread; report 18337, from Qatada: dread; report 18338, from him: the fear left him. His words 'he pleaded with us for the people of Lot' mean he disputed with us; reports 18339 and 18340, from Mujahid: disputed with us. A Basran claimed that 'yujadiluna' means 'speaks to us' because Abraham does not dispute with God but asks Him and petitions Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 402}

Al-Tabari says: this is ignorance of speech, since God told us in His Book that he pleaded for Lot's people, and to say that Abraham does not dispute, suggesting that the view of those who said 'yujadiluna' means he disputes with us says Abraham disputed with his Lord, is ignorance in speech; his disputing was with the messengers, by way of argument with them, and the sense is 'the glad tidings came to him as he disputed with Our messengers', but when the meaning was understood 'the messengers' was dropped. His disputing with them was as in report 18341, from Sa'id: when Gabriel and those with him came they said to Abraham, we are about to destroy the people of this town, for its people were wrongdoers; Abraham said, will you destroy a town with four hundred believers? They said no. Three hundred? No. Two hundred? No. Forty? No. Fourteen? No; and Abraham was counting them as fourteen with Lot's wife; and he fell silent and his heart was at ease. Report 18342, from Ibn Abbas: the angel said to Abraham, if there are five in it who pray, the punishment will be lifted from them. Report 18343, from Qatada, begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 403}

Report 18343, from Qatada: 'he pleaded with Us for the people of Lot' — it was told us that his pleading with them was that he said to them, do you see, if there were fifty believers in it, would you punish it? They said no, down to ten; he said, if there were ten in it would you punish them? They said no; and they were three towns with as many people as God willed. Report 18344, from him: it reached us that he said that day, if there were fifty Muslims among them? They said if fifty were among them we would not punish them; forty? and forty; thirty? and thirty; down to ten; they said even if ten; he said, a people without ten among them has no good. Ma'mar said it reached us that Lot's town held four million people or as many as God willed. Report 18345, from al-Suddi: he pleaded for Lot's people, saying, if there were a hundred Muslims in it would you destroy them? They said no; he kept lowering until ten Muslims, and they said we would not punish them if ten Muslims were among them; then they said 'Abraham, turn away from this; only a household of believers is in it', that is Lot and his household; and the angels said 'Abraham, turn away from this; your Lord's command has come and a punishment not to be turned back will come to them'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 404}

Report 18346, from Ibn Ishaq: the people of the Torah claim that Abraham's pleading with them for Lot's people, to turn the punishment from them, was that he said to the messengers, if there were a hundred believers among them would you destroy them? They said no; ninety? no; eighty? no; seventy? no; sixty? no; fifty? no; down to one Muslim man? no; they did not tell Abraham there was a single believer in it; he said, Lot is in it, by whom punishment is held off; they said, we know best who is in it, we shall save him and his family, as in [[passage/quran-29-32|29:32]]; they said 'Abraham, turn away from this; your Lord's command has come'. Report 18347, from Ibn Jurayj: Abraham said, would you destroy them if you find a hundred believers among them? then ninety, down to five; and Lot's town had four million. Report 18348, from Abu al-Muthanna and al-Husayl al-Ashja'i: Abraham said, will You punish a world of Yours with a hundred men among them? He said no, by My might, nor fifty; forty? thirty? down to five, and He said no, by My might, I would not punish them if five among them worshipped Me; God said 'We found in it only one household of Muslims' in [[passage/quran-51-36|51:36]], that is Lot and his two daughters; then the punishment came on them. The editor doubts the soundness of this chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 405}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
