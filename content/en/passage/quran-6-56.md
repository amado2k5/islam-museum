## Summary
Verse 56 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 56 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: I am forbidden to worship those you call on besides God; say: I do not follow your desires, for then I should have gone astray and not be among the guided" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to these polytheists of your people who hold idols and rivals equal to him and call you to agree with them on their religion and the worship of idols: God has forbidden me to worship those you call on besides him, so I shall not follow you in what you call me to, nor agree with you on it, nor give you the love and desire you want in it; and if I did that, I would have left the highway of truth and followed other than guidance and become astray like you, off the straight way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 396}

The Arabs have two forms of "dalaltu", with fatha and with kasra on the lam, the eloquent, well-known one the fatha, which most readers of the cities read and which he reads for its fame among the Arabs, the kasra being not dominant in their speech and few readers using it; whoever says "dalaltu" says in the future "adillu", and whoever says "dalaltu" with kasra says "adallu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 397}

The editor notes on p. 397 that the page image is confused in its middle, so only what is given of the two forms could be read, and that al-Tabari cites the reading elsewhere in the Qur'an, "when we have gone astray", in Surat al-Sajda, with fatha, and refers for "error" to the language indexes.

Verse 56 closes here at p. 397, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-57|6:57]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
