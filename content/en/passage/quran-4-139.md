**Commentary on verse 139: taking the unbelievers as allies, and might belongs to God (pp. 319-320)**

Al-Tabari says that taking unbelievers as allies instead of believers is a mark of the hypocrites, and the address is to the Prophet: announce to the hypocrites, who take the people of unbelief and deviation in God's religion as helpers and close friends, instead of believers, that they seek might among them. Does 'seeking might among them' mean seeking strength and protection by taking them as allies rather than believers? Might belongs to God altogether; they are the humbled and the few. Why not take believers as allies and seek might and support from God, who gives might to whom He wills and abases whom He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 319}

Al-Tabari explains that the root of 'might' is hardness: the hard, firm ground is called 'izaz'; it is said that a sick person was made grievously ill (ustu'izza 'ala) when his illness grew severe and he nearly recovered; flesh 'toughens' when it grows firm; and 'it is hard for me that this should be so' means it weighs heavily on me. The editor explains 'ustu'izza' as the illness growing severe and bringing him near death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 320}
