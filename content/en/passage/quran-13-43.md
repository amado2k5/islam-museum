## Summary
Verse 43 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 43 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 43 (pp. 500-507)**

On "those who disbelieve say: you are not a messenger. Say: God suffices as a witness between me and you, and whoever has knowledge of the Book", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: those of your people who disbelieved in God say, Muhammad, you are not sent, denying you and rejecting your prophethood; say to them when they say that, "God suffices", that is, say God is enough for me, "as a witness", meaning a witness, "between me and you", over me and over you, of my truthfulness and your lying, "and whoever has knowledge of the Book"; "man", when it is read so, is in the genitive, conjoined to the name of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 500}

So the readers of the regions read it, "wa-man 'indahu 'ilmu al-kitab", meaning those who have knowledge of the Book, that is, the Books sent down before the Qur'an such as the Torah and the Gospel, and on this reading the interpreters interpreted it. Report 20535, from the nephew of Abd Allah ibn Salam, through Ali ibn Sa'id al-Kindi, Abu Muhayya Yahya ibn Ya'la and Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr: Abd Allah ibn Salam said, "God suffices as a witness between me and you, and whoever has knowledge of the Book" was sent down about me. Report 20536, from Abd Allah ibn Salam, through al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Suda'i, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi, Shu'ayb ibn Safwan, Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr and Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Abd Allah ibn Salam: "say: God suffices as a witness between me and you, and whoever has knowledge of the Book" was sent down about me; in the note the editor identifies the narrators of the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 501}

Report 20537, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "say: God suffices as a witness between me and you, and whoever has knowledge of the Book", those who have knowledge of the Book are the People of the Book, the Jews and Christians. Report 20538, from Mujahid, through Abu Kurayb, al-Ashja'i, Sufyan and Layth: "and whoever has knowledge of the Book", he said, it is Abd Allah ibn Salam. Report 20539, from Abu Salih, through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Hushaym and Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: "and whoever has knowledge of the Book", he said, a man of humankind, whom he did not name. Report 20540, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: it is Abd Allah ibn Salam. Report 20541, from Mujahid, through Yahya ibn Abbad, Shu'ba and al-Hakam: "and whoever has knowledge of the Book"...; the editor notes that the report is incomplete in the printed text and the manuscript and thinks it is what al-Suyuti related, that Mujahid used to read "and whoever has knowledge of the Book" and said it is Abd Allah ibn Salam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 502}

Report 20542, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "those who disbelieve say, you are not a messenger", he said, the saying of the polytheists of Quraysh; "say: God suffices as a witness between me and you, and whoever has knowledge of the Book", people of the People of the Book who bore witness to the truth and acknowledged it and knew that Muhammad is the Messenger of God, as it is reported that among them was Abd Allah ibn Salam. Report 20543, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Ibn Thawr: "and whoever has knowledge of the Book", he said, among them were Abd Allah ibn Salam, Salman al-Farisi and Tamim al-Dari. Report 20544, from Qatada, through al-Hasan, Abd al-Wahhab and Sa'id: it is Abd Allah ibn Salam. It is reported of a number of earlier authorities that they used to read it "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", meaning that from God the Book was made known. Report 20545, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata', Harun, Ja'far ibn Abi Wahshiyya and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 503}

"wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", he says, from God the Book was made known. Report 20546, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and al-Hakam: "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", he said, from God. Report 20547, from Mujahid, through Ibn Abi Adi, Shu'ba and al-Hakam: "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", he said, from God the Book was made known, and in another report he said, it is God; so al-Hasan read. Report 20548, from al-Hasan, through Shu'ba and Mansur ibn Zadhan: the like. Report 20549, from al-Hasan, through Ali ibn al-Ja'd, Shu'ba and Mansur ibn Zadhan: "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", he said, God; Shu'ba said, I mentioned that to al-Hakam and he said, Mujahid said the same. Report 20550, from al-Hasan, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Mansur ibn Zadhan: he said of this verse, from God. Report 20551, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Hawdha and Awf: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 504}

"wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", he said, from God the Book was made known. Report 20552, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: he said, from God is knowledge of the Book, so Ibn Abd al-A'la said. Report 20553, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: al-Hasan used to read it "say: God suffices as a witness between me and you, and 'wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab'", saying from God the Book and its totality were made known; Abu Ja'far says, so Bishr told us, "'ulima al-kitab", and I think he erred in it and that it is "wa-min 'indihi 'ilmu al-kitab", because "its totality" is a noun, and a noun is not conjoined to a past verb. Report 20554, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan, Abd al-Wahhab and Harun: "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", he says, from God the Book was made known. Report 20555, from Abu Bishr, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal and Abu Awana: I said to Sa'id ibn Jubayr, "and whoever has knowledge of the Book", is it Abd Allah ibn Salam? He said, this sura is Meccan, so how could it be Abd Allah ibn Salam! He said, and he used to read it "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", he says, from God. Report 20556, from Abu Bishr, through al-Hasan, Sa'id ibn Mansur and Abu Awana: I asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr about God's saying "and whoever has knowledge of the Book", {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 505}

is it Abd Allah ibn Salam? He said, how, when this sura is Meccan? And Sa'id used to read it "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab". Report 20557, from al-Hasan and Juwaybir from al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Abbad and Awf: "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", they said, from God. Abu Ja'far says: a report has been related from the Messenger of God authenticating this reading and this interpretation, though its chain is open to question, which is what Ibn Umar relates in report 20558, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Abbad ibn al-Awwam, Harun al-A'war, al-Zuhri and Salim ibn Abd Allah: that the Prophet read "wa-min 'indihi 'ulima al-kitab", with God the Book was made known. Abu Ja'far says: this report has no basis among the reliable companions of al-Zuhri; the editor notes that the chain is broken, since Harun al-A'war did not hear from al-Zuhri, and that al-Haythami attributed it to Abu Ya'la and said it has in it Sulayman ibn Arqam, who is abandoned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 506}

Al-Tabari says: that being so, and the readers of the regions of the Hijaz, Syria and Iraq being on the other reading, which is "wa-man 'indahu 'ilmu al-kitab", the interpretation in the sense of the reading of the readers of the regions is more correct than what opposes it, since the reading on which they are agreed is the more deserving of being correct. With this the commentary on Sura al-Ra'd ends. The editor notes that after this the manuscript has the copyist's closing, in which he praises God, asks blessing and peace on the Prophet and his family, and announces that the commentary on Sura Ibrahim follows, God willing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 507}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
