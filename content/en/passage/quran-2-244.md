## Summary
Verse 244 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 244 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens ([[passage/quran-2-244|2:244]]) on p. 280 by saying that believers are addressed, that fighting in God's way means fighting in His religion that He guided them to and not in obedience to Satan, against the enemies of their religion who bar the way of their Lord; that they should not shrink from meeting them or be cowardly in war, since their life and death are in His hand; and that fear of death should not keep them from the encounter so that they flee and are humiliated, and the death they feared comes to them in their refuge, as it came to those who left in flight from death, while those left behind were not harmed by what they did not fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 280} The editor explains in the margin the meaning of several Arabic words (guarding oneself, fleeing in rout, seeking refuge, the soul) and notes that the printed text's correctors altered the manuscript's wording, which he criticises. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 280}

Al-Tabari says God tells the believers He hears the saying of those among their hypocrites who say of the one killed in His way that had they obeyed us and stayed at home they would not have been killed, and knows what their breasts conceal of hypocrisy, unbelief and scant gratitude; and that the believers are called to thank Him by obeying Him in fighting their enemy; He encompasses it all until He repays each for his deed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 281}

Al-Tabari then says there is no ground for the view that the command to fight is addressed to those who left their homes after their revival, since the command would have to be joined either to the command to die, which is impossible with a command to fight addressed to the dead; or to the report of reviving, and a command cannot be joined to a past report; or the word "said" is omitted, and he argues that omission is allowed only where the plain speech points to it, citing the verse in Surat al-Sajda ([[passage/quran-32-12|32:12]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 281} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 282} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument.
