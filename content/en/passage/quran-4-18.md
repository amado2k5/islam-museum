## Summary
Verse 18 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 18 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of the denial of repentance and the state of the dying (pp. 98-99).** In interpreting [[passage/quran-4-18|4:18]], Abu Jafar explains that the denial of repentance is for those who do misdeeds out of persistence in disobeying God, until one of them reaches the death rattle, sees the angels of his Lord coming to take his soul, and says that he has now repented, when he is already overpowered in himself and cut off from his understanding by his preoccupation with the agony of the rattle: for such a one there is no repentance with God, since he spoke when it was no time for repentance. In his note on p. 98 the editor refers to the commentary on the word in the language indexes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 98} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 99}

**Reports 8860-8864 (pp. 99-100).** Al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8860, from Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from Yala ibn Numan from one who heard Ibn Umar) reports that repentance is open as long as a person is not being driven, and that Ibn Umar recited the verse and then said that presence at death is nothing but the driving. Yunus (8861, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that once death shows in him, God does not accept repentance from him; al-Qasim (8862, from al-Husayn from Muhammad ibn Fudayl from Abu al-Nadr from Abu Salih) reports from Ibn Abbas that such a person has no repentance with God; Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna (8863, from Muhammad ibn Jafar from Shuba from Ibrahim ibn Maymun from a man of Banu al-Harith from a man among them) reports from Abd Allah ibn Amr that whoever repents a year before his death is relented upon, even a month, an hour, a fuwaq; a man asked how this could be with the verse that denies repentance to the one death reaches, and Abd Allah said he would tell him what he had heard from the Messenger of God. Ibn Waki (8864, from his father from Sufyan from Ibrahim ibn Muhajir) reports from Ibrahim that it used to be said that repentance is open until a person is seized by the throat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 99} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 100}

**The editor's notes on 8860, 8863 and 8864 (pp. 99-100).** On 8860 the editor says Yala ibn Numan is a trustworthy Kufan with entries in al-Bukhari's al-Tarikh al-Kabir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Tajil al-Manfaa, who narrated from Ikrima and Bilal ibn Abi al-Darda and was narrated from by al-Ala ibn al-Musayyab, al-Thawri and al-Zuhri; al-Suyuti traced the report in al-Durr al-Manthur (2:131), also to Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi; and he explains the driving as the agony as death comes. On 8863 he says Ahmad in his Musnad and al-Tayalisi gave it, and that his brother Ahmad says in his commentary on the Musnad that its chain is weak because of the unnamed man of Banu al-Harith narrating from a Successor; he explains the fuwaq as the time between two milkings, used to mean a very short time. On 8864 he explains the throat as the passage of the breath at the neck, says the manuscript reads "was seized" in place of the form in the printed text, a copyist's error, and that Ibn al-Athir narrated it and al-Suyuti traced it in al-Durr al-Manthur (2:131) to Ibn Jarir and Ibn al-Mundhir in the wording the first publisher gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 99} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 100}

**Whom does the verse mean? (pp. 100-101, reports 8865-8867).** Abu Jafar records three views. First, that it means the hypocrites: al-Muthanna (8865, from Ishaq from Ibn Abi Jafar from his father) reports from al-Rabi that the first verse came down about the believers, the middle one about the hypocrites and the last about the unbelievers. Second, that it means the people of Islam: al-Muthanna (8866, from Suwayd ibn Nasr from Ibn al-Mubarak) reports from Sufyan al-Thawri that it reached them that they are the Muslims, arguing from the last part of the verse being about unbelievers. Third, that the verse came down about the believers but was abrogated: al-Muthanna (8867, from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Muawiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that God sent down after it the verse of [[passage/quran-4-116|4:116]] (with its counterpart at [[passage/quran-4-48|4:48]]), which made forgiveness unlawful for one who died an unbeliever and deferred the people of divine oneness to his will without cutting off their hope of forgiveness. The editor says al-Suyuti traced it in al-Durr al-Manthur (2:131), also to Abu Dawud in his book on abrogation, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 100} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 101}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 101-102).** Abu Jafar chooses what al-Thawri mentioned, that they are the people of Islam. His argument is that the hypocrites are unbelievers, so had they been meant, the verse's closing mention of those who die as unbelievers would have no intelligible sense, since the two groups would be one in meaning, all being unbelievers, with no ground to distinguish their rulings when the reason repentance is voided is one. In God's distinguishing their names and descriptions, calling one group unbelievers and describing the other as doers of misdeeds without calling them unbelievers, is evidence that their meanings differ, and in its soundness is the soundness of what he said and the falsity of what opposes it. These views and the choice are al-Tabari's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 102}

**Grammar and sense of the end of the verse (p. 102).** Abu Jafar says the meaning is that there is no repentance for those who die in unbelief, and that the relative pronoun is in the genitive because it is joined to what precedes; the editor refers to al-Farra' in Maani al-Quran (1:259). He then explains that those for whom a grievous torment is made ready are these who died as unbelievers, because they are farthest from repentance, having died in unbelief, and he joins this to a report introduced by "as", which is cut off at the end of the page and finishes on p. 103. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 102}

**The editor's notes on pp. 101-102.** On p. 101 the editor gives al-Suyuti's tracing of 8867 and says, in note 2, that by "what al-Thawri mentioned" al-Tabari means report 8866. On p. 102, in note 1, he says the text in the manuscript was badly disturbed, but that the copyist had struck out the lam of "because they" with a light pen stroke, which showed him that what follows is "since those before them were", that a conjunction was dropped by the heedless copyist, and that the copyist also omitted the word "for them", which he put in brackets, whereupon the sentence became straight. He says the first publisher did "the worst of harm", not noticing what the copyist had struck out, making it "they and those before them", replacing three expressions with others and adding a word meaning "accepted" of his own at the end, and so spoiled the sentence; the note ends with a prayer for al-Tabari and for the copyist and praise to God for guidance. Note 3 begins by saying that this too is another piece of tampering by the first publisher, who could not read the manuscript well, and is cut off at the end of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 102}

**End of the editor's note from p. 102, and report 8868 (p. 103).** The third note ends by saying that the first publisher could not read a word left undotted in the manuscript, and so turned the sentence over in a way that voided its meaning and uprooted what Abu Jafar intended, writing a clause to the effect that they are the farthest from repentance because of their being in unbelief, thinking that the copyist's word for their death was a word for their being; the editor says he tampered with the text in a way no scholar would approve, and refers to the wording in the report that follows. Then report 8868: al-Qasim (from al-Husayn from Muhammad ibn Fudayl from Abu al-Nadr from Abu Salih) reports from Ibn Abbas that those who die as unbelievers are the farthest from repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 103}

**The sense of the preparing (p. 103).** The grammarians differed on the word for preparing at the end of the verse. Some Basrans, whom the editor identifies as Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Quran (1:120), said its form is taken from the word for equipment and that it means the same as the plain word for preparing; some Kufans said the two words have one meaning. So for Abu Jafar the sense is that God made ready for them a torment that hurts and pains, and the editor refers to the commentary on the word for painful in the language indexes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 103}
