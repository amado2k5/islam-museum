**Commentary on verse 92 (pp. 194-198)**

On [[passage/quran-10-92|10:92]] al-Tabari says God says to Pharaoh: today We place you on a height of the land in your body, for those who denied your destruction to see you dead, 'to be a sign for those after you', that is, a lesson for people after you, from which they take warning against disobeying God, disbelieving in Him and spreading corruption in His land. The 'najwa' is the place raised above what is around it, and he cites a line of Aws b. Hajar describing clouds and rain so violent that they cover every place and every person, so that the one in the open yard is like the one on high ground and the one who hid is like one walking in the open. Report 17868, from Qays b. Abbad and others: the Children of Israel said to Moses that Pharaoh had not died; so God brought him out to them for them to look at, like a red bull. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 194}

Report 17869, from Qays b. Abbad, who was one of the people who related most about the Children of Israel: when the first of Pharaoh's troops reached the sea the horses shrank from the gap; a mare in heat appeared to a stallion among them, and he caught her scent and slipped away, and the others followed. When the last of Pharaoh's troops were in the sea and the last of the Israelites had come out, God ordered the sea and it closed on them. The Children of Israel said that Pharaoh had not died and would never die, and God heard their denial of His prophet and threw him on the shore, looking like a red bull, for the Israelites to see. The editor says the 'lahb' is the pit between two mountains, which is the cleft made in the sea, and that the manuscript has another word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 195}

Report 17870, from Abd Allah b. Shaddad: 'his body' means his corpse, which the sea threw up; reports 17871, 17872 and 17873, from Mujahid: 'with your body' means with your corpse. Report 17874, from Ibn Abbas: when Moses crossed the sea with all who were with him the sea closed on Pharaoh and his people and drowned them; Moses' companions said we fear Pharaoh has not drowned and we do not believe in his destruction; so he prayed to his Lord, who brought him out, and the sea cast him up until they were sure of his destruction. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 196}

Report 17875, from Qatada: groups of the Children of Israel denied that, and God cast him on the sea's shore for them to look at; report 17876, from Qatada: when God drowned Pharaoh a group of people did not believe it, so God brought him out as a sign and a lesson; report 17877, from Abu al-Salil from Qays b. Abbad, to the same effect; reports 17878 and 17879, from Mujahid: with your corpse; report 17880, from Ibn Jurayj: some of the Children of Israel denied Pharaoh's death and he was thrown up on the sea shore for them to see, red, like a bull. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 197}

Others said it means: you will be saved from the sea in your body and We bring it out. Report 17881, from Ibn Abbas: God saved Pharaoh from the sea for the Children of Israel, and they looked at him after he had drowned. If someone asks what the point is of 'with your body', and whether He could save him other than with his body, so that the speech needs the phrase, the reply is that it was possible for Him to save him in his form alive as he had entered the sea; since that was possible, 'We save you in your body' was said to make it known that He saves him bodily without a soul, but dead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 197}

His words 'many people are heedless of Our signs' mean heedless of Our proofs and evidences that worship and divinity are Ours alone, 'heedless' meaning negligent, who do not reflect on them or take a lesson from them. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'sign', 'heedlessness' and 'settling'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 198}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
