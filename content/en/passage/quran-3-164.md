## Summary
Verse 164 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 164 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 164: the favour of sending the messenger (8177-8178, pp. 369-371).** Abu Ja'far: God showed favour to the believers when He sent among them a messenger from themselves, that is a prophet from the people of their own tongue, and did not make him from those of another tongue so that they would not understand what he says; he recites to them the verses of His Book and revelation; purifies them of their sins through their following him and obeying him in what he commanded and forbade; teaches them God's Book that was sent down to him and explains to them its interpretation and meanings; and by wisdom he means the sunna that God laid down for the believers through His messenger's tongue and his explanation to them. As for their having been before in clear error, he means before God showed them favour by sending His messenger with this description: in ignorance and bewilderment away from guidance, knowing no truth and refuting no falsehood. He recalls that he had explained the root of "error" earlier, that it is taking a path without guidance, and that "clear" is what becomes plain to whoever considers it with his reason and ponders it with his understanding, that it is without uprightness or guidance (p. 370). The editor's notes refer to the earlier explanations of reciting, purifying, wisdom, error and "clear" (the reference numbers are garbled in the scanned text). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 369} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 370}

Qatada (8177), through Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id: God showed favour to them without any call or wish from this community, and made him a mercy to them to bring them out of the darknesses into the light and guide them to a straight path; wisdom is the sunna; and on clear error he said it is not, by God, as the people of Harura' say, a prevailing trial in which whoever misses it has his blood shed, but God sent His prophet to a people who did not know and taught them, and to a people without manners and gave them manners. The editor notes that the people of Harura' are the Kharijites and that this was their doctrine (this is the report of Qatada and the editor's note, and the museum gives no verdict on it). Ibn Ishaq (8178), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: God has shown you favour, believers, in sending among you a messenger from yourselves who recites His verses to you and purifies you in what you newly did and what you wrought, teaches you good and evil so that you know the good and act on it and the evil and guard against it, and tells you of His pleasure with you when you obey so that you increase in obedience and avoid what angers Him of disobedience, and so escape His vengeance and reach His reward from His Paradise; and you were before in a blindness of pre-Islamic ignorance, not knowing a good deed nor asking forgiveness for a wrong, deaf to the truth, blind to guidance (p. 371). In the notes: the printed text has a wording meaning "what you took" and its publisher did not read the manuscript well, and the right reading is from the manuscript and from Ibn Hisham's Sira; the printed text has a meaningless word in place of "ask forgiveness", the manuscript has an undotted word that is most likely an error, and the correct word is that of Ibn Hisham; the report is in the Sira (3: 124) as a continuation of reports ending with 8172 and 8176; and the last sentence in Ibn Hisham describes its subjects as deaf to good, dumb to truth and blind to guidance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 370} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 371}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 371}
