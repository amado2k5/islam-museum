## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "thus We have made you a middle community" al-Tabari says: as We guided you, believers in Muhammad and in what came to you from God, and singled you out for success in the qibla and creed of Abraham, preferring you over the followers of other creeds, so We singled you out by making you a middle community. He notes that "umma" is a generation or a class of people, and that "wasat" in Arab usage is the best, citing Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma on a people who are "a middle, whose judgement all people accept". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 141} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 142}

Al-Tabari holds that "wasat" here is the part between two extremes, and that God described them so for their moderation in religion: neither excessive like the Christians in monasticism and what they said of Jesus, nor remiss like the Jews who altered God's book and killed their prophets, but moderate and balanced, since the dearest of matters to God are the middlemost. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 142}

He says the interpretation reports "wasat" as just, which is the sense of "best", because the best of people are their just ones, and reports this from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Abu Hurayra from the Prophet with the word "just" (plural), and the same from Mujahid, Qatada and al-Rabi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 142} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 143} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 144} The editor flags problems in some chains and notes other transmissions have the singular form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 143} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 144}

Al-Tabari also reports "a middle community" as meaning just, from Ibn 'Abbas, from Habban ibn Abi Jabala with a chain to the Prophet, from 'Ata', Mujahid and 'Abd Allah ibn Kathir, and from Ibn Zayd that they are a middle between the Prophet and the nations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 145}

On "that you may be witnesses over the people and the Messenger a witness over you" he says "shuhada'" is the plural of "shahid", and the sense is: thus We made you a middle, just community so that you are witnesses for My prophets and messengers over their nations that they delivered what they were commanded to deliver, and My messenger Muhammad is a witness over you through your belief in him and in what came to you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 145} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 146} He then reports from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri from the Prophet that Noah will be called on the Day of Resurrection and asked whether he delivered; he says yes; his people are asked and say no warner came to us; he is asked who testifies for him, and says Muhammad and his community; in another version they are called and testify that he delivered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 146} In another report from Abu Sa'id the sense is witnesses over the people that the messengers delivered, and the Messenger a witness over you for what you did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 146} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 147} From Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah from the Prophet, he and his community will be on a mound on the Day of Resurrection overlooking creation, and for every prophet whose people denied him this community will testify that he delivered his Lord's messages and advised them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 147} The editor discusses the narrators of this chain in the notes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 147} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 148}

He reports from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet passed a funeral that people praised and said "it is due", then another that people spoke ill of and said "it is due"; Ubayy ibn Ka'b asked, and he said it is God's saying "that you may be witnesses over the people"; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 148} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 149} and from Salama ibn al-Akwa' similarly, with the saying that the angels are God's witnesses in heaven and you God's witnesses on earth, so what you testify to becomes due, after which he recited the verse from Surat al-Tawba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 149} The editor examines the narrators of these chains and judges some sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 148} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 149}
