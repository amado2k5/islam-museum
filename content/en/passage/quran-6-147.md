## Summary
Verse 147 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 147 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Verse 147, "if they deny you, say: your Lord is of vast mercy": God tells His prophet that if these Jews deny you about what We told you, that We forbade them and permitted them as explained in this verse, then say: your Lord is of mercy toward us and toward those of His servants who believe in Him and toward others of His creation, vast enough to encompass all His creation, the doer of good and the doer of wrong; the note says the printed text read the plural "they denied you" and the right reading is from the manuscript. He does not hasten punishment for one who disbelieves in Him or vengeance for one who disobeys Him, and does not forsake the honouring of one who believes and obeys Him or deprive him of the reward of his work, out of mercy to both groups; but His might, His assault and punishment, cannot be turned back by anything when He sends it down in wrath upon the criminals. "The criminals" are those who committed crimes and earned sins and wrongs; the editor points to the earlier commentary on "vast", "might" and "criminal". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 207}

He gives (14126) and (14127) from Mujahid that "if they deny you" refers to the Jews, followed by "say: your Lord is of vast mercy"; and (14128) from al-Suddi that the Jews used to say that Israel forbade it, meaning the tharb and the fat of the two kidneys, so they forbid it; that is the meaning of "if they deny you, say: your Lord is of vast mercy, and His might cannot be turned back from the criminal people". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 207}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
