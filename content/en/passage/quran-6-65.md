## Summary
Verse 65 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 65 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: he is able to send upon you a punishment from above you or from beneath your feet" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to those who hold idols and images equal to their Lord: he who delivers you from the darknesses of land and sea and every distress, and then you return to joining others with him, is able to send upon you a punishment from above you or from beneath your feet because of your associating with him, your claiming another god with him, and your ingratitude for his favours although he has lavished his bounties and benefactions on you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 416}

The interpreters differed on the meaning of the punishment. Some said the punishment from above them is stoning and from beneath them the swallowing of the earth. Report 13344, from Abu Malik: the swallowing; 13345, from Abu Malik and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, the same; 13346, from Mujahid: the swallowing; 13347, from al-Suddi: punishment from heaven, or the earth is made to swallow you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 417}

13348, from Ibn Zayd: Ibn Mas'ud used to cry out in the assembly or on the pulpit: people, it has come upon you; God says "he is able to send upon you a punishment from above you", and had a punishment from heaven come, none of you would remain; "or from beneath your feet", and had the earth swallowed you it would have destroyed you and none would remain; "or confuse you in sects and make some of you taste the might of others"; the worst of the three has come upon you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 417}

The editor says the printed text differs from the manuscript, that the manuscript has errors in this report which the printed text corrects, and that he kept from the manuscript what he judged right elsewhere; and for 13344 that he restored the manuscript's wording in place of the verse text given in print.

Others said that the punishment from above you means evil rulers, and "from beneath your feet" servants and the lowly. 13349, from Amir ibn Abd al-Rahman, that Ibn Abbas used to say: the punishment from above you is evil rulers and from beneath your feet evil servants; 13350, from Ibn Abbas: from your rulers, and from beneath your feet your lowly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 418}

For 13349 the editor identifies Khallad ibn Sulayman al-Hadrami al-Misri, an unlettered tailor who could not write and was one of those who heard unseen voices, from whom Ibn Wahb related, and who is trusted; and says Amir ibn Abd al-Rahman is named by al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim in Khallad's entry, but that he found no entry for him in the references at hand, which he finds strange.

Al-Tabari holds the better of the two views the one that says the punishment from above them is stoning or the flood and the like that descends on them from above their heads, and from beneath their feet the swallowing and the like, because the familiar sense in Arab speech of "above" and "beneath the feet" is that and no other; though what is reported from Ibn Abbas is a sound sense, when the meaning of a speech is disputed, taking it in its most common and familiar sense is fitter and better unless a proof prevents it that must be accepted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 418}

The first part of verse 65 closes here at p. 418, and al-Tabari goes on to the rest of the verse.

On "or confuse you in sects and make some of you taste the might of others" al-Tabari says: or he mixes you "in sects", factions, the singular being "shi'a". As for "yalbisakum", it is from "labastu alayhi al-amr" when you confuse a matter, "I confuse it"; he says so because the readers do not differ over the kasra of the ba, which is clear proof that it is from "labasa yalbisu", meaning to mix, and the sense is: or he mixes you in differing desires and scattered parties. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 419}

13351, from Mujahid: scattered desires; 13352, from al-Suddi: he divides you; 13353, from Mujahid: the trials and disagreements among you; 13354, from Ibn Zayd: what people are in today of disagreement and desires and shedding one another's blood. 13355 and 13356, from Ibn Abbas: desires and disagreement, the sects being the differing desires. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 420}

The editor says the printed text has "from division" and the manuscript an unpointed word, and he kept what he thought the right reading; and refers for "labs" to earlier passages.

"And make some of you taste the might of others" means that some of you kill others at their hands. The Arabs say of a man who strikes another with a weapon and kills him: so-and-so made so-and-so taste death and taste his might; the origin is tasting food, then it was used for anything that reaches a man of pleasure and sweetness or bitterness, hardship and pain. Al-Tabari has already explained the sense of "ba's". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 420}

The editor says Abu Jafar did not explain "dhawq" fully in earlier places and made it entirely plain here, a form of his abridgement in his commentary.

We stop here at the foot of p. 420, at the opening of report 13357 on "make some of you taste the might of others", and al-Tabari continues on p. 421.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
