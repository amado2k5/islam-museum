## Summary
Verse 65 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 65 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: he is able to send upon you a punishment from above you or from beneath your feet" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to those who hold idols and images equal to their Lord: he who delivers you from the darknesses of land and sea and every distress, and then you return to joining others with him, is able to send upon you a punishment from above you or from beneath your feet because of your associating with him, your claiming another god with him, and your ingratitude for his favours although he has lavished his bounties and benefactions on you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 416}

The interpreters differed on the meaning of the punishment. Some said the punishment from above them is stoning and from beneath them the swallowing of the earth. Report 13344, from Abu Malik: the swallowing; 13345, from Abu Malik and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, the same; 13346, from Mujahid: the swallowing; 13347, from al-Suddi: punishment from heaven, or the earth is made to swallow you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 417}

13348, from Ibn Zayd: Ibn Mas'ud used to cry out in the assembly or on the pulpit: people, it has come upon you; God says "he is able to send upon you a punishment from above you", and had a punishment from heaven come, none of you would remain; "or from beneath your feet", and had the earth swallowed you it would have destroyed you and none would remain; "or confuse you in sects and make some of you taste the might of others"; the worst of the three has come upon you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 417}

The editor says the printed text differs from the manuscript, that the manuscript has errors in this report which the printed text corrects, and that he kept from the manuscript what he judged right elsewhere; and for 13344 that he restored the manuscript's wording in place of the verse text given in print.

Others said that the punishment from above you means evil rulers, and "from beneath your feet" servants and the lowly. 13349, from Amir ibn Abd al-Rahman, that Ibn Abbas used to say: the punishment from above you is evil rulers and from beneath your feet evil servants; 13350, from Ibn Abbas: from your rulers, and from beneath your feet your lowly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 418}

For 13349 the editor identifies Khallad ibn Sulayman al-Hadrami al-Misri, an unlettered tailor who could not write and was one of those who heard unseen voices, from whom Ibn Wahb related, and who is trusted; and says Amir ibn Abd al-Rahman is named by al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim in Khallad's entry, but that he found no entry for him in the references at hand, which he finds strange.

Al-Tabari holds the better of the two views the one that says the punishment from above them is stoning or the flood and the like that descends on them from above their heads, and from beneath their feet the swallowing and the like, because the familiar sense in Arab speech of "above" and "beneath the feet" is that and no other; though what is reported from Ibn Abbas is a sound sense, when the meaning of a speech is disputed, taking it in its most common and familiar sense is fitter and better unless a proof prevents it that must be accepted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 418}

The first part of verse 65 closes here at p. 418, and al-Tabari goes on to the rest of the verse.

On "or confuse you in sects and make some of you taste the might of others" al-Tabari says: or he mixes you "in sects", factions, the singular being "shi'a". As for "yalbisakum", it is from "labastu alayhi al-amr" when you confuse a matter, "I confuse it"; he says so because the readers do not differ over the kasra of the ba, which is clear proof that it is from "labasa yalbisu", meaning to mix, and the sense is: or he mixes you in differing desires and scattered parties. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 419}

13351, from Mujahid: scattered desires; 13352, from al-Suddi: he divides you; 13353, from Mujahid: the trials and disagreements among you; 13354, from Ibn Zayd: what people are in today of disagreement and desires and shedding one another's blood. 13355 and 13356, from Ibn Abbas: desires and disagreement, the sects being the differing desires. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 420}

The editor says the printed text has "from division" and the manuscript an unpointed word, and he kept what he thought the right reading; and refers for "labs" to earlier passages.

"And make some of you taste the might of others" means that some of you kill others at their hands. The Arabs say of a man who strikes another with a weapon and kills him: so-and-so made so-and-so taste death and taste his might; the origin is tasting food, then it was used for anything that reaches a man of pleasure and sweetness or bitterness, hardship and pain. Al-Tabari has already explained the sense of "ba's". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 420}

The editor says Abu Jafar did not explain "dhawq" fully in earlier places and made it entirely plain here, a form of his abridgement in his commentary.

13357, from al-Suddi: "make some of you taste the might of others" is by swords. 13358, from Nawf al-Bikali: it is, by God, men with spears in their hands thrusting at your flanks. 13359, from Ibn Abbas: some of you are set on others with killing and punishment. 13360, from Mujahid: the punishment of this community, those who profess, is by the sword, "or confuse you in sects and make some of you taste the might of others", while the punishment of those who deny is the cry and the quake. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 421}

The interpreters then differed over whom the verse means. Some said the Muslims of the community of Muhammad, and that it was revealed about them. 13361, from Abu al-Aliya: they are four and all are punishments; two came to rest twenty-five years after the death of God's messenger, when they were mixed in sects and made to taste one another's might, and two remain that must come, the swallowing and the transformation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 421}

The editor says the printed text has a different wording from the manuscript, whose reading is plain; identifies Muhammad ibn Isa al-Damghani, a teacher of Abu Jafar; and refers to Abu al-Aliya's report from Ubayy ibn Ka'b at 13380.

13362 and 13363, from Mujahid: "from above you or from beneath your feet" was for the community of Muhammad and he spared you it, "or confuse you in sects" is the trials and disagreements among you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 422}

13364, from Qatada: we were told that God's messenger prayed the dawn prayer one day and made it long, and one of his household said he had prayed a prayer he did not usually pray; he said it was a prayer of hope and fear, in which he asked his Lord three things: that he not set over his community an enemy from outside to destroy them, which was granted; that he not send famine on his community, which was granted; and that he not confuse them in sects or make them taste one another's might, which was refused. It was also reported that he used to say: a party of my community will not cease fighting for the truth, prevailing, unharmed by those who desert them, until God's command comes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 422}

13365 and 13366, from Jabir: when "say: he is able to send upon you a punishment from above you or from beneath your feet" was revealed, the Prophet said: I take refuge in your face; and when "or confuse you in sects and make some of you taste the might of others" was revealed, he said: these two are easier, or lighter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 422}

The editor says Ahmad ibn al-Walid al-Qurashi in 13365 appeared earlier without a lineage and his brother Ahmad Shakir said he did not know him; he adds that he found Abu Jafar in his history relating from a teacher Ahmad ibn al-Walid al-Ramli, collected to settle his identity; he explains that Amr in both reports is ibn Dinar, that al-Bukhari related it in the Fath through Hammad ibn Zayd from Amr, and that al-Tirmidhi related it in the commentary and called it good and sound.

13367, from Nafi ibn Khalid al-Khuza'i from his father: the Prophet prayed a light prayer with full bowing and prostration, and said it was a prayer of hope and fear in which he asked God three things and two were given and one remained: that God not strike you with a punishment that struck those before you, which was granted; that he not set over you an enemy who would violate your "bayda", which was granted; and that he not confuse you in sects and make you taste one another's might, which was refused. Abu Malik said: I asked him, did your father hear this from God's messenger? He said yes, I heard him tell it to the people, that he heard it from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 423}

The editor gives a long tracing of this report and its men: Ziyad ibn Ubaydallah al-Muzani, whom his brother did not know earlier; Marwan ibn Mu'awiya al-Fazari, trusted, a teacher of Ahmad; Abu Malik al-Ashja'i, Sa'd ibn Tariq, whom Ahmad trusted; and Nafi ibn Khalid al-Khuza'i, whom neither al-Bukhari nor Ibn Abi Hatim faulted. He points out a mistake in Lisan al-Mizan that confuses the entry of Khalid al-Khuza'i with that of another, unknown Khalid, which he says should be corrected; that the father Khalid al-Khuza'i had companionship; that the men of the report are trusted as Ibn Hajar said in al-Isaba; that al-Haythami in Majma al-Zawa'id, al-Bukhari in his history, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti traced it; and that al-Tirmidhi has the like from Khabbab, at 13370. He explains "violates their bayda" as their body, their root and the seat of their authority, since the destruction of the egg's root is the destruction of what is in it, or alternatively the people's courtyard and the main part of their abode, which he finds nearer.

13368, from Shaddad ibn Aws, tracing it to the Prophet: God drew the earth together for me until I saw its east and west, and my community's dominion will reach what was drawn together for me of it; I was given the two treasures, the red and the white; and I asked my Lord that he not destroy my people by a general famine and not confuse them in sects or make them taste one another's might. He said: Muhammad, when I decree a decree it is not turned back; I have given you for your community that I will not destroy them by a general famine and will not set over them an enemy from others who would destroy them wholesale, until some of them destroy others, some kill others and some enslave others. The Prophet said: I fear for my community the misleading leaders, and once the sword is placed among my community it will not be lifted from them until the day of resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 425}

The editor says the right reading comes from the Musnad in one verb, the printed and manuscript versions being mixed up; he identifies Abu al-Ash'ath al-San'ani, Shurahil ibn Adah, a trusted Follower, Abu Asma al-Rahabi, Amr ibn Marthad, a trusted Follower, and Shaddad ibn Aws al-Ansari, a Companion; that Ahmad related it in his Musnad, Ibn Hajar pointed to it and called its chain sound, Ibn Kathir traced it and said its chain is good and strong and that it is not in the six books, and that Ibn Mardawayh gave it from Thawban, as did Muslim and Abu Dawud; he explains "zawa" as gathering and drawing the earth together so that he saw all of it, and "sana" as famine; and quotes al-Nawawi that the report holds clear signs that came to pass, the two treasures being gold and silver or the treasures of Khosrow and Caesar.

13369, from Shaddad ibn Aws, the like, except that he said: I fear for my community only the misleading leaders. 13370, from al-Zuhri: Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, a veteran of Badr, watched the Prophet pray until he finished, it being the dawn prayer, and said he had never seen him pray like it; he said yes, it is a prayer of hope and fear, I asked my Lord three things and two were given and one refused: that he not destroy us as he destroyed the nations, granted; that he not set an enemy over us, granted; and that he not confuse us in sects, refused. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 426}

The editor says Ahmad related this in his Musnad and al-Tirmidhi in the Trials, connected through al-Zuhri from Abdullah ibn Abdullah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal, from Abdullah ibn Khabbab, from Khabbab ibn al-Aratt; that Ibn Kathir noted al-Nasa'i, Ibn Hibban and al-Tirmidhi relating it from al-Zuhri, al-Tirmidhi calling it good and sound, and that al-Suyuti has it; and he vowels "ragh" and "rahab" as hope and fear.

13371, from al-Zuhri, the like, except that he said three qualities. 13372, from Jabir ibn Abdullah: when "say: he is able to send upon you a punishment from above you" was revealed, the Prophet said: I take refuge in your face; "or from beneath your feet", he said: I take refuge in your face; "or confuse you in sects", he said: this is lighter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 427}

13373, from al-Hasan: the Prophet said: I asked my Lord four things and was given three and refused one: I asked that he not set over my community an enemy from outside who would violate their bayda, nor set hunger over them, nor gather them on error, and I was given these; and I asked that he not confuse them in sects or make them taste one another's might, and that was refused. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 428}

13374, from al-Suddi: God's messenger said: I asked my Lord for several things, and he gave me three and refused one: I asked that my community not become unbelievers all at once, and he gave it; I asked that he not let an enemy from outside prevail over them, and he gave it; I asked that he not punish them as he punished the nations before them, and he gave it; and I asked that he not make their might fall among themselves, and he refused it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 428}

13375, from al-Hasan: when "make some of you taste the might of others" was revealed, then God told Muhammad, calling him as witness against them, "see how we vary the signs that they may understand"; God's messenger got up, made ablution, and asked his Lord not to send on them a punishment from above them or beneath their feet, nor confuse his community in sects and make them taste one another's might as he had made the Children of Israel taste it. Gabriel came down and said: Muhammad, you asked your Lord four things; he gave you two and refused two: no punishment will come on them from above or beneath their feet to uproot them, for those are the two punishments of every community that united in denying its prophet and rejecting its Lord's book, but he will confuse them in sects and make them taste one another's might, and these two are punishments for those who profess the book and believe the prophets, who are punished for their sins. And it was revealed to him, [[passage/quran-43-41|43:41]] and [[passage/quran-43-42|43:42]], that if he were taken away God would take vengeance on them, or would show him in his life what he promised them, being able over them. He got up and went back to his Lord and said: what affliction is worse than that I see my community punishing one another? And [[passage/quran-29-1|29:1]] to [[passage/quran-29-3|29:3]] on trial was revealed to him, telling him that his community was not singled out from the nations for trials and would be tried as the nations were. Then [[passage/quran-23-93|23:93]] and [[passage/quran-23-94|23:94]] came down, a prayer not to be placed among the wrongdoing people; he sought refuge, God gave it, and he saw nothing of his community but unity, harmony and obedience. Then [[passage/quran-8-25|8:25]] was revealed, warning his companions of trial, telling him it would strike some of them and not others; so it struck some of the Companions after him and spared others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 429}

The editor says that in 13375 the printed text and manuscript have a verb form that is permissible though a different form is better, and he fears it is the copyist's.

13376, from Abu al-Aliya: when Gabriel came to the Prophet and told him of the division and disagreement that would come to his community, it weighed on him and he prayed: God, make the best of them prevail over them, sparing them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 429}

The editor says the printed text has "fear" in place of "sparing", the manuscript being unpointed, and he kept "sparing", meaning mercy toward the one who is overcome.

13377, from Abu al-Zubayr: when "say: he is able to send upon you a punishment from above you" was revealed, the Prophet said: I take refuge in God from that; "or from beneath your feet", he said: I take refuge in God from that; "or confuse you in sects", he said: this is easier; had he sought refuge from it, God would have given it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 429}

The editor identifies Khalid ibn Yazid al-Jumahi al-Misri and Abu al-Zubayr Muhammad ibn Muslim al-Makki.

13378, from Zayd ibn Aslam: when the verse was revealed the Prophet said: do not return after me as unbelievers, striking one another's necks with swords. They said: we bear witness there is no god but God and that you are his messenger? He said: yes. Some people said: this will never be; so God revealed "see how we vary the signs that they may understand. Your people denied it though it is the truth; say: I am not a guardian over you; for every tiding there is a settled time, and you will know". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 430}

The editor says al-Mu'ammal al-Basri is Mu'ammal ibn Isma'il, who has come up often, and that he found no mention of Ya'qub ibn Isma'il ibn Yasar al-Madini in the biographical books, which he finds strange.

Others said that part of it means the polytheists and part the people of Islam. 13379, from al-Hasan: "from above you or from beneath your feet" is for the polytheists, and "or confuse you in sects and make some of you taste the might of others" is for the Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 430}

Al-Tabari holds that the right view is that God threatened by this verse the polytheists who worship idols and addressed it to them, because it falls between report about them and address to them: it follows "say: who delivers you from the darknesses of land and sea..." and "say: God delivers you from it and from every distress; yet you associate others", and is followed by "your people denied it though it is the truth", and it is impossible that the believers were the deniers of it; so with the verse between these two it is plain that it is a threat to those whose associating God described before and whose denial was reported after, not to those not mentioned. Yet its threat extends to everyone who follows their way among those who oppose God and his messenger and deny God's signs, in this and other matters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 431}

As for the reports that he said "I asked my Lord three things and he gave me two and refused one", it is possible that the verse came down then as a threat to the polytheists and those of their way who oppose their Lord, and God's messenger asked his Lord to spare his community what the nations were tried with that earned these punishments by their disobedience, and God spared them through his prayer and desire from the sins that would bring the harshest of the four punishments, though he did not spare them what earns two of them. As for those who understood the whole verse to mean this community, he thinks they took it that some in this community would commit sins and do what angers God as earlier nations did, and the like of what befell those before them of exemplary punishments and vengeances would befall them; so Abu al-Aliya said, two came twenty-five years after God's messenger and two remain, the swallowing and the transformation, because it is reported from him that he said there will be in this community swallowing, transformation and pelting, and that some of his community would spend the night at amusement and play and wake as apes and swine, and that, if it comes, is surely like what befell the nations that rebelled against their Lord in denial and rejected his signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 432}

The editor says the first report is from A'isha, related by al-Tirmidhi in the Trials, saying that at the end of this community there will be swallowing, transformation and pelting, and that she asked whether they would perish with the righteous among them and he said yes, when corruption appears; al-Tirmidhi called it strange and Yahya ibn Sa'id spoke of the memory of its narrator Abdullah ibn Umar; and that al-Bukhari in the Fath relates from Abu Malik and Abu Amir al-Ash'ari that some of this community would make lawful fornication, silk, wine and instruments, and a people would camp by a mountain and be told to return tomorrow, and God would strike them by night, drop the mountain on them and transform others into apes and swine until the day of resurrection.

13380, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b: four qualities, all punishments, all falling before the day of resurrection; two passed twenty-five years after the Prophet's death, when they were mixed in sects and tasted one another's might, and two are bound to come, the swallowing and the stoning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 432}

The editor says the chain is sound and Ahmad related it in his Musnad; that al-Haythami called its men trusted and said the words "two passed" onward appear to be Abu al-Aliya's own, since Ubayy did not live to the time of the trial; that Ibn Hajar in the Fath says the same, adding that the report was faulted for contradicting Jabir's report and answered that the sparing in Jabir's report is limited to the time of the Companions and the best generations, after which it may occur, and he cites the report of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas that the verse is yet to come and its interpretation has not arrived; and that Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti mention it.

Verse 65 closes here on p. 433.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
