## Summary
Verse 45 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 45 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Glad tidings and the Word.** Al-Tabari says God means that Muhammad was not with them when they disputed, nor when the angels gave Mary glad tidings; glad tidings are informing a person of news that pleases him (the editor says the printed text has a different word, the manuscript is undotted, and he chose the reading given). He says that 'a word from Him' means a message from God and news from Him, as one says that someone cast a word to me which pleased me, and cites [[passage/quran-4-171|4:171]], meaning God's glad tidings to Mary of Jesus, which He cast to her. The sense of the passage is thus that Muhammad was not with the people when the angels gave Mary tidings from God, a child of hers with a name and description. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 410} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 411}

**The view of Qatada.** Al-Tabari says some held, and this is the view of Qatada, that the Word is God's command for a thing to be, and gives report 7061 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada). God called him His Word because he came about by His word, as one says of what God decreed that it is God's decree and ordaining, meaning it came from them, and as in [[passage/quran-4-47|4:47]] and [[passage/quran-33-37|33:37]], meaning what God commanded, the commanded thing that came to be by His command. The editor notes a bracketed addition without which the sentence does not stand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 411}

**The view of Ibn Abbas.** Al-Tabari says others held that it is a name of Jesus by which God named him, as He names His other creatures with whatever names He wishes, and it is reported from Ibn Abbas that the Word is Jesus. Report 7062 (Ibn Waki, from his father, from Isra'il, from Simak, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas) says Jesus is the Word from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 412}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says the closest view to the correct one in his opinion is the first: that the angels gave Mary tidings of Jesus from God, through His message and His word which He commanded them to cast to her, namely that God would create from her a child without husband or male. That is why the pronoun in 'his name' is masculine though the word is feminine: the word here is not meant as the name that stands for 'so-and-so' but as glad tidings, so its pronoun is masculine as the pronoun of 'offspring', 'creature' and titles is; the editor says the pronoun is the term the Kufans use. He then says some Basran grammarians claimed it is masculine because the Word is Jesus in meaning, giving the example of the soul addressed in [[passage/quran-39-56|39:56]] to [[passage/quran-39-59|39:59]] and the phrase 'the one with the small breast' because his hand, and the account breaks off at the end of p. 412. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 412}

**The Kufan grammarians.** Al-Tabari says that one of the Kufan grammarians agreed with the Basrans that the pronoun refers to the Word but differed in the reason: the Arabs, he held, use the masculine pronoun for epithets, titles and names not set down to identify a particular 'so-and-so', such as 'offspring', 'successor' and 'beast', so for him the adjective of 'offspring' may be masculine or feminine, but it is not allowed with a name such as Talha or Mughira. Some rejected the reasoning from 'the one with the small breast', saying the feminine ending was added because a piece of the breast was meant, as one says 'we were in a piece of meat and of date wine' for a portion of it; al-Tabari says this is close to his own saying. The editor notes that the story of the man so named is well known and refers to Abu Dawud's chapter on fighting the Kharijites and to earlier pages (362-363). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 413}

**The lineage of Jesus.** Al-Tabari says God informed His servants of the lineage of the Messiah and that he is the son of his mother Mary, thereby denying of him what the heretical among the Christians attribute, sonship to God, and what the slanderous Jews charged his mother with; the editor says the printed text had a different word for 'charged' and that the manuscript is right, the 'slanderous' being the group that slandered her. Report 7063 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says the sense is that such was his case, not what they say about him; the editor refers to the Sira of Ibn Hisham (2:229-230), the remainder of the reports whose last was 7060. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 413} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 414}

**The meaning of the Messiah.** Al-Tabari says the word is a form turned from 'anointed' (passive) into the intensive form, so it means one whom God anointed and so purified of sins; hence Ibrahim said the Messiah is the truthful one. Report 7064 (Ibn Waki, from his father, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Ibrahim) is the same, as is 7065 (Ibn Humayd, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Ibrahim). Others said he was anointed with blessing, and report 7066 (Ibn al-Barqi, from Amr ibn Abi Salama) has Sa'id saying he was called the Messiah because he was anointed with blessing. The editor says there is certainly a gap in the text where the dots stand, that he moved the sentence 'others said' to its place before 7066, that many other explanations of the word exist which he does not think al-Tabari overlooked, and that he suspects an old lacuna in the copy, unless al-Tabari omitted them for brevity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 414}

**Eminence and nearness.** Al-Tabari says an eminent person is one with standing and a high rank with God, honour and dignity, the word used of a man whom kings and people honour, and that the word for rank is a reversal of the word for face; a verb from it is heard from the Arabs for confronting someone with something greater. The accusative on the adjective is by cutting off from Jesus (the editor explains this means the circumstantial accusative, with a reference to al-Farra, 1:213), because Jesus is definite and the adjective indefinite, though the genitive, following the Word, would be allowed. Report 7067 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) gives the sense as eminent with God in this world and the next; the editor traces it to the Sira (2:230) and says the manuscript's reading was preferred over the printed one. Of the 'brought near' al-Tabari says it means he is one whom God brings near on the Day of Resurrection, lodging him in His neighbourhood. Report 7068 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid ibn Zuray, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says the same, as do 7069 (from Ammar ibn al-Hasan, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) and 7070 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 415} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 416} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
