## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and We shaded you with the clouds" al-Tabari takes it as joined to "then We raised you up after your death", and "ghamam" as what covers the sky, whether cloud, murk or anything veiling it, the Arabs calling anything covered "maghmum". He records that it was said the "ghamam" was not ordinary cloud: Mujahid says it was not cloud but the "ghamam" in which God comes on the Day of Resurrection, though in another report he says it was like cloud; Ibn Abbas says it was a cloud cooler and sweeter than this, which was with them in the wilderness. Al-Tabari holds that what God shaded the Israelites with is no more to be described as cloud than as anything else that covers the face of the sky, and notes the saying that it was the white of the clouds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 90} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 91}

On "and We sent down upon you the manna" al-Tabari says the commentators differ on what manna was. Mujahid says it was a gum; Qatada that it came down on them like snow; al-Rabi' ibn Anas that it was a honey-like drink they mixed with water and drank; Ibn Zayd that it was honey sent from the sky; and al-Sha'bi that "your honey is one part in seventy of the manna". Wahb ibn Munabbih says it was thin bread like corn or fine flour; al-Suddi that it fell on ginger trees; Ibn Abbas, al-Dahhak and al-Sha'bi that it fell on trees and they ate it. He also records a view that it was "taranjabin", one that it was what falls on the thumam and ushar plants and is sweet as honey, citing a line of al-A'sha, one that it was a sweet drink they boiled, and lines of Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt that make it dripping honey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 91} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 92} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 93} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 94} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 95}
He cites, without a chain, a report that truffles are of the manna and their water is a cure for the eye, saying such reports were widespread from the Prophet; the editor says it was transmitted by Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim and al-Tirmidhi from Sa'id ibn Zayd, Abu Sa'id and Jabir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 94}
On "and the quails" al-Tabari says "salwa" is the name of a bird resembling the quail, singular and plural alike, though some say its singular is "salwat". He reports from al-Suddi, through his usual chain from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions, that it was a bird like the quail, and from him also that it was larger than the quail; from Qatada that the south wind drove them to the Israelites; from Mujahid that it was a bird; and from Wahb that it was a plump bird like a dove. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 96}

Al-Tabari completes the reports on the quail: Ibn Zayd, al-Rabi' ibn Anas, al-Sha'bi, al-Dahhak and Ibn Abbas say it is the quail. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 97}
On the reason for the shading cloud and the sending of manna and quail he says the scholars differ and gives what he has at hand. Al-Suddi says that when God accepted the repentance of Moses's people and revived the seventy He told them to go to Jericho, the land of Bayt al-Maqdis; Moses sent twelve leaders, his people said "go, you and your Lord, and fight, we sit here", Moses called down a curse on them and the wilderness of forty years was decreed, and Moses regretted it. They asked for water and food, so God sent the manna onto the taranjabin trees and the quail, Moses struck the rock with his staff and twelve springs flowed, one for each tribe, the cloud shaded them, and their clothes grew with them and never wore out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 97} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 98}
Ibn Ishaq says that after repentance Moses was told to lead them to the Holy Land; when they reached the wilderness between Egypt and Syria, which had neither cover nor shade, Moses prayed as the heat troubled them and God shaded them with the cloud and sent manna and quail in answer to his prayer for provision. Al-Rabi' says they stayed within an area of five or six leagues, setting out each morning and by evening finding themselves where they had left, for forty years, with a stone from Mount Tur that Moses struck. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 98} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 99}
From Wahb ibn Munabbih comes an exchange between the Israelites and Moses about food, clothing, water and light: the manna came as ready-baked bread, the wind brought the quail, of which they took from one Sabbath to the next; no garment wore out and no sandal strap broke in forty years, a child's garment grew with him, a pillar of light lit the camp and the cloud shaded them. Ibn Abbas says clothes were made for them that did not wear or soil, and Ibn Jurayj that whoever took more manna and quail than a day's food found it spoiled, except that on Friday they took the food of the Sabbath, which did not spoil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 99} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 100} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 101}
On "eat of the good things We have provided you" al-Tabari holds that "and We said to you" is omitted, and that "good things" is better understood as pleasant, enjoyable things than merely lawful ones, since the verse describes the easy life they were given, "ma" meaning "that which". "They wronged Us not, but themselves they wronged" likewise omits something the wording indicates. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 101}

Al-Tabari explains "they wronged Us not" as: they did not make their deed and disobedience a harm or a loss to Us, but made it so to themselves, and reports from Ibn Abbas that "yazlimun" here means they harm. He adds that no one's disobedience harms God and no one's obedience benefits Him; the wrongdoer wrongs only himself and the obedient benefits only himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 102}
