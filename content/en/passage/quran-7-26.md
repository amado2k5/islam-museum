## Summary
Verse 26 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 26 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Children of Adam, We have sent down clothing to cover your nakedness, and finery", God speaks to the ignorant among the Arabs who used to go naked for circumambulation, following Satan's command and leaving obedience to God; He made them know how they had been deceived by his delusion, until he got hold of them and stripped them of the covering God had favoured them with, so that he exposed their private parts to one another, despite God's favour in enabling them to cover them; and that he went with them his way with their parents Adam and Eve, whom he led on with delusion until he stripped them of the covering God had favoured them with and exposed their private parts, laying them bare of it: "Children of Adam, We have sent down clothing to you", meaning by sending it down to them that He created it for them and provided it for them; "clothing" is what they wear of garments; "to cover your nakedness" means to veil your private parts from eyes; and "saw'at" is an euphemism for "awrat". Its singular is "saw'a", a "fa'la" from "su'" (harm), so called because the exposure of it from his body harms its owner, as the poet said in a line the note says it could not attribute, about people who tore the collar of their young woman, not caring for her sanctity; the note gives its transmission and source. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 361}

The commentators said the like of what he said: he gives (14418) and (14419) from Mujahid that some of the Arabs used to circle the House naked, none of them wearing a garment he had circled in; (14420) from him that four verses were revealed about Quraysh, who in the Jahiliyya would not circle the House except naked; (14421) from Ma'bad al-Juhani that the clothing is what you wear; (14422) from Mujahid that Quraysh used to circle naked, none wearing a garment he had circled in, and that some of the Arabs circled the House naked; (14423) from Ma'bad al-Juhani that the clothing that covers your nakedness is these clothes of yours; (14424) from al-Suddi that it is garments; (14425) from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr that clothing is garments; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 362}

and (14426) from al-Dahhak that what is meant is the garments a man wears. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 363}

Then, on "and finery", he says the readers differ: most of the readers of the lands read "rishan" without alif, and it is reported of Zirr ibn Hubaysh and al-Hasan al-Basri that they read it "riyashan", and he gives (14427) from Asim that Zirr ibn Hubaysh read it "riyashan". Al-Tabari says the right reading is that of those who read "rishan" without alif, for the consensus of the readers, though a report is given from the Prophet, with a doubtful chain, that he read "riyashan", and the note refers to report 14446. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 363}

Whoever reads "riyashan" may have meant the plural of "rish", as "dhi'b" has plural "dhi'ab" and "bi'r" "bi'ar", or may have meant a verbal noun from the saying "God clothed him with finery", as one says "he wore it, wearing"; and someone cited a line of Humayd ibn Thawr with kasra on the lam of "libs", whose setting in the description of a howdah decked out by girls the note explains, with its words. "Riyash" in Arab speech is furnishings and the visible garments of goods, what is worn or stuffed, bedding or cover, while "rish" is goods and property in their usage; they sometimes use it of garments and clothing apart from other property, saying "he gave him a saddle with its rish" and "a camel-saddle with its rish", meaning its covering and gear, and "he has fine rish of clothes"; "riyash" is also used of plenty and ease of life. The commentators said the like of what he said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 364}

Then he gives the views on "riyash". Of those who said it is wealth: (14428) from Ibn Abbas that "finery" means wealth; (14429) and (14430) from Mujahid that it is wealth; (14431) from al-Suddi that "riyash" is the riyash of wealth; (14432) from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr that riyash is wealth; and (14433) from al-Dahhak that it means wealth. The note says that where the reading "riyash" appears in these reports it has been left as it is rather than changed to the reading adopted here. Of those who said it is clothing and ease of life: (14434) from Ibn Abbas that riyash is clothing, livelihood and comfort; and (14435) and (14436) from Ma'bad al-Juhani that riyash is livelihood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 365}

Others said "rish" is beauty, and (14437) is given from Ibn Zayd. Then, on "and the clothing of piety, that is best", he says the commentators differ. Some said "the clothing of piety" is faith: (14438) from Qatada, (14439) from al-Suddi and (14440) from Ibn Jurayj. Others said it is modesty: (14441) from Ma'bad al-Juhani that it is what God mentioned in the Qur'an, modesty, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 366}

and (14442) and (14443) from him the like. Others said it is righteous action: (14444) from Ibn Abbas that "the clothing of piety" is righteous action. Others said rather it is good bearing: (14445) from Ibn Abbas that it is good bearing in the face, and the note records that a narrator's name in the chain is unclear; and (14446) from al-Hasan that he saw Uthman ibn Affan on the Messenger of God's pulpit in a Quhi shirt with the collar button undone, and heard him order the killing of dogs and forbid playing with pigeons, and then say {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 367}

O people, fear God in these secret things, for I heard the Messenger of God say: by Him in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, no one ever does a deed in secret but God clothes him with a public cloak of it, good if good and evil if evil; then he recited this verse, reading "riyashan" and not "rishan", "and the clothing of piety, that is best; that is among God's signs", and said it is good bearing. The note says Sulayman ibn Arqam in the chain is very weak and abandoned in hadith, that Ibn Kathir gave it and weakened it, and that al-Shafi'i, Ahmad and al-Bukhari in al-Adab al-Mufrad gave by sound chains from al-Hasan that he heard Uthman order the killing of dogs and the slaughter of pigeons on the pulpit on Friday. Others said it is fear of God: (14447) from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr that "the clothing of piety" is fear of God. Others said that "the clothing of piety" in these places is covering the nakedness: (14448) from Ibn Zayd that he fears God and so covers his nakedness, that is the clothing of piety. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 368}

The readers differ on this. Most readers of Mecca, Kufa and Basra read "wa libasu al-taqwa dhalika khayr" with "libas" in the nominative; most readers of Medina read "wa libasa al-taqwa" with "libas" accusative, which is also the reading of some Kufan readers. Whoever put "libas" in the accusative did so as a coordination on "rish", the sense being: We have sent down to you clothing that covers your nakedness and finery, and We have sent down the clothing of piety. As for the nominative, the Arabists differ over what raises "libas": some Basran grammarians said it is raised as a subject with its predicate in "that is best", and other Arabists faulted him, saying this is a mistake because nothing in the sentence returns to "libas"; some Kufan grammarians said "libas" is raised by "the clothing of piety is best", making "that" its qualifier, which is al-Farra's view. Al-Tabari says this is the sounder view to him on what raises "libas", because there is no basis for the nominative except that it be raised by "khayr", and when "khayr" is raised there is no basis except to make "libas" a qualifier {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 369}

That being so, the meaning if "the clothing of piety" is in the nominative is: the clothing of piety, that which you have known, is better for you, children of Adam, than the clothing of garments that cover your nakedness and than the finery We sent down to you, so wear it so. As for the meaning of those who read it in the accusative: children of Adam, We have sent down to you clothing that covers your nakedness, and finery, and the clothing of piety; this that We sent down to you, of clothing that covers your nakedness and finery and the clothing of piety, is better for you than nakedness and stripping off garments in your circumambulation of the House; so fear God and wear the finery God has provided you, and do not obey Satan by stripping and going bare, for that is his mockery and deception of you, as he did with your parents Adam and Eve, deceiving them until he stripped them of the clothing of God with which He had clothed them, through their obeying him in eating of what God had forbidden them of the fruit of the tree whose eating they disobeyed in. Al-Tabari says this reading, the accusative of "the clothing of piety", is the sounder of the two to him, because its meaning is sound as he showed, and because God began the report of sending down the clothing that covers our nakedness and the finery as a rebuke to the idolaters who stripped during their circumambulation, commanding them to take their garments and cover themselves with them in every state, with faith in Him and following obedience to Him, and teaching them that all that is better than everything they persisted in of disbelief in God and nakedness, not that some of what was sent down to them is better than other parts. What shows the soundness of his view are the verses after this one, from "children of Adam, let not Satan tempt you" to "and that you say about God what you do not know", in all of which He commands the taking of adornment of garments, the use of clothing, the leaving of stripping and nakedness, faith in Him, following His command and acting in obedience, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 370}

and forbids shirk and following Satan's command, confirming in all of it what He summed up in His words "children of Adam, We have sent down to you clothing to cover your nakedness, and finery, and the clothing of piety, that is best". Al-Tabari says the soundest view on "the clothing of piety" is the souls' feeling of God-fearing in desisting from what God forbade of His disobediences and acting on what He commanded of obedience, which gathers faith, righteous action, modesty, fear of God and good bearing, for whoever fears God is a believer in Him, a doer of what he is commanded, fearful of Him, mindful of Him and ashamed to be seen in what He dislikes of His servants; and whoever is so, the traces of good appear on him, his bearing and manner are good and the radiance and light of faith are seen on him. He says "clothing of piety" was meant as the soul's and heart's feeling of this because "clothing" is only the donning of what is worn, the putting on of what is put on, or the covering of the body or part of it with it; whoever dons something so that it or its trace is seen on him is a wearer of it. Hence God made men a garment for women and women a garment for men, as in [[passage/quran-2-187|2:187]], and made the night a garment for His servants, as in [[passage/quran-78-10|78:10]]. The notes correct the printed wording by the manuscript and give a line of Labid on "ijtab". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 371}

He mentions those who interpreted it in the sense he gave when read in the nominative: he gives (14449) from al-Suddi that "the clothing of piety" is faith and "that is best" means better than the finery and the clothing that covers your nakedness; and (14450) from Qatada that the clothing of piety is best, and it is faith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 372}

Then, on "that is among God's signs, so that they may remember", he says: what I mentioned to you that I sent down to you, people, of clothing and finery, is among God's proofs and evidences by which those who disbelieve may know the soundness of God's oneness and the error of the misguidance they persist in; "so that they may remember" means I made that for them a proof of what I described, that they may remember, take heed and turn to the truth and leave falsehood, a mercy from Me to My servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 372}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
