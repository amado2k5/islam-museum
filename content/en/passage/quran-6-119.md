## Summary
Verse 119 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 119 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "why should you not eat of that over which the name of God has been mentioned, when He has detailed for you what He has forbidden you, except what you are compelled to", al-Tabari says the scholars of the Arabic language differed over "wa ma lakum alla ta'kulu". Some Basran grammarians said it means "what is there for you in not eating?", like "what is the matter with us that we do not fight" (2:246), meaning what is there in it for us to leave fighting; he said that if "la" were redundant the verb would not be transitive, and if it had the sense of "what is with us and such", it would be "wa ma lana wa an la nuqatil". Another said that "la" entered for prevention, because "ma laka" and "ma mana'aka" (what prevented you) are the same, so "ma mana'aka la taf'al" and "ma laka la taf'al" mean the same, and in this place either "la" or "an" may be used, as in "God makes clear to you that you not go astray" and "an la tadillu" (4:176), meaning He keeps you from error by the explanation (the footnote says al-Tabari treated this fully earlier, as did al-Farra'). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 68}

Al-Tabari gives his choice: the better of the two sayings in my view is that of those who said that the meaning of "wa ma lakum" here is: what prevents you from eating of that over which the name of God has been mentioned. For God had earlier told the believers that what God's name was mentioned over is lawful and that it is permitted to eat what was slaughtered by His religion or the religion of those who adhere to some laws of His known books, and that what was dedicated to other than Him of animals is forbidden, and had warned them off listening to what the devils inspire to one another of adorned speech about carrion, the strangled, the fallen, and the other foods God forbade. Then He said: what prevents you from eating what was slaughtered by My religion that I approved, when I have set out for you in detail the lawful from the unlawful in what you eat, and made it clear to you by My saying "forbidden to you are carrion, blood, the flesh of swine, and what was dedicated to other than God" to "whoever is compelled by hunger, not inclining to sin" (5:3), so you are not confused over its unlawful and its lawful, such that you would hold back from eating the lawful for fear of falling into the unlawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 68}

If that is the meaning, there is no basis for the interpretation "what is there for you in not eating", because that is said to one who held back from eating it hoping for a reward for holding back, and that would be of someone who believed in the holding back and held back in obedience to God's command and submission to His ruling; and we know of none of the predecessors of this community who held back from eating what God made lawful of slaughtered animals hoping for God's reward for leaving it and believing God had forbidden it to him. So it is clear that the better of the two interpretations is what we have said. He has shown earlier that "fassala", "fassalna" and "fussila" mean made clear (the editor refers to p. 60). Report 13791, from Qatada: He has made clear to you what He forbade you; 13792, from Ibn Zayd: the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 69}
