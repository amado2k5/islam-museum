## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 36 to 40 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "So We subjected the wind to his command with ease, wherever it struck": We answered his prayer and granted him an angel that no one after him should have. "So We subjected the wind to him" instead of the horses that distracted him from prayer, "flowing gently by his command," meaning a gentle softness, which is one of the softness. Al-Hasan also narrated that when the Prophet Solomon was presented with horses, he was distracted from the Asr prayer "until it was covered with a veil." He became angry with God, so he ordered it to be cut off, so God replaced it sooner than it did. He subjected the wind to blow by his command wherever he wished. He would leave Elijah, travel to Qazvin, then leave Qazvin and spend the night in Kabul. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Hasan, and Ibn Zayd.
