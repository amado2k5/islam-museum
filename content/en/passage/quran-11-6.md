**Commentary on verse 6 (pp. 240-243)**

On [[passage/quran-11-6|11:6]] al-Tabari says: 'there is no creature moving on the earth but its provision is upon God' means no creature crawls on the earth; a 'dabba' is the active form from 'dabba, yadibbu'; 'its provision is upon God' means the provision that reaches it is from God, who is its guarantor, its food, its nourishment and what it lives by. Report 17959, from Mujahid: whatever provision comes to it is from God, and sometimes He does not provide for it until it dies of hunger, but whatever provision there is, is from God; report 17960, from Ibn Abbas: every creature; report 17961, from al-Dahhak: every creature, and people are among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 240}

One of the people of knowledge of Arabic speech from Basra, who is Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an, claimed that every animal or property is a 'dabba' and that the sense is 'no creature on the earth', with 'min' redundant. 'He knows its resting place' means where it settles, which is the shelter it takes by night or day, 'and its repository' the place where it is entrusted, either by its dying in it or by burial. Reports 17962, 17963 and 17964, from Ibn Abbas: the resting place is where it shelters and the repository where it dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 241}

Others said: its resting place is the womb and its repository the loins. Report 17965, from Mujahid, report 17966, from Ibn Abbas: the resting place is what is in the womb and the repository what is in the loins, and report 17967, from al-Dahhak, likewise. Others said: the resting place is the womb and the repository where it dies; reports 17968 and 17969, from Abd Allah b. Mas'ud: the resting places are the wombs and the repository the earth in which it dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 242}

Others said: its resting place is the days of its life and its repository where it dies and from where it is raised; report 17970, from al-Rabi' b. Anas. Al-Tabari says he chose the view he chose because God reported that whatever provision creatures receive is from Him, so it is fitting to follow that with His knowing their abode and resting place, rather than a report of His knowing what the loins and wombs contain. By 'all in a clear Book' he means that it states the number of every creature, the amount of its provision, the measure of its stay in its resting place and the length of its milk in its repository, all of it written and fixed in a book with God, clear to whoever reads it that it was fixed and written before He created and brought it into being. This is God's word to those who bent their breasts to hide from Him that He knew all things and set them in a book before creating them; so how could He miss what their souls conceal when they bend their breasts over it and wrap their garments? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 243}

We stop here at the foot of p. 243, where the commentary on verse 6 closes; the next batch begins at p. 244, with verse 7. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 243}
