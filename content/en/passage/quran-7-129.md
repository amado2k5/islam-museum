**Commentary on verse 129 (pp. 43-45)**

Al-Tabari says: Moses's people said to Moses, when he told them to seek God's help and be patient: "We were harmed" by the killing of our sons "before you came to us", that is, before you came with God's message to us, since Pharaoh killed their male children when the time of Moses drew near; "and after you came", meaning after you came with God's message, because when Pharaoh's sorcerers were beaten and he said to his chiefs what he said, he wished to renew the torment by killing their sons and sparing their women. It is also said that Moses's people said this when they feared Pharaoh would overtake them as they fled and the two hosts came in sight of each other: they said, they used to slaughter our sons and spare our women, and after you came, today Pharaoh will overtake us and kill us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 43}

Report 14973, from Mujahid through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "before you came to us" means before God sent you and after it; a second report the same follows through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl (numbered 14973 bis). Report 14974, from al-Suddi through Musa, Amr and Asbat: when the two hosts came in sight and the Israelites saw Pharaoh close behind them (the editor glosses "close behind" as following), they said "we shall be overtaken", and said they had been harmed before and after Moses came; today Pharaoh will overtake and kill us. The editor says this is part of a long report that al-Tabari distributed through his commentary, and gave in his History (vol. 1, p. 214). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 44}

Report 14975, from Ibn Abbas through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim, Sufyan, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: Moses marched with the Children of Israel until they came upon the sea; looking back, they saw the dust of Pharaoh's mounts and said, we were harmed before you came to us and after: here is the sea in front of us and here is Pharaoh with his men! He said: "It may be that your Lord will destroy your enemy and make you successors in the land, then see how you act." {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 44}

On "perhaps your Lord will destroy your enemy" he says: Moses told his people that perhaps their Lord would destroy their enemy Pharaoh and his people, "and make you successors", meaning make you dwell in their land after their destruction, fearing neither them nor anyone else; "and see how you act" means your Lord will see what you do after them, whether you hasten to obey Him or are slow to. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "perhaps", to Abu Ubayda's "Majaz al-Qur'an", and to "destroying", "succession" and "reminder". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 45}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
