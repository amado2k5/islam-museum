**Commentary on verse 4 (pp. 516-517)**

On "We sent no messenger except in the language of his people, to make clear to them; then God leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills, and He is the Almighty, the Wise", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: We sent to no nation of the nations, Muhammad, before you and before your people a messenger except in the tongue and language of the nation to which We sent him, "to make clear to them", that is, to make them understand what God sent him to them with of His command and prohibition, so as to establish God's proof against them; then enabling and abandoning are in God's hand: He abandons from accepting what His messenger brought from Him whom He wills of them and enables to accept it whom He wills; for that reason "fa-yudillu" is in the nominative, because a new start is meant, not a conjunction with what precedes, as it was said "to make clear to you; and We settle in the wombs what We will" [[passage/quran-22-5|22:5]]; "and He is the Almighty", who is not prevented from what He wills of straying or {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 516}

guidance for whom He wills it, "the Wise" in His enabling to faith whom He enables to it, in His guiding to it whom He guides, in His leading astray from it whom He leads astray, and in the rest of His ordering. The interpreters said the same: report 20560, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "We sent no messenger except in the language of his people", that is, in the language of his people, whatever it was; God, mighty and exalted, said "to make clear to them" that to which he was sent, so as to take the proof by that; God, mighty and exalted, said "then God leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills, and He is the Almighty, the Wise". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 517}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
