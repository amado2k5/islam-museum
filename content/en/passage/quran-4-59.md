## Summary
Verse 59 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 59 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The command to obey God and the Messenger (p. 495, report 9851, note 3).** Al-Tabari says the address is to the believers to obey their Lord in what He commanded and forbade, and to obey His messenger Muhammad, since obeying him is obeying their Lord, for they obey him on God's command to obey him. Ibn Humayd (9851, from Jarir, from al-A'mash, from Abu Salih) reports from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said: whoever obeys me has obeyed God, whoever obeys my commander has obeyed me, whoever disobeys me has disobeyed God, and whoever disobeys my commander has disobeyed me. The editor notes that Ahmad gave it in the Musnad many times by different routes, that the two Sahih compilers and others gave it, and that Ibn Kathir calls it agreed upon as sound, and it is as he says. Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over the sense of the command to obey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 495}

**The view: following his sunna (p. 496, reports 9852-9854).** Al-Tabari notes the interpreters differed over the command to obey; some said it is God's command to follow the Messenger's practice. Al-Muthanna (9852, from Amr, from Hushaym, from Abd al-Malik) reports from Ata that obeying the Messenger means following his sunna; al-Muthanna (9853, from Ishaq, from Ya'la b. Ubayd, from Abd al-Malik) from Ata that it means following the Book and the sunna; and al-Muthanna (9854, from Suwayd, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Abd al-Malik) from Ata likewise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 496}

**The view: obedience during his lifetime (p. 496, report 9855).** Others said it is a command to obey the Messenger in his lifetime. Yunus (9855, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that it means obeying him if he is alive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 496}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 496, notes 1-2).** Abu Ja'far says the sound view in his judgement is that it commands obeying the Messenger in his lifetime in what he commanded and forbade, and after his death by following his sunna, because God made the command to obey him general and did not confine it to one condition; so it stays general unless something that must be accepted narrows it. He then says the interpreters differed over who "those holding command" are, whom God told His servants to obey. In two notes the editor records differences between the printed text and the manuscript in two phrases, and says he kept the manuscript's reading in the second. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 496}

**The view: the commanders (p. 497, reports 9856-9858, notes 1-3).** Some said they are the commanders. Abu al-Sa'ib Salm b. Junada (9856, from Abu Mu'awiya, from al-A'mash, from Abu Salih) reports from Abu Hurayra that they are the commanders. al-Hasan b. al-Sabbah al-Bazzar (9857, from Hajjaj b. Muhammad, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ya'la b. Muslim, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) reports from Ibn Abbas that the verse came down about a man the Prophet sent out with a raiding party; al-Qasim (9858, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah b. Muslim b. Hurmuz, from Sa'id) from Ibn Abbas that it came down about Abdullah b. Hudhafa b. Qays al-Sahmi when the Prophet sent him with the party. In his notes the editor says the report from Abu Hurayra stops at him and its chain is sound in his view; that Ya'la b. Muslim is trustworthy, one whom the two Shaykhs used and Ibn Ma'in and Abu Zur'a declared reliable; that Ahmad gave the report in the Musnad, naming the man, and al-Bukhari gave it too; that Ibn Kathir cites al-Bukhari and says the rest of the compilers except Ibn Majah gave it through Hajjaj b. Muhammad, and that al-Tirmidhi called it good but strange, known only through Ibn Jurayj; and that the story of Abdullah b. Hudhafa is in Ahmad from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and from Ali. He adds that Abdullah b. Muslim is Ya'la's brother, and in a note running onto the next page that this Abdullah has some weakness although al-Thawri narrates from him, the weakness seeming to lie in his memory, and that both manuscript and print wrongly give "Ubayd Allah"; the report agrees in sense with the one before. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 497}

**The view: leaders of raiding parties and the sultans (p. 498, reports 9859-9860, notes 1-2).** Ibn Humayd (9859, from Hukkam, from Anbasa, from Layth) reports that Maslama asked Maymun b. Mihran about the verse and he said they are the heads of raiding parties in the Prophet's time. Yunus (9860, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd, from his father, that they are the sultans, and that the Prophet said of obedience that it carries trial; his father added that had God willed He would have put the command in the prophets, meaning He had placed it with them while they were present, and he pointed to their judgement in the killing of Yahya b. Zakariyya. The editor says he kept the manuscript's wording in one place and supplied the word "the command" in brackets, thinking it had dropped out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 498}

**The view: the story of Khalid and Ammar (pp. 498-499, report 9861, notes 1-4).** Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9861, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the Prophet sent out a party led by Khalid b. al-Walid with Ammar b. Yasir in it. They travelled toward the people they sought and, near them, halted at the end of the night; a spy came and warned the people, who were gone by morning save one man, who told his household to gather their goods and walked through the dark to Khalid's camp, asked for Ammar, told him he had become Muslim and that his people had fled when they heard of the force, and asked whether his Islam would help him or he should flee. Ammar said it would help him, so he stayed. At dawn Khalid raided and found only that man, took him and his goods. When Ammar heard, he told Khalid to release him since he was under Ammar's protection; they quarrelled and went to the Prophet, who confirmed Ammar's pledge but forbade him to give protection a second time against a commander. They quarrelled again before him, and Khalid asked whether he would let this slave insult him; the Prophet told Khalid not to revile Ammar and said of whoever reviled, hated or cursed him that God would do the same to him. Ammar angrily rose; Khalid followed, took hold of his garment, apologised, and Ammar was reconciled. Al-Suddi says God then sent down the verse. The editor explains that "tae'ris" is halting at night's end for rest before travelling at dawn, that "the two-eyed one" means a spy, that he chose between the manuscript and the print at two points and says why, and that Ibn Kathir gives the report and says Ibn Abi Hatim related it through two routes from al-Suddi as a mursal, and Ibn Mardawayh through al-Hakam b. Zuhayr from al-Suddi, from Abu Salih, from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 499}

**The view: the people of knowledge and understanding (pp. 499-501, reports 9862-9873, notes).** Others said they are the people of knowledge and fiqh. Report 9862 opens in the printed text with an incomplete chain going back through Sufyan b. Waki, his father, Ali b. Salih and Abdullah b. Muhammad b. Aqil to Jabir b. Abdullah, with dots where the text is missing. The editor holds that the chain of 9863 was confused with it, that "Jabir b. Nuh" in the place of 9862 is a plain error, and that 9863 most likely began with Abu Kurayb, who narrates from Jabir b. Nuh as seen repeatedly (nearest 9842); he left the text as it stood and put dots for what dropped. The reports then run: Jabir b. Nuh (9863, from al-A'mash) from Mujahid, those with understanding; Abu Kurayb (9864, from Ibn Idris, from Layth) from Mujahid, those of fiqh and knowledge; Muhammad b. Amr (9865, from Abu Asim, from Isa) from Ibn Abi Najih, those of understanding in religion and intellect; al-Muthanna (9866, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, likewise; al-Muthanna (9867, from Abdullah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas, the people of fiqh and religion; Ahmad b. Hazim (9868, from Abu Nu'aym, from Sufyan, from Husayn) from Mujahid, the people of knowledge; Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9869, from Hushaym, from Abd al-Malik) from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, those of knowledge and fiqh; al-Muthanna (9870, from Amr b. Awn, from Hushaym, from Abd al-Malik) from Ata, the jurists and scholars; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9871, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from al-Hasan, the scholars; Abd al-Razzaq (9872, from al-Thawri, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid, the people of fiqh and knowledge; and al-Muthanna (9873, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) from Abu al-Aliya, the people of knowledge, who points to another passage of the sura, on bringing a piece of news about security or fear before the men of judgement, as showing that those who work out its sense are the people of knowledge ([[passage/quran-4-83|4:83]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 501}

**The view: the Companions of Muhammad (p. 501, report 9874, notes 1-2).** Others said they are Muhammad's Companions. Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9874, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that he used to say the Companions of Muhammad, and sometimes those of intellect, understanding and God's religion. The editor says the print has a different wording from the manuscript and he kept the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 501}

**The view: Abu Bakr and Umar (pp. 501-502, report 9875, notes 1-2).** Others said they are Abu Bakr and Umar. Ahmad b. Amr al-Basri (9875, from Hafs b. Umar al-Adani, from al-Hakam b. Aban) reports from Ikrima that they are Abu Bakr and Umar. The editor says he did not find Ahmad b. Amr al-Basri in the biographical works and supposes him to be Ahmad b. Amr b. Abd al-Khaliq al-Bazzar, author of the Musnad, whom al-Khatib calls trustworthy and a memoriser and who died in al-Ramla in 291, so that al-Tabari may have met him in Baghdad or al-Ramla; that Hafs b. Umar al-Adani was treated at 6796; and that the word al-Tabari adds in brackets in the next sentence is, in his view, an indispensable addition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 502}

**Al-Tabari's choice: the commanders and rulers (p. 502, report 9876, note 3).** Abu Ja'far says the soundest view in his judgement is of those who said they are the commanders and rulers, because of the sound reports from the Prophet commanding obedience to leaders and rulers in what is obedience to God and a benefit to the Muslims. An example is what Ali b. Muslim al-Tusi (9876, from Ibn Abi Fudayk, from Abdullah b. Muhammad b. Urwa, from Hisham b. Urwa, from Abu Salih al-Samman) reports from Abu Hurayra, that the Prophet said rulers would come after him, the righteous ruling by his righteousness and the wicked by his wickedness, and that one should hear and obey in all that accords with the truth and pray behind them: if they do well it is to their credit and the people's, and if ill the people have the benefit and they the burden. The editor notes that Ibn Abi Fudayk is Muhammad b. Isma'il, a well-known trustworthy narrator and teacher of al-Shafi'i and Ahmad; that Abdullah b. Muhammad b. Urwa is called abandoned and very weak by Abu Hatim, and by Ibn Hibban one who narrates fabrications from the trustworthy; so he judges the report very weak, found only in this place, and that Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti transmit it without ascribing it to anyone but al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 502}

**Two reports on the limits of obedience (p. 503, reports 9877-9878, note 1).** Ibn al-Muthanna (9877, from Yahya, from Ubayd Allah, from Nafi) reports from Abdullah b. Umar, from the Prophet, that a Muslim must hear and obey in what he likes and dislikes unless ordered to disobedience, and that if ordered to disobey there is no obedience; and Ibn al-Muthanna (9878, from Khalid, from Ubayd Allah, from Nafi, from Ibn Umar) similarly. The editor notes that Yahya is al-Qattan, Khalid is Ibn al-Harith al-Hujaymi and Ubayd Allah is Ibn Umar al-Umari; that the print errs in two names in the chains and the manuscript is right; that Ahmad gave it in the Musnad, and Ibn Kathir cites it from Abu Dawud and then ascribes it to the two Shaykhs; and that he faults al-Suyuti for ascribing it only to Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn Jarir when it is in the Musnad and the two Sahihs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 503}

**Al-Tabari's argument continues (p. 503).** Al-Tabari says it is known that no obedience is obligatory to anyone but God, His messenger or a just leader, and God has commanded obeying "the people of our affairs"; so those whose obedience He commanded are the leaders and those the Muslims have appointed, not other people, though accepting from anyone a command to leave disobedience to God and a call to obey Him is a duty. No obedience is owed to anyone in what he commands or forbids where no proof of its being obligatory stands, except to the leaders whom God bound His servants to obey in what they order their subjects for the general good, and likewise in all that is not disobedience to God. In note 2 the editor says he kept the manuscript's phrase "and whom the leaders appointed over the Muslims' affairs" and that Abu Ja'far did not mean what the print says. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 503}

**The close of the argument and the rule for dispute (p. 504, report 9879, note 1).** Al-Tabari says that, this being so, the soundness of the interpretation he chose over the others is known. He then explains the clause on disagreement: should you fall into difference, believers, over some matter of your religion, among yourselves or between you and those who govern you, and fall into wrangling, then seek the ruling from God, that is, from His book, and follow what you find; if you find no knowledge of it in God's book, seek it from the Messenger if he is alive, and if he has died, from his sunna; and do this if you accept God and the return in which there is reward and punishment, for if you do you will have God's great reward and if not a painful punishment. Abu Kurayb (9879, from Ibn Idris, from Layth) reports from Mujahid that the sense is that when the scholars dispute they bring the matter back to God's book and His messenger's sunna. The editor refers to an earlier explanation of the word for dispute (the page number is scrambled in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 504}

**Reports on returning the matter to the Book and the sunna (p. 505, reports 9880-9885).** Al-Tabari records that Mujahid supported this by a passage elsewhere in the sura on bringing a report before those able to deduce its meaning ([[passage/quran-4-83|4:83]]). Al-Muthanna (9880, from Suwayd, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan, from Layth) reports from Mujahid that it means God's book and His prophet's sunna; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9881, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from Layth) from Mujahid that returning to God is returning to His book and to the Messenger is to his sunna; Ibn Humayd (9882, from Hukkam, from Anbasa, from Layth) that Maslama asked Maymun b. Mihran, who answered that "God" is His book and "the Messenger" his sunna, and it was as if he had put a stone in the questioner's mouth (that is, silenced him); Ahmad b. Hazim (9883, from Abu Nu'aym, from Ja'far, whose father's name the scan reads as Marwan) from Maymun b. Mihran that the return to God is to His book, and to the Messenger in his lifetime, and once God has taken him, to the sunna; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9884, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada that it means bringing it back to God's book and His messenger's sunna; and Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9885, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi that the return is to the Messenger if he is alive and to God means to His book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 505}

**The better course and the good outcome (pp. 506-507, reports 9886-9890, note 1).** Al-Tabari says what is pointed to is bringing back what you disputed to God and the Messenger; this is the better course for you with God in the return and more beneficial in your worldly life, since it calls to harmony and to leaving dispute and division, and it is a more praiseworthy resort and a finer outcome. He recalls that he explained earlier that ta'wil is a verbal noun of ta'awwala and that "the matter came back to such a thing" means it returned, and he does not repeat it; the editor refers to a passage in volume 6. Muhammad b. Amr (9886, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it means the best recompense; al-Muthanna (9887, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid likewise; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9888, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada, the best reward and a better outcome; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9889, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi, outcome; and Yunus (9890, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd, the best outcome, and that ta'wil means confirmation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 507}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 507}
