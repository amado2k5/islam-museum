**Commentary on verse 107 (pp. 481-485)**

On [[passage/quran-11-107|11:107]], by 'abiding in it' He means staying in it, and by 'as long as the heavens and the earth endure' He means for ever; the Arabs, wishing to describe something as lasting for ever, say this lasts as long as the heavens and the earth, and likewise he stays while night and day alternate, meaning for ever, so God addressed them in what they know among themselves. Ibn Zayd said something like it, report 18572: as long as the earth is earth and the sky sky. Then He said 'except as your Lord wills', and the people of knowledge and interpretation differ on its sense: some said this is an exception God made for the people of God's oneness, that He will bring them out of the Fire when He wills, after having entered them into it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 481}

Report 18573, from Qatada: God knows best His exception, and it was told us that some people will be touched by a scorch of the Fire for sins they committed and then He will enter them into Paradise; 18574, from Qatada, the same, adding that they are called the Jahannamiyyun; 18575, from Qatada: Anas b. Malik told us that the Prophet said a people will come out of the Fire, and Qatada said we do not say what the people of Harura say, the editor explaining that they are the Kharijites, who say the committer of a grave sin abides in the Fire; 18576, from Abu Sinan: an exception for the people of God's oneness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 482}

Report 18577, from al-Dahhak: a people come out of the Fire and enter Paradise, and they are those He excepted; 18578, from Khalid b. Ma'dan: both verses concern the people of God's oneness, this one and 'dwelling in it for ages' in [[passage/quran-78-23|78:23]]. Others said the exception is for the people of God's oneness but that 'except as your Lord wills' means unless your Lord wills to pass over them and not enter them into the Fire, directing the exception to 'those who are wretched are in the Fire', not to the abiding; report 18579, from Abu Nadra, from Jabir or Abu Sa'id or a man of the Companions: this verse comes over the whole Qur'an, wherever 'abiding in it' occurs it comes upon it; and I heard Abu Mijlaz say it is his recompense, and if God wills He passes over his punishment. Others said it means the people of the Fire and all who enter it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 483}

Report 18580, from Ibn Abbas: 'abiding in it as long as the heavens and the earth endure' means they do not die and do not leave it, 'except as your Lord wills' is God's exception, He commanding the Fire to consume them; and Ibn Mas'ud said a time will come on Hell when its doors flap with no one in it, after they have stayed in it for ages; 18581, from al-Sha'bi: Hell is the quicker of the two abodes to fill and the quicker to be emptied. Others said God told us of His will for the people of Paradise and showed the sense of His exception with 'a gift never cut off', that it is an addition to the length of the heavens and earth, but He did not tell us His will for the people of the Fire, and it may be an addition or a reduction; 18582, from Ibn Zayd, to that effect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 484}

Al-Tabari says: the soundest of these views is the one we reported from Qatada and al-Dahhak, that it is an exception regarding the people of God's oneness among those who committed grave sins, that He enters them into the Fire to abide for ever, except for what He wills of leaving them there for less than that, then brings them out and enters them into Paradise, as we have explained elsewhere. We say this because God threatened those who associate others with Him with abiding in the Fire, as the reports from the Prophet confirm, so it cannot be an exception for the polytheists; and reports from the Prophet are mutually reinforcing that God enters some of the believers into the Fire for sins and then brings them out and enters them into Paradise, so it cannot be an exception for the people of God's oneness before entering it. If we made it so we would be entering into the saying that no sinner enters Paradise and no believer the Fire, which goes against the positions of the scholars and the reports. When those two are ruled out, no view held by the leaders of knowledge remains but the third. The grammarians have another path that we shall mention later. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 485}

As for 'your Lord does what He wills', it means that nothing prevents your Lord, Muhammad, from doing what He wills to those who disobeyed Him and went against His command, in taking vengeance on them, but He does what He wills, His doing and decree taking effect on them and on whom He wills of His creation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 485}
