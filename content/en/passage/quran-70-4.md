## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 4 to 8 as a single unit. He explains the phrase “A questioner asked about a punishment bound to befall”: Abu Ja`far said: The reciters differed in their reading of the phrase “A questioner asked.” The majority of the reciters of Kufa and Basra read it as “A questioner asked,” with the hamza in “asked a questioner,” meaning a questioner from among the disbelievers asked about God’s punishment, which is bound to befall. Some reciters of Medina read it as “A questioner asked,” without the hamza in “asked,” and they justified this by saying it is a verb form of “to flood.” The more correct of the two readings is the one that reads it with the hamza, because the authoritative reciters agree on this, and most of the early scholars of interpretation interpreted it with the hamza. Those who interpreted it similarly said that its interpretation is similar to what we said about it: My father told me, he said: My uncle told me, he said: My father told me, on the authority of his father, on the authority of Ibn Abbas, regarding his statement: “A questioner asked about a punishment bound to befall.” He said: That is the question of the disbelievers about God’s punishment, and it is bound to befall. And in the passage, statements are quoted from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Zayd.
