## Summary
Verse 231 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 231 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Part five of Shakir's edition opens with the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-231|2:231]]). Al-Tabari says that, when men divorce their wives and they reach the end of their term, this means the time He set for them, the passing of the three quru' for those who count by them and the passing of the months for those who count by months; that "keep them" means take them back, if you want to, in a divorce within which return is allowed, namely one or two divorces, as in the verse on divorce being twice ([[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]]). What is right, for him, is what is permitted of return, namely having witnesses to the return before the waiting period ends, not return by intercourse, since that is lawful only after the return, together with companionship and dealing as God commanded. To release them in what is right means to leave them until they complete their waiting period and the rest of their term passes, paying them their full rights of dower, consolation gift, maintenance and the like. Not to keep them to harm them means not to take them back within their waiting period to injure them, to lengthen the time of their waiting or to take part of what they were given when they ask for khul' because of the injury; and to transgress means to wrong them by exceeding the limits God set out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 7} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 8} The editor notes a difference between the manuscript and the printed text in two phrases and attributes it to the copyist's haste. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 7}

The interpreters said the same. From Masruq, he divorces her, and when she is about to complete the term he takes her back, then divorces her and leaves her until she is again about to complete it, takes her back without wanting to keep her; that is the one who harms and takes God's signs in mockery (4909). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 8} From al-Hasan, a man would divorce a woman, take her back, divorce her, take her back, to harm her, and God forbade it (4910). From Mujahid, God forbade harm, which is that a man divorces his wife and takes her back on the last day left of her term, until nine months have gone by, to harm her (4911, 4912). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 8} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 9} From Ibn 'Abbas, a man would divorce his wife and take her back before her term ended, then divorce her; he did this to harm and constrain her, and God sent down this verse (4913). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 9} From al-Rabi', a man would divorce his wife with one divorce, leave her until her term was about to end, then take her back without need of her, only wanting to harm her; God forbade it (4914). From Ibn Shihab, it is not lawful for him to take her back to harm her when he has no desire for her except to harm her (4915). From Qatada, it concerns the man who swears by the divorce of his wife, and when a little of her term remains takes her back to harm her and prolong it for her (4916). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 10} From Thawr ibn Zayd al-Dili, a man divorced his wife and took her back without need of her and without wishing to keep her, in order to lengthen her waiting period to harm her, and the verse was revealed (4917), which the editor traces to the Muwatta'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 10} From al-Dahhak, a man divorces his wife once, takes her back, divorces her, takes her back, to harm her so that she seeks khul' (4918). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 10} From al-Suddi, it was revealed about a man of the Ansar called Thabit ibn Yasar, who divorced his wife and, when two or three days of her term remained, took her back, then divorced her, and did so until nine months had passed, harming her (4920). In a narration from 'Abd al-'Aziz about harmful divorce: he divorces, takes back, divorces, takes back (4921). From 'Atiyya, he divorces her once and leaves her until she has three menses, takes her back, divorces her, holds back from her for three menses, then takes her back; he said, they should not be made to wait long (4922). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 10} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 11} The editor fixes the reading of the name from the manuscript and notes where the report appears in al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 10}

Al-Tabari then says the root of the word for release is the pasture-herd of a people, what is let out of their livestock to graze; one says of animals sent out to graze, this is the people's herd, and from that the verse in al-Nahl on sending them out to pasture ([[passage/quran-16-6|16:6]]); so a woman whom her husband has set free and separated from himself was said to be "released", by way of comparison. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 11} The editor observes that al-Tabari sometimes postponed the explanation of a word for brevity and had not explained it where it first occurred. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 11}

Al-Tabari explains the sentence saying that whoever does this wrongs himself: whoever takes his wife back after divorcing her in a divorce in which he has the right of return, in order to harm her and exceed God's limit in her case, has wronged himself, that is, earned himself sin and made God's punishment due to him; the meaning of wrongdoing was explained earlier, putting a thing in the wrong place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 11} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 12} He then explains the sentence forbidding taking God's signs in mockery: do not take God's landmarks and divisions between His lawful and forbidden, His command and prohibition in His revelation, in jest and play, for He has made clear in His revelation what return you have over your wives in a divorce where you have the right of return and what you do not, as mercy and favour to you, so that some of you may have the way out and relief, by divorce and separation, from something disliked in which there is harm from the other; and not that you should make what He set out in His Book into play and mockery. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 12} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 13} The editor describes the confusion of the sentence in the manuscript and gives his reconstruction of its structure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 12}

The interpreters said the same. From al-Hasan, in the time of the Prophet a man would divorce or free a slave and when asked what he had done say, I was only joking; the Prophet said, whoever divorces in jest or frees in jest, it takes effect on him; al-Hasan said, and the verse was revealed about it (4923). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 13} The editor says the hadith is weak because it is mursal and because of the weakness of its narrator Sulayman ibn Arqam, and that it is reported with a better chain, though still mursal, in Ibn Abi Hatim, cited by Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 13}
