## Summary
Surat at-Tawba: surah 9 in the order of the Mushaf, Medinan, 129 verses, and revelation order 113 among the surahs. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Content
Its verses are recorded in the museum one by one under the records passage/quran-9-‹verse number›.

## In Tantawi's al-Tafsir al-Wasit
In his introduction to the sura in [[work/al-tafsir-al-wasit|al-Tafsir al-Wasit]], [[person/muhammad-sayyid-tantawi|Tantawi]] says that it is ninth in the order of the mushaf and has 129 verses for the Kufans and 130 for most scholars. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2498} Since the Prophet's time it has been known by several names, the best known being "al-Tawba" (Repentance), because it speaks again and again of repentance and those who repent, and "Bara'a" (Disavowal), because it opens with the disavowal of the idolaters; another is "al-Fadiha" (the Exposer), for its long treatment of the hypocrites — al-Bukhari reports from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] said: al-Tawba is the Exposer; it kept coming down "and among them … and among them …" until they thought it would leave none of them unnamed. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2498} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2499} It is also called al-Munaqqira, al-Muthira, al-Mub'athira and al-Mudamdima, and Tantawi says that no sura of the Qur'an has more names than it and al-Fatiha. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2499}

On when it was revealed he quotes [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] that it is among the last revelations, and the author of al-Manar that it is Medinan by agreement, with views exempting certain verses that he rejects. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2499} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2500} He then quotes Sayyid Qutb's *Fi Zilal al-Qur'an* that the sura as a whole came down in year 9 in three stages — before [[event/expedition-to-tabuk|the Tabuk expedition]] in Rajab, during the preparations for it and on the way, and after the return — and that its opening up to verse 28 came down at the end of the year shortly before the pilgrimage season; Tantawi calls this the view "the mind rests on". {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2500} He sees it as defining the believers' relations with idolaters, People of the Book and hypocrites after the conquest of Mecca, and as dealing at length with the events of the Tabuk expedition. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2500}

Explaining its opening, he quotes Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ishaq that the Prophet sent [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] as leader of the pilgrimage that year and then sent [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] after him to convey the opening of Bara'a on his behalf and proclaim to the people at Mina on the day of sacrifice that after that year no idolater would make the pilgrimage and no one would circle the House naked, and that whoever had a treaty kept it until its term. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2515} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2516} Why the sura has no basmala is recorded under [[question/why-no-basmala-in-surat-al-tawba|Why does Surat al-Tawba not open with the basmala?]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2501}

## In al-Shawkani's Fath al-Qadir
In [[work/fath-al-qadir|Fath al-Qadir]], [[person/al-shawkani|al-Shawkani]] says that Surat al-Tawba is Medinan, quoting al-Qurtubi that this is agreed. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.552} He reports from Ibn Abbas that Bara'a came down after the conquest of Mecca and that Surat al-Tawba came down in Medina, with similar reports from Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and Qatada, and from al-Bara' — in al-Bukhari, al-Nasa'i and others — that the last sura revealed complete was Bara'a. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.552} He then gives the scholars' views on why the basmala is missing at its head, and reports that Abu Raja' asked al-Hasan al-Basri whether al-Anfal and Bara'a were two suras or one and was told: two; and from Hudhayfa that people call it Surat al-Tawba "while it is the sura of punishment". {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.552} {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.553}
