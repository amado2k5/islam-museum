## Summary
Verse 61 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/delegation-of-najran|Delegation of Najran]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 61 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 61.** Al-Tabari says the opening means: whoever argues with you, Muhammad, about the Messiah Jesus son of Mary; the pronoun returns to the mention of Jesus, and could also return to the truth mentioned just before. The knowledge meant is what God made clear to him about Jesus, that he is God's servant. The verse invites to a mutual invocation: each side calls its sons, women and selves and they pray for a curse, which falls on the liar among them in the matter of Jesus, namely his claim that he is God's servant and messenger and not God. The editor says the printed text's wording is meaningless and the manuscript's is right, and refers to report 7164 and its words about him being God, and to al-Tabari's explanation of the invitation on pp. 483-485. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 473} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 474}

**The meaning of the mutual invocation and Labid's verse.** Al-Tabari says the word means to invoke a curse, as people say "may God curse him", and cites a line by Labid about a people who perished, in which fate called down destruction on them. The editor cites Labid's Diwan and his elegy for Arbad, al-Zamakhshari's Asas al-Balagha and the Amali of al-Murtada, judges both al-Tabari's and al-Zamakhshari's explanations good, proposes that the word means to give oneself freely to a matter and strive in it, and reads "fate looked at them" as a look of awe at their deeds that then envied their glories, linking it to the Arab name for envy as the eye; this is the editor's own suggestion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 474}

**Reports 7171 to 7175.** Report 7171 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada): whoever argues with you about Jesus after the knowledge that he is God's servant and messenger, from His word and spirit, call them to the mutual invocation. Report 7172 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr): after what I related to you of his account and how he was. Report 7173 (Ammar, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi): whoever argues with you about Jesus after the knowledge that reached you. Report 7174 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd): the curse on the liar, from us and from you. Report 7175 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Lahia, from Sulayman ibn Ziyad al-Hadrami, from Abdullah ibn al-Harith ibn Jaz al-Zubaydi) says he heard the Prophet say: would that there were a veil between me and the people of Najran so that I do not see them nor they me, from how hard they used to dispute with him. The editor calls Sulayman ibn Ziyad a trustworthy Successor whom Ibn Main and others authenticated and Abu Hatim called a sound-hadith shaykh, and Abdullah ibn al-Harith a Companion who settled in Egypt and was the last Companion to die there; he fixes the spelling of Jaz and notes that the report is in Ibn Abd al-Hakam's Futuh Misr and that al-Suyuti attributes it to Ibn Jarir alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 474} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 475}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
