**Commentary on verse 61 (pp. 114-118)**

On [[passage/quran-10-61|10:61]] he says to His Prophet: "you are not in any state", Muhammad, meaning in any work, "nor do you recite any of the Qur'an", nor read any of God's Book, "nor do you do any deed", people, of good or evil, "but We are witnesses over you", that is, We witness your works and your affairs when you do them and press on in them. This is related from Ibn Abbas and others, and report 17696, from Ibn Abbas, glosses "when you plunge into it" as "when you do". The editor refers to the glosses on "reciting" and "plunging". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 114}

Others said it means when you spread falsehood about the Qur'an (report 17697, from al-Dahhak; the editor notes that the printed text misread the manuscript at the start of the phrase). Others said it means when you plunge into the truth: reports 17698, 17699 and 17700, from Mujahid: into the truth, whatever it was. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 115}

Al-Tabari says he chose his view because God reported that His servants do no deed without His witnessing it, and then joined that to "when you plunge into it"; so it was clear that this is a statement about the time of the doers' work, that He is witness to it, and not about the time of the Prophet's reciting the Qur'an, for if it were about His witnessing when the people were engrossed in the Qur'an, the reading with ya would be a report about the deniers. If someone says it is not a report about the deniers but an address to the Prophet, that He witnesses him when he recites, then it would have been "when you plunge" in the singular, because the Prophet is one, as in "nor do you recite" where he is addressed alone; rather it begins by addressing him in the singular and then turns to the plural, like the form at [[passage/quran-65-1|65:1]], where "when you divorce women" is a clear sign of turning the address to the Muslims together with the Prophet and others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 115}

It is also a report that none of His servants does any deed without His being its witness, counting and knowing it, as He says "nothing escapes your Lord", Muhammad, of His creation's deed, and no knowledge of anything is lost to Him wherever it is, on earth or in heaven. The root is from a man's being away from his family with his herd; one says the verb with damma or kasra, two eloquent usages each read by a group of readers, and whichever a reader chooses he is right, since the meanings agree and both are common in Arab speech, though he leans to the damma as more common among the famous readers. "Of an atom's weight" means the weight of a tiny ant; the Arabs are reported to say "take this, it is lighter in weight than that". The editor refers to the gloss on "weight" and Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 116}

The atom is the singular of the small ants. It is a report that nothing smaller escapes Him, however light in weight, nor its measure and sum, nor anything larger however heavy, nor how much it is. So God says to His creation: let your deeds, people, be what pleases your Lord, for We are witnesses to your works and none is hidden from Us, and We count them and repay you for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 117}

The readers differed on "nor smaller than that nor larger". Most read both with fatha on the ra, meaning the genitive, joining the smaller to the atom and the larger to the smaller, with a fatha because the two are diptotes. Some Kufans read them with damma, joined to the sense of "weight", because if "of" were dropped the weight would be in the nominative, like the two readings at [[passage/quran-35-3|35:3]]. Al-Tabari says the better of the two readings is that with fatha, as genitive joined to the atom, because it is the reading of the readers of the main cities and the general body of readers and is sounder in Arabic, though the other has a recognised aspect. The editor notes that al-Tabari did not mention the damma reading in its place in the commentary on Surat Fatir, which he takes as another sign that al-Tabari abridged his commentary in places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 117}

"Except in a clear book" means all that is only in a book with God that makes plain the reality of God's report to anyone who looks in it, that nothing was or will be but He has counted it there, and that no knowledge of any of His creation is lost to Him wherever it is in His heaven and earth. Reports 17701 and 17702, from Ibn Abbas: "nothing escapes" means it is not absent from Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 118}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
