## Summary
Verse 44 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 44 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**What the pointing word covers.** Al-Tabari says God means by the pointing word the accounts He gave His servants about the wife of Imran, her daughter Mary, Zakariyya and his son Yahya, and the rest of what is told in the verses that begin at [[passage/quran-3-33|3:33]]; He gathered them all under that word and called them accounts of the unseen, that is, the hidden affairs of the people which Muhammad and his people had not seen and which only a few rabbis and monks of the two books knew. God then told His Prophet He had revealed this to him, as proof of his prophethood, a confirmation of his truthfulness and a cutting off of the excuse of those among the unbelieving People of the Two Books who deny his message and who know that he reached these hidden accounts, though they were hidden or at best diffused among their holders, only because God told him, since they knew he was unlettered, did not write so as to read books, and had not accompanied the People of the Books so as to take their knowledge. He adds that the unseen is a verbal noun from the saying that a man was absent from something. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 404} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 405}

**The sense of revealing.** Al-Tabari says the verb here means that God sends it down to him, and that the root sense of revealing is the one who reveals casting something to the one to whom it is revealed, which can be by writing, sign and gesture, inspiration or a message. He cites the bee [[passage/quran-16-68|16:68]] (He cast it to her and inspired her), the disciples [[passage/quran-5-111|5:111]] (He cast the knowledge to them as inspiration), a line of a rajaz poet for a command cast to the earth, and the account of Zakariyya [[passage/quran-19-11|19:11]] (he cast it to them by gesture). The editor says the poet is al-Ajjaj and that the line comes from a poem praising God, and that the printed text and manuscript add a word 'also' in one place, a plain error. Al-Tabari then adds the devils' revealing to their friends as whispering [[passage/quran-6-121|6:121]] and the Qur'an revealed to the Prophet through Gabriel's coming from God [[passage/quran-6-19|6:19]]. He says the noun for revelation means what falls from the one who reveals onto the one to whom it is revealed, which is why the Arabs call a line or a book by this name, since it settles in what is written; he cites a line of Kab ibn Zuhayr about poems that stayed fixed as a carving in stone, and a line of Ruba that likens a vanished campsite to a finely ornamented book of rabbis. The editor explains the lines, the context of Kab's poem in his answer to Muzarrid, brother of al-Shammakh, and the senses of Ruba's words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 405} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 406}

**The casting of pens.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is that Muhammad was not with those people when they cast the arrows with which they drew lots over the guardianship of Mary, so as to know of their affairs what he had not witnessed, and that he knows them only because God made them known to him; the pens are their arrows, as he explained at [[passage/quran-3-37|3:37]] (the editor refers to earlier pages). Report 7052 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Hisham ibn Amr, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says the one addressed is Muhammad. Report 7053 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says they are Zakariyya and his companions, who drew lots with their pens over Mary when she came to them, as does 7054 through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. Report 7055 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says Mary was the daughter of their imam and master and the Children of Israel vied for her and drew lots with their arrows, and Zakariyya, husband of her sister, won, so he took her in; the editor says the phrase means the husband of the sister of Mary's mother, not of Mary's sister, and that when the report was abridged the mention of the mother dropped out. Report 7056 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says they drew lots and Zakariyya won. Report 7057 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) says that when Mary was placed in the mosque the people of the prayer-place, who were writing revelation, drew lots over her with their pens. Report 7058 (al-Tabari, from al-Husayn, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd, from al-Dahhak) says they cast their pens and Zakariyya won. Report 7059 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says it happened when they drew lots over Mary, a matter unseen by Muhammad until God told him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 409}

**The grammar of the question.** Al-Tabari says the wording implies an omitted clause meaning that they cast so as to see who was worthier of the guardianship. He says that anyone who thinks the interrogative should take the accusative is wrong: looking, making clear and knowing, when followed by that word, require a question and an inquiry, and in an inquiry the word takes the subject position and the verb's action upon it is voided, so a speaker who says he will see who stood means he will ask people who stood; the same holds for the verb for knowing. He adds that he already showed that the sense of guardianship is taking in. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 409}

**The dispute.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that Muhammad was not present with Mary's people when they disputed over which of them had the better claim. He says that although this addresses the Prophet it is a rebuke to those among the People of the Two Books who deny him: how can they doubt him while he tells them these accounts, which he did not witness, without having read their books or sat with their scholars? Report 7060 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says the sense is that he was not with them when they disputed, God thereby telling him what they concealed of the knowledge they hold, to confirm his prophethood and to argue against them; the editor refers to the Sira of Ibn Hisham (2:229). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 410}
