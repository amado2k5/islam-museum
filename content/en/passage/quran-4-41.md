## Summary
Verse 41 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 41 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the verse (pp. 368-369, note 3).** Al-Tabari says: if God does not diminish his servants of anything due to them, how will it be with them when a witness is brought from each community to testify about its deeds and whether it believed or denied its messengers, and Muhammad is brought as witness over his community. Note 3 refers to the earlier discussion of the word for witness (the volume and page numbers are partly smudged in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 369}

**The witness of the prophets and of Muhammad's community (p. 369, reports 9515-9517, note 1).** Muhammad b. al-Husayn related from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi (9515) that on the day of resurrection the prophets come, each with those of his people who believed with him, one, two, ten, fewer or more, until the people of Lut are brought and only his two daughters believed with him. They are asked whether they conveyed what they were sent with, and say yes; asked who testifies, they say the community of Muhammad. That community is told to testify, since the messengers deposited a testimony with it (note 1 says the printed edition words the start of this statement differently and the editor kept the manuscript), and it testifies that they conveyed, as it testified in the world; asked who testifies to that, they say Muhammad, who is called and testifies that his community believed and the messengers conveyed. Al-Suddi said this is the meaning of [[passage/quran-2-143|2:143]]. Al-Qasim related from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj (9516) that each community's witness is its messenger, who testifies that he conveyed what God sent him with, and that when the Prophet reached this point his eyes overflowed. Ibn Humayd related from Yahya b. Wadih, from al-Hasan, from Yazid al-Nahwi, from Ikrima (9517), on [[passage/quran-85-3|85:3]], that the witness is Muhammad and the witnessed is Friday, and counted this as part of the sense here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 369}

**Ibn Mas'ud's recitation and the Prophet's words (pp. 370-371, reports 9518-9519, notes 1-2).** Abdullah b. Muhammad al-Zuhri related from Sufyan, from al-Mas'udi, from Ja'far b. Amr b. Huraith, from his father, from Abdullah (9518; the report number is smudged in the scan and taken from the sequence) that the Prophet spoke along the lines reported of Jesus in [[passage/quran-5-117|5:117]]: that he is witness over them while he is among them, and when God takes him God Himself keeps watch over them. In note 1 the editor says Sufyan is Ibn Uyayna; that al-Mas'udi here is Ma'n b. Abd al-Rahman b. Abdullah b. Mas'ud, trustworthy and used by the two Shaykhs, listed by al-Bukhari in al-Kabir and by Ibn Abi Hatim; that Ja'far b. Amr b. Huraith al-Makhzumi is trustworthy and listed by the same two; that his father Amr b. Huraith was a Companion (as the scan reads); and that, though the chain is sound, he found the report nowhere apart from al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir (2:453) and al-Suyuti (2:164) attributing it to no one else, and that the quoted verse is an adaptation of the one in sura 5, verse 117. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 371}

Muhammad b. al-Muthanna related from Ibrahim b. Abi al-Wazir, from Sufyan b. Uyayna, from al-Mas'udi, from al-Qasim (9519) that the Prophet said to Ibn Mas'ud: recite to me. He asked: shall I recite to you when it was sent down to you? He said: I like to hear it from someone else. Ibn Mas'ud recited sura 4 until he reached this point; the Prophet's eyes welled with tears and Ibn Mas'ud stopped. Then al-Mas'udi said that Ja'far b. Amr b. Huraith related to him from his father the same words as in the previous report. Note 2 (which runs from p. 370 onto p. 371) says Ibrahim b. Abi al-Wazir (the name of Abu al-Wazir was Umar) b. Mutarrif al-Makki, client of Banu Hashim, is trustworthy, vouched for by Muhammad b. Bashshar and others, and listed in al-Tahdhib, al-Kabir and Ibn Abi Hatim. The editor says this is really two reports. The first is al-Mas'udi from al-Qasim, apparently al-Qasim b. Abd al-Rahman b. Abdullah b. Mas'ud, al-Mas'udi's brother, a trustworthy Successor, who is not said here to relate from Ibn Mas'ud, so it is mursal; but it is established as sound through connected chains, among them al-Bukhari (9:81 in the Fath) through al-A'mash, from Ibrahim, from Ubayda, from Abdullah, Ahmad in the Musnad (4118 and 3606) through al-A'mash, and Ahmad (3550) from Abu Hayyan al-Ashja'i and (3551) through Abu Razin, both from Ibn Mas'ud. Ibn Kathir in Fada'il al-Qur'an (p. 77) quotes it from al-Bukhari and says the Jama'a, except Ibn Majah, transmitted it through al-A'mash, and in the commentary (2:452-453) says it is transmitted by many routes from Ibn Mas'ud; al-Suyuti (2:163) adds Ibn Abi Shayba, Abd b. Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi in al-Dala'il. The second is al-Mas'udi from Ja'far b. Amr b. Huraith from his father, which repeats the earlier report but makes it Amr b. Huraith's without mentioning Ibn Mas'ud, so it is a Companion's mursal and sound in any case. Al-Hakim has it in the Mustadrak (3:319) through Ja'far b. Awn, from al-Mas'udi, from Ja'far b. Amr b. Huraith, from his father, at length, with the story of Ibn Mas'ud reading these verses to the Prophet; al-Hakim called the chain sound though the two Shaykhs did not extract it, and al-Dhahabi agreed; al-Suyuti (2:163) gives al-Hakim's version in brief and attributes it to no other.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 371}
