## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "among the people are those who take besides God equals, loving them as they love God; but those who believe are stronger in love for God" al-Tabari says some people take besides God equals, meaning likenesses, loving them as the believers love God, while the believers love God more than these love their equals. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 279} He gives the interpreters' differing views on the "equals": some said they are the gods they worshipped besides God, from Qatada, Mujahid, al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd; others said they are their chiefs whom they obeyed in disobedience to God, from al-Suddi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 279} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 280} To the question how "as they love God" can be said, he answers that it is like saying "I sold my slave as your slave was sold", so the sense is that they love them as you, believers, love God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 280} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 281}

On "if those who did wrong could see, when they see the punishment, that all power belongs to God and that God is severe in punishment" he sets out the readers' differences: most of the people of Medina and Syria read "if you could see" with a ta and "they see" with a ya, with "anna" opened; some earlier readers read "inna" with a kasra as a fresh statement of His power; most of the Kufans, Basrans and Meccans read "if they could see" with a ya and "anna" opened; and he reports the grammatical explanations of the Basran and Kufan grammarians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 281} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 282} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 283} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 284} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 285} He chooses the reading "if you could see those who did wrong" with the ta, meaning you would have seen that all power belongs to God; the address is to the Prophet but others are meant, since he knew this, as in "do you not know that God has the dominion of the heavens and the earth"; and "if you had seen" is not said to one who has seen, which is why he does not choose the ya reading. "When they see the punishment" means when they witness it, from al-Rabi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 285} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 286} In his sense: if you could see those who wronged themselves by taking equals besides Him, when they witness His punishment on the Day of Resurrection, you would know that all power is God's and that the equals avail them nothing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 286}
