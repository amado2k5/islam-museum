## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari recites the verse within verses 100 to 102 in a single passage. He explains His saying, "So when you recite the Qur'an, seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Satan": And if, O Muhammad, you are a reciter of the Qur'an, seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Satan. Some Arabs claimed it was from the 'al-Mu'takhar' meaning to come forward. It seems the meaning of the statement for him is: 'And if you seek refuge with Allah from the accursed Satan, then recite the Qur'an.' There is no basis for what he said, because if that were the case, whenever someone sought refuge from the accursed Satan, he would have to recite the Qur'an, but its meaning is as we have described. His saying: "So seek refuge in Allah from the accursed Satan" is not a necessary command; rather, it is a warning and recommendation, because there is no disagreement among all that whoever recites the Qur'an and does not seek refuge with Allah from the accursed Satan before or after reading it has not neglected a mandatory obligation. Ibn Zayd used to say about this similar to what we have said. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid, Ibn Zayd, Ibn Jurayj, Al-Rabi', and Sufyan.
