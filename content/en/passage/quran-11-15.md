## Summary
Verse 15 of [[passage/quran-11|Surat Hud]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 15 of 123 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 15 (pp. 262-268)**

On [[passage/quran-11-15|11:15]] al-Tabari says: whoever intends by his deed the life of this world and seeks its adornment by it, We pay them in full the wages of their works in it and their reward; 'and they in it' means in the world 'will not be cheated' meaning their wage will not be reduced, but they will be paid it in full in it. The editor says the printed text has 'and its furnishings', which spoils the speech, and the manuscript is right, and refers to the earlier commentaries on 'adornment', 'paying in full' and 'cheating'. Report 18012, from Ibn Abbas: this is what God gives them of the world for their good deeds, because they are not wronged by a date-stone's thread; whoever does good seeking the world, fasting, praying or standing at night only to seek the world, God says I pay him what he sought in the world of reward, and his work done to seek the world comes to nothing, and in the next life he is among the losers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 263}

Report 18013, from Sa'id b. Jubayr: the reward of what they did of good in the world is given them in the world, and they have in the next life nothing but the Fire, and what they did in it comes to nothing; report 18014, from him, to the same effect, saying it is like the verse in al-Rum, meaning [[passage/quran-30-39|30:39]]; report 18015, from him: whoever works for the world is paid in full for it in the world. The commentary on the verse continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 263}

Report 18016, from Mujahid: whoever does a deed of what God ordered, such as prayer or charity, not intending God's face by it, God gives him in the world the reward of it equal to what he spent, and that is 'We pay them in full for their deeds in it'; reports 18017 and 18018, from him: of one from whom it is not accepted, he is repaid and given his reward quickly in the world. Report 18019, from Qatada: 'they will not be cheated' means not wronged; whoever makes the world his concern, his passion, his quest and his intention, God repays him for his good deeds in the world and he reaches the next life with no good deed to be repaid for, while the believer is repaid for his good deeds in the world and rewarded for them in the next. The editor explains 'sadam' as devotion to a thing and eagerness for it, and distress in seeking it. Report 18020, from Qatada: that was a reckoning for him for his work. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 264}

Report 18021, from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi, that the Prophet said: there is no doer of good whose wage has not fallen to God, in the present world and the later one; the editor says it is a mursal report. Report 18022, from al-Dahhak: whoever does a good deed without piety, meaning among the polytheists, is given a wage for it in the world: he keeps ties of kinship, gives to the beggar, has pity on the needy and the like of such good works, and God hastens the reward of his deed in the world, widens his living and provision, gladdens his eyes with what he is granted and keeps hardships of the world from him, but in the next life he has no share. Report 18023, from Anas: it concerns the Jews and Christians; reports 18024, 18025 and 18026, from al-Hasan: 'We pay them in full for their deeds' means their good things. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 265}

Report 18027, from Mujahid, that they are the people of ostentation. Report 18028, from Shufayy b. Mati' al-Asbahi, that he entered Medina and found a man with people crowded around him, and when told it was Abu Hurayra asked him for a hadith he had heard from the Messenger of God. Abu Hurayra gasped and recovered, then said: the Messenger of God told me in this house, with none in it but me and him, and gasped hard again and fell on his face, then recovered and said: the Messenger of God told me that when the Day of Resurrection comes God descends to His servants to judge between them, every nation kneeling; the first to be called are a man who gathered the Qur'an, a man killed in God's cause and a man of great wealth. God says to the reciter: did I not teach you what I sent down on My messenger? He says yes, Lord. God says: what did you do with what you were taught? He says I stood in it through hours of night and day. God says: you lie, and the angels say you lie; rather you wanted it said, so-and-so is a reciter, and that was said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 266}

The man of wealth is brought and God says: did I not widen your provision so you had need of none? He says yes, Lord. God says: what did you do with what I gave you? He says I kept kinship ties and gave in charity. God says: you lie, and the angels say you lie; rather you wanted it said, so-and-so is generous, and that was said. The one killed in God's cause is brought and asked: for what were you killed? He says I was ordered to fight in Your cause and I fought until I was killed. God says: you lie, and the angels say you lie; rather you wanted it said, so-and-so is brave, and that was said. Then the Messenger of God struck my knee and said: Abu Hurayra, those three are the first of God's creation with whom the Fire is kindled on the Day of Resurrection. The report adds that Shufayy entered on Mu'awiya and told him this, and that al-Ala' b. Abi Hakim, who was Mu'awiya's swordsman, said a man told him this from Abu Hurayra; Mu'awiya wept so hard they thought he had died, then recovered, wiped his face and said, God and His Messenger spoke truly, and recited the verse to 'and vain is what they used to do'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 267}

The editor says al-Tirmidhi relates it in the 'Book of Asceticism' and calls it good and strange, that its strangeness lies in Shufayy b. Mati's reporting from Abu Hurayra, that Shufayy is a trustworthy Egyptian Follower whom Ibn Jarir counts among the Companions without it being established, and that al-Nasa'i relates it in brief by another route from Sulayman b. Yasar. Report 18029, from Mujahid: of one from whom it is not accepted, who fasts and prays wanting the world by it, and the worry of the next life is kept from him; 'they will not be cheated in it' means not reduced. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 268}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
