**Commentary on verse 19 (pp. 1-7)**

Al-Tabari says: a party of travellers passing along the road came; their "waterdrawer" is the one who goes to the watering place or the halting place, and his "coming" is his arrival at it and entry into it; "he let down his pail" means he sent his bucket down into the well, and one says "adlaytu" for letting a bucket down and "dalawtu" for drawing water. There is an omission in the wording that what is mentioned makes clear: he let down his pail, Joseph clung to it and came out, and the one who had let it down said, "Good news, this is a boy!" The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "caravan" and "coming". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 1}

Report 18880, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: Joseph clung to the rope and came out, and when the holder of the rope saw him he called a man of his companions named "Bushra". Report 18881, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the boy held fast to the bucket, and when he came out the man spoke. Report 18882, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they sent their messenger, and when he let down his pail the boy held on to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 2}

They differed on the meaning of "Good news, this is a boy!" Some said it is the bucket-lowerer giving his companions good news that he had found a slave. Report 18883, from Qatada: they rejoiced in him when they drew him out, and it is a well in the land of Bayt al-Maqdis whose place is known; report 18884, from him through Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: their waterdrawer gave them the good news when he found Joseph. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "good news". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 2}

Others said it is rather the name of one particular man of the caravan, whom the bucket-lowerer called when Joseph came out clinging to the rope. Report 18885, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: he called a man of his companions named "Bushra"; report 18886, from al-Suddi, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Khalaf ibn Hisham, Yahya ibn Adam and Qays ibn al-Rabi': his companion's name was "Bushra"; report 18887, from him, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hammad and al-Hakam ibn Zuhayr: the boy's name was "Bushra", and he called him as one says "O Zayd". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 3}

The readers differed in how to read it. Most of the people of Medina read it with the pronoun of possession kept and the alif merged into it, seeking the kasra that always falls before the speaker's yā' as in "my boy" and "my girl"; this is the dialect of Tayy, and of Hudhayl too, as al-Asma'i said. Al-Tabari cites a line of Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali, who mourns in it his sons who died of the plague in a single year, five of them, and the editor explains the line and where it stands in the divan and the commentary on the Mufaddaliyyat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 3}

Most of the Kufans read it with the yā' dropped and no possessive, which allows two interpretations: one is al-Suddi's, that it is the name of a man called by name as one says "O Zayd", so that it stands in the nominative as a vocative; the other is that he meant to attach the word to himself and dropped the yā' while intending it, as the Arabs say "O soul, be patient" and "O my soul, be patient", and "O my son" with either vowelling. Al-Tabari says: the reading I like best is that of the one who dropped the yā' and made it silent, for if it is the name of a particular man known among them, as al-Suddi said, that is the sound reading without doubt, and if it comes from "giving good news" it allows that too, as he explained; as for the doubled form with the possessive, it is an irregular reading he does not hold with, although it is a known dialect, because the authoritative readers agreed against it. The editor notes an error in the manuscript and printed text in one phrase, and that the better wording is the one he printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 4}

On "and they hid him as a treasure" the people of interpretation differed. Some said the one who came to the well, the drawer, and his companions among the merchants with them hid it from the others, telling them he was merchandise some people of Egypt had entrusted to them, because they feared that if the others learned the price they paid they would ask for a share. Report 18888, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the owner of the bucket and those with him told their companions "we only took him as merchandise", fearing the others would share in him if they knew his price; his brothers followed them, telling the bucket-lowerer and his companions to hold him fast lest he run away, until they stopped him in Egypt, where he said: who will buy me and be glad of me? The king bought him, and the king was a Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 5}

Reports 18889, 18890, 18891 and 18892, from Mujahid, are similar, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; then al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; then Ishaq and Abd Allah; then al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj, with differences in some words, and the editor restores from the manuscript the phrase "until they stopped him" and refers to report 18892. Report 18893, from al-Suddi: when the two men bought him they feared the company would say "we bought him" and ask them for a share, so they said, if they ask us what this is, we will say it is merchandise the people of the water entrusted to us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 5}

Others said it means that the merchants hid it from one another. Reports 18894 and 18895 are from Mujahid, the first through Abu Kurayb, Waki' and Sufyan from a man, the second through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym al-Fadl and Sufyan. Others said they hid his sale; report 18896, from Qatada, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar, and report 18897, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Qays and Jabir: they told the people of the water that he was only merchandise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 6}

Others said that what is meant is Joseph's brothers: they hid his affair and concealed that he was their brother, saying he was their slave. Report 18898, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: they hid his affair and concealed that he was their brother; Joseph concealed his affair for fear his brothers would kill him and chose the sale; his brothers mentioned him to the company's drawer, who called to his companions: Good news, a boy for sale! And his brothers sold him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 6}

Al-Tabari said: the soundest of these statements is that of the one who said that the company's drawer who let down the pail and those of his companions with him kept secret the matter of Joseph, that they had bought him, fearing the others would ask for a share, and told them he was merchandise the people of the water had entrusted to them with. His reason is that it follows directly after the report about him, so that what comes next being about him is likelier than its being about someone with whom the report is not connected. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "concealing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 7}

On "and God was aware of what they did" he says: God has knowledge of what Joseph's sellers and buyers did with him, nothing of it hidden from Him, but He left it unchanged so that His decree, prior in His knowledge, might run through him and them, and so that Joseph's brothers, Joseph and his father might see His power in him. He adds that although this is a report about Joseph, it is also a reminder from God to His Prophet Muhammad and a consolation for the harm he met from his relatives and kinsmen among the idolaters: be patient, Muhammad, for I am able to change what befalls you, as I was able to change what Joseph met from his brothers while they did what they did; I did not leave it because Joseph was of little account with Me but because of what My knowledge had already decided about him and his brothers; so I leave unchanged what these idolaters do to you, not because you are of little account, but for the prior decree of My knowledge about you and them, and then your affair and theirs will end in your being raised above them and their submission to you, as the affair of Joseph's brothers ended in their submission to Joseph and his rank over them. The editor notes in a footnote that the twelfth part of their manuscript ends at this point. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 7}
