## Summary
Verse 93 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 93 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the opening of the verse (p. 57, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says [[passage/quran-4-93|4:93]] concerns one who takes a believer's life on purpose, intending to destroy his life: his recompense is Hell, that is its torment; abiding means remaining in it, the pronoun referring to Hell; God's anger is for killing him deliberately; the curse is removal from His mercy and disgrace; and the punishment prepared for him is of a size only God knows. The editor refers each word to its earlier treatment and explains "bada'a" the flesh as cutting it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 57}

**What counts as deliberate killing: first view (pp. 57-58, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says commentators agree that one who strikes another with the edge of iron that wounds or cuts, and does not stop until he destroys his life while meaning to strike him, is a deliberate killer, and differ about the rest. Some said nothing is deliberate except this. Report 10174 (Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Abi Za'ida, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ata: deliberate is the weapon, or iron; and from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: the weapon. Report 10175 (Abu Kurayb and Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: deliberate is with iron, anything less is near-deliberate with no retaliation. Report 10176 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: deliberate is with iron, near-deliberate with wood, and near-deliberate occurs only against the person. The editor notes that number 10177 is missing from the numbering, so the sequence goes from 10176 to 10178. Report 10178 (Ahmad ibn Hammad al-Dawlabi, from Sufyan, from Amr) from Tawus: one killed in factional fighting by thrown stones, whipping or sticks is a mistake with the blood-money of mistake, while one killed deliberately brings retaliation for what his hand did (the editor kept the manuscript reading). Report 10179 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from al-Mughira) from al-Harith and his companions, on a man who strikes another who stays ill until death: the witnesses are asked; if it was with a weapon there is retaliation, otherwise near-deliberate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 58}

**Second view (pp. 58-59, notes 3 and 1).** Others said that whatever the striker used to destroy the person is deliberate if what he struck with is mostly lethal. Report 10180 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Yahya, from Hibban ibn Abi Jabala) from Ubayd ibn Umayr: what could be more deliberate than striking a man with a stick and not stopping until he dies? Report 10181 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Abu Hashim) from Ibrahim: if he strangled him with a rope or struck him with wood until he died, there is retaliation. The editor says Hibban ibn Abi Jabala al-Qurashi, a client, an Egyptian, narrated from Amr ibn al-As and the Abd Allahs except Ibn al-Zubayr; that he could not identify Abd al-Rahman ibn Yahya and fears the right name is Abd al-Rahman ibn Ziyad ibn An'um al-Ifriqi; and that a word is missing in both printed text and manuscript, which he supplied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 59}

**The argument of those who call everything but iron a mistake (pp. 59-60, note 2).** Report 10182 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from Jabir, from Abu Azib) from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir: the Prophet said that everything is a mistake except the sword, and every mistake has a compensation. The editor goes through the chain at length: Sufyan is al-Thawri; Jabir is Jabir ibn Yazid al-Ju'fi, very weak and accused of lying, as he showed elsewhere; Abu Azib is an unknown Kufan, called Muslim ibn Amr or Muslim ibn Arak, from whom only Jabir and al-Harith ibn Ziyad narrated, and al-Harith is unknown according to Ibn Abi Hatim and his father; al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim have entries for Abu Azib, yet he stays unknown since no known reliable narrator transmitted from him. He traces the report to Ahmad's Musnad (4: 272 and 275) with slightly different wording, and to al-Bayhaqi's al-Sunan al-Kubra (8: 42) by three chains through Jabir al-Ju'fi and then through Qays ibn al-Rabi', quotes al-Bayhaqi that the report turns on Jabir al-Ju'fi and Qays, neither of whom is used as proof, and mentions al-Zayla'i in Nasb al-Raya (4: 333) and what he quotes from the author of al-Tanqih that Abu Azib is not known (these assessments are the editor's and those he quotes; the museum gives no verdict). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 60}

**The argument of those who treat whatever kills as the sword (p. 60, notes 1-2).** Report 10183 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abu al-Walid, from Hammam, from Qatada) from Anas ibn Malik: a Jew killed a girl for her silver ornaments between two stones; he was brought to the Prophet, who had him killed between two stones. They said the Prophet ordered retaliation against one who killed with a stone, which is not iron, and so for anyone who kills with something mostly lethal. The editor says this is an abridgement of a sound agreed-upon report, traces it to al-Bukhari (12: 174-188) and Muslim (2: 26-27) through Hammam from Qatada from Anas and by other routes from Anas, notes that al-Majd ibn Taymiyya in al-Muntaqa says "the group" narrated it, and explains the ornaments as plural of a sound silver dirham, later used of jewellery made from such coins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 60}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 60-61, note 2).** Al-Tabari says the right view with him is that anyone who strikes a person with something mostly destructive, and does not stop until he destroys him, is a deliberate killer whatever he struck with, on the strength of the report from the Prophet that he cited (his choice). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 61}

**The recompense of Hell: first view (p. 61).** Al-Tabari then says the commentators differed on the sense of the recompense. Some said his recompense is Hell if He recompenses him. Report 10184 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Sulayman al-Taymi) from Abu Mijlaz: that is his recompense, and if He wills He passes over it. Report 10185 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Abu al-Nu'man al-Hakam ibn Abd Allah, from Shu'ba, from Yasar) from Abu Salih: his recompense is Hell if He recompenses him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 61}

**Second view: a particular man (pp. 61-62, notes).** Others said a particular man is meant, who became Muslim, apostatised and killed a believer, so that the sense is whoever takes a believer's life on purpose counting his killing lawful. Report 10186 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ikrima: a man of the Ansar killed the brother of Miqyas ibn Subaba; the Prophet gave Miqyas the blood-money and he accepted, then he pounced on his brother's killer and killed him. Ibn Jurayj added that others said the Prophet laid the blood-money on Banu al-Najjar, then sent Miqyas on an errand with a man of Banu Fihr; Miqyas lifted the Fihri, a strong man, struck him to the ground and crushed his head between two stones, and was then found chanting a poem in which he claimed to have taken his vengeance and put the burden of the blood-money on Banu al-Najjar. The Prophet said he thought Miqyas had done something wrong and that if so he would give him no safety in the lawful ground or the sanctuary, in peace or war; and Miqyas was killed on the day of the conquest. Ibn Jurayj said the verse came down about him. The editor says Miqyas al-Fihri is better known as al-Sahmi, being of Banu Sahm ibn Amr, a descent from Fihr; that the word for strong is explained; refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 305-306), al-Tabari's History (3: 66) and Mu'jam al-Buldan under "Fari'"; gives the poem as four lines of which the quoted line is the last, and corrects a reading in it; and says Fari' is a fortress in Medina of Banu al-Najjar that belonged to Hassan ibn Thabit (the OCR of this note is partly damaged, so it is summarised). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 62}

**Third view: except one who repents (p. 62).** Others said the sense is except for one who repents. Report 10187 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mansur, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, or from al-Hakam from Sa'id ibn Jubayr): I asked Ibn Abbas about the verse and he said that when a man knows Islam and its laws and then takes a believer's life on purpose, his recompense is Hell, and there is no repentance (the report continues on the next page). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 62}

**The close of report 10187 and the view of no repentance (pp. 63-64, notes).** Sa'id ibn Jubayr ends the report by saying he mentioned this to Mujahid, who said: except one who regretted. Al-Tabari then gives a fourth view: that the verse is a threat from God against one who kills a believer deliberately, whoever the killer, as described in His book, and that He gave the doer no repentance; every deliberate killer of a believer has the promised punishment and remaining in the Fire, with no repentance, and they said it came down after the verse of sura 25. Report 10188 (Ibn Humayd and Ibn Waki', from Jarir, from Yahya al-Jabir, from Salim ibn Abi al-Ja'd): we were with Ibn Abbas after he lost his sight, and a man asked him about one who kills a believer deliberately. He recited the verse's threat, and the man asked: and if he repents, believes, does good and is guided? Ibn Abbas said: may his mother be bereft of him, how could he have repentance and guidance! He then related that he heard the Prophet say that on the Day of Resurrection the slain man comes holding his killer with one hand, with the veins of his neck flowing blood before the throne of the Merciful, saying: ask this one why he killed me. Ibn Abbas swore that the verse came down and nothing abrogated it until their Prophet died, and no proof came down after it. The editor says Yahya al-Jabir is Yahya ibn al-Muhabbar (as he renders the OCR-damaged name) ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Harith al-Taymi, whom his brother al-Sayyid Ahmad declared reliable in the Musnad edition; he judges the report sound through Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba, lists its places in the Musnad (some numbers are damaged in the OCR and not given) and refers to Ibn Kathir (2: 537-539). He explains that the flowing means pouring with a sound, that the awdaj are the veins around the throat, and that the front of the throne is what lies before it where one looks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 64}

**Reports 10189-10190 (p. 64, notes 1-2).** Report 10189 (Ibn Waki', from Abu Khalid, from Amr ibn Qays, from Yahya ibn al-Harith al-Taymi, from Salim, from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet): he recited the verse and was asked: even if he repents, believes and does good? He said: how could he have repentance. Report 10190 (Abu Kurayb, from Musa ibn Dawud, from Humam, from Yahya, from a man, from Salim) is like the above, adding the Prophet's saying, in Ibn Abbas's words, that no prophet came after their prophet and no book came down after their book. The editor says Abu Khalid al-Ahmar is Sulayman ibn Hayyan al-Azdi; Amr ibn Qays is al-Mula'i; 10189 is an abridgement of the one before; Musa ibn Dawud al-Dabbi al-Tarsusi was a teacher of Ahmad and Ali ibn al-Madini, reliable, who served as judge of Tarsus; Humam ibn Yahya ibn Dinar al-Azdi is reliable; and 10190 is another route to the preceding, placing a man between Yahya al-Jabir and Salim, which does no harm since Yahya also heard Salim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 64}

**Reports 10191-10194 (pp. 65-66, notes 1-2).** Report 10191 (Abu Kurayb, from Qabisa, from Ammar ibn Ruzayq, from Ammar al-Duhni, from Salim, from Ibn Abbas) is similar, with Ibn Abbas saying it came down and nothing abrogated it, and that he heard the Prophet say: woe to the killer of a believer, who comes on the Day of Resurrection holding his head in his hand. Report 10192 (Ibn Bashshar, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Sa'id, from Abu Bishr, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr): Abd al-Rahman ibn Abza told me that Ibn Abbas was asked about the verse and said nothing abrogated it, and that about the verse of sura 25 ([[passage/quran-25-68|25:68]]) he said it was revealed about the polytheists. Report 10193 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Mansur, from Sa'id) says Ibn Abza told him to ask Ibn Abbas about the two verses, and gives the like. Report 10194 (Abu Kurayb, from Talq ibn Ghannam, from Za'ida, from Mansur, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, or from one who reported from him) says Ibn Abza told him to ask Ibn Abbas about the two verses, in sura 4 and sura 25; Ibn Abbas said that when a man enters Islam, knows its laws and then kills a believer deliberately, he has no repentance; as for sura 25, when it was revealed the polytheists of Mecca said they had associated others with God, killed the soul God forbade and committed indecencies, so what would Islam profit them? Then came the exception for one who repents ([[passage/quran-25-70|25:70]]). The editor says Ammar ibn Ruzayq al-Dabbi, Abu al-Ahwas, is reliable according to Ibn Ma'in, that the printed text had a wrong name which he corrected, that Muslim (18: 158) and al-Bukhari (Fath al-Bari 8: 380) narrated 10192 and 10193 through Ibn Bashshar and Ibn al-Muthanna, that al-Bukhari (Fath 8: 379) and Muslim (18: 159) narrated 10194 through other routes he names, and that the manuscript omits a phrase from it and lacks the last word of the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 66}

**Reports 10195-10200: nothing abrogated it (pp. 66-67, note 2).** Report 10195 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from al-Mughira ibn al-Nu'man, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) from Ibn Abbas: nothing abrogated it. Report 10196 (from Shu'ba, from al-Mughira) from him: it is among the last revealed, nothing abrogated it. Report 10197 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from al-Mughira): the people of Kufa differed about killing a believer, so Sa'id asked Ibn Abbas, who said it came down among the last of the Qur'an and nothing abrogated it. The editor says al-Bukhari and Muslim narrated these three, that Ibn Hajar discusses them at length in the Fath, and that he adopted a wording from the manuscript. Report 10198 (al-Muthanna, from Adam al-Asqalani, from Shu'ba, from Abu Iyas Mu'awiya ibn Qurra, from Shahr ibn Hawshab) from Ibn Abbas: it came down a year after the repentance exception in sura 25. Report 10199 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Salm ibn Qutayba, from Shu'ba, from Mu'awiya ibn Qurra) is similar. In report 10200 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Abd al-Samad, from Shu'ba, from Abu Iyas) Abu Iyas had it from one who heard Ibn Abbas, and when Shu'ba asked who told him, he named Shahr ibn Hawshab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 67}

**Reports 10201-10204 (pp. 67-68, note 1).** Report 10201 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from Abu Hasin, from Sa'id) from Ibn Abbas: the killer has no repentance unless he seeks God's forgiveness. Report 10202 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) from Ibn Abbas: Atiyya said he was asked about it and claimed it came down after the verse of sura 25 ([[passage/quran-25-68|25:68]]) by eight years (the number is read from damaged OCR and is the editor-agent's inference). Report 10203 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from Mutarrif, from Abu al-Safar, from Naji(y)a) from Ibn Abbas: they are the two closed ones, shirk and killing. The editor explains "closed" as a verse with no way out, like a sealed door that cannot be opened, because the recompense of shirk and killing is abiding in Hell; he cites the saying "four closed things: the vow, marriage, divorce and manumission", another wording "four locked", and refers to an earlier explanation of the word in another sense. Report 10204 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas: the greatest of the major sins are associating others with God and killing the forbidden soul, for what the verse threatens. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 68}

**Reports 10205-10210 (pp. 68-69, notes 1-2).** Report 10205 (al-Muthanna, from Amr ibn Awn, from Hushaym, from some Kufan elders of his, from al-Sha'bi, from Masruq) from Ibn Mas'ud: it is firm, and grows only in severity. Report 10206 (Abu Kurayb, from Uthman ibn Sa'id, from Hayyaj ibn Bistam, from Muhammad ibn Amr, from Musa ibn Uqba, from Abu al-Zinad, from Kharija ibn Zayd) from Zayd ibn Thabit: sura 4 came down six months after sura 25. Report 10207 (Ibn al-Barqi, from Ibn Abi Maryam, from Nafi ibn Yazid, from Abu Sakhr, from Abu Mu'awiya al-Bajali, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) from Ibn Abbas: the slain comes on the Day of Resurrection holding his head in his right hand, his veins pouring blood, saying "my blood is with so-and-so"; the two are taken to the throne, and Ibn Abbas said he did not know what would be decided between them; then he recited the verse and swore that God had not abrogated it since He sent it down. Report 10208 (Abu Kurayb, from Yahya ibn Adam, from Ibn Uyayna, from Abu al-Zinad, who heard a man tell Kharija, from Zayd ibn Thabit): I heard your father say that the severe came down after the easy by six months, meaning this verse after the one in sura 25 ([[passage/quran-25-68|25:68]]). Report 10209 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Abu al-Zinad) is similar, at Mina, with the narrator thinking it was six months, and meaning that it came after the verse that God does not forgive shirk ([[passage/quran-4-48|4:48]], [[passage/quran-4-116|4:116]]). Report 10210 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Salama ibn Nubayt, from al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim): nothing has abrogated it since it came down, and he has no repentance. The editor says Hayyaj ibn Bistam al-Harawi has a biography at report 9603; Ibn al-Barqi is Ahmad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahim al-Barqi; Ibn Abi Maryam is Sa'id ibn al-Hakam al-Jumahi; the printed text had a wrong name for the first, which he corrected; and report 10207 is missing from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 69}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 69-70, notes).** Abu Ja'far says the most correct view with him is that of those who said: one who kills a believer deliberately has as his recompense, if He recompenses him, abiding in the Fire, but God pardons and shows favour to people of faith in Him and His messenger, so He either pardons them and does not admit them to the Fire, or admits them and then brings them out by His mercy, on account of His earlier promise to the believers who transgressed against themselves in the verse of sura 39 ([[passage/quran-39-53|39:53]]) (his choice). If someone thought that the killer is included in this verse, he says, the polytheist would be included too, since shirk is a sin; yet God has told that He does not forgive shirk for anyone ([[passage/quran-4-48|4:48]]), and killing is below shirk. The editor says he adopted the manuscript wording in two places, and that the manuscript has a plain error in the phrase about "below shirk", where he kept the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 70}
