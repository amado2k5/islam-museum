## Summary
Verse 93 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 93 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the opening of the verse (p. 57, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says [[passage/quran-4-93|4:93]] concerns one who takes a believer's life on purpose, intending to destroy his life: his recompense is Hell, that is its torment; abiding means remaining in it, the pronoun referring to Hell; God's anger is for killing him deliberately; the curse is removal from His mercy and disgrace; and the punishment prepared for him is of a size only God knows. The editor refers each word to its earlier treatment and explains "bada'a" the flesh as cutting it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 57}

**What counts as deliberate killing: first view (pp. 57-58, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says commentators agree that one who strikes another with the edge of iron that wounds or cuts, and does not stop until he destroys his life while meaning to strike him, is a deliberate killer, and differ about the rest. Some said nothing is deliberate except this. Report 10174 (Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Abi Za'ida, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ata: deliberate is the weapon, or iron; and from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: the weapon. Report 10175 (Abu Kurayb and Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: deliberate is with iron, anything less is near-deliberate with no retaliation. Report 10176 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from al-Mughira) from Ibrahim: deliberate is with iron, near-deliberate with wood, and near-deliberate occurs only against the person. The editor notes that number 10177 is missing from the numbering, so the sequence goes from 10176 to 10178. Report 10178 (Ahmad ibn Hammad al-Dawlabi, from Sufyan, from Amr) from Tawus: one killed in factional fighting by thrown stones, whipping or sticks is a mistake with the blood-money of mistake, while one killed deliberately brings retaliation for what his hand did (the editor kept the manuscript reading). Report 10179 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from al-Mughira) from al-Harith and his companions, on a man who strikes another who stays ill until death: the witnesses are asked; if it was with a weapon there is retaliation, otherwise near-deliberate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 58}

**Second view (pp. 58-59, notes 3 and 1).** Others said that whatever the striker used to destroy the person is deliberate if what he struck with is mostly lethal. Report 10180 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Yahya, from Hibban ibn Abi Jabala) from Ubayd ibn Umayr: what could be more deliberate than striking a man with a stick and not stopping until he dies? Report 10181 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Abu Hashim) from Ibrahim: if he strangled him with a rope or struck him with wood until he died, there is retaliation. The editor says Hibban ibn Abi Jabala al-Qurashi, a client, an Egyptian, narrated from Amr ibn al-As and the Abd Allahs except Ibn al-Zubayr; that he could not identify Abd al-Rahman ibn Yahya and fears the right name is Abd al-Rahman ibn Ziyad ibn An'um al-Ifriqi; and that a word is missing in both printed text and manuscript, which he supplied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 59}

**The argument of those who call everything but iron a mistake (pp. 59-60, note 2).** Report 10182 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from Jabir, from Abu Azib) from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir: the Prophet said that everything is a mistake except the sword, and every mistake has a compensation. The editor goes through the chain at length: Sufyan is al-Thawri; Jabir is Jabir ibn Yazid al-Ju'fi, very weak and accused of lying, as he showed elsewhere; Abu Azib is an unknown Kufan, called Muslim ibn Amr or Muslim ibn Arak, from whom only Jabir and al-Harith ibn Ziyad narrated, and al-Harith is unknown according to Ibn Abi Hatim and his father; al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim have entries for Abu Azib, yet he stays unknown since no known reliable narrator transmitted from him. He traces the report to Ahmad's Musnad (4: 272 and 275) with slightly different wording, and to al-Bayhaqi's al-Sunan al-Kubra (8: 42) by three chains through Jabir al-Ju'fi and then through Qays ibn al-Rabi', quotes al-Bayhaqi that the report turns on Jabir al-Ju'fi and Qays, neither of whom is used as proof, and mentions al-Zayla'i in Nasb al-Raya (4: 333) and what he quotes from the author of al-Tanqih that Abu Azib is not known (these assessments are the editor's and those he quotes; the museum gives no verdict). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 60}

**The argument of those who treat whatever kills as the sword (p. 60, notes 1-2).** Report 10183 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abu al-Walid, from Hammam, from Qatada) from Anas ibn Malik: a Jew killed a girl for her silver ornaments between two stones; he was brought to the Prophet, who had him killed between two stones. They said the Prophet ordered retaliation against one who killed with a stone, which is not iron, and so for anyone who kills with something mostly lethal. The editor says this is an abridgement of a sound agreed-upon report, traces it to al-Bukhari (12: 174-188) and Muslim (2: 26-27) through Hammam from Qatada from Anas and by other routes from Anas, notes that al-Majd ibn Taymiyya in al-Muntaqa says "the group" narrated it, and explains the ornaments as plural of a sound silver dirham, later used of jewellery made from such coins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 60}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 60-61, note 2).** Al-Tabari says the right view with him is that anyone who strikes a person with something mostly destructive, and does not stop until he destroys him, is a deliberate killer whatever he struck with, on the strength of the report from the Prophet that he cited (his choice). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 61}

**The recompense of Hell: first view (p. 61).** Al-Tabari then says the commentators differed on the sense of the recompense. Some said his recompense is Hell if He recompenses him. Report 10184 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Sulayman al-Taymi) from Abu Mijlaz: that is his recompense, and if He wills He passes over it. Report 10185 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Abu al-Nu'man al-Hakam ibn Abd Allah, from Shu'ba, from Yasar) from Abu Salih: his recompense is Hell if He recompenses him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 61}

**Second view: a particular man (pp. 61-62, notes).** Others said a particular man is meant, who became Muslim, apostatised and killed a believer, so that the sense is whoever takes a believer's life on purpose counting his killing lawful. Report 10186 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ikrima: a man of the Ansar killed the brother of Miqyas ibn Subaba; the Prophet gave Miqyas the blood-money and he accepted, then he pounced on his brother's killer and killed him. Ibn Jurayj added that others said the Prophet laid the blood-money on Banu al-Najjar, then sent Miqyas on an errand with a man of Banu Fihr; Miqyas lifted the Fihri, a strong man, struck him to the ground and crushed his head between two stones, and was then found chanting a poem in which he claimed to have taken his vengeance and put the burden of the blood-money on Banu al-Najjar. The Prophet said he thought Miqyas had done something wrong and that if so he would give him no safety in the lawful ground or the sanctuary, in peace or war; and Miqyas was killed on the day of the conquest. Ibn Jurayj said the verse came down about him. The editor says Miqyas al-Fihri is better known as al-Sahmi, being of Banu Sahm ibn Amr, a descent from Fihr; that the word for strong is explained; refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 305-306), al-Tabari's History (3: 66) and Mu'jam al-Buldan under "Fari'"; gives the poem as four lines of which the quoted line is the last, and corrects a reading in it; and says Fari' is a fortress in Medina of Banu al-Najjar that belonged to Hassan ibn Thabit (the OCR of this note is partly damaged, so it is summarised). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 62}

**Third view: except one who repents (p. 62).** Others said the sense is except for one who repents. Report 10187 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mansur, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, or from al-Hakam from Sa'id ibn Jubayr): I asked Ibn Abbas about the verse and he said that when a man knows Islam and its laws and then takes a believer's life on purpose, his recompense is Hell, and there is no repentance (the report continues on the next page). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 62}


These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
