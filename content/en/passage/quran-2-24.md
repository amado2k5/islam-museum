## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and if you do not" he says it means that if they do not bring a surah like it, even after they and their helpers have joined forces, their own inability and that of all creation becomes clear to them, and they know it is from God yet persist in denial; "and you never will" means they will never produce one. He reports Qatada's gloss, "you cannot do it and have no strength for it", and Ibn Abbas's, "the truth has been made clear to you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 379}
On "then fear the Fire" he says: guard against entering the Fire by denying His messenger after it has become plain that the book is His speech; the Fire is then described as fuelled by people and stones. "Waqud" with a fatha on the waw is firewood, and with a damma it is the verbal noun of "the fire blazed". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 380}
He asks why stones are singled out. He reports from Ibn Mas'ud that they are stones of sulphur that God created on the day He created the heavens and the earth, in the lowest heaven, preparing them for the unbelievers; from Ibn Abbas and others of the Companions that they are black sulphur in the Fire with which they are punished; and from Ibn Jurayj, quoting Amr ibn Dinar, that they are harder and greater than these. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 381} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 382}
On "prepared for the disbelievers" he recalls that "kafir" in Arabic is one who covers something, and that the unbeliever is so named for denying and covering God's favours, so the Fire is prepared for those who deny that God alone is their Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 382}

On "prepared for the disbelievers" al-Tabari reports from Ibn Abbas that it means for those who hold the same disbelief as the people addressed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 383}
