## Summary
Verse 272 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 272 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-272|2:272]]), p. 587. Al-Tabari explains the address to Muhammad: it is not for him to guide the polytheists to Islam by withholding voluntary charity from them and not giving it, so that they enter Islam out of need for it, but guidance to Islam is in God's hand, who grants success in it to those of His creation He chooses, so he should not withhold charity from them; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 587} Report 6201: the Prophet used not to give charity to the polytheists, and the verse came down, and he gave them charity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 587} In 6202, Ibn Abbas: they did not give gifts to their polytheist relatives, and the verse came down; the editor gives the biography of Ja'far ibn Iyas (Ibn Abi Wahshiyya), calling him reliable and among the most sure of the narrators from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, notes that Ibn Kathir relates it from al-Nasa'i without ascribing it to Abu Jafar, takes that as showing that Ibn Kathir and others quote Abu Jafar little after volume 1, and explains rakhd as a gift between little and much; these are the editor's assessments alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 587} We stop at 6202, the last complete report in the pages read. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation, choice and grammatical argument are his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-272|2:272]]), pp. 587 to 589. Report 6203 concludes, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: they avoided giving gifts to their polytheist relatives until the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 587} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 588} 6204, Ibn Abbas: they did not give to their polytheist kin, the verse came down and it was permitted to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 588} 6205, Ibn Abbas: some of the Ansar had kin from Qurayza and al-Nadir and avoided giving them charity, wanting them to become Muslim, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 588} 6206, Qatada: they were told that men among the Prophet's Companions said, shall we give charity to those not of our religion, and God sent down the verse about it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 588} 6207, al-Rabi ibn Anas: a Muslim who had a needy polytheist relative would not give him charity, saying he is not of my religion, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 588} 6208, al-Suddi: the guidance meant is of the polytheists, while spending's recipients were then specified; the editor corrects the narrator's name in manuscript and print to Musa ibn Harun from Amr ibn Hammad, says this chain has recurred since the start of the commentary, and refers to report 6211 and a note on report 168. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 588} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 589}

6209, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: they gave charity to the poor of the protected non-Muslims, and when poor Muslims grew many the Prophet said give only to those of your religion, and the verse came down permitting charity to those not of the religion of Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 589} The editor notes that the passage was cut off here in manuscript and print and the copyist ran it on as one text; that he added the bracketed words from what al-Qurtubi relates in his commentary from Sa'id ibn Jubayr as a mursal report from the Prophet on the occasion of revelation, judged it likely the dropped report, and left dots where the break is; this supplement is the editor's, not in the manuscript text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 589} 6210, Ibn Zayd on the last part of the verse: it is returned to you, so why do you hurt him and reproach him, your spending is for yourself and for God's face and God repays you; the editor notes a break before this report whose extent he cannot estimate, and that al-Suyuti cites it in al-Durr al-manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 589}
