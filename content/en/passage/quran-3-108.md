## Summary
Verse 108 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 108 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The pointing word and the signs (p. 97).** Al-Tabari says the word of distant pointing at the start of the verse means "these", since the Arabs put the distant pointing word in place of the near one, as he explained earlier. God's signs are His admonitions, lessons and proofs, and reciting them means reading and relating them to the Prophet with truth and certainty. He says what is meant are the verses in which the matters of the believing Ansar of God's messenger, of the Jews of Banu Isra'il and the People of the Book are given, with what He will do to those who keep His covenant and to those who change His religion and break His covenant after acknowledging it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 97}

**The denial of wronging (pp. 97-98).** God told His prophet that He recites this to him with truth and informed him that His punishing of those He punishes, by darkening faces and abiding in painful torment, and His rewarding of those He rewards, by whitening faces, honour and everlasting bliss, is not wronging either group but a due they earned and deeds they sent ahead. So the denial of wishing wrong means that in darkening some faces, making them taste the great punishment, whitening others and giving them bliss in His garden, He seeks to put nothing out of its place, informing His servants that nothing but what He promised the people of obedience and belief and threatened the people of disobedience and disbelief fits His wisdom with His creation, as a warning to some and a good tidings to others. The editor records in the notes of both pages the manuscript's readings where the printed text differs and judges al-Tabari's wording sound in his manner of expression. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 97} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 98}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 98}
