**Commentary on verse 8 (pp. 145-150)**

Al-Tabari says: how can these polytheists who broke their covenant, or who have none with you, believers, have a covenant and protection, when if they prevail over you and overcome you they will not respect in you any kinship or covenant tie. The word "how" is left to suffice as the indication of the sense because what is meant precedes it, and the Arabs do the same, omitting the verb when they repeat the particle after its sense has passed; he cites a line by Ka'b ibn Sa'd al-Ghanawi from his well-known elegy for his brother, expressing wonder at supposing death to lie only in the villages when here are a hill and a dune where no one escapes it, the verb after "how" being left out because what is meant came before. The editor gives the sources of the poem and the story of the poet's brother, who died after fleeing a plague-stricken land. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 145}

The interpreters differed over the words about respecting "ill" and "dhimma" in you. Some said the meaning is that they do not respect God in you, nor any covenant. Report 16499, from Mujahid, on the similar verse at [[passage/quran-9-10|9:10]]: "ill" is God; report 16500, from Abu Mijlaz, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and Sulayman: it is like Jibra'il, Mika'il and Israfil, as if adding "jabr", "mika" and "israf" to "il", meaning servant of God, so that he says they do not respect God; report 16501, from Mujahid: they do not respect God or anyone else. Others said "ill" is kinship. Report 16502, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: kinship and no covenant, "dhimma" being the covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 146}

Report 16503, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "ill" is kinship and "dhimma" the covenant, meaning the covenant polytheists, their protection; report 16504, from al-Dahhak: kinship; report 16505, from Ibn Abbas through Ikrima: kinship, and "dhimma" the covenant; report 16506, from al-Dahhak: "ill" is kinship and "dhimma" the pledge; report 16507, from al-Suddi: if the polytheists prevail over you, they will not respect in you any covenant, kinship or pledge. Others said the meaning is alliance; report 16508, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "ill" is alliance and "dhimma" covenant. Others said "ill" is the covenant but repeated because the two words differ though their sense is one. The editor records the manuscript reading in the chain of report 16504. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 147}

Report 16509, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "ill" means a covenant. Report 16510, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: they will not respect a covenant or a pledge in you, the two being related like "forgiving" and "merciful", one word that differs in form, and the covenant is the pledge. Reports 16511 and 16512, from Mujahid: "dhimma" is the covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 148}

Al-Tabari then chooses the view that God reports of these polytheists, whom He told His prophet and the believers to kill after the sacred months, that if they overcame the believers they would not respect "ill" in them. "Ill" is a noun covering the meanings of covenant, contract, alliance and kinship, and also meaning God; since God did not single out one of them, the right course is to take it generally, so that they do not respect in a believer God, kinship, covenant or pledge. As evidence that it means kinship he gives a line by Ibn Muqbil blaming a bad generation that cut "ill" and the ties of blood, and a line by Hassan ibn Thabit addressed to Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith, likening his kinship with Quraysh to a foal's kinship with the young of an ostrich; for the sense of covenant he gives a line by an unknown poet saying that one who holds "ill" and a covenant does not lie. The editor explains the words of Ibn Muqbil's line and says Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith, the Prophet's cousin and foster brother, used to harm and satirise him, then became Muslim at the conquest, stood firm at Hunayn holding the bridle of the Prophet's mule, and afterward could not raise his head to him for shame. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 149}

Some Basrans with a reputation for knowing Arabic, namely Abu Ubayda in his Majaz al-Qur'an, claimed that "ill", covenant, pledge and oath are one and that "dhimma" here is the sparing of one who has no covenant. Ibn Ishaq used to say the verse meant the holders of the general covenant. Report 16513, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "how, if they prevail over you" means the polytheists with no covenant to a term among the holders of the general covenant, "they will not respect in you ill or dhimma". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 150}

As for "they please you with their mouths", it means they give you with their tongues words contrary to the enmity and hatred they hide in themselves, "while their hearts refuse", that is their hearts refuse to submit to the truth of what their tongues show; God warns the believers of their affair and urges them to kill and uproot them wherever found and not to fall short in harming them in any way they can; "most of them are sinners" means violators of your covenant, breaking it, disbelieving in their Lord and leaving His obedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 150}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
