## Summary
Verse 100 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 100 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains that the verse means those who set up equals and rivals to their Lord made the jinn partners to God, as in the verse of al-Saffat (37:158) that speaks of a kinship between Him and the jinn. The accusative of "jinn" has two aspects: it explains (substitutes for) "partners", or the sense is that they made the jinn partners to God although He is their creator. He then turns to the reading of "wa khalaqahum": the readers of the regions read it as a verb, that God created them, alone in creating them; a different reading, with the lam unvowelled, is reported from Yahya ibn Ya'mar in report 13680 (Ahmad ibn Yusuf, from al-Qasim ibn Sallam, from Hajjaj, from Harun, from Wasil the client of Abu Uyayna, from Yahya ibn Aqil). The editor notes that "tafsir" here means the substitute, and cites al-Farra' on the point. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 7}

Al-Tabari chooses the reading "wa khalaqahum" (He created them) because the authoritative readers agree on it. Of "kharaqu" he says it means "they invented": one says "so-and-so invented a lie against so-and-so" when he fabricated and forged it (the editor refers to al-Farra' and Abu Ubayda). He then gives what the interpreters said: report 13681, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha, that they conjectured; 13682, from Ibn Abbas, that they made sons and daughters for Him without knowledge; 13683 and 13684, from Mujahid, that they lied; and 13685, from Qatada, that they lied: the Arabs made daughters for God while keeping for themselves the sons they desire, and the Jews made a kinship between Him and the jinn. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 8}

Report 13686, from Qatada: they conjectured sons and daughters for Him. Report 13687, from al-Suddi: "qata'u" sons and daughters for Him, the Arabs saying that the angels are God's daughters and the Jews and Christians that the Messiah and Uzayr are God's sons. The editor notes that this word stands thus in both the manuscript and the print, in the sense of "invented, claimed, ascribed", that he did not find the usage in the dictionaries, finds it close to sound, and allows that it may be a corruption of a word he could not make out. Report 13688, from Ibn Zayd: "kharaqu" means they lied, since God has no sons or daughters; the Christians said the Messiah is God's son, and the polytheists said the angels are God's daughters. Report 13689, from Ibn Jurayj: the saying that the jinn are partners is the saying of the zindiqs (heretics), and from Mujahid that they lied. Report 13690, from al-Dahhak: they ascribed to Him. Report 13691, from Abu Amr: its meaning is that they lied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 9}

Al-Tabari gives the meaning as: they made the jinn partners to God in their worship of Him, while He alone created them, with no partner, helper or supporter; and they conjectured lies about God, inventing sons and daughters without knowledge of the truth of what they said, out of ignorance of God and His greatness, and of the fact that one who is God cannot have sons, daughters, a consort or a partner in His creation. On "glory be to Him and exalted above what they describe" he says God is far above and raised over what these ignorant ones ascribe to Him, because that belongs to creatures, from whom intercourse arises and whom weakness and desire drive to take a consort; God is neither powerless nor weak and in need. He notes that "ta'ala" is of the pattern "tafa'ala", from "'uluww" (height). Report 13692, from Qatada, glosses "'amma yasifun" as "of what they lie". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 10}

Al-Tabari supposes that Qatada meant only that they lie in describing God as they did, by claiming sons and daughters for Him, not that he took the meaning of "wasf" (describing) to be lying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 11}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
