## Summary
Verse 278 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 278 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-278|2:278]]), pp. 22 and 23. Al-Tabari explains the address to the believers as those who affirmed God and His Messenger; fearing God as fearing Him for themselves, obeying what He ordered and stopping from what He forbade; the command to leave what remains of usury as leaving off claiming the surplus that remained due over the capital they had before they added to it; and the condition if you are believers as making their faith true in words, and their affirmation by their tongues, by their deeds; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 22} The editor notes that the phrase by your deeds attaches to making it true. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 22} Al-Tabari says it is reported that this verse came down about people who became Muslim while owed money from usury they had charged others, having collected some and some remained, so God pardoned what they had collected before the verse came down and forbade them to claim what remained. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 22} In 6258, al-Suddi: it came down about al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib and a man of the Banu al-Mughira who were partners in the pre-Islamic period, lending on usury to people of Thaqif of the Banu Amr, and Islam came while they had great sums owed in usury, so God sent down that they leave what remained of the surplus from the pre-Islamic period. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 22} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 23} The editor notes that manuscript and print have a past-tense form and that the right one is what he printed from al-Durr al-manthur and al-Baghawi, and explains the word for lending. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 23}

In 6259, Ibn Jurayj: Thaqif had made peace with the Prophet on the term that what was due to them in usury from people and what people owed them in usury was set aside; at the conquest he appointed Attab ibn Usayd over Mecca, and the Banu Amr ibn Umayr ibn Awf were taking usury from the Banu al-Mughira, who had lent to them on usury before Islam, and Islam came while much was owed them; the Banu Amr came asking for their usury and the Banu al-Mughira refused to give it in Islam and took it to Attab ibn Usayd, who wrote to the Messenger of God, and the verse came down, through to the end of the passage at ([[passage/quran-2-279|2:279]]); the Prophet wrote it to Attab and said if they agree, and if not tell them of war. From Ibn Jurayj from Ikrima: these were Banu Amr ibn Umayr, Mas'ud, Abd Yalil, Habib and Rabi'a, and Abd Yalil, Habib, Rabi'a, Hilal and Mas'ud became Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 23} The editor refers to what Ibn Hajar says in al-Isaba under Bilal al-Thaqafi, and thinks the mention of Thaqif's treaty before the words at the conquest open to question, which he addresses in the occasions of revelation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 23} We stop at 6259; report 6260 begins at the foot of p. 23. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanations are his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.
