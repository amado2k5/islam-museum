## Summary
Verse 117 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 117 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "your Lord knows best who strays from His way, and He knows best the guided", al-Tabari says: Muhammad, your Lord, who forbade you to obey these who equate idols with Him, lest they lead you astray from His way, knows better than you and than all His creation which of His creatures strays from His way through the embellished speech that the devils inspire to one another, so that they turn from obeying Him and following what He commanded; "and He knows best the guided" means that He knows better than you and than they who is upright and sound, none of them hidden from Him. So follow, Muhammad, what I commanded you, and desist from what I forbade you of obeying those I forbade you to obey, for I know better than you who among My creatures is the guide and who the misleader. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 65}

The grammarians differed over the place of "man" in "knows best who strays". Some Basran grammarians said it is genitive, with a "ba" understood, the sense being "your Lord knows best of whoever strays" (the editor notes this is al-Akhfash's view); some Kufan grammarians said it is nominative, since it means "which" and "yadillu" governs it (this is al-Farra's view). Al-Tabari says the correct view is that it is nominative with "yadillu", in the sense of "which", and that a noun in the genitive with no genitive-causing particle is unknown in Arab speech, so this view has no parallel. Some claimed that "a'lam" here means "ya'lam", citing a line of Hatim al-Ta'i and a line of al-Khansa' in her elegy of her brother Sakhr (the footnote says Hatim's line is not in his diwan but in al-Qurtubi's commentary, explains the word "hilf", and gives the setting of al-Khansa's line). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 66}

Al-Tabari says: what the holder of this interpretation said, though permissible in Arab speech, does not apply to God's saying "your Lord knows best who strays from His way". For He joined to it "and He knows best the guided", and the entering of the "ba" before "the guided" shows that "a'lam" does not mean "ya'lam", since when it has the sense of "yaf'al" (a verb) it is not linked with the "ba", as one does not say "he ya'lam bi-Zayd" in the sense of "he knows Zayd". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 67}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
