**Commentary on verse 11 (pp. 33-36)**

He then explains "If God were to hasten evil for the people as they hasten good, their term would have been decided for them; but We leave those who do not hope to meet Us wandering blindly in their transgression". Had God hastened for the people the answering of their prayers for evil, in what would harm them in person or property, as He hastens to answer when they ask for good, they would have perished and their death, which is the term, been hastened. "Would have been decided" means their term would have been finished for them and cast to them; the editor refers to the glosses on "term" and "decided" and says the printed text changed one word because it could not read the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 33}

For "decided" in the sense of "finished" he cites Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali's description of two coats of mail that David finished or Tubba' made (the line described, not quoted), which the editor traces to the second volume. "We leave those who do not hope to meet Us" means We leave those who do not fear Our punishment or believe in resurrection and revival "in their transgression", their rebellion and arrogance, "wandering blindly", that is hesitating. God told of these deniers of resurrection their transgression and hesitation when the answer to their prayer for evil is hastened, because that would lead them to draw near to the idol one of them associates with God, or to ascribe it to being its doing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 34}

The interpreters said the same. Reports 17572 and 17573, from Mujahid, on "if God were to hasten evil for the people as they hasten good": it is a person's saying of his child and his wealth when angry with it, "O God, do not bless it, and curse it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 34}

Al-Tabari completes "If God were to hasten evil for the people": had God hastened the answer for them in that, as He answers in good, He would have destroyed them. Report 17574, from Mujahid: it is a person's saying to his child and his wealth when angry, "O God, do not bless it and curse it"; "their term would have been decided" means He would have destroyed and killed whoever was prayed against. Report 17575, from Mujahid, is the same and adds: We do not destroy the people of polytheism, but leave them in their transgression, wandering blindly. Report 17576, from Qatada: it is a man's praying against himself and his wealth for what he would hate to have answered. Ibn Zayd on "their term would have been decided": We would have destroyed them, and he recited "He would not have left a living creature on its back", saying He would destroy them all. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 35}

He explains that "their hastening" is in the accusative because the verb "hasten" falls upon it, as one says "I stood today your standing" meaning like your standing; it is not a verbal noun of "hasten", for if it were the comparing particle could not suitably enter it; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. He notes the readers' difference on "their term would have been decided": most readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read it in the passive with "term" in the nominative, and most Syrians read the active, "God would have decided their term for them". He says the two readings agree in meaning, so whichever one reads is right, but he reads the passive because most readers do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 36}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
