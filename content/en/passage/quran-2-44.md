## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "Do you command people to righteousness and forget yourselves" al-Tabari records the differences over the meaning of "birr", while all agree that every act of obedience to God is called birr. From Ibn Abbas he gives that it means forbidding people to disbelieve in what they hold of prophecy and covenant from the Torah while neglecting themselves, and through al-Dahhak that it is commanding people to enter Muhammad's religion and establish prayer. From al-Suddi and Qatada he gives that they bade people obey God and be mindful of Him while disobeying Him; from Ibn Jurayj that the People of the Book and hypocrites told people to fast and pray while leaving the action undone; from Ibn Zayd that when someone came asking for something in which they had no stake, the Jews told him to follow the truth; and from Abu al-Darda that a man does not fully understand until he abhors people for God's sake and then turns back on himself and abhors himself more. The editor traces this last report and notes that Abu Qilaba's transmission from Abu al-Darda is mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 7} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 8} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 9}
Al-Tabari's own judgement is that these views are close in meaning, since all agree they commanded what pleases God and went against it in their deeds, and that the reading supported by the wording is: do you command people to obey God while leaving yourselves to disobey Him, a reproach. He takes their "forgetting themselves" like "they forgot God, so He forgot them", meaning they abandoned obedience and He withheld His reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 9}
On "while you recite the Book" he says the sense is that they study and read, the Book being the Torah. On "do you not reason" he says: do you not grasp the ugliness of the disobedience you forbid to others, knowing that your duty to God and to follow Muhammad is the same as that of those you command; he holds this confirms what he said, that the Jewish rabbis commanded others to follow Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 10}
