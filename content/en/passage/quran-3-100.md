## Summary
Verse 100 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 100 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Who is addressed (p. 58).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over who is meant. Some said the believers are the Aws and Khazraj and the People of the Book is the Jew Shas ibn Qays, as in the report of Zayd ibn Aslam given earlier (7524). Others agreed but said the man who had words with other Ansar until they were ready to fight, and in whom the Jew found a weak point, was Tha'laba ibn 'Unma al-Ansari. The editor corrects the spelling of 'Unma with the unpointed letter ayn, as the manuscript has it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 58}

**Al-Suddi on Tha'laba ibn 'Unma (7529, pp. 58-59).** Al-Tabari relates from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi that the verse was revealed about Tha'laba ibn 'Unma al-Ansari, who had words with some of the Ansar; a Jew of the Banu Qaynuqa went between them and set them on one another until the two parties of the Aws and Khazraj were ready to take up arms and fight. The verse came down and its sense, in this report, is that if they took arms and fought they would be in disbelief. The editor glosses the verb used as meaning to incite among them and stir up trouble. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 58} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 59}

**The report of Mujahid (7530, p. 59).** Al-Tabari relates from al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ja'far ibn Sulayman, Humayd al-A'raj, Mujahid that the Ansar were two clans, the Aws and Khazraj, with war, blood and hatred between them in the pre-Islamic period until God favoured them with Islam and the Prophet, put out the war and joined them. While a man of the Aws and a man of the Khazraj sat talking with a Jew beside them, he kept reminding them of their days and enmity until they insulted and then fought each other; each called his people, they came out armed and ranked against each other. The Prophet was in Medina that day, came and kept walking between the two sides to calm them until they returned and laid down their arms; God revealed the Qur'an on it up to [[passage/quran-3-105|3:105]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 59}

**Al-Tabari's interpretation (pp. 59-60).** Al-Tabari says the addressees are those who believed in God and His messenger and acknowledged what their Prophet brought them, and that the sense is that if they obey a group of those who profess the Book, of the people of the Torah and Gospel, and accept what they command, they will lead them astray and turn them back after their belief in their Lord's messenger to denial of the truth they had believed. God forbade them to take them as counsellors or accept opinion or advice from them, and informed them that they hold toward them rancour, deceit, envy and hatred. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 59} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 60}

**Qatada's warning (7531-7532, p. 60).** Al-Tabari relates from Qatada (7531, via Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid, Sa'id) that God had told them beforehand about those people, warned them and informed them of their error, so they should not trust them with their religion or take their counsel for themselves, for they are enemies, envious and astray; how could they trust a people who disbelieved in their book, killed their messengers, were bewildered in their religion and unable to manage themselves, people of suspicion and enmity. Al-Rabi gave the like (7532, via al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father). These are reports as transmitted; the museum describes them and gives no verdict on them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 60}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 60}
