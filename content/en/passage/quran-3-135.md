## Summary
Verse 135 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 135 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The first reports (p. 217).** Abu Ja'far says the Garden described is prepared for the God-fearing who spend in ease and hardship, and for those who commit an indecency or wrong themselves and then remember God and ask forgiveness; all these attributes belong to the God-fearing mentioned in [[passage/quran-3-133|3:133]].
- From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ja'far ibn Sulayman, Thabit al-Bunani (7844): I heard al-Hasan recite the previous verse about those who spend and then this verse up to the mention of the reward of the workers, and he said these two descriptions are of one man. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 217}
- From Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Mansur, Mujahid (7845): these are two sins; the indecency is one sin and wronging themselves another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 217}

**The meaning of "fahisha" (p. 218).** Abu Ja'far says "fahisha" is an adjective for an omitted noun: they did an ugly deed, the ugly act that goes beyond what God permitted. The root "fuhsh" is ugliness and going beyond bound and measure in anything: an excessively tall man is called "fahish al-tul", ugly speech "kalam fahish", and the speaker "afhasha in his speech". The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "fahsha'" (4: 571 and 3: 303, as printed). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 218}

**Those who took it to mean adultery (p. 218).** It was also said that the indecency here means adultery.
- From al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Azim, Hibban, Hammad, Thabit, Jabir (7846): by the Lord of the Ka'ba, the people committed adultery. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 218}
- From Muhammad, Ahmad, Asbat, al-Suddi (7847): the indecency is adultery. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 218}

**The meaning of wronging themselves (p. 218).** Abu Ja'far says it means they did to themselves other than what they ought, by committing disobedience that made them deserve punishment.
- From Ibn Waki', his father, Sufyan, Mansur, Ibrahim (7848): the wrong is part of the indecency and the indecency part of the wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 218}

**Remembering and asking forgiveness (p. 219).** Abu Ja'far says they remembered God's threat for the disobedience they had committed and asked their Lord to cover their sins by His pardon from punishment; and who, he asks, pardons the doer of a sin and veils it for him except God. He explains persisting as not staying on the sins they committed, and the knowing as not staying on them deliberately while knowing that God forbade them and threatened the doer with punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 219}

**The report on this verse as a lightening for the community (pp. 219-220).** It was said the verse came down specially to lighten and ease for this community what the Children of Israel were tried with of grave affliction over their sins; the editor says the printed text read "ummuna" for "ummatana", the object of "specially".
- From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Ata ibn Abi Rabah (7849): they said: Prophet of God, the Children of Israel are more honoured with God than we are; when one of them sinned, the atonement for his sin was found written on his doorstep: cut off your ear, cut off your nose, do it. The Prophet was silent, and the verses from [[passage/quran-3-133|3:133]] to this one came down; he said: shall I not tell you something better than that? and recited these verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 219}
- From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Umar ibn Abi Khalifa al-Abdi, Ali ibn Zayd ibn Jud'an (7850): Ibn Mas'ud said: when the Children of Israel sinned, the sin and its atonement were written on his door; we were given better than that, this verse. The editor says Abi Khalifa's name is Hajjaj ibn Utab, trustworthy, died 189 as in al-Tahdhib, and the printed text and manuscript wrongly read "Umar ibn Khalifa". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 220}

**Iblis weeping (p. 220).**
- From Ibn Humayd, Yahya ibn Wadih, Ja'far ibn Sulayman, Thabit al-Bunani (7851): when the verse at [[passage/quran-4-110|4:110]] came down, Iblis wept in alarm at it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 220}
- From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ja'far ibn Sulayman, Thabit (7852, the printed number is garbled): it reached me that Iblis wept when this verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 220}

**The report on ablution, prayer and asking forgiveness (pp. 220-222).**
- From Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba, Uthman the freedman of the family of Abu Aqil al-Thaqafi, Ali ibn Rabi'a, a man of Fazara called Asma' or Ibn Asma', Ali (7853): when I heard something from the Prophet God benefited me by what He willed; Abu Bakr told me, and Abu Bakr spoke true, from the Prophet: no servant (Shu'ba said: I think he said "Muslim") commits a sin, then makes ablution, prays two rak'as and asks God's forgiveness for that sin, but he is forgiven. Shu'ba said he recited one of the two verses, [[passage/quran-4-123|4:123]] and [[passage/quran-3-135|3:135]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 220}
- From Ibn Waki', his father, and al-Fadl ibn Ishaq, Waki', Mis'ar and Sufyan, Uthman ibn al-Mughira al-Thaqafi, Ali ibn Rabi'a al-Walibi, Asma' ibn al-Hakam al-Fazari, Ali ibn Abi Talib (7854): when I heard a hadith from the Prophet God benefited me by what He willed, and when someone else told me of him I made him swear, and if he swore I believed him; Abu Bakr told me, and Abu Bakr spoke true, that he said: no man commits a sin, then makes ablution and prays (one narrator said two rak'as, the other said "then prays") and asks God's forgiveness, but he is forgiven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 221}
- From al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar, Sa'd ibn Sa'id ibn Abi Sa'id al-Maqburi, his brother, his grandfather, Ali (7855): no one told me from the Prophet but I asked him to swear by God that he heard it, except Abu Bakr, who did not lie; Abu Bakr told me that the Prophet said: no servant commits a sin, then rises at the remembrance of it, makes ablution, prays two rak'as and asks God's forgiveness for it, but God forgives him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 222}

**The editor's notes on these reports (pp. 220-222).** On 7853 Shakir identifies Uthman the freedman as Uthman ibn al-Mughira, freedman of Thaqif, trustworthy (so held by Ahmad and Ibn Ma'in), Ali ibn Rabi'a ibn Nadla al-Walibi al-Asadi as a trustworthy Successor reported by the two Sahihs and the Sunan compilers, and Asma' or Ibn Asma' (Shu'ba's doubt) as Asma' ibn al-Hakam al-Fazari, a trustworthy Successor vouched for by al-Ijli, against whom Ibn Abi Hatim records no criticism. He notes that al-Bukhari in the Kabir mentioned his narrating this report and said it was not corroborated and that Companions narrated from one another without making each other swear, which Shakir says, as al-Mizzi did, does not harm the report. He traces it: al-Tayalisi from Shu'ba (the first hadith of his Musnad); Ahmad's Musnad (48 from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba, 47 from Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, 56 from Abu Kamil from Abu Awana from Uthman ibn Abi Zur'a, who is Uthman ibn al-Mughira); and al-Tirmidhi (no. 406 with the editor's commentary, and in the Tafsir), who called it a good report known only by this route, raised by Shu'ba and others such as Abu Awana but stopped at Ali by Sufyan al-Thawri and Mis'ar. Shakir says a stopped version does not undermine a raised one, and that al-Tirmidhi was badly mistaken about Mis'ar and Sufyan, whose narration, next in al-Tabari, is also raised. On 7854 he calls it a shortened repeat of the previous report, says he does not know al-Fadl ibn Ishaq, al-Tabari's teacher, and found no biography, perhaps a corruption of another name, and says Ahmad's Musnad (no. 2) has it from Waki' from Mis'ar and Sufyan raised, which answers al-Tirmidhi. He adds that Ibn Kathir took it from the Musnad and listed Ali ibn al-Madini, al-Humaydi, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba, the Sunan authors, Ibn Hibban, al-Bazzar and al-Daraqutni as narrating it by routes through Uthman ibn al-Mughira; that al-Suyuti added Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi in the Shu'ab; and that al-Mundhiri in al-Targhib gave it briefly and said Ibn Khuzayma put it in his Sahih without a chain, mentioning two rak'as. On 7855 he calls it a third chain, very weak: al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar is trustworthy and a genealogist, nephew of Mus'ab al-Zubayri, author of the Nasab Quraysh; Sa'id ibn Sa'id al-Maqburi, Abu Hatim said, is upright himself but narrates from his brother Abd Allah ibn Sa'id, weak in hadith, so one cannot tell whether it is from him or his brother; the brother is very weak and was accused of lying. He says the two earlier chains suffice for the report's soundness without this feeble one (the note ends there in the printed edition). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 222}

**Explaining the remembering and asking forgiveness (pp. 222-223).** Abu Ja'far says the sense of remembering and asking forgiveness is as he has explained.
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7856): that is, if they commit an indecency or wrong themselves by disobedience, they remember God's prohibition of it and what He forbade them, ask forgiveness for it and know that none forgives a sin but He. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 115-116), the continuation of the reports ending at 7839. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 223}

**The grammar (p. 223).** Abu Ja'far says the divine name after "illa" in the clause on the forgiving of sin is in the nominative though no negation precedes it. What follows "illa" takes the case of what precedes it when that is indefinite and accompanied by negation, as in "no one in the house except your brother", whereas for "the people stood except your father" the right form is the accusative; "man" with its clause is definite, and the nominative came because the sense is: does anyone forgive a sin, or: no one forgives a sin except God, so the word is raised according to the sense of the sentence rather than its wording. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 234) and says the manuscript and printed text read "ma ba'da illa min Allah", where the word "name" must be added. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 223}

**The disagreement over the meaning of persisting (pp. 223-226).** Abu Ja'far says the interpreters differed on three views.
- First view: they did not hold fast to the sins they committed nor stay on them, but repented and asked forgiveness. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7857, the number unreadable in print and inferred from the sequence): beware of persistence, for those who perish are the persisters who press on, whom fear of God does not restrain from the forbidden and who do not repent of a sin until death comes. From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar, Qatada (7858): pressing on in disobedience to God, not restrained by fear of God until God's command came. From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7859): they did not stay in disobedience to Me like one who associates others with Me in his disbelief (from Ibn Hisham, 3: 116, continuing 7856). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 224}
- Second view: they did not commit the sin when they intended it. From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar, al-Hasan (7860): a servant's committing a sin is persistence until he repents. From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7861): they did not fall into it. The editor notes that the manuscript only repeats the wording of the verse and that the printed text is closer to the right sense, as al-Tabari's preference below shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 224}
- Third view: persistence is staying silent about the sin and leaving the asking of forgiveness. From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7862): they keep silent and do not ask forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 224}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 225-226).** Abu Ja'far says the soundest view in his opinion is that persistence is staying on the sin deliberately and leaving repentance from it (the editor notes the copies read "or leaving repentance" and that he put "and"). He finds no sense in the view that persistence is committing the sin, because God praised for leaving persistence the one who did commit it, and if the one who commits were persistent by committing, asking forgiveness would have no understandable point, since it is repentance and regret, and there is no point asking forgiveness for a sin its doer did not commit. He cites a saying reported from the Prophet: he did not persist who asked forgiveness, even if he returned seventy times in a day. This is report 7863, from al-Husayn ibn Yazid al-Subay'i, Abd al-Hamid al-Himmani, Uthman ibn Waqid, Abu Nusayra, a freedman of Abu Bakr, Abu Bakr, the Prophet. He then argues that if committing were persisting the saying would have no meaning, since committing, if it were persistence, would carry a name that nothing but its own removal could lift, as repentance does not remove from the adulterer the name adulterer nor from the killer the name killer; the report shows that the one asking forgiveness is not persistent, so persistence is not committing but staying on it, as he said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 225}
- The editor's note on 7863 (pp. 225-226): al-Husayn ibn Yazid al-Subay'i was doubted in his attribution at 2892 and may be a teacher of al-Tabari otherwise unknown; Abd al-Hamid al-Himmani al-Kufi is trustworthy (Ibn Ma'in) and in the two Sahihs; Uthman ibn Waqid is trustworthy (Ibn Ma'in; Ahmad said he saw no harm in him); Abu Nusayra al-Wasiti, named Muslim ibn Ubayd, is a trustworthy Successor. Ibn Kathir (2: 248) gave the report from Abu Ya'la through al-Himmani, with a misprint, Shakir thinks, in the byname, name and attribution of Abu Nusayra, and noted that Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Bazzar narrated it from Uthman ibn Waqid, that Abu Nusayra was vouched for by Ahmad and Ibn Hibban, and that the remark of Ibn al-Madini and al-Tirmidhi that its chain is not that strong, because the freedman of Abu Bakr is unknown, does no harm, since he is a senior Successor whose link to Abu Bakr suffices, so the report is good (the note continues on the next page). Al-Suyuti added Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi in the Shu'ab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 226}

**The meaning of "while they know" (p. 226).** They differed on it.
- Some said: they know they have sinned. From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7864): they know they have sinned and then stayed and did not ask forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 226}
- Others said: they know that what they did is disobedience to Him. From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7865): they know what I forbade them of worshipping other than Me. The editor says the manuscript reads "disobedience to God", and that the copies read a different preposition where he kept Ibn Hisham's wording (3: 116, continuing 7859). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 226}
- Abu Ja'far says his explanation of the better view has already been given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 226}
