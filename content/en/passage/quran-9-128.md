**Commentary on verse 128 (pp. 584-587)**

Al-Tabari says to the Arabs: a Messenger of God has come to you, people, from among yourselves, whom you know and who is not from others, so you cannot suspect him in his counsel to you; "it grieves him that you suffer" means that hardship, ill and harm befalling them is grievous to him; "he is concerned for you" means eager for the guidance of those of you who are astray and for their repentance and return to the truth; "to the believers he is kind and merciful". The editor refers to earlier discussion of "from among themselves", dearness, suffering, eagerness, kindness and mercy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 584}

Report 17504 is from Ja'far ibn Muhammad from his father, through Ibn Waki' and Ibn Uyayna: nothing of polytheism touched him in his birth. Report 17505 is from him, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn Uyayna: nothing of the births of the pre-Islamic age touched him, and the Prophet said, "I was born of marriage, not of fornication"; report 17506 gives it similarly. Report 17507 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: God made him from among themselves so they would not envy him for the prophethood and honour God gave him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 585}

On "it grieves him that you suffer" the interpreters differed. Some said it means that you go astray; report 17508 is from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Talq ibn Ghannam, al-Hakam ibn Zuhayr and al-Suddi: that you go astray. Others said it means grievous to him is the hardship of your believer; report 17509 is from Qatada. Al-Tabari says the better of the two is Ibn Abbas's, because God stated generally that the hardship of his people was grievous to him and did not limit it to believers, so the hardship of all of them was grievous to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 586}

If someone asks how he can be described as grieved by the hardship of all of them when he fights their unbelievers, takes their children captive and takes their property, the answer is that their becoming Muslim, if they had, was dearer to him than their staying in unbelief and denying him until they deserved that from God; God described him as grieved by their hardship because it was grievous to him that they should do what brings them hardship, that is, go astray and so earn God's hardship by killing and captivity. The word "what" in "what you suffer" is the subject of "grievous to him". The editor notes a gap in the manuscript in one phrase which he completed by his own judgment. As for "he is eager for you", its sense is as explained. Report 17510 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: eager that God guide those of them who are astray; the next report from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: eager that those who have not become Muslims do so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 587}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
