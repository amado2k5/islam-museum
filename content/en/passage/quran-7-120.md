**Commentary on verses 120 to 122 (p. 32)**

Al-Tabari says the sorcerers, when they saw God's great power, fell on their faces in prostration to their Lord, saying "we believe in the Lord of the worlds", meaning we hold true what Moses brought, and the one we must worship is the one who owns jinn, men and all things and orders it all, "the Lord of Moses and Aaron", not Pharaoh. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "prostrate" and "worlds". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 32}

Report 14954, from Ibn Abbas through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar, Sufyan, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: when the sorcerers saw what they saw, they knew it was a matter from heaven and not sorcery, so they fell down in prostration and said they believed in the Lord of the worlds, the Lord of Moses and Aaron. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 32}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
