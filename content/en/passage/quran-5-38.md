**Commentary on verse 38: "the thief, male and female, cut off their hands", pp. 294-296**

Al-Tabari says: whoever steals, man or woman, cut off the hand as recompense for what they earned, an exemplary punishment from God by which people are deterred; "the thief" is in the nominative since they are not specified, and had a particular thief and thief-woman been meant the accusative would have been fitting. It is reported that Ibn Mas'ud read "the thieves, men and women"; reports 11907 and 11908, from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, say it was so in their reading and 'Abdallah's, with "cut off their right hands", which proves his sense and the nominative. He says the hands meant are the right hands, as report 11909, from al-Suddi, and report 11910, from al-Sha'bi on 'Abdallah's reading, give.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 294}

They then differed over the thief God meant. Some said a thief of three dirhams or more, the view of a group of the people of Medina including Malik ibn Anas, who argued that the Prophet cut the hand for a shield worth three dirhams, as report 11911 says; the editor traces it to the Muwatta', al-Bukhari and Muslim. Others said a thief of a quarter dinar or its value, among them al-Awza'i, who argued from a report from 'A'isha that the Prophet said cutting is for a quarter dinar or more, report 11912.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 295}

Others said ten dirhams or more, among them Abu Hanifa and his companions, who argued from a report from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr and Ibn 'Abbas that the Prophet cut the hand for a shield worth ten dirhams, report 11913, whose attribution the editor checks. Others said the thief of little or much, arguing that the verse is on its apparent sense and no one may specify any of it except by a proof one must accept, that no report is sound from the Prophet that it concerns a particular kind of thief, that the reports of what he cut for are confused and varied, and that Ibn al-Zubayr cut for a dirham. It is reported from Ibn 'Abbas that the verse is general; report 11914 says Najda al-Hanafi asked him whether "the thief, male and female" is specific or general and he said it is general.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 296}

Al-Tabari chooses that the sound view is that the verse means a particular kind of thief, those who steal a quarter dinar or more or its value, because of the sound report from the Prophet that cutting is for a quarter dinar or more. He says he went through the opponents' views with their arguments and the clarification of the soundest in his book on theft and disliked repeating it here. "Recompense for what they earned, an exemplary punishment from God" means repayment to them for their stealing and their work in robbery in disobedience of God, and punishment from God for their banditry.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 297}

Report 11915, from Qatada, says: do not pity them so as to hold back applying the penalties on them, for by God, God never commanded anything but it is right nor forbade anything but it is corrupt; the editor comments in a note on a time when penalties were suspended under claim of pity for one who incurred one. 'Umar ibn al-Khattab used to say: be hard on thieves and cut them, hand for hand and foot for foot.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 297}

"And God is Mighty, Wise" means mighty in His vengeance on this thief and others who disobey Him, wise in His ruling on them and His decree on them; so, believers, do not be slack in applying My ruling to thieves and others of the criminals on whom I made penalties binding in this world as punishment, for in My wisdom I decreed it for them, knowing it is good for them and for you.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 298}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
