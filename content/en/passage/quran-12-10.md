**Commentary on verse 10 (pp. 564-567)**

On [[passage/quran-12-10|12:10]] al-Tabari says: one of Joseph's brothers said 'do not kill Joseph'. It was said that the speaker was Reuben, who was Joseph's mother's sister's son; report 18799, from Qatada: it was told us that he was Reuben, the eldest of them, Joseph's maternal cousin, and he forbade them to kill him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 564}

Report 18800, from Ibn Ishaq: it was told me, and God knows best, that the one who said it was Reuben, the eldest of Jacob's sons, the most sound of them in view about him; 18801, from Qatada: the eldest of his brothers and Joseph's maternal cousin, and he forbade killing him. It was said that the speaker was Simeon; 18802, from Mujahid: he is Simeon; the editor notes that report 18831 gives another name, Judah. As for 'and cast him into the bottom of the well', it means cast him to the depth of the well where news of him would be lost. The readers differ: most readers of Medina read 'ghayabati l-jubb' in the plural, and most of the other cities 'ghayabati l-jubb' with the singular 'ghayaba'; al-Tabari says the singular reading is dearer to him. The 'jubb' is a well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 565}

It was said that it is the name of a well of Jerusalem; reports 18803 and 18804, from Qatada: a well in Jerusalem. The 'ghayaba' is anything that hides something, and the 'jubb' is a well not lined with stones; reports 18805, 18806 and 18807, from Qatada: in some part of it, at the bottom; 18808, from Ibn Abbas: it was said by their eldest, the one who stayed behind, and the jubb is a well in Syria. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 566}

Report 18809, from Ibn Abbas: meaning the well; 18810, from al-Dahhak: the jubb is the well. As for 'some travellers may pick him up', it means some passers-by of those on the road take him, 'if you are going to act' meaning if you are going to do what I tell you; it is reported that some bedouin picked him up; report 18811, from Ibn Abbas: some bedouin picked him up. It is reported of al-Hasan al-Basri that he read it with a feminine ta', report 18812, al-Hasan taking the action of some of the travellers as their action, and the Arabs do this in speech about a thing annexed to a feminine, where what is said of part is said of all, and he cites a line of Jarir about the passing years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 567}
