**Commentary on verse 202 (pp. 337-340)**

Al-Tabari says: the brothers of the devils are drawn on by the devils into error, by "they draw them on" meaning they add to them, "then they do not fall short" of what falls short… The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "error". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 337}

…those who fear God, when a touch of Satan touches them. This is a report from God about the party of belief and the party of unbelief: that the party of belief and the people of God-fearing, when Satan makes them slip, remember God's greatness and punishment, and dread of Him holds them back from disobeying Him and returns them to repentance and turning to God from what slip they made; and that the party of the unbelievers, Satan adds to their error when they commit a sin against God, and neither God-fearing nor fear of the return to Him holds them from persisting in it and adding to it, so they are always adding to committing sin and Satan always adds to it, the human not falling short of any of the vile deeds nor Satan of drawing him on in it. The editor says the printed edition reads "then they do not fall short of what those who fear God fell short of", that he kept the manuscript though in doubt of its quality, that Abu Hayyan mentioned a sense close to it, and that al-Tabari used singular pronouns after a plural, as often happens in his commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 337} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 338}
Report 15564, from Ibn Abbas: neither do the humans stop the evil they do, nor do the devils hold back from them; report 15565, from Ibn Abbas: they are the jinn who inspire their friends among humans, "then they do not fall short" meaning they do not tire; report 15566, from al-Suddi: the brothers of the devils among the polytheists, whom Satan draws on into error; report 15567, from Abd Allah ibn Kathir: their brothers of the jinn draw on their brothers of humankind, "then they do not fall short" meaning the human does not, and "drawing on" is adding, meaning the people of association, who do not fall short as those who fear God fall short, do not turn back, and are not held back by belief; and from Mujahid: "their brothers" of the devils "draw them on into error", urging them on in ignorance, they draw on the people of association; and Ibn Jurayj recalled [[passage/quran-7-179|7:179]], saying: these are the humans, of whom God says "their brothers draw them on into error"; report 15568, from Qatada: the brothers of the devils, whom the devils draw on into error; report 15569, from Mujahid: of the devils, who draw them on in making them ignorant. The editor explains that "they do not turn back" is from "he turned back from the ugly", meaning he regretted it and drew away and held off. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 338} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 339}
Some took "then they do not fall short" to mean that the devils too do not fall short in drawing their brothers on into error, and report 15570, from Qatada: from them, and they do not have mercy on them. Al-Tabari says: we have already shown the better of the two interpretations in our view, and we chose what we chose because God described in the verse before it the people who believe in Him and their drawing back from disobeying Him to what He loves when they remember His greatness, then followed that with the report of the brothers of the devils and their committing disobedience to Him, so it was better to describe their persisting in it, since it follows the report of the believers falling short of it. As for "yamuddunahum", the reciters differed: some Medinans read it with damma on the first letter, from "amdadtu", and most Kufan and Basran reciters read it with fatha, from "madadtu". Al-Tabari chooses the fatha, because what the devils draw their brothers the polytheists on with is an addition of the same kind as the one drawn on, and when it is of the same kind as the thing drawn on, Arab usage is "madadtu" and not "amdadtu". As for "yaqsirun", the reciters follow the dialect of those who say "aqsartu", the Arabs having two forms, "qasartu" from a thing and "aqsartu" from it. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and says he corrected a vocalisation there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 339} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 340}
