## Summary
Verse 198 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 198 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "there is no blame on you in seeking bounty from your Lord": al-Tabari says it means there is no blame (junah, which means hardship) on you, believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 162} From Ibn 'Abbas through 'Ali ibn Abi Talha: there is no hardship on you in buying and selling before ihram and after it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 163} Seeking bounty means looking for it from God; the Arabs say "ibtaghaytu" and "baghaytu", and he cites a line of 'Abd Banu al-Hashas; the editor notes that al-Tabari's version has "hatta" in the sense of "illa", a rare usage that Ibn Hisham mentions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 163} It is also said that it means seeking God's provision through trade, and that the verse came down about people who thought it wrong to trade once in ihram, seeking righteousness thereby; God told them there is no righteousness in that and that they may seek His bounty by buying and selling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 163}

From Mujahid: they performed hajj and did not trade, so the verse came down, and he said it applies at the season; in another report, some people did not trade during the days of hajj and the verse came down about them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 164} From Buraida: when you are in ihram, you may buy and sell. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 164} From Abu Umama al-Taymi: I said to Ibn 'Umar, we are people who hire out our camels, do we have a hajj? He said, do you not circle the House, come to the standing place, throw the pebbles and shave your heads? We said yes. He said, a man came to the Prophet and asked what you asked me, and he did not know what to say until Jibril came down with this verse, and the Prophet said, you are pilgrims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 164} The editor describes the narrators (Tulayq ibn Muhammad al-Wasiti, reliable; Asbat; al-Hasan ibn 'Amr al-Fuqaymi; Abu Umama al-Taymi, a reliable Successor), and notes that Ahmad reported it in the Musnad, that Ibn Kathir quoted it, and that al-Tabari gives it again by another chain as 3789. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 164} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 165}

From 'Ikrima, and from Ibn 'Abbas through several routes from 'Ata', that he read the verse with the added words "in the seasons of hajj", a reading attributed to him as the narrators report it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 165} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 166} From Mansur: it is trade, buying and selling, and there is no harm in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 165} From Ibn 'Abbas: the marketplaces of the people in the Jahiliyya were 'Ukaz and Dhu al-Majaz, and when Islam came it was as if they disliked that, until the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 165} From Abu Umayma: Ibn 'Umar was asked about a man who performs hajj carrying trade goods and recited the verse; the editor thinks this is Abu Umama al-Taymi and that the report is a statement of Ibn 'Umar, and quotes Ibn Kathir that it is a sound statement with a strong chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 165} From Ibn 'Abbas: they did not trade in the days of hajj and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 166} From Mujahid: trade in this world and reward in the next; they did not buy or sell at 'Arafa in the Jahiliyya, and trade at the seasons was made lawful for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 166}

From Qatada: this tribe of the Arabs would not stop for a broken-down animal or a stray on the night of departure, which they called the night of return, and sought no trade or sale in it; God made all of this lawful for the believers; al-Rabi' ibn Anas the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 166} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 167} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 168} From Ibn al-Zubayr, that he read the verse with the added words "in the seasons of hajj"; the editor corrects the printed "say" to "read" from the manuscript of 'Abd al-Razzaq's tafsir and notes the chain is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 167} From Ibn 'Abbas by several routes: Dhu al-Majaz, 'Ukaz and Majanna were markets in the Jahiliyya and when Islam came they felt it was sinful, so they asked the Prophet and the verse came down; when in ihram they did not trade until they finished their hajj and God made it lawful for them; they avoided buying and selling in the season, saying these are days of remembrance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 167} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 168} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 169} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr: some pilgrims were called the dajj and lodged on the left side of Mina, and they did not trade until the verse came down and they made hajj; the editor explains the dajj as the hired men, camel-drivers, helpers and servants who accompanied the pilgrims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 167} From Mujahid: some people performed hajj without trading until the verse came down and they were allowed trade, riding and provisions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 167} From al-Suddi: it is trade, they traded at the seasons. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 168} From Ibrahim: there is no harm in trade during the hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 168} From Abu Salih, client of 'Umar, who asked him: Commander of the Faithful, did you trade during the hajj? He said: what were their livelihoods other than the hajj? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 168} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 169}

From a man of Banu Taym Allah (the editor identifies him as Abu Umama al-Taymi): he came to Ibn 'Umar and said we are people who hire out camels and they claim we have no hajj; he said, do you not assume ihram as they do, circle as they do and throw as they do? He said yes, he said then you are a pilgrim; a man came to the Prophet and asked what you asked and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 169} From Qatada: when they departed from 'Arafat they did not trade nor stop for a broken-down animal or a stray, and God made that lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 169} The editor notes that the Ibn 'Abbas report on the markets is in al-Bukhari and not Muslim, and was quoted by Ibn Kathir, and that Ahmad reported Ibn 'Umar's report in the Musnad with a sound chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 169} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 170}

On "when you pour forth from 'Arafat": al-Tabari says it means when you return from where you began; hence the man who shakes divining arrows among the gamblers is called a "mufid", for gathering the arrows and then letting them go, and he cites Bishr ibn Abi Khazim al-Asadi; the editor corrects "Ibn Abi Hazim", explains the arrows and the gamblers, and says he could not locate the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 170} The grammarians then differed over "'Arafat" and the reason it takes the nunation though it is a proper name, and whether it names one place or several. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 171} Some Basran grammarians said it was a plural name like "muslimat" and "mu'minat" given to a single place, and it keeps the nunation it had before being a name, the ta' being like the ya' and waw in "muslimin" and "muslimun" and the tanwin like the nun; some Arabs do not give it nunation when so named, treating the ta' like the feminine ha', which is weak and ugly, and they cited Imru' al-Qays's line about Adhri'at. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 171} Some Kufan grammarians said it takes nunation because it is a feminine plural in ta', and any such plural used as the name of a man, place, land or woman takes nunation, the Arabs rarely naming anything by a plural unless it was a plural and then making it singular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 171} The editor attributes the first view to al-Akhfash and explains that Adhri'at is a town in Syria and Yathrib was the name of Medina in the Jahiliyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 171}
