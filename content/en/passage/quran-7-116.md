**Commentary on verse 116 (p. 27)**

Al-Tabari says Moses told the sorcerers, "Throw what you are going to throw", and they threw what they had. "They bewitched the eyes of the people" means that by their illusions and tricks they made the people's eyes imagine the ropes running; "they overawed them" means they frightened the people by what they had bewitched in their eyes, until they feared the staffs and ropes, thinking them snakes; "they brought a great sorcery" means a great and mighty illusion and deception. The editor refers to the explanation of "sorcery" on p. 19. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 27}

Report 14938, from al-Suddi through Musa ibn Harun, Amr and Asbat: Moses said to them, throw what you will throw; and they threw their ropes and staffs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 27}

The rest of report 14938, from al-Suddi: they were some thirty-odd thousand men, each with a rope and a staff; "when they threw, they bewitched the eyes of the people and overawed them" means they terrified them (the editor glosses the word as bringing on fear), "and Moses felt a fear within himself". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 28}

Report 14939, from Ibn Abbas through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar, Sufyan, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: they threw thick long ropes and long pieces of wood, and it seemed to him from their sorcery that they were running. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 28}

Report 14940, from Ibn Ishaq through Ibn Humayd and Salama: fifteen thousand sorcerers were drawn up, each with his ropes and staffs. Moses came out with his brother, leaning on his staff, until he reached the assembly, while Pharaoh sat with the nobles of his kingdom. The sorcerers said, either you throw or we shall be the first to throw; he said, rather you throw, and their ropes and staffs seemed to him, by their sorcery, to be running ([[passage/quran-20-65|20:65]], [[passage/quran-20-66|20:66]]). The first thing they seized by their sorcery was the sight of Moses and Pharaoh, then the sight of the people. Then each threw what was in his hand and they were snakes like mountains, filling the valley and climbing on one another; Moses felt fear within himself ([[passage/quran-20-67|20:67]]) and said: by God, they were staffs in their hands and have become snakes, and what is my staff compared with them? or words to that effect. The editor's footnotes: the word for "terrified" derives from a word for fear; the printed text and manuscript break off the verse before its end and the correction is from the History; "ropes" for "mountains" is a slip corrected from the History, and "my staff" is restored from it; and the report is part of a long one al-Tabari gives in his History (vol. 1, pp. 210-211), continuing report 14934. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 28}

Report 14941, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Ibn Ulayya and Hisham al-Dastuwa'i: Pharaoh gathered seventy thousand sorcerers, who threw seventy thousand ropes and seventy thousand staffs, so that it seemed to him from their sorcery that they were running. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 28}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
