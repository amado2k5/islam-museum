**Commentary on verse 5 (pp. 346-350)**

On "and if you wonder, then wonderful is their saying: when we are dust, shall we really be in a new creation? Those are the ones who disbelieved in their Lord; those have shackles on their necks; those are the companions of the Fire, they abide in it", al-Tabari says: "and if you wonder", Muhammad, at these polytheists who take as gods to worship besides Me what neither harms nor benefits, "then wonderful is their saying: when we are dust", and have decayed and perished, "shall we really be in a new creation?", that our making anew and restoration will be a new creation as we were before our death! in denial of God's power and rejection of reward, punishment and the resurrection after death. Report 20128, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and if you wonder", if you wonder, Muhammad, "then wonderful is their saying: when we are dust, shall we really be in a new creation?", the Merciful, blessed and exalted, wondered at their denial of the resurrection after death. Report 20129, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "and if you wonder, then wonderful is their saying", he said, if you wonder at their denial, when they have seen of God's power, His command and the parables He struck for them, and He showed them the revival of the dead in the lifeless earth, if you wonder at these, then wonder at their saying "when we are dust, shall we really be in a new creation?"; do they not see that We created them from a sperm drop? Is creation from a drop harder, or creation from dust and bones? The editor notes that only the printed text has "Ibrahim" in place of "Ibn Wahb". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 346}

The grammarians differed over the reason for repeating the question in "shall we really be in a new creation?" after the first question in "when we are dust". One of the Basran grammarians said: the first is an adverbial, and the second is the one on which the question falls, as when you say "on Friday, is Zayd leaving?" He said: whoever puts another question on "when we are dust" makes it an adverbial for something mentioned before it, as if they had been asked "will you be raised?" and they said "when we are dust?", and then he makes this a further question; he said this is far-fetched. He said: if you wish, you do not make "a-idha" a question and make the question in the wording fall on "a-inna", as if you said "on Friday, is Abd Allah leaving?" with its negation implied; this is the place where "a-idha" is begun with, and it is not frequent in speech; had you said "today, Abd Allah is leaving" it would not be good, though it is allowed. And the Arabs have said "ma 'alimtu innahu la-salih", meaning "innahu la-salih ma 'alimtu". The editor notes what he corrected in the wording of the manuscript and printed text here and points out that al-Tabari had earlier said that he would come to the right account of omitting the repeated question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 347}

In an important note the editor says that al-Tabari's words at this place need explanation because he made them obscure, that he fears something has dropped out of the text, and that al-Tabari meant to explain the repetition of the question. He sets out the four points. First, that "idha" is an adverbial attached to something implied after it, indicated by "shall we really be in a new creation?", namely "the raising", as if he said "when we are dust, are we raised?", so the question falls on the implied verb, as in "on Friday is Zayd leaving?" meaning "is Zayd leaving on Friday?"; that is the interpretation of the Basran grammarians. Second, that "idha" the adverbial is attached to something implied before it, namely what was said to them, "you will be raised?", and they said "when we are dust?", so the question falls on the adverbial; this is far-fetched because it brings in an implied element before the adverbial with nothing in the speech to indicate it. Third, that the first question is superfluous and you imply its negation, so you repeat the question as in "on Friday, is Abd Allah leaving?", and this repetition is not frequent in speech. Fourth, that "today, Abd Allah is leaving" with "inna" is not good though allowed, because it would require "idha" to be an adverbial attached to the predicate of "inna", and the predicate of "inna" cannot precede it, so even more so what the predicate governs; and he argued for its permissibility from the Arabs' saying "ma 'alimtu innahu la-salih", where "ma" is temporal, "in my knowledge". The editor says he found no commentator or author of Qur'an grammar books who takes up this explanation of Abu Ja'far, and that even Abu Hayyan, for all his following of the grammarians, skipped it; he hopes his explanation is satisfactory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 348}

Another said: "a-idha" is a conditional, not a time, and what follows is its answer if the second has no question, the sense belonging to it because it is what is sought. He said: do you not see that you say "a-in taqum yaqum Zayd" and "yaqumu"? Whoever jussives does so because it falls in the place of the answer to the condition, and whoever raises does so because the question belongs to it; he cited the verse of al-Ra'i, in which he swears that if one travels by night there will not cease to be ahead of him one of his tents moving, with the answer of the oath in the jussive because it fell in the place of the answer to a condition, though raising is the better form. He said: so it is with this verse; and whoever brings in the question a second time does so because it is what is relied upon, leaving the first condition. The editor explains that when a question enters a conditional it belongs to the answer and not the condition, and that al-Tabari had said earlier that any question entering a conditional means it applies to its answer, since the answer is a statement that stands by itself while the condition is a condition for that statement; so when the question is brought to the answer, the first question entering the condition is as if cancelled. On "those are the ones who disbelieved in their Lord" he says, mighty be His praise: those who denied the resurrection and rejected reward and punishment and said "when we are dust, shall we really be in a new {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 349}

creation?" are those who rejected their Lord's power and called His messenger a liar, and they are the ones with shackles on their necks on the Day of Resurrection in the Fire of Hell, "those are the companions of the Fire", the dwellers of the Fire on the Day of Resurrection, "they abide in it", they remain in it for ever, neither dying in it nor leaving it. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "aghlal" and says al-Tabari did not explain it sufficiently. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 350}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
