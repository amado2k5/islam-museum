**Commentary on verse 5 (pp. 346-347)**

On "and if you wonder, then wonderful is their saying: when we are dust, shall we really be in a new creation? Those are the ones who disbelieved in their Lord; those have shackles on their necks; those are the companions of the Fire, they abide in it", al-Tabari says: "and if you wonder", Muhammad, at these polytheists who take as gods to worship besides Me what neither harms nor benefits, "then wonderful is their saying: when we are dust", and have decayed and perished, "shall we really be in a new creation?", that our making anew and restoration will be a new creation as we were before our death! in denial of God's power and rejection of reward, punishment and the resurrection after death. Report 20128, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and if you wonder", if you wonder, Muhammad, "then wonderful is their saying: when we are dust, shall we really be in a new creation?", the Merciful, blessed and exalted, wondered at their denial of the resurrection after death. Report 20129, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "and if you wonder, then wonderful is their saying", he said, if you wonder at their denial, when they have seen of God's power, His command and the parables He struck for them, and He showed them the revival of the dead in the lifeless earth, if you wonder at these, then wonder at their saying "when we are dust, shall we really be in a new creation?"; do they not see that We created them from a sperm drop? Is creation from a drop harder, or creation from dust and bones? The editor notes that only the printed text has "Ibrahim" in place of "Ibn Wahb". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 346}

The grammarians differed over the reason for repeating the question in "shall we really be in a new creation?" after the first question in "when we are dust". One of the Basran grammarians said: the first is an adverbial, and the second is the one on which the question falls, as when you say "on Friday, is Zayd leaving?" He said: whoever puts another question on "when we are dust" makes it an adverbial for something mentioned before it, as if they had been asked "will you be raised?" and they said "when we are dust?", and then he makes this a further question; he said this is far-fetched. He said: if you wish, you do not make "a-idha" a question and make the question in the wording fall on "a-inna", as if you said "on Friday, is Abd Allah leaving?" with its negation implied; this is the place where "a-idha" is begun with, and it is not frequent in speech; had you said "today, Abd Allah is leaving" it would not be good, though it is allowed. And the Arabs have said "ma 'alimtu innahu la-salih", meaning "innahu la-salih ma 'alimtu". The editor notes what he corrected in the wording of the manuscript and printed text here and points out that al-Tabari had earlier said that he would come to the right account of omitting the repeated question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 347}

We stop here at the foot of p. 347, in the middle of the commentary on verse 5 (in the grammarians' difference over repeating the question); the next batch begins at p. 348. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 347}
