**Commentary on verse 36 (pp. 94)**

On "and two young men entered the prison with him" al-Tabari says this points to something left out, namely that they imprisoned him and put him in the prison and two young men entered with him, the words relying on this to omit the mention of their putting Joseph in. The two youths were, it is said, two servants of the great king of Egypt, one his cupbearer and the other his baker. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "fata". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 94}

Report 19266, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Joseph was thrown into prison, and "two young men entered the prison with him", two servants of the great king al-Rayyan ibn al-Walid, one over his drink and the other over some matter of his, in a displeasure he felt toward them; the name of one, as printed, was Mujlith and the other Nabu, Nabu being the one over the drink. Report 19267, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: one was the king's baker and the other his cupbearer. The reason the king jailed them, it is said, is in report 19268, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: the king was angry with his baker on hearing that he wished to poison him, and jailed him and his cupbearer, thinking he had colluded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 95}

On "one of them said: I see myself pressing wine" it is said that when Joseph was put in prison he told the prisoners, who asked what he did, that he interprets dreams, and one of the youths said to his companion, come, let us test him. Report 19269, from al-Suddi: one youth said to the other, come, let us try this Hebrew slave; they pretended to him, and asked, without having seen anything; the baker said he saw himself carrying bread on his head from which birds ate, and the other that he saw himself pressing wine. The editor takes the right wording "they pretended" from the manuscript and the History, and notes that al-Tabari also gave the report in his History. Report 19270, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Umara ibn al-Qa'qa' and Ibrahim: Joseph's two companions saw nothing; they only feigned dreams to test his knowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 95}

Some said they asked about a dream they had truly seen, trusting Joseph for his knowledge of interpretation. Report 19271, from Ibn Ishaq: when the youths saw Joseph they said, by God, young man, we loved you the moment we saw you. Report 19272, from Mujahid: Joseph said to them, I adjure you by God not to love me; by God nobody ever loved me but some trial came to me through that love; my aunt loved me and a trial came through her love, then my father loved me and a trial came through his, then the wife of this master of mine loved me and a trial came through her love; so do not love me. They insisted on loving him and keeping his company and were struck by his understanding and mind. They had each seen a dream on entering the prison: Mujlith saw himself carrying bread on his head from which birds ate and Nabu saw himself pressing wine, and they asked him about them, saying: tell us its interpretation, we see you among the doers of good, if you do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 96}

"Pressing wine" means: I see in my sleep that I am pressing grapes, and so it is in Ibn Mas'ud's reading, as reported of him. Report 19273, from Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya: in the reading of Ibn Mas'ud, "I see myself pressing grapes", through Ibn Waki', his father, Abu Salama al-Sa'igh and Ibrahim ibn Bashir al-Ansari. The editor says Abu Salama al-Sa'igh is Rashid al-Fazari and that al-Bukhari mentioned this report in his entry for Ibrahim ibn Bashir. It is said this is the dialect of the people of Oman, who call grapes khamr; reports 19274 to 19276, from al-Dahhak and Ibn Abbas: grapes, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh, Ubayd, Abu Kurayb, Waki', Salama ibn Nubayt, al-Qasim, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj. Report 19277, from Ikrima: one came to him and said, I saw in a dream that I planted a vine cutting, it grew and bunches came out, I pressed them and gave the king to drink; he said, you will stay in prison three days, then leave and serve him wine. The editor explains the "habla" as a cutting of the grape vine that is planted and grows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 97}

On "and the other said: I see myself carrying bread on my head from which the birds eat" he says: on my head, using "fawqa" for "ala"; "from it" means from the bread. "Tell us its interpretation" means tell us what the dream we reported leads to; report 19278, from Mujahid: "of it"; al-Harith said that Abu Ubayd said Mujahid means that the interpretation of a thing is the thing itself, and the interpretation of a dream is what it comes back to. On "we see you among the doers of good" the interpreters differed over the "ihsan" the youths described Joseph with. Some said it was that he visited their sick, consoled their grieving and collected for any of them in need; report 19279, from al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, asked what Joseph's good doing was: when someone was ill he attended him, when he was in need he collected for him, when he was straitened he gave room. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 98}

Report 19280 says the same of al-Dahhak, in the prison. Report 19281, from Qatada: we were told his good doing was that he treated their sick, consoled their grieving and strove for his Lord; and that when Joseph reached the prison he found people whose hope was cut off, whose trial was severe and whose grief was long, and he began to say, rejoice and be patient and you will be rewarded, for this has a reward and a recompense. They said, young man, God bless you, how fair your face and character, we are blessed by your neighbourhood, we would not wish to be elsewhere since we were jailed, for what you tell us of reward, expiation and purification; who are you? He said, I am Joseph, son of Ya'qub the chosen of God, son of Ishaq whom God had offered, son of Ibrahim the friend of God. Love was upon him. The prison's keeper told him, young man, if I could I would let you go, but I will treat your neighbourhood and your confinement well, so be in whichever cell you like. Report 19282, from al-Dahhak: he made room for a man in his seat and looked after the sick. Others said the meaning is, we see you among the doers of good if you give us the interpretation of our dreams; report 19283, from Ibn Ishaq: they asked him for a ruling on their dream and said, if you do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 99}

Al-Tabari says that the soundest of the views is what he gave from al-Dahhak and Qatada. If it is asked what the sense of the words is then, since their asking Joseph to tell them the interpretation has nothing to do with describing him as visiting the sick and doing good to the needy, and one says to a man, tell us the interpretation of this, for you are knowing, where describing knowledge is fitting, the answer is that they said: tell us the interpretation of our dream, doing good to us in telling us, as we see you do good in all your actions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 100}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
