**Commentary on verse 12 (pp. 36-37)**

He then explains "When harm touches man he calls on Us, lying on his side or sitting or standing; but when We remove his harm from him he passes on as if he had never called on Us for a harm that touched him; thus what they used to do is made fair-seeming to the transgressors". When hardship and distress befall a person he calls on Us "on his side", that is he cries to Us for relief lying on his side, "or sitting or standing", in whatever state he is in when the harm comes; "when We removed his harm" means We eased the distress from him; "he passes on as if he had never called on Us" means he carries on in his earlier way before the harm struck, forgets or feigns to forget the distress and trial, gives up thanks to the Lord who relieved him, and returns to polytheism and to claiming gods and idols as lords alongside Him. The editor refers to the glosses on "touching", "harm", "removal" and "passing on". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 36}

On "thus what they used to do is made fair-seeming to the transgressors": as this person's continuing in his disbelief after God removed the harm has been made fair-seeming to him, so what they did of disobeying God and associating others with Him was made fair-seeming to those who went too far in lying about God and His prophets, going beyond what God allowed them in speaking about them. The editor refers to the glosses on "making fair-seeming" and "excess". Report 17578, from Ibn Jurayj: "on his side" means lying down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 37}
