**Commentary on verse 42 (pp. 271-272)**

Al-Tabari says: God says to the Prophet, when a group of his Companions had asked his leave to stay behind when he set out for Tabuk and he had given it: if what you call the stay-behinds and those who ask leave not to go out with you on the campaign to which you summoned them were "an immediate gain", that is booty at hand, "and a moderate journey", a near and easy place, they would have followed you and gone out with you to it; but you summoned them to a distant place and burdened them with a journey hard on them, since you roused them in the heat, the time of the burning summer and the need for shade; "they will swear by God, had we been able we would have gone out with you" means they will swear to you, Muhammad, those who ask leave not to go out with you, excusing themselves falsely so that you will accept their excuse and let them stay behind, swearing by God falsely that had they been able to go out with you, with means, mounts, beasts and what a traveller and fighter must have, and health of body and strength, they would have gone with you against your enemy; "they destroy themselves" means that by their false oaths by God they make destruction and ruin due to themselves, bringing on themselves God's wrath and earning His painful punishment; "and God knows they are liars" in their swearing that had they been able they would have gone out, for they were able to go out, having the means from the wealth they had, which a fighter needs in his campaign and a traveller on his journey, and health of body and strength. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 271}

Report 16760, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: they were able to go out, but it was holding back from themselves and the devil and a lack of desire for good; report 16761, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: this is the Tabuk campaign; report 16762, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "God knows they are liars" means they are able. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 272}
