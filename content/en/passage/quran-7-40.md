## Summary
Verse 40 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 40 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "those who deny Our signs and are arrogant about them, the gates of heaven will not be opened for them", God says: those who denied Our proofs and evidences and did not believe in them and did not follow Our messengers and were arrogant about them, that is, were proud about affirming them, disdained following them and submitting to what they brought, the gates of heaven will not be opened for them, for their souls, when they ascend from their bodies, and in their lifetimes no word or deed of theirs rises to God, because their deeds are vile and only the good word and the righteous deed are raised, as in [[passage/quran-35-10|35:10]]. The commentators then differ over "the gates of heaven will not be opened for them". Some said it means the gates of heaven will not be opened to the souls of these disbelievers: he gives (14603) from Ibn Abbas that heaven is not opened to their souls and is opened to the souls of the believers, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 421}

(14604) from him that heaven is opened to a believer's soul and not to a disbeliever's; and (14605) from al-Suddi that when the disbeliever's soul is taken, the angels of the earth strike him until he rises to heaven, and when he reaches the nearest heaven the angels of heaven strike him and he descends, then the angels of the earth strike him and he rises, and when he reaches the nearest heaven the angels of the nearest heaven strike him and he descends to the lowest of the earths; but if he is a believer, his soul is breathed out and the gates of heaven are opened for him, and he passes no angel but it greets him and wishes him peace until he reaches God, who gives him his need, and then God says, return my servant's soul to the earth, for I created him from earth, to earth he returns and from it he comes out. Others said the meaning is that no righteous deed or prayer of theirs rises to God: he gives (14606) from Ibn Abbas that no word or deed of theirs rises; (14607) from him that nothing of their deed rises to God; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 422}

(14608) from him that it is not opened for good they do; (14609) from Mujahid that no word or deed rises for them; (14610) from Ibrahim that no deed or prayer is raised for them, and the note says it found no biography for Matar ibn Muhammad al-Dabbi, al-Tabari's shaykh; and (14611) and (14612) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr the like. Others said the meaning is that the gates of heaven are not opened for their souls nor their deeds, and (14613) is given from Ibn Jurayj. Al-Tabari says he chose in the interpretation of this what he chose because of the generality of God's report that the gates of heaven are not opened to them, and the report is not specified as opened for them in anything, so it stands as God's report generalised, that it is not opened for them in anything, with the support of the report from the Messenger of God for what he said, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 423}

which is what (14614) gives from al-Bara' ibn Azib, that the Messenger of God spoke of the taking of the sinner's soul and that it is carried up to heaven: they carry it up, and pass no company of angels but they say, what is this foul soul? and they say, so-and-so, by the ugliest names he was called by in the world, until they reach heaven with it and ask for it to be opened, and it is not opened; then the Messenger of God recited, "the gates of heaven will not be opened to them, nor will they enter Paradise until the camel passes through the eye of the needle". The note says al-Minhal ibn Amr and Zadhan are trustworthy, and that Ahmad, al-Tayalisi, Abu Dawud and al-Hakim gave the report, the last calling it sound by the standard of the two Shaykhs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 424}

He gives (14615) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said: angels attend the dead person; if he is a righteous man they say, come out, good soul that was in a good body, come out praised and receive glad tidings of rest and fragrance and a Lord not angry; they say that until it is carried up to heaven and its opening is asked, and it is said, who is this? They say, so-and-so. It is said, welcome to the good soul that was in a good body, enter praised and receive glad tidings of rest and fragrance and a Lord not angry; this is said to it until it reaches the heaven where God is. If he is an evil man they say, come out, foul soul that was in a foul body, come out blamed and receive glad tidings of boiling water and pus and other things of its kind in pairs; they say that until it comes out; then it is carried up to heaven and its opening is asked, and it is said, who is this? They say, so-and-so; they say, no welcome to the foul soul that was in a foul body, return blamed, for the gates of heaven are not opened to you; so it is sent between heaven and earth and goes to the grave. And (14616) from Abu Hurayra, from the Prophet, the like. The note says Abd al-Rahman ibn Uthman al-Bakrawi in the chain of the first is weak, that Ibn Majah gave the report, Ibn Kathir mentions it and al-Suyuti cites it, and that the chain of 14616 is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 425}

The readers differ on how to read this. Most readers of Kufa read "la yuftahu lahum abwab al-sama'" with ya in "yuftahu" and a light ta, meaning they will not all be opened for them at once in one opening; and some Medinese and Kufans read "la tufattahu" with ta and a doubled second ta, meaning that a door is not opened for them after a door, one thing after another. Al-Tabari says the right view is that they are two famous readings, both sound in meaning, since the souls of the disbelievers, and their vile deeds, will not have the gates of heaven opened for them all at once, nor time after time, door after door; so both meanings are sound. Likewise with the ya and ta in "yuftahu" and "tuftahu": the ya is built on the singular verb for the unity of the word, and the ta because "the gates" is plural and is reported of with a plural's report. The note refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 426}

Then, on "nor will they enter Paradise until the camel passes through the eye of the needle; thus We repay the criminals", God says: these who denied Our signs and were arrogant about them will never enter the Paradise God prepared for His believing friends, just as the camel never passes through the eye of the needle, which is the hole of the needle. Every hole in an eye or a nose or other, the Arabs call "samm" and make its plural "sumum", though "simam" as the plural of the killing "samm" (poison) is more famous and eloquent than "sumum", and for the plural of "samm" meaning a hole "sumum" is more eloquent, and both are widespread among the Arabs; the singular of the "sumum" that are holes may be said "samm" and "summ", with fatha or damma on the sin, and of "samm" meaning hole is al-Farazdaq's line in which he says he relieved him from his two nostril-holes until he breathed. The note gives the story of that line in the exchange of satire between Jarir, al-Farazdaq and al-Ba'ith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 427}

As for "al-khiyat", it is "al-mikhyat", the needle; it is called "khiyat" and "mikhyat" as one says "qina'" and "miqna'", "izar" and "mi'zar", "qiram" and "miqram", "lihaf" and "milhaf". The readers of all the lands read "fi samm al-khiyat" with fatha on the sin and agreed on "al-jamal" with fatha on the jim and the mim, light; but of Ibn Abbas, Ikrima and Sa'id ibn Jubayr it is reported that they read "al-jummal" with damma on the jim and a doubled mim, with disagreement over this from Sa'id and Ibn Abbas. Those who read it with fatha on both letters and lightened directed the interpretation to the known camel and explained it so: he gives (14617) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that "al-jamal" is the young of the she-camel or the she-camel's mate; and (14618), (14619), (14620) and (14621) from him that it is the she-camel's mate, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 428}

(14622) from al-Hasan that it is the camel that stands in the pen; (14623) from him that it means until the camel enters the eye of the needle; (14624) and (14625) from him that it is the camel, and when they pressed him he said, it is the "ashtar", and the note says "ashtar" is Persian for camel; (14626) from Yahya that al-Hasan read it so, and when some tried to ask him about it, he said, ashtar, ashtar; (14627) from Abu al-Aliya that it is the camel with four legs; and (14628) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that it is the she-camel's mate, meaning the camel, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 429}

(14629) and (14630) from al-Dahhak that it is the one with four legs; (14631) from al-Hasan that it is the one in the pen; (14632) from Mujahid, from Ibn Mas'ud, that he used to read "until the yellow camel passes through"; and (14633) from al-Hasan that it is the young of the she-camel or her mate. Those who differed from this reading themselves differ; two reports are given from Ibn Abbas on it, one agreeing with this reading and this interpretation, and (14634) is given from him that "al-jamal" is the one with legs; and it is mentioned that Ibn Mas'ud said so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 430}

He gives (14635) from Ibn Abbas that "al-jamal" is the huge camel that cannot enter the hole of the needle because it is bigger than it. The other report is (14636) from Mujahid, from Ibn Abbas, that he read "al-jummal" and said it is the ship's "qalas", and the note says a "qalas" is a thick, heavy rope of fibre or palm leaf and is among ship's ropes; (14637) from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, that he used to read "al-jummal", meaning the thick rope, and he said that he mentioned that to al-Hasan, who said "al-jamal", and Khalid said he meant the camel; (14638) from Mujahid, from Ibn Abbas, that he read "al-jummal" doubled and said it is the ship's rope; (14639) from him that it is ships' ropes; and (14640) from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, that it is the thick rope, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 431}

and (14641) from Mujahid, from Ibn Abbas, that it is the rope that is on the ship. There is also disagreement from Sa'id ibn Jubayr on this, and two reports are given from him, one like what is reported of Ibn Abbas, with damma on the jim and a doubled mim, and he gives (14642) from him that he read it "al-jummal", meaning the ships' qulus, that is, the thick ropes. The other is with damma on the jim and a light mim, and he gives (14643) from Salim ibn Ajlan al-Aftas that he recited to his father "al-jummal" and he said "al-jumal", light, it is the ship's rope, so Sa'id ibn Jubayr taught me to recite it. As for Ikrima, he used to read it "al-jummal" with damma on the jim and a doubled mim, and to interpret it as (14644) is given from him, that it is the rope with which one climbs to a palm tree, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 432}

and (14645) from Ikrima that it is the thick rope in the needle's eye; (14646) from Mujahid that it is the ship's rope in the eye of the needle; and (14647) from Abdullah ibn Kathir that he heard Mujahid say it is a rope of the ships' ropes. It is as if the one who read it with a light mim and damma on the jim, as is reported of Sa'id ibn Jubayr, on the pattern of "surad" and "ju'al", took it as the plural of a collection of ropes, gathered as "jumal", as "zulma" is gathered "zulam" and "hurba" "hurab". Some Arabists rejected the doubling of the mim and said the narrator meant "al-jumal" light and the one who heard it did not understand it from him and doubled it; and (14648) is given from al-Farra' from al-Kisa'i, who said that the one who narrated it from Ibn Abbas was a non-Arab. As for one who doubled the mim and put damma on the jim, he took it as one noun, the thick rope or cord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 433}

Al-Tabari says the right reading in this to him is that of the readers of the lands, "until the camel passes through the eye of the needle", with fatha on the jim and mim of "al-jamal", light, and fatha on the sin of "samm", because it is the widespread reading among the readers of the lands, and it is not permitted to go against what the proof agrees on in reading. That being the right reading, the meaning is: and they will not enter Paradise until the camel passes through the eye of the needle, "passing" being entering, which is its hole; "thus We repay the criminals" means thus We reward those who offended in the world, for what they earned, with painful punishment from God in the Hereafter. The commentators said the like of what he said on "the needle's eye": he gives (14649) from al-Hasan that it is the hole of the needle. The note gives the biographies of Suwayd al-Kalbi and Yahya ibn Atiq, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 434}

(14650) from Ikrima that it is the hole of the needle; (14651) from al-Hasan the like; (14652) from al-Suddi that it is the burrow of the needle; (14653) from Ibn Abbas that it is the burrow of the needle; and (14654) from Mujahid that it is its hole. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 435}
