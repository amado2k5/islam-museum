## Summary
Verse 40 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 40 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "those who deny Our signs and are arrogant about them, the gates of heaven will not be opened for them", God says: those who denied Our proofs and evidences and did not believe in them and did not follow Our messengers and were arrogant about them, that is, were proud about affirming them, disdained following them and submitting to what they brought, the gates of heaven will not be opened for them, for their souls, when they ascend from their bodies, and in their lifetimes no word or deed of theirs rises to God, because their deeds are vile and only the good word and the righteous deed are raised, as in [[passage/quran-35-10|35:10]]. The commentators then differ over "the gates of heaven will not be opened for them". Some said it means the gates of heaven will not be opened to the souls of these disbelievers: he gives (14603) from Ibn Abbas that heaven is not opened to their souls and is opened to the souls of the believers, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 421}

(14604) from him that heaven is opened to a believer's soul and not to a disbeliever's; and (14605) from al-Suddi that when the disbeliever's soul is taken, the angels of the earth strike him until he rises to heaven, and when he reaches the nearest heaven the angels of heaven strike him and he descends, then the angels of the earth strike him and he rises, and when he reaches the nearest heaven the angels of the nearest heaven strike him and he descends to the lowest of the earths; but if he is a believer, his soul is breathed out and the gates of heaven are opened for him, and he passes no angel but it greets him and wishes him peace until he reaches God, who gives him his need, and then God says, return my servant's soul to the earth, for I created him from earth, to earth he returns and from it he comes out. Others said the meaning is that no righteous deed or prayer of theirs rises to God: he gives (14606) from Ibn Abbas that no word or deed of theirs rises; (14607) from him that nothing of their deed rises to God; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 422}

(14608) from him that it is not opened for good they do; (14609) from Mujahid that no word or deed rises for them; (14610) from Ibrahim that no deed or prayer is raised for them, and the note says it found no biography for Matar ibn Muhammad al-Dabbi, al-Tabari's shaykh; and (14611) and (14612) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr the like. Others said the meaning is that the gates of heaven are not opened for their souls nor their deeds, and (14613) is given from Ibn Jurayj. Al-Tabari says he chose in the interpretation of this what he chose because of the generality of God's report that the gates of heaven are not opened to them, and the report is not specified as opened for them in anything, so it stands as God's report generalised, that it is not opened for them in anything, with the support of the report from the Messenger of God for what he said, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 423}

which is what (14614) gives from al-Bara' ibn Azib, that the Messenger of God spoke of the taking of the sinner's soul and that it is carried up to heaven: they carry it up, and pass no company of angels but they say, what is this foul soul? and they say, so-and-so, by the ugliest names he was called by in the world, until they reach heaven with it and ask for it to be opened, and it is not opened; then the Messenger of God recited, "the gates of heaven will not be opened to them, nor will they enter Paradise until the camel passes through the eye of the needle". The note says al-Minhal ibn Amr and Zadhan are trustworthy, and that Ahmad, al-Tayalisi, Abu Dawud and al-Hakim gave the report, the last calling it sound by the standard of the two Shaykhs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 424}
