**Commentary on verse 85 (pp. 168-171)**

On [[passage/quran-10-85|10:85]] al-Tabari says: the people of Moses said to Moses 'in God we have put our trust', that is, we have relied on Him and left our affair to Him. Their words 'our Lord, make us not a trial for the wrongdoing people' mean that they prayed to their Lord, saying: our Lord, do not test these disbelieving people and do not try them through us, meaning Pharaoh's people. The interpreters differed on what they asked of their Lord in seeking to be shielded from Pharaoh's people being tried by them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 168}

Some said they asked Him not to let them overcome them, so that they would think themselves better and that they were given power over them only for their standing with Him and the lowliness of the others. Reports 17783 and 17784, from Abu Mijlaz: let them not overcome us so they see themselves as better than us; report 17785, from Abu al-Duha: do not give them power over us so their trial increases. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 169}

Others said: rather the meaning is do not give them power over us so that they persecute us. Report 17786, from Mujahid: do not give them power over us so they persecute us; a report from Mujahid follows: so they lead us astray; report 17788, from Mujahid likewise; report 17789, from Mujahid: do not punish us at the hands of Pharaoh's people nor with a punishment from You, so that Pharaoh's people say 'if they were on the truth they would not have been given power over nor punished', and be tried through us. Report 17790, from Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid, is the same, and report 17791 from him: do not strike us with a punishment from You nor at their hands, so they are tried. Report 17792, from Ibn Zayd: do not try us, our Lord, so that we are strained and You make it a trial for them, this trial; and he recited [[passage/quran-37-63|37:63]] and said: they are the polytheists, when they harmed the Prophet and the believers and shot at them; is that not a trial for them and an evil for them, and a calamity for the believers? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 170}

Al-Tabari says the right view is that the people asked God to shield them from being a test and a trial for Pharaoh's people; whatever was a barrier for them against following Moses, acknowledging him and what he brought was undoubtedly a trial for them and among the greatest things keeping them from belief in God and His messenger. A barrier of that kind would also be if a trial from God had come quickly upon Moses' people in themselves, with a calamity falling on them; so the people sought God's refuge from every meaning that would bar Pharaoh's people from belief in God because of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 171}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
