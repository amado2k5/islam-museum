**Commentary on verse 111 (p. 20)**

Al-Tabari says the chiefs of Pharaoh's people said to Pharaoh, "Put him off", that is, delay him; some said it means "detain him". "Delaying" in Arabic speech is "irja'", from which one says "I put this matter off". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 20}

He adds that the same word occurs in "You defer whom You will of them" ([[passage/quran-33-51|33:51]]), meaning put back. Al-Tabari says the hamza form belongs to some of the Qays tribes, who say "arja'tu this matter", and the form without hamza to Tamim and Asad, who say "arjaytuhu". The editor says the detail of the dialects and their tribes is in no dictionary, so it is an addition to be noted there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 21}

The readers differed over the word. Most readers of Medina and some Iraqis read it without hamza and with a vowelled final pronoun; some readers of Kufa read it without hamza and with the pronoun unvowelled, following the dialect of those who pause on the pronoun even in running speech when the preceding letter is vowelled, and he cites a verse of rajaz by Duwayd ibn Zayd al-Qudai and another about a wolf behind a gazelle. In his footnote the editor gives the ascription of the lines, their variants, the sources and al-Tabrizi's commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 21}

Some Basrans read it with hamza and a damma on the final pronoun, in the dialect of the Qays tribes he mentioned. Al-Tabari holds that the soundest reading is the best known and most eloquent in Arabic speech, namely without hamza and with the pronoun vowelled, though the other is allowed, because what he chose is the most eloquent dialect and the most common on the tongues of the eloquent Arabs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 22}

The interpreters then differed on "put him off". Some said "delay him" (report 14924, from Ibn Abbas through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and Ata' al-Khurasani). Others said "detain him" (report 14925, from Qatada through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 22}

On "send into the cities summoners" he says: those who will summon the sorcerers and gather them to you; some said they are the police. Reports 14926 to 14930 say they are the police: from Ibn Abbas through Abbas ibn Abi Talib, Muslim ibn Ibrahim, al-Hakam ibn Zuhayr and al-Suddi (14926); from Mujahid through Ibn Waki', his father and Isma'il ibn Ibrahim ibn Muhajir from his father (14927); from al-Suddi (14928); from Ibn Abbas through al-Muthanna and Abu Nu'aym (14929); and from Ibn Abbas through Abd al-Karim ibn al-Haytham, Sufyan, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima (14930). The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "gathering". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 23}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
