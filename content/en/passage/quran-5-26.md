**Commentary on verse 26: "He said: it is forbidden to them for forty years, they will wander in the land", pp. 190-193**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over what governs "forty years". Some said it is "forbidden": God forbade those of Moses' people who disobeyed and refused to fight the giants from entering their city for forty years, then opened it to them and settled them in it and destroyed the giants after a war, once the forty years were over and they left the wilderness.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 190}

Report 11690, from al-Rabi', says that when the people spoke and Moses prayed against them, God revealed that it was forbidden to them for forty years, they would wander, and he should not grieve over the wicked; they were then, it is said, six hundred thousand fighters, and stayed forty years in about six leagues or less, walking earnestly each day to get out until they wearied and halted, finding themselves where they had set out. They complained to Moses, so manna and quails were sent down, and clothes that did not wear out and that grew with the child; Moses asked for water and the rock of the Tur was brought, from which twelve springs came when he struck it with his staff, one for each tribe. When the forty years had passed, as a punishment for their transgression, Moses was told to order them to go to the Holy Land, for God had spared them their enemy, and to say at the gate, prostrating, "hitta", meaning that their sins be lowered; most refused, prostrated on their cheeks and said "hinta", wheat, and God said of them what is in the second surah [[passage/quran-2-59|2:59]]. The editor notes that al-Tabari gave this chain earlier without completing it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 190}

Others said the governing word is "they will wander", so that the sense is that it is forbidden to them forever and they will wander forty years; none of those who said "we shall never enter" entered the giants' city, only Joshua and Caleb and the children of those who were forbidden.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 191}

Reports 11691 and 11692, from Qatada, say "forbidden to them" means forever and "wander in the land" means forty years. Report 11693, from 'Ikrima, says the forbidding is the wilderness, an expanse in which one is lost; the editor sets out his reading of the manuscript and where al-Tabari gives the report in his history.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 192}

Report 11694, from al-Suddi, says Moses was angry with his people and prayed against them, and when the wandering was imposed on them he regretted it, and those who obeyed him came and asked what he had done to them; they stayed in the wilderness, and when they left it the manna and quails were lifted and they ate vegetables. Moses met 'Awj, leapt ten cubits into the air, with a staff of ten cubits and a height of ten cubits, struck his heel and killed him; none who had refused to enter the giants' village remained alive to see the conquest. After the forty years God sent Joshua son of Nun as a prophet; the people pledged to him, he routed the giants and they pursued and killed them, so that bands of Israelites gathered on one man's neck and struck it without cutting it. The editor shows where the report is in al-Tabari's history and how the manuscript writes 'Awj's name.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 192}

Report 11695, from Ibn 'Abbas, says everyone over twenty who entered the wilderness died in it, Moses died there and Aaron before him, and Joshua attacked the giants' city with those who remained and conquered it. Report 11696, from Qatada, says they could not go down into any village but followed the wells for forty years, that Moses died in that time, and that only their children and the two men who spoke entered Jerusalem. The editor explains the wells as ones lined with stones.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 193}

The rest of report 11697, from Ibn Ishaq, from some people of the earlier scripture, says that after the Children of Israel did what they did in disobeying their prophet and intending harm to Caleb and Joshua, God's glory appeared in a cloud at the door of the tent of assembly, and God asked Moses how long this people would disobey and not believe the signs, saying He would strike them and make of Moses a stronger and greater people. Moses pleaded that the nations who had heard God's name would say He killed them because He could not bring them into the land, and asked forgiveness through His many favours and as He had forgiven them since Egypt. God replied that He had forgiven at his word, but those who saw His glory and signs and tested Him ten times would not see the land sworn to their fathers, except His servant Caleb, who had another spirit and followed Him, whom He would bring in.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 194}

The report continues that God told Moses and Aaron He had heard the Children of Israel's murmuring and would do to them as they said: their bodies would fall in this wilderness, all those twenty and over, and only Caleb and Joshua would enter, while their children who knew not good from evil would; they would wander forty years, a year for each day they scouted the land; and the men sent out who spread the evil report died suddenly, while Joshua and Caleb lived. The editor remarks that this is another old translation of Numbers chapter 14, notes its wording and the differences from the version in circulation, and explains the "tent of assembly".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 195}

The report goes on that the people grieved greatly and went up to the mountain top saying they would climb to the land since they had erred; Moses told them not to oppose God's word, for God was not with them and they would be broken before their enemies; they went up, the ark and Moses stayed in the camp, and the Amalekites and Canaanites came down, burned them, drove them off and killed them. God then made them wander forty years for the disobedience until those who deserved it perished. When the young grew up and the forty years ended, Moses set out with Joshua and Caleb, who was, they claim, married to Maryam daughter of 'Imran, sister of Moses and Aaron, and their in-law; Joshua went ahead to Jericho and entered it with them, killing the giants there; then Moses entered with the Children of Israel, stayed as long as God willed, and God took him, with no creature knowing his grave.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 197}

The editor notes that Ibn Ishaq's wording differs entirely from his words elsewhere in the biographies, and judges the old translation to date from the first Hijri century, hoping to trace it further.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 197}

Al-Tabari then gives his choice: the sounder of the two views is that "forty" is governed by "forbidding" and that the forbidding covered all of Moses' people, not some of them, since God made the address general; God kept His promised punishment, making them wander forty years and forbidding all of them the Holy Land, so no one entered, young or old, righteous or not, until the years ended; then those left and their offspring were permitted to enter with Moses and the two favoured men, and Moses conquered the giants' village with Joshua at the vanguard.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 198}

His reasoning is that the scholars of early reports agree that Moses killed 'Awj ibn 'Unaq; had that happened before the wandering, the Children of Israel would not have shown such fear of the giants, so it was after the passing of the generation that despaired and disobeyed. He adds that they also agree that Bal'am ibn Ba'ur aided the giants by prayer against Moses, which is impossible while Moses' people were refusing to fight, since help is needed only by one being sought. The editor fixes the spelling of 'Awj from the manuscript.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 198}

Report 11698, from Nawf, says 'Awj's bed was eight hundred cubits, and Moses leapt ten cubits into the air, with a staff of ten and a height of ten, struck 'Awj and hit his heel, and he fell dead and became a bridge for people to cross. Report 11699, from Ibn 'Abbas, is similar and says he was a bridge for the people of the Nile for a year. The editor comments that al-Tabari's accounts of 'Awj and similar reports of the giants' huge size are exaggerations taken from the people of the earlier scripture, and are not to be relied on.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 199}

Al-Tabari explains that "they will wander in the land" means they are bewildered and lost in it, hence the man lost from the right path is called one who wanders; their wandering was that for forty years they set out earnestly each day over about six leagues to leave it and by evening were where they began. Report 11700, from al-Rabi', says so, and report 11701, from Mujahid, says they woke where they had spent the evening and spent the evening where they had woken.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 199}

**Commentary on verse 26, continued: "so do not grieve over the wicked people", p. 200**

Al-Tabari says "do not grieve" means do not sorrow; one says that someone grieved over something, and he cites a verse of Imru' al-Qays from his famous ode meaning do not perish of grief, with the editor giving its place in his diwan. Report 11702, from Ibn 'Abbas, says the same. Report 11703, from al-Suddi, says that when the wandering was imposed on them Moses regretted it, and God revealed that he should not grieve over those He had called wicked, and he did not.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 200}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
