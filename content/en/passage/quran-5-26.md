**Commentary on verse 26: "He said: it is forbidden to them for forty years, they will wander in the land", pp. 190-193**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over what governs "forty years". Some said it is "forbidden": God forbade those of Moses' people who disobeyed and refused to fight the giants from entering their city for forty years, then opened it to them and settled them in it and destroyed the giants after a war, once the forty years were over and they left the wilderness.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 190}

Report 11690, from al-Rabi', says that when the people spoke and Moses prayed against them, God revealed that it was forbidden to them for forty years, they would wander, and he should not grieve over the wicked; they were then, it is said, six hundred thousand fighters, and stayed forty years in about six leagues or less, walking earnestly each day to get out until they wearied and halted, finding themselves where they had set out. They complained to Moses, so manna and quails were sent down, and clothes that did not wear out and that grew with the child; Moses asked for water and the rock of the Tur was brought, from which twelve springs came when he struck it with his staff, one for each tribe. When the forty years had passed, as a punishment for their transgression, Moses was told to order them to go to the Holy Land, for God had spared them their enemy, and to say at the gate, prostrating, "hitta", meaning that their sins be lowered; most refused, prostrated on their cheeks and said "hinta", wheat, and God said of them what is in the second surah [[passage/quran-2-59|2:59]]. The editor notes that al-Tabari gave this chain earlier without completing it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 190}

Others said the governing word is "they will wander", so that the sense is that it is forbidden to them forever and they will wander forty years; none of those who said "we shall never enter" entered the giants' city, only Joshua and Caleb and the children of those who were forbidden.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 191}

Reports 11691 and 11692, from Qatada, say "forbidden to them" means forever and "wander in the land" means forty years. Report 11693, from 'Ikrima, says the forbidding is the wilderness, an expanse in which one is lost; the editor sets out his reading of the manuscript and where al-Tabari gives the report in his history.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 192}

Report 11694, from al-Suddi, says Moses was angry with his people and prayed against them, and when the wandering was imposed on them he regretted it, and those who obeyed him came and asked what he had done to them; they stayed in the wilderness, and when they left it the manna and quails were lifted and they ate vegetables. Moses met 'Awj, leapt ten cubits into the air, with a staff of ten cubits and a height of ten cubits, struck his heel and killed him; none who had refused to enter the giants' village remained alive to see the conquest. After the forty years God sent Joshua son of Nun as a prophet; the people pledged to him, he routed the giants and they pursued and killed them, so that bands of Israelites gathered on one man's neck and struck it without cutting it. The editor shows where the report is in al-Tabari's history and how the manuscript writes 'Awj's name.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 192}

Report 11695, from Ibn 'Abbas, says everyone over twenty who entered the wilderness died in it, Moses died there and Aaron before him, and Joshua attacked the giants' city with those who remained and conquered it. Report 11696, from Qatada, says they could not go down into any village but followed the wells for forty years, that Moses died in that time, and that only their children and the two men who spoke entered Jerusalem. The editor explains the wells as ones lined with stones.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 193}

We stop here at the foot of p. 193, at the start of report 11697 from Ibn Ishaq, from some people of the earlier scripture; the rest of verse 26 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
