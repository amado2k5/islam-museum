## Summary
Verse 113 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/year-of-sorrow|Year of Sorrow]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 113 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verses 113 and 114 (pp. 509-536)**

Al-Tabari says: it was not fitting for the Prophet and those who believed with him to pray for forgiveness for polytheists, even close kin, after it became clear that they died in polytheism and belong to the people of the Fire, since God decreed not to forgive a polytheist. If it is said that Abraham sought forgiveness for his father, that was only because of a promise he had made him; when it became clear to him that his father was an enemy of God, he left him and disowned him. The interpreters differed over the occasion of revelation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 509}

Some said it was revealed about Abu Talib, the Prophet's uncle, when the Prophet wished to seek forgiveness for him after his death and God forbade it. Report 17324 is from Ma'mar, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Muhammad ibn Thawr: when death came to Abu Talib the Prophet went in, with Abu Jahl and Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya present, and said, "Uncle, say there is no god but God, a word I can plead for you with before God." They said, "Do you turn away from the religion of Abd al-Muttalib?" The Prophet said he would ask forgiveness for him unless forbidden, and the verse was revealed, along with [[passage/quran-28-56|verse 28:56]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 509}

Report 17325 is from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab from his father, through Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Wahb, his uncle Abdullah ibn Wahb, Yunus and al-Zuhri: the same, adding that the Prophet kept offering the word and repeating it until Abu Talib's last words to them were that he was on the religion of Abd al-Muttalib, refusing to say it, and the Prophet said he would ask forgiveness for him unless forbidden; then God revealed the two verses. The editor says the report is sound, transmitted by al-Bukhari in his Sahih by three routes, by Muslim, and by Ahmad in his Musnad, and that report 17328 will give it from Sa'id without raising it to his father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 510}

Report 17326 is from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the believers said, "Shall we not seek forgiveness for our fathers, when Abraham sought it for his unbelieving father?" and God revealed the verse on Abraham's promise. Report 17327 is from Amr ibn Dinar: the Prophet said that Abraham sought forgiveness for his father while a polytheist, and that he would continue to do so for Abu Talib until his Lord forbade him; his companions said they would do the same for their fathers, and the verse was revealed. Report 17328 is from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, through Ibn Waki', Yazid ibn Harun, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and al-Zuhri: when death came to Abu Talib the Prophet said, "Uncle, you have the greatest right over me, so say a word through which intercession becomes due for me on the Day of Resurrection," and the rest follows the earlier report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 511}

Others said it was revealed about the Prophet's mother, when he wished to seek forgiveness for her and was prevented. Report 17329 is from Atiyya, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Fudayl: when the Prophet came to Mecca he stood at his mother's grave until the sun grew hot on him, hoping for permission to ask forgiveness for her, until the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 511}

Report 17330 is from Buraydah, through Abu Ahmad, Qays, Alqama ibn Marthad and Sulayman ibn Buraydah: the Prophet came to the remains of a grave and sat by it, as if speaking, then rose weeping. Buraydah said they had seen what he did, and he answered that he had asked his Lord for permission to visit his mother's grave and was given it, and had asked for permission to seek forgiveness for her and was not given it; he was never seen weeping more than that day. The editor discusses the manuscript and printed readings of one phrase, and says the chain is trustworthy and sound, cited by Ibn Kathir and given by Ahmad in his Musnad by several routes. Report 17331 is from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father and his uncle: the Prophet wished to seek forgiveness for his mother and God forbade it; he said that Abraham, God's friend, had sought it for his father, and God revealed the verse on Abraham's seeking forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 512}

Others said it was revealed because some believers had been asking forgiveness for their dead polytheists and were forbidden. Report 17332 is from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: they used to ask forgiveness for them until the verse came, and then they held back for their dead, though they were not forbidden to do so for the living until they died; then God revealed the verse on Abraham's seeking forgiveness. Report 17333 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that some men among the Prophet's companions said that some of their fathers had been good neighbours, kept kinship ties, freed captives and honoured pacts, and asked whether they should seek forgiveness for them. The Prophet said he would do so for his father as Abraham did for his, and the verse was revealed; then God excused Abraham. It adds that the Prophet said words were revealed to him that he must not seek forgiveness for one who died a polytheist, and that whoever gives his surplus wealth has done well and whoever withholds it has done ill, with no blame for sufficiency. The editor explains kafaf as provision equal to one's need with nothing left over. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 513}

The grammarians differed over the phrase "it was not for them to seek forgiveness". Some Basran grammarians said it means they had no right to, as in the words in Surat Yunus that no soul may believe except by God's leave; some Kufan grammarians said it means it was not fitting for them, since "an" after "kana" carries the sense of "fitting", as in the words in Al Imran that it is not for a prophet to defraud, and that "an" marks the future. Then the scholars differed on why the words about Abraham's seeking forgiveness were revealed: some said because the Prophet and his companions were asking forgiveness for their dead polytheists, thinking Abraham had done so when God reported his words in Surat Maryam. Report 17334 is from Ali, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Khalil: I heard a man ask forgiveness for his polytheist parents and said so; he replied that Abraham had done it for his father; I told the Prophet and the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 514}

Report 17335 is from Ali through Yahya: the Prophet used to ask forgiveness for his polytheist parents until the verse was revealed. The editor says Abu al-Khalil is Abdullah ibn Abi al-Khalil al-Hamdani, who is trustworthy, that Ahmad gave the report in his Musnad, and that his other wording concerns a man among the Muslims. It was also said that "on account of a promise" means after a promise, as one says a matter arose from a certain cause, meaning after it or for it. Some read the prohibition as concerning forgiveness after death, because of the words "after it became clear to them", saying this becomes clear only through death in unbelief, so while one lives the believers may seek forgiveness for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 515}

Report 17336 is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Sulayman ibn Umar al-Raqqi, Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak, Sufyan al-Thawri and al-Shaybani: a Jewish man died whose son was Muslim and did not go out with the bier; Ibn Abbas, when told, said he should have walked with it, buried him and prayed for his well-being while he lived, and once dead left him to his case; he then recited the verse and did not pray for him. Report 17337 is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Waki', Fudayl and Durar ibn Murra: a Christian died and his son left him to his own people; Ibn Abbas said it would have cost him nothing to walk with him, bury him and ask forgiveness for him, and recited the verse. The editor says al-Shaybani is Durar ibn Murra, trustworthy, and that the wording of the second report is plainly inconsistent with the first, which forbids asking forgiveness after death, and he does not know the source of this difference. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 516}

Others took "seeking forgiveness" here to mean prayer. Report 17338 is from Ata ibn Abi Rabah, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Kathir ibn Hisham, Ja'far ibn Burqan and Habib ibn Abi Marzuq: I never give up praying over anyone of this direction of prayer, even an Abyssinian woman pregnant by fornication, since I have not heard God withhold prayer except from polytheists. Others took it as supplication. Report 17339 is from Abu Hurayra, through Ibn Waki', his father, Isma ibn Zamil and his father: he asked God's mercy on whoever asked forgiveness for him and his mother; asked about his father, he said no, for my father died a polytheist. The editor gives the biographical notices of Isma ibn Zamil and his father, with al-Daraqutni's remark on their chain, and says he kept the manuscript reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 517}

Al-Tabari says he has shown that seeking forgiveness is the servant's asking his Lord to pardon sins, which can occur inside or outside prayer, so neither of the two views is invalid, because God forbade it for the polytheist after it was clear he belongs to the people of the Blaze, and made no exception for a state in which it is allowed. "After it became clear to them that they are the people of the Blaze" means after they know that he died an unbeliever and is of the Fire; the "people of the Blaze" are its dwellers, as one calls dwellers of a house its people. Report 17340 is from Qatada, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: it became clear to the Prophet, when Abu Talib died, that repentance had been cut off from him; report 17341 gives the same through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la; report 17342 is from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: when they die polytheists, and he then cites verse 72 of Surat al-Ma'ida on Paradise being forbidden to one who associates others with God. The editor corrects the recitation in the footnote. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 518}

The interpreters differed over "when it became clear to him he was an enemy of God, he disowned him". Some said it means when it became clear to him by his father's death as a polytheist, he disowned him and gave up asking forgiveness. Reports 17343 to 17345 are from Ibn Abbas through several chains: Abraham kept asking forgiveness for his father until he died; when he died it became clear to him that he was an enemy of God and he did not ask forgiveness for him. Report 17346 is from Ibn Abbas: he asked forgiveness for him while he lived, then held back at his death. Reports 17347 to 17350 are from Mujahid through several chains: when he died, that is, his death as an unbeliever. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 519}

Report 17351 is from al-Hakam, through Ibn Abi Ghunya and his father: when he died without believing; report 17352 is from Amr ibn Dinar: his death as an unbeliever; 17353 from al-Dahhak: when he died; 17354 from Qatada: when he died in his polytheism, he disowned him. Report 17355 is from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: Abraham hoped his father would believe while he lived, and when he died in polytheism he disowned him. Report 17356 is from Mujahid: his death as an unbeliever; reports 17357 and 17358 are from Ibn Abbas through two chains: Abraham kept asking forgiveness for his father until he died, and when he died it became clear to him that he was an enemy of God and he did not ask forgiveness for him. The editor corrects transmitter names in the first chain and gives notices on Ibn Abi Ghunya, his father and Ahmad ibn Ishaq al-Ahwazi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 520}

Others said the clarity came in the hereafter: his father clings to him as he is about to cross the Bridge, and just as he is about to pass, Abraham glances back and sees his father in the form of a hyena, and lets go of him and disowns him then. Report 17359 is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Amr ibn Ali, Hafs ibn Ghiyath and Abdullah ibn Sulayman: on the Day of Resurrection Abraham says "My Lord, my father, my Lord, my father," and on the third time his hand is taken, he turns and finds a male hyena, and disowns him. The editor explains that the term used means a male hyena. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 521}

Report 17360 is from Ubayd ibn Umayr, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mansur: you will be gathered on one plain, the caller will make you hear and the sight will take you in; Hell will let out a breath, and every angel brought near and every sent prophet will fall to his knees trembling, saying "myself, myself." The Bridge is set over Hell, sharp as a sword and slippery, with angels on both sides holding hooks like thorns; people cross like lightning, wind, birds, and fine riding camels and fine runners, while angels say "Lord, keep safe, keep safe"; one is saved unharmed, one saved though scratched, one is thrown into the Fire. Abraham tells his father that he used to bid him in the world and he disobeyed, and that he will not leave him today, so he should take hold of his waist; he takes his arms and he is changed into a hyena, and seeing this, Abraham disowns him. The editor discusses manuscript and printed readings of several phrases, explains the rare words, and refers to al-Hakim's Mustadrak (from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, sound in chain, without any mention of Abraham) and to the Majma al-Zawa'id (a report from Aisha with Ibn Lahi'a in the chain). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 522}

Al-Tabari says the soundest view is God's own word, His report that when it became clear to Abraham that his father was God's enemy he disowned him, which is the state of his knowledge and certainty that he was an enemy of God and associated others with Him, the state of his death in polytheism. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 523}

On "Abraham was awwah and forbearing", the interpreters differed over awwah. Some said it is the one who prays much. Reports 17361 to 17363 are from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud through several chains: awwah is the one who prays much; reports 17364 to 17367 give the same from him; reports 17368 and 17369 are from Ubayd ibn Umayr. The editor notes that al-Haythami gave Zirr's report from Ibn Mas'ud in the Majma al-Zawa'id as transmitted by al-Tabarani, with Asim ibn Abi al-Najud in the chain, trustworthy though criticised. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 523}

Others said it is the compassionate. Report 17370 is from Abu al-Ubaydayn, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Salama and Muslim al-Batin: Abdullah was asked about awwah and said it is the compassionate. The editor says al-Tabari gives the Abu al-Ubaydayn report in a succession of chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 524}

Reports 17371 to 17378 are from Abu al-Ubaydayn, Mu'awiya ibn Sabra al-Suwa'i, who was blind, through many chains: he asked Ibn Mas'ud, in one telling saying "whom shall we ask if not you?" so that Ibn Mas'ud softened toward him, what awwah means, and he said the compassionate. The editor gives notices on Salama ibn Kuhayl, Muslim al-Batin, Abu al-Ubaydayn and Yahya ibn al-Jazzar, and says al-Haythami cited it in the Majma al-Zawa'id as transmitted by al-Tabarani through several chains, the men of the first two reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 525}

Reports 17379 to 17381 are from Abu Maysara through several chains: awwah is the compassionate; 17382 from al-Hasan: the compassionate; reports 17383 and 17384 are from Qatada: we were told that awwah is the compassionate, and that Abraham was compassionate. Abd al-Karim al-Jazari reported the same from Abu Ubayda from Ibn Mas'ud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 526}

Reports 17385 to 17389 complete the section: from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan, Abd al-Karim and Abu Ubayda; from Abu al-Ubaydayn; from Amr ibn Sharahbil; and from al-Hasan, who said it is the one compassionate toward God's servants; and in the last, from Amr ibn Sharahbil, that it is the compassionate in the dialect of the Abyssinians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 527}

Others said it is the one with certainty. Reports 17390 to 17392 are from Ibn Abbas through several chains: awwah is the one with certainty, in some in the tongue of the Abyssinians. The editor notes that the manuscript here and in most later places has "the one granted success" and in some "the one with certainty", and that the printed reading seems nearer to correct, so he left it until he finds something to decide it. Reports 17393 to 17401 are from Sufyan, Ata, Ikrima, Mujahid, Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak: the one with certainty; some said the jurist with certainty; some add the Abyssinian tongue; Mujahid's report through Isa and Ibn Abi Najih says trustworthy and certain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 528}

Others said it is an Abyssinian word meaning the believer. Report 17402 is from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father and uncle: awwah is the believer in the Abyssinian tongue; 17403 from him: the believer who turns back often; 17404 from him through Mujahid: the believer; 17405 from Ibn Jurayj: the believer in the Abyssinian tongue. The editor corrects the wording for Abyssinian in two places. Others said it is the one who glorifies God and remembers Him often. Report 17406 is from Sa'id: the glorifier; report 17407 is from al-Hasan ibn Muslim ibn Yanna: a man used to remember God and glorify Him often, and when this was mentioned to the Prophet he said he was an awwah; report 17408 is from Uqba ibn Amir: awwah is one who remembers God often. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 529}

Others said it is the one who recites the Qur'an often. Report 17409 is from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Yaman, al-Minhal ibn Khalifa, Hajjaj ibn Arta'a and Ata: the Prophet buried a dead man and said God have mercy on you, you were an awwah, meaning one who recited the Qur'an often. The editor explains the form as meaning one who recites much. Others said it comes from the sigh "awwah". Report 17410 is from a storyteller in Mecca: a man in the circuit of the Ka'ba kept saying "awwah", and Abu Dharr complained of him to the Prophet, who said to leave him, for he is an awwah. Report 17411 is from Abu Yunus al-Qushayri or al-Bahili, from a man of Roman origin, from Abu Dharr, the same, adding in Abu Kurayb's version that Abu Dharr went out one night and the Prophet was burying that man by lamplight. The editor says Abu Yunus is Hatim ibn Abi Saghira, trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 530}

Reports 17412 to 17414 are from Ka'b through several chains: awwah is one who, on hearing the Fire mentioned, says "awwah, from the Fire." The editor gives notices on the transmitters, corrects the name Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd al-Samad al-Ammi, and says Ka'b is the well-known Ka'b al-Ahbar. Others said it means jurist; report 17415 is from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: a jurist. Others said it is the humbly entreating. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 531}

Report 17416 is from Abdullah ibn Shaddad ibn al-Had, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, Abd al-Hamid ibn Bahram and Shahr ibn Hawshab: while the Messenger of God was sitting, a man asked what awwah is; he said the one who entreats humbly, and then recited the verse; report 17417 gives the same: the humble, entreating one. The editor notes that Abd al-Hamid ibn Bahram is trustworthy though criticised in his reports from Shahr, that Shahr is trustworthy though criticised, and that Abdullah ibn Shaddad is a trustworthy Successor, so the report is mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 532}

Al-Tabari says the soundest view in his opinion is that of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, reported through Zirr, that it means one who prays much, because God mentioned it and described Abraham with it after describing his praying and seeking forgiveness for his father, and then says Abraham was one who prayed much to his Lord, grateful to Him, and forbearing toward whoever reviled him and harmed him. For he promised his father to ask forgiveness and to pray for God to forgive him when his father threatened him and promised to abuse him after he rejected his counsel for God's sake, as in Surat Maryam, verses 46 to 48; he kept his word about forgiveness until it became clear that his father was God's enemy, and God described him as one who prays much and is forbearing toward the one who wrongs him. The editor refers to reports 17361 to 17368. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 532}

Its root is "ta'awwuh", entreaty and asking with grief and apprehension, as Abdullah ibn Shaddad reported from the Prophet and as Uqba ibn Amir reported. Report 17418 is from Uqba ibn Amir, through Yahya ibn Uthman ibn Salih al-Sahmi, his father, Ibn Lahi'a, al-Harith ibn Yazid and Ali ibn Rabah: he said of a man called Dhu al-Bijadayn that he was an awwah, because he used to remember God often with the Qur'an and prayer, raising his voice. The editor gives notices on the transmitters, says Uqba ibn Amir al-Juhani was a Companion who governed Egypt, that Ahmad gave the report in his Musnad and al-Haythami in the Majma al-Zawa'id called both chains good, and begins a discussion of a difficulty about Abdullah Dhu al-Bijadayn al-Muzani that continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 533}

The editor's note continues: hence one in pain from illness is told not to sigh "awwah"; he cites a verse of al-Muthaqqib al-Abdi about his camel yearning for her homeland and a verse of al-Ja'di from the ode of al-Nabigha that the Prophet heard, explaining its words and comparing the readings of the manuscript and the diwan. He returns to the difficulty about Dhu al-Bijadayn: it is said he was so named because his mother cut a woven cloak into two, so he wrapped one half as a waist cloth and the other as a mantle, and that when the Prophet migrated he wished to guide him but his father refused and stripped his clothes from him. But the biographies show that the Prophet's guide on the migration was Abdullah ibn Uraqit al-Laythi, who was not a Muslim, and he is not Dhu al-Bijadayn al-Muzani, who died at Tabuk; so the difficulty of their seeming to be one man is removed, though the matter of Dhu al-Bijadayn's guiding needs a clarification the biographical works do not give. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 534}

The next note says the Arabs scarcely form a verb of this root on the pattern fa'ala yaf'ulu, saying instead ta'awwaha yata'awwahu and awwaha yu'awwihu, and he cites two lines, one in rajaz and one in poetry, for the expression "awwah from you", with a transmission from al-Farra; had the verb followed the root it would be aha yaw'hu. Al-Tabari says that because the meaning is to feel pain, grief and entreaty, the interpreters differed as above. Those who said it means mercy held that it was out of Abraham's tenderness toward his father, and mercy toward him and other people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 535}

Others said it was from his firm certainty, his good knowledge of God's greatness and his humility before Him; others, from the soundness of his faith in his Lord; others, when he recited what God revealed; others, when he remembered his Lord. Al-Tabari says all this returns to what he said, and the meanings are close, because one who grieves, entreats his Lord and is humble in heart is overcome by it when he asks and prays for his needs, and the states the interpreters pointed to all occur in him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 536}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
