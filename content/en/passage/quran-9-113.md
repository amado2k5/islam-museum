## Summary
Verse 113 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/year-of-sorrow|Year of Sorrow]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 113 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verses 113 and 114 (pp. 509-519)**

Al-Tabari says: it was not fitting for the Prophet and those who believed with him to pray for forgiveness for polytheists, even close kin, after it became clear that they died in polytheism and belong to the people of the Fire, since God decreed not to forgive a polytheist. If it is said that Abraham sought forgiveness for his father, that was only because of a promise he had made him; when it became clear to him that his father was an enemy of God, he left him and disowned him. The interpreters differed over the occasion of revelation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 509}

Some said it was revealed about Abu Talib, the Prophet's uncle, when the Prophet wished to seek forgiveness for him after his death and God forbade it. Report 17324 is from Ma'mar, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Muhammad ibn Thawr: when death came to Abu Talib the Prophet went in, with Abu Jahl and Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya present, and said, "Uncle, say there is no god but God, a word I can plead for you with before God." They said, "Do you turn away from the religion of Abd al-Muttalib?" The Prophet said he would ask forgiveness for him unless forbidden, and the verse was revealed, along with [[passage/quran-28-56|verse 28:56]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 509}

Report 17325 is from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab from his father, through Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Wahb, his uncle Abdullah ibn Wahb, Yunus and al-Zuhri: the same, adding that the Prophet kept offering the word and repeating it until Abu Talib's last words to them were that he was on the religion of Abd al-Muttalib, refusing to say it, and the Prophet said he would ask forgiveness for him unless forbidden; then God revealed the two verses. The editor says the report is sound, transmitted by al-Bukhari in his Sahih by three routes, by Muslim, and by Ahmad in his Musnad, and that report 17328 will give it from Sa'id without raising it to his father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 510}

Report 17326 is from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the believers said, "Shall we not seek forgiveness for our fathers, when Abraham sought it for his unbelieving father?" and God revealed the verse on Abraham's promise. Report 17327 is from Amr ibn Dinar: the Prophet said that Abraham sought forgiveness for his father while a polytheist, and that he would continue to do so for Abu Talib until his Lord forbade him; his companions said they would do the same for their fathers, and the verse was revealed. Report 17328 is from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, through Ibn Waki', Yazid ibn Harun, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and al-Zuhri: when death came to Abu Talib the Prophet said, "Uncle, you have the greatest right over me, so say a word through which intercession becomes due for me on the Day of Resurrection," and the rest follows the earlier report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 511}

Others said it was revealed about the Prophet's mother, when he wished to seek forgiveness for her and was prevented. Report 17329 is from Atiyya, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Fudayl: when the Prophet came to Mecca he stood at his mother's grave until the sun grew hot on him, hoping for permission to ask forgiveness for her, until the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 511}

Report 17330 is from Buraydah, through Abu Ahmad, Qays, Alqama ibn Marthad and Sulayman ibn Buraydah: the Prophet came to the remains of a grave and sat by it, as if speaking, then rose weeping. Buraydah said they had seen what he did, and he answered that he had asked his Lord for permission to visit his mother's grave and was given it, and had asked for permission to seek forgiveness for her and was not given it; he was never seen weeping more than that day. The editor discusses the manuscript and printed readings of one phrase, and says the chain is trustworthy and sound, cited by Ibn Kathir and given by Ahmad in his Musnad by several routes. Report 17331 is from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father and his uncle: the Prophet wished to seek forgiveness for his mother and God forbade it; he said that Abraham, God's friend, had sought it for his father, and God revealed the verse on Abraham's seeking forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 512}

Others said it was revealed because some believers had been asking forgiveness for their dead polytheists and were forbidden. Report 17332 is from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: they used to ask forgiveness for them until the verse came, and then they held back for their dead, though they were not forbidden to do so for the living until they died; then God revealed the verse on Abraham's seeking forgiveness. Report 17333 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that some men among the Prophet's companions said that some of their fathers had been good neighbours, kept kinship ties, freed captives and honoured pacts, and asked whether they should seek forgiveness for them. The Prophet said he would do so for his father as Abraham did for his, and the verse was revealed; then God excused Abraham. It adds that the Prophet said words were revealed to him that he must not seek forgiveness for one who died a polytheist, and that whoever gives his surplus wealth has done well and whoever withholds it has done ill, with no blame for sufficiency. The editor explains kafaf as provision equal to one's need with nothing left over. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 513}

The grammarians differed over the phrase "it was not for them to seek forgiveness". Some Basran grammarians said it means they had no right to, as in the words in Surat Yunus that no soul may believe except by God's leave; some Kufan grammarians said it means it was not fitting for them, since "an" after "kana" carries the sense of "fitting", as in the words in Al Imran that it is not for a prophet to defraud, and that "an" marks the future. Then the scholars differed on why the words about Abraham's seeking forgiveness were revealed: some said because the Prophet and his companions were asking forgiveness for their dead polytheists, thinking Abraham had done so when God reported his words in Surat Maryam. Report 17334 is from Ali, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Khalil: I heard a man ask forgiveness for his polytheist parents and said so; he replied that Abraham had done it for his father; I told the Prophet and the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 514}

Report 17335 is from Ali through Yahya: the Prophet used to ask forgiveness for his polytheist parents until the verse was revealed. The editor says Abu al-Khalil is Abdullah ibn Abi al-Khalil al-Hamdani, who is trustworthy, that Ahmad gave the report in his Musnad, and that his other wording concerns a man among the Muslims. It was also said that "on account of a promise" means after a promise, as one says a matter arose from a certain cause, meaning after it or for it. Some read the prohibition as concerning forgiveness after death, because of the words "after it became clear to them", saying this becomes clear only through death in unbelief, so while one lives the believers may seek forgiveness for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 515}

Report 17336 is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Sulayman ibn Umar al-Raqqi, Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak, Sufyan al-Thawri and al-Shaybani: a Jewish man died whose son was Muslim and did not go out with the bier; Ibn Abbas, when told, said he should have walked with it, buried him and prayed for his well-being while he lived, and once dead left him to his case; he then recited the verse and did not pray for him. Report 17337 is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Waki', Fudayl and Durar ibn Murra: a Christian died and his son left him to his own people; Ibn Abbas said it would have cost him nothing to walk with him, bury him and ask forgiveness for him, and recited the verse. The editor says al-Shaybani is Durar ibn Murra, trustworthy, and that the wording of the second report is plainly inconsistent with the first, which forbids asking forgiveness after death, and he does not know the source of this difference. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 516}

Others took "seeking forgiveness" here to mean prayer. Report 17338 is from Ata ibn Abi Rabah, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Kathir ibn Hisham, Ja'far ibn Burqan and Habib ibn Abi Marzuq: I never give up praying over anyone of this direction of prayer, even an Abyssinian woman pregnant by fornication, since I have not heard God withhold prayer except from polytheists. Others took it as supplication. Report 17339 is from Abu Hurayra, through Ibn Waki', his father, Isma ibn Zamil and his father: he asked God's mercy on whoever asked forgiveness for him and his mother; asked about his father, he said no, for my father died a polytheist. The editor gives the biographical notices of Isma ibn Zamil and his father, with al-Daraqutni's remark on their chain, and says he kept the manuscript reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 517}

Al-Tabari says he has shown that seeking forgiveness is the servant's asking his Lord to pardon sins, which can occur inside or outside prayer, so neither of the two views is invalid, because God forbade it for the polytheist after it was clear he belongs to the people of the Blaze, and made no exception for a state in which it is allowed. "After it became clear to them that they are the people of the Blaze" means after they know that he died an unbeliever and is of the Fire; the "people of the Blaze" are its dwellers, as one calls dwellers of a house its people. Report 17340 is from Qatada, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: it became clear to the Prophet, when Abu Talib died, that repentance had been cut off from him; report 17341 gives the same through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la; report 17342 is from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: when they die polytheists, and he then cites verse 72 of Surat al-Ma'ida on Paradise being forbidden to one who associates others with God. The editor corrects the recitation in the footnote. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 518}

The interpreters differed over "when it became clear to him he was an enemy of God, he disowned him". Some said it means when it became clear to him by his father's death as a polytheist, he disowned him and gave up asking forgiveness. Reports 17343 to 17345 are from Ibn Abbas through several chains: Abraham kept asking forgiveness for his father until he died; when he died it became clear to him that he was an enemy of God and he did not ask forgiveness for him. Report 17346 is from Ibn Abbas: he asked forgiveness for him while he lived, then held back at his death. Reports 17347 to 17350 are from Mujahid through several chains: when he died, that is, his death as an unbeliever. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 519}

We stop here at the foot of p. 519, in the commentary on verses 113 and 114 (after report 17350); the next batch begins at p. 520. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 519}
