## Summary
Verse 258 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 258 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens ([[passage/quran-2-258|2:258]]) on p. 429: the one addressed by the word for seeing is Muhammad, and it means sight by the heart; the one meant is the man who disputed with Abraham, God's prophet, about his Lord because God had given him kingship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 429} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} He says this is God's wonder expressed to His prophet at the man who disputed with Abraham, which is why the preposition is used, as the Arabs do when they wonder at an act they disapprove of; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} He then reports that the disputant was a tyrant in Babylon called Nimrod son of Canaan son of Cush son of Shem son of Noah; and it is said he was Nimrod son of Falikh son of Eber son of Shalikh son of Arpachshad son of Shem son of Noah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} From Mujahid (5861 to 5864): he is Nimrod son of Canaan; the editor corrects the name of a transmitter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} From Qatada (5865, 5866): we were told that he was a king called Nimrod, the first to be tyrannical on earth and the builder of the tower at Babylon; from al-Rabi' (5867), the same; from al-Suddi (5868), Nimrod son of Canaan; from Ibn Zayd (5869), Ibn Ishaq (5870) and Zayd ibn Aslam (5871), that it was Nimrod; and from Ibn Jurayj (5872), through Abdullah ibn Kathir from Mujahid, that it was Nimrod, said to be the first king on earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 431} These are reports on the man's identity and genealogy as transmitted, concerning earlier peoples, and the museum does not rule on them.

Al-Tabari then explains the first part of Abraham's answer: when Abraham told him that his Lord is the one in whose hand are life and death, giving life to whom He wills and causing death to whom He wills after life, the other said that he did the same, sparing one he wished to kill, which was giving life, and killing another, which was causing death; the Arabs call sparing 'giving life', as in ([[passage/quran-5-32|5:32]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432} Abraham then said that God, his Lord, raises the sun on the eastern side, so let him, if truthful in claiming godhood, raise it on the opposite side; the disbeliever's argument was cut off and fell; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432} He explains that the verb for being confounded means to be cut short, and that the same root also means to slander someone with a lie, and notes that some readers read the verb in the active, meaning that Abraham confounded the disbeliever. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432} In the note the editor says one verbal noun al-Tabari gives is not in the language books though regular by analogy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432}
