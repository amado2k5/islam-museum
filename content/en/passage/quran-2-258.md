## Summary
Verse 258 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 258 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens ([[passage/quran-2-258|2:258]]) on p. 429: the one addressed by the word for seeing is Muhammad, and it means sight by the heart; the one meant is the man who disputed with Abraham, God's prophet, about his Lord because God had given him kingship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 429} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} He says this is God's wonder expressed to His prophet at the man who disputed with Abraham, which is why the preposition is used, as the Arabs do when they wonder at an act they disapprove of; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} He then reports that the disputant was a tyrant in Babylon called Nimrod son of Canaan son of Cush son of Shem son of Noah; and it is said he was Nimrod son of Falikh son of Eber son of Shalikh son of Arpachshad son of Shem son of Noah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} From Mujahid (5861 to 5864): he is Nimrod son of Canaan; the editor corrects the name of a transmitter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 430} From Qatada (5865, 5866): we were told that he was a king called Nimrod, the first to be tyrannical on earth and the builder of the tower at Babylon; from al-Rabi' (5867), the same; from al-Suddi (5868), Nimrod son of Canaan; from Ibn Zayd (5869), Ibn Ishaq (5870) and Zayd ibn Aslam (5871), that it was Nimrod; and from Ibn Jurayj (5872), through Abdullah ibn Kathir from Mujahid, that it was Nimrod, said to be the first king on earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 431} These are reports on the man's identity and genealogy as transmitted, concerning earlier peoples, and the museum does not rule on them.

Al-Tabari then explains the first part of Abraham's answer: when Abraham told him that his Lord is the one in whose hand are life and death, giving life to whom He wills and causing death to whom He wills after life, the other said that he did the same, sparing one he wished to kill, which was giving life, and killing another, which was causing death; the Arabs call sparing 'giving life', as in ([[passage/quran-5-32|5:32]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432} Abraham then said that God, his Lord, raises the sun on the eastern side, so let him, if truthful in claiming godhood, raise it on the opposite side; the disbeliever's argument was cut off and fell; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432} He explains that the verb for being confounded means to be cut short, and that the same root also means to slander someone with a lie, and notes that some readers read the verb in the active, meaning that Abraham confounded the disbeliever. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432} In the note the editor says one verbal noun al-Tabari gives is not in the language books though regular by analogy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 432}

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-258|2:258]]) from p. 433: from Qatada (5873): they were told that the disputant called two men, killed one and spared the other, saying, I give life to this one and kill whom I wish; Abraham then said that God raises the sun on the eastern side, so let him raise it from the opposite side, and the denier was cut short. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 433} From Mujahid (5874): I kill whom I wish and spare whom I wish, leaving him alive; and he said that the whole earth east and west was ruled by four men, two believers and two disbelievers: the believers Solomon son of David and Dhu al-Qarnayn, the disbelievers Nebuchadnezzar and Nimrod son of Canaan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 433} From Zayd ibn Aslam (5875): the first tyrant on earth was Nimrod, and people went out to buy food from him; Abraham went out to buy with them, and as people passed he asked, who is your Lord, and they said, you, until Abraham passed and was asked, and he answered as before, and the same exchange took place, so Nimrod sent him away without food; Abraham returned toward his family, passed a red sand dune and took from it to ease his family's hearts, then slept; his wife opened his baggage and found the finest food, cooked from it for him, and he knew God had provided and praised God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 433} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 434} Then God sent an angel to the tyrant three times to call him to belief, on condition that he be left in his kingdom, and he refused; the angel told him to gather his forces within three days, he did, and God commanded the angel to open on them a door of gnats so thick they hid the sun, which ate their flesh and drank their blood until only bones remained while the king was untouched; God then sent a gnat into his nostrils, and for four hundred years his head was beaten with mallets, the kindest to him being the one who clasped his hands and beat his own head; he had been a tyrant four hundred years, so God punished him four hundred years, and then caused him to die; he is the one who built a tower to the sky, whose building God destroyed from its foundations as in ([[passage/quran-16-26|16:26]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 434} This is a report about earlier peoples and the museum does not rule on its soundness. In the notes the editor discusses the spelling of Nimrod with dal or dhal, notes that al-Tabari relates this report in his History, corrects wording by the manuscript, and notes a duplication in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 433} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 434}

From Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn Aslam (5876): he is Nimrod, who was in Mosul, and people came to him and he asked them, who is your Lord, and they said you, and he would say, supply them with provisions; when Abraham came in with a camel on which to buy food for his child, Nimrod presented all of them, and presented Abraham twice, and when asked Abraham said that his Lord holds life and death; Nimrod said, I give life and death, if I wish I will kill you and if I wish I will spare you; Abraham answered with the sun, and the denier was cut short; Nimrod said, take this man out and give him nothing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 434} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 435} The people left with their supplies while Abraham's two sacks flapped empty; when he saw the dark outline of the mountains of his family's place he said, my little ones Ishmael and Isaac will grieve; if I fill these sacks with earth from this valley, their eyes will be cooled, and at night I will pour it out; he filled and stitched them and came back, and the boys threw themselves on them in joy; he put his head in Sarah's lap, and she rose to prepare food, opened one sack and found the finest white flour, kneaded and baked it and put it before him; asked where it came from, she said from your sack, and he looked at the other and found it the same, and knew its source. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 435} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 436} In the notes the editor explains the sack, the fine flour and the word for provisions and corrects the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 435}

From al-Rabi' (5877): when Abraham said his Lord is the giver of life and death, Nimrod said the same, calling two men, sparing one and killing the other, and Abraham answered with the sun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 436} From al-Suddi (5878): when Abraham came out of the fire they brought him in to the king, whom he had not met before; asked who his Lord was, he said the one who holds life and death; Nimrod said, I shut four men in a house with no food or drink till they starve, then feed and water two, who live, and leave two, who die; Abraham knew he had power by his authority to do that, and told him his Lord raises the sun, so bring it from the opposite side; the denier was cut short and said, this man is mad, take him out, do you not see that in his madness he dared break your gods and the fire did not consume him; he feared being disgraced among his people, and this is what ([[passage/quran-6-83|6:83]]) refers to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 436} From Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid (5879): I grant life by not killing and bring death by killing; he had brought two men, killed one and left the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 436} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 437} From Muhammad ibn Ishaq (5880): we were told, and God knows best, that Nimrod asked Abraham what his God was; Abraham said that his Lord is the giver of life and death; Nimrod said that he did too, taking two men deserving death by his judgement, killing one and pardoning the other; Abraham then answered with the sun so that Nimrod would know it was as he claimed; Nimrod was cut short, said nothing back and knew he could not do it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 437} These reports are transmitted as they came and the museum does not rule on them or choose among them.

Al-Tabari then explains the close of the verse: God does not guide people of disbelief to an argument by which they can overturn the argument of the people of truth in debate, since the arguments of falsehood are refuted; he refers to his earlier explanation that wrongdoing is putting a thing in the wrong place, so the disbeliever put his denial in the wrong place and wronged himself; this is his interpretation, and he says Ibn Ishaq said the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 437}

From Muhammad ibn Ishaq (5881) on the close of ([[passage/quran-2-258|2:258]]): God does not guide them in argument at the time of dispute, because of the error they are in. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 438}
