**Commentary on verses 74 and 75 (pp. 182-184)**

On "they said, then what is the penalty for it, if you are liars? They said, its penalty is that the one in whose baggage it is found, he himself is its penalty; thus do we repay the wrongdoers" (verses 74 and 75, which al-Tabari explains together and which are therefore gathered here under verse 74), al-Tabari says: Joseph's men said to his brothers: what is the penalty for theft if you are liars in your saying "we did not come to spread corruption in the land and we were not thieves"? "They said, its penalty is that the one in whose baggage it is found": Joseph's brothers said, the penalty for theft for the one in whose goods the stolen thing is found is himself; the one in whose baggage it is found has as his penalty that he be handed over for his theft to the one he stole from, to be enslaved; "thus do we repay the wrongdoers" means thus do we do to whoever wrongs, doing what he may not do by taking another's property by theft. The editor notes that "saraq" is the verbal noun of the thief's act and that the stolen thing is also called "saraq", which is sound Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 182}

The interpreters said the same: report 19556, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "he himself is its penalty" means he is handed over; "thus do we repay the wrongdoers" means thus do we do to whoever steals from us. Report 19557, from Ma'mar, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd al-Razzaq: we were told that they informed Joseph of the rule in their lands, that whoever steals is taken as a slave, and so they said its penalty is the one in whose baggage it is found. Report 19558, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: you take him, he is yours. Al-Tabari says the sense is: they said the penalty for the theft is the one in whose baggage it is found, as though it were said its penalty is the enslavement of the one in whose baggage it is found, the word "enslavement" then being dropped since its meaning is known, and the speech begins afresh, "he is its penalty; thus do we repay the wrongdoers". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 183}

Al-Tabari mentions two further grammatical readings: one, that the meaning is that they said the penalty for theft is that the one in whose baggage the stolen thing is found, the thief himself, is its penalty, so that the first "jaza'uhu" is raised by the whole predicate clause that follows; the other, that "man" is conditional, raised by the pronoun referring back to it in the "hu" of "rahlihi", with the "fa" in "fa-huwa" as the answer to the condition and the second "jaza'uhu" raised by "huwa", so that the sense is: the penalty for theft, whoever has the stolen thing found in his baggage, that is his penalty, he is enslaved and made a servant. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an on this verse and notes differences between the printed text and the manuscript in some words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 183}
