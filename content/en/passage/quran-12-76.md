## Summary
Verse 76 of [[passage/quran-12|Surat Yusuf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 76 of 111 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 76 (pp. 184-194)**

On "so he began with their bags before the bag of his brother, then he brought it out of his brother's bag; thus We planned for Joseph; he could not have taken his brother under the king's law unless God willed; We raise in rank whom We will, and above every possessor of knowledge is a knower", al-Tabari says: Joseph searched their bags and baggage looking for the king's cup, and began his search with the bags of his brothers by his father, searching them one by one before the bag of his brother by his father and mother, whose search he put off; then he searched the last, his brother's bag, and brought the cup out of it. The interpreters said the same: report 19559, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: we were told that he looked into no bag without asking God's forgiveness, shying from sin over what he had charged them with, until only his brother remained, the youngest of them, and he said: I do not think this one took anything! They said: yes, clear him! The editor explains "istabri'hu" as seeking clearance from a thing, and notes that "bal" is used here other than as an answer to a denial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 184}

Report 19560, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: whenever he opened a piece of baggage he asked forgiveness, repenting of what he had done, until he reached the boy's baggage and said, I do not think this one took anything! They said: yes, clear him. Report 19561, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: when the boy's baggage remained he said, this boy would not have taken it! They said: by God, he will not be left until you look in his baggage, so that we go with your mind at ease; and he put in his hand and brought it out of his baggage. Report 19562, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when the messenger said to them "for whoever brings it a camel's load, and I guarantee it", they said, we know nothing of it among us or with us. He said: you will not leave until I search your belongings and am excused in seeking it from you! He began with their bags, one by one, searching and looking at what was in them, until he came to his brother's bag, searched it, brought it out, took him by the neck and went back with him to Joseph. Report 19563, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: we were told that whenever he searched a man's baggage he asked his Lord's forgiveness, shying from sin, already knowing where what he was seeking was, until his brother remained, and knowing that what he sought was in it he said, I do not think this boy took it, and I do not mind not searching his baggage! His brothers said, it is better for your mind and ours that you clear his baggage also; and when he opened his baggage he brought out what he sought. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 185}

The grammarians differed on the pronoun in "then he brought it out of his brother's bag". One of the Basran grammarians said it refers to the mention of the "suwa'", and it is made feminine although the verse says "for whoever brings it" in the masculine, because the cup is meant; "suwa'" is masculine and some make it feminine, and here the "siqaya" is meant, which is feminine, the two being names for one thing like "thawb" and "milhafa", masculine and feminine for one thing. One of the Kufan grammarians said it goes to the feminine "saraqa" (the theft); and if "suwa'" has the sense of "sa'" perhaps the feminine comes from that, or, if you wish, you may take it as the feminine of "siqaya"; "suwa'" is masculine, "sa'" is both feminine and masculine, so whoever makes it feminine says "thalath asu'" and whoever makes it masculine says "asw'". Another said it was made feminine when feminine because "siqaya" was meant and masculine when masculine because "suwa'" was meant, like "khiwan" and "ma'ida", or "sinan al-rumh", where two names meet, one masculine and one feminine. The editor corrects the printed "and if the suwa' is not" by dropping "not", since "sa'" is both masculine and feminine. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 186}

On "thus We planned for Joseph", he says: thus We acted for Joseph so that he could free his brother by his father and mother from his brothers by their father, through their own admission that he may take him from them, keep him in his hands and stand between him and them; for they said, when asked "what is its penalty if you are liars", that the penalty of one who steals the cup is that the one in whose baggage it is found is enslaved for it, and that was their ruling in their religion. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "kayd". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 186}

So God planned for Joseph, as He has described to us, until he took his brother from them and the brother became his by their ruling and God's doing for him. On "he could not have taken his brother under the king's law unless God willed", he says: Joseph could not have taken his brother under the ruling, decree and obedience of the king of Egypt, since it was not that king's ruling or decree that anyone be enslaved for theft; so it was not for Joseph to take his brother under the law of the king of his land unless God willed, by the plan He devised for him, until his brothers and companions handed over the one in whose bag the cup was found by their own judgment on him, with their souls content to hand him over. The interpreters said the same: report 19564, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: except a pretext that God devised for him and Joseph took as his pretext. Report 19565, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa, is the same. Report 19566, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: "thus We planned for Joseph", God devised it for him and it became a pretext for Joseph. Report 19567, from Mujahid and Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "kidna" means We did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 187}

Report 19568, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: We did for Joseph. Report 19569, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: We did for Joseph. The interpreters differed over "under the king's law". Some said: he could not have taken his brother under the king's authority: report 19570, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father, and report 19571, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman. Others said it means in his ruling and decree: report 19572, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was not in the king's decree to enslave a man for theft. Report 19573, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: that was not in the king's "din", meaning his ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 188}

Report 19574, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih Muhammad ibn Layth al-Marwazi, a man he named, Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak and Abu Mawdud al-Madani: the king's law does not take a thief at all, but God planned for his brother so that they spoke as they spoke, and He took them by their word and not by the king's decree. The editor says he found no biography of Muhammad ibn Layth al-Marwazi, and that Abu Mawdud al-Madani is Abd al-Aziz ibn Abi Sulayman al-Hudhali, a storyteller of Medina, pious and virtuous, who preached, saw Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and other Companions, and is reliable. Report 19575, from Ma'mar, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya and Abd al-Razzaq: it reached him that the king's rule was that a thief's fine was doubled. Report 19576, from al-Suddi: in the king's ruling. Report 19577, from Ibn Ishaq: he could not have taken his brother under the king's law, that is, unjustly, but God planned for Joseph to join his brother to him. Report 19578, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: it is not in the king's law that a thief be taken for his theft; the rule among the prophets, Ya'qub and his sons, was that the thief is taken as an enslaved servant for his theft. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 189}

Al-Tabari says: these statements, though their speakers' wording differs on the meaning of "the king's law", are close in meaning, because whoever took him under the king's authority treated him according to his own deed, so he took him with his consent and not otherwise, and that is a ruling on him, and his ruling on him is his decree. The root meaning of "din" is obedience, which he has shown elsewhere. The editor notes that the printed wording here is garbled and that he corrects it from the unpointed manuscript. On "unless God willed": report 19579, from al-Suddi: but We did it for him, in that they said "he is its penalty". Report 19580, from Mujahid: except by a pretext that God devised and Joseph took as his pretext. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 190}

On "We raise in rank whom We will" the readers differed. Some read "nar-fa'u darajati man nasha'" with the ranks in construct with "man", meaning We raise the standings and ranks of whom We will in this world by knowledge over others, as We raised Joseph's rank and standing in this world over the standings and ranks of his brothers. Others read "darajatin" with tanwin, meaning We raise whom We will in ranks and degrees of knowledge over others, as We raised Joseph; "man" is accusative on this reading and genitive on the first, which he has explained in Sura al-An'am. Report 19581, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: Joseph and his brothers were given knowledge, and We raised Joseph above them in knowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 190}

On "and above every possessor of knowledge is a knower", he says: above every scholar is one who knows more, until that reaches God; he means that Joseph was the most knowing of his brothers and that above Joseph is one who knows more than Joseph, until it reaches God. The interpreters said the same: report 19582, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abu Amir al-Aqadi, Sufyan, Abd al-A'la al-Tha'labi and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he related a report and a man beside him said "and above every possessor of knowledge is a knower"; Ibn Abbas said: how badly you spoke! God is the Knower, and He is above every scholar. Report 19583, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki', Sufyan, Abd al-A'la and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: Ibn Abbas related a report and a man beside him said, praise be to God, "above every possessor of knowledge is a knower"; Ibn Abbas said: the Knower is God, and He is above every scholar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 191}

Report 19584, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri and Abd al-A'la: we were with Ibn Abbas, who related a report and a man marvelled and said, praise be to God, above every possessor of knowledge is a knower; Ibn Abbas said: how badly you spoke, God is the Knower and is above every scholar. Report 19585, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad, Isra'il and Salim: this one may know more than that one, and that one more than another, and God is above every scholar. Report 19586, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Sa'id ibn Mansur, Abu al-Ahwas, Abd al-A'la and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: God, the Aware, the Knowing, is above every scholar. Report 19587, through al-Muthanna, Ubayd Allah, Isra'il, Abd al-A'la and Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Ibn Abbas: God is above every scholar. Report 19588, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki' and Abu Ma'shar: a man asked Ali a question and he answered it; the man said, it is not so, but thus and thus; Ali said: you are right and I was wrong, "and above every possessor of knowledge is a knower". Report 19589, from Ikrima, through Ya'qub, Ibn Waki', Ibn Ulayya and Khalid: God's knowledge is above everyone. Report 19590, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr and Nadr: God, mighty and exalted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 192}

Report 19591, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Waki', Ya'la ibn Ubayd, Sufyan and Abd al-A'la: God knows more than anyone. Report 19592, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Ibn Shubruma: there is no scholar but above him is a scholar, until knowledge reaches God. Report 19593, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Asim, Juwayriya and Bashir al-Hujaymi: I heard al-Hasan recite this verse one day, then stop and say: by God, no scholar on the face of the earth lives to evening but above him is one who knows more, until knowledge returns to the One who taught him. Report 19594, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Ali, Jarir and Ibn Shubruma: above every scholar is a scholar until knowledge reaches God. Report 19595, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: until knowledge reaches God; from Him it began, the scholars came before, and to Him it returns; and in Abd Allah's reading it is "and above every scholar is a knower". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 193}

Al-Tabari says: if someone asks us, how was it allowed for Joseph to put the cup in his brother's baggage and then have innocent people charged with theft, saying "O caravan, you are thieves"? It is said: the words "O caravan, you are thieves" are God's report of a crier who cried them, not a report of Joseph's words; and it is possible that the crier cried so when the cup was missed, without knowing what Joseph had done. It is also possible that the crier cried so at Joseph's order, which he allowed because he knew they had stolen once on some occasion, and so told the crier to call them by their description as thieves, Joseph meaning that earlier theft and not their stealing the cup. Some interpreters have said that it was an error in Joseph's act, and God punished him through the people's answer, "if he steals, a brother of his stole before"; al-Tabari has given the report for that earlier. The editor corrects the wording "that the cup was missing" in the printed text and the manuscript, and refers to reports 19559 to 19563. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 193}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
