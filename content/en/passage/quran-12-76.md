**Commentary on verse 76 (pp. 184-185)**

On "so he began with their bags before the bag of his brother, then he brought it out of his brother's bag; thus We planned for Joseph; he could not have taken his brother under the king's law unless God willed; We raise in rank whom We will, and above every possessor of knowledge is a knower", al-Tabari says: Joseph searched their bags and baggage looking for the king's cup, and began his search with the bags of his brothers by his father, searching them one by one before the bag of his brother by his father and mother, whose search he put off; then he searched the last, his brother's bag, and brought the cup out of it. The interpreters said the same: report 19559, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: we were told that he looked into no bag without asking God's forgiveness, shying from sin over what he had charged them with, until only his brother remained, the youngest of them, and he said: I do not think this one took anything! They said: yes, clear him! The editor explains "istabri'hu" as seeking clearance from a thing, and notes that "bal" is used here other than as an answer to a denial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 184}

Report 19560, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: whenever he opened a piece of baggage he asked forgiveness, repenting of what he had done, until he reached the boy's baggage and said, I do not think this one took anything! They said: yes, clear him. Report 19561, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: when the boy's baggage remained he said, this boy would not have taken it! They said: by God, he will not be left until you look in his baggage, so that we go with your mind at ease; and he put in his hand and brought it out of his baggage. Report 19562, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when the messenger said to them "for whoever brings it a camel's load, and I guarantee it", they said, we know nothing of it among us or with us. He said: you will not leave until I search your belongings and am excused in seeking it from you! He began with their bags, one by one, searching and looking at what was in them, until he came to his brother's bag, searched it, brought it out, took him by the neck and went back with him to Joseph. Report 19563, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: we were told that whenever he searched a man's baggage he asked his Lord's forgiveness, shying from sin, already knowing where what he was seeking was, until his brother remained, and knowing that what he sought was in it he said, I do not think this boy took it, and I do not mind not searching his baggage! His brothers said, it is better for your mind and ours that you clear his baggage also; and when he opened his baggage he brought out what he sought. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 185}

We stop here at the foot of p. 185, in the middle of the commentary on verse 76 (after report 19563 and before the explanation of "thus We planned for Joseph"); the next batch begins at p. 186. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 185}
