## Summary
Verse 97 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 97 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The reading and what the signs are (pp. 26-27, 7448-7451).** Al-Tabari says the readers differed in reading this phrase: the readers of the cities read the evident marks in the plural, and Ibn Abbas read it in the singular, meaning by it the standing-place of Abraham, one evident mark. Then the interpreters differed over these marks and what they are. Some said: the standing-place of Abraham, the sacred monument and the like; Muhammad ibn Sad relays from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas that they are the standing-place of Abraham and the monument (7448); al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada and Mujahid that the standing-place of Abraham is among the evident marks (7449). The editor says the manuscript and the printed text have "Ishaq ibn Yahya" and the correct name is al-Hasan ibn Yahya, a chain that recurs throughout the commentary, the nearest being 7442, and that they did not give the phrase in full so he supplied the full recitation. Others said: the marks are the standing-place of Abraham and the security of anyone who entered the sanctuary; Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan to that effect (7450). Others said: the marks are the standing-place of Abraham itself; Muhammad ibn al-Husayn relays from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that the evident marks are that place (7451). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 27}

**The singular reading (7452 and 7453, pp. 27-28).** Al-Tabari says those who read it in the singular meant by the evident mark the standing-place of Abraham. Muhammad ibn Amr relays from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that his two feet in the Maqam are an evident mark, and that the security of one who enters is another matter (7452); the editor says the manuscript and the printed text have the plural, that Mujahid is reporting Ibn Abbas's singular reading and the copyist wrote it according to his own plural reading by oversight, so he restored it, and that the next report shows the same slip. Ammar relays from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from Layth, from Mujahid that the trace of his feet in the Maqam is an evident mark (7453). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 28}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 28).** Al-Tabari says the most correct of the views is that of those who said that the evident marks include the standing-place of Abraham, which is the saying of Qatada and Mujahid that Mamar relayed from them, so the word "among them" is understood and omitted since the sentence indicates it. If someone asks what the other marks are that justify the plural, it is said: among them the standing-place, the Hijr and the Hatim. He holds that the sounder of the two readings is the plural, because the readers of the Muslim cities agreed on it to the exclusion of any other. As for the interpreters' differences over the meaning of the standing-place of Abraham, he mentioned them in Surat al-Baqara and stated there the soundest view, which is, for him, the place known by that name; the editor refers to the earlier place in volume 3. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 28}

**Summary of the meaning of the signs (p. 29).** Al-Tabari says the meaning of the verse is therefore that the earliest house made for the people, a house of blessing and a guide for all beings, is the one at Bakka, and in it are evident marks of God's power and of the traces of His friend Abraham, among them the trace of his friend's foot in the stone on which he stood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 29}

**Security in the sanctuary: the view that it was in the Jahiliyya (7454-7458, pp. 29-30).** Al-Tabari reports that the interpreters differed over the security of the one who enters the sanctuary. Some said it is a report that whoever in the Jahiliyya had incurred an offence and then took refuge in the House was not seized for it. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that this was in the Jahiliyya, when a man who had brought every offence on himself and took refuge in God's sanctuary was neither touched nor pursued, but in Islam the sanctuary does not prevent the limits of God: a thief in it is cut, an adulterer in it has the penalty applied, and a killer in it is killed; and from Qatada that al-Hasan used to say the sanctuary does not prevent God's limits, and that if a man incurred a limit outside the sanctuary and then took refuge in it, this did not prevent the limit being applied to him; Qatada held what al-Hasan said (7454). Al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada that this was in the Jahiliyya, and that today a thief in it is cut, a killer in it is killed, and if the polytheists were overpowered in it they would be killed (7455). Said ibn Yahya al-Umawi relays from Abd al-Salam ibn Harb, from Khusayf, from Mujahid, on a man who kills and then enters the sanctuary, that he is seized, taken out of the sanctuary and then the penalty, meaning killing, is applied (7456); Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna relays from Muhammad ibn Jafar, from Shuba, from Hammad the same as Mujahid's saying (7457); Abu Kurayb and Abu al-Saib relay from Ibn Idris, from Hisham, from al-Hasan and Ata, on a man who incurs a limit and takes refuge in the sanctuary, that he is taken out and the limit applied to him (7458). Al-Tabari says the meaning of the verse on their view is: in it are evident marks, and the person who entered it was secure by them in the Jahiliyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 30}

**The other view: a conditional sense, and the report of Ibn Abbas (7459, pp. 30-31).** Others said the sense is conditional, like a speaker's "whoever stands for me I honour", meaning whoever will stand; that this was the state in the Jahiliyya, when the sanctuary was the refuge of every frightened person and the shelter of every offender, no one with an offence being harassed there and no man molesting the killer of his father or son; and that it is the same in Islam because Islam increased it in reverence and honour. Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Abi al-Shawarib relays from Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad, from Khusayf, from Mujahid, from Ibn Abbas that when a man incurs a limit, by killing or theft, and enters the sanctuary, no one pledges with him or shelters him until he becomes weary and leaves the sanctuary, whereupon the limit is applied; Mujahid said: I told Ibn Abbas that I did not hold that view and held that he should be seized with his rope, taken out of the sanctuary and the limit applied, since the sanctuary only adds to his severity (7459). The editor says the rope is a piece of cord with which a captive or killer is bound when led to execution in retaliation, and that "seized with his rope" means seized by force until killed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 31}

**The report of Ibn al-Zubayr and Said, and the next (7460-7461, p. 31).** Abu Kurayb and Abu al-Saib relay from Ibn Idris, from Abd al-Malik, from Ata that Ibn al-Zubayr seized Said, a client of Muawiya, who was in a fortress at al-Taif, and sent to Ibn Abbas to consult him about them, saying they were enemies of theirs; Ibn Abbas sent back: if I found my father's killer I would not molest him. Ibn al-Zubayr sent: shall we not bring them out of the sanctuary? Ibn Abbas sent: why not before you brought them into the sanctuary? Abu al-Saib added: he brought them out and crucified them, paying no heed to Ibn Abbas's words (7460). The editor says the printed text has a word that makes no sense and the manuscript a corrupt one, so he put "enemy" as the nearest reading, fearing it may not be right; that the last wording also differs between the two and he kept the closest; that after effort he did not find this report in this form; that al-Azraqi in Akhbar Makka relates something similar saying "client of Utba" and not "client of Muawiya"; that al-Tabari in his History mentions Said the client of Muawiya as his chamberlain; and that this needs a verification he could not make. Yaqub ibn Ibrahim relays from Hushaym, from Hajjaj, from Ata, from Ibn Abbas that one who committed an offence outside the sanctuary and then took refuge in it is not confronted, not pledged with, not spoken to and not sheltered until he leaves it, and when he leaves he is seized and the limit applied; and one who committed an offence in the sanctuary has it applied there (7461); the editor says the printed text adds the word for "the limit" wrongly and he kept the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 31}

**Further reports on the one who takes refuge (7462-7467, pp. 32-33).** Abu Kurayb relays from Ibrahim ibn Ismail ibn Numayr al-Sulami, from Ibn Abi Habiba, from Dawud ibn Husayn, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas that one who committed an offence and sought the protection of the House is secure, and Muslims have no right to punish him for anything until he leaves, and when he leaves they apply the limit (7462); the editor says Ibrahim ibn Ismail is al-Tabban and Ibn Abi Habiba is Ibrahim ibn Ismail ibn Abi Habiba al-Ashhali, both of whom appeared earlier. Yaqub relays from Hushaym, from Hajjaj, from Ata, from Ibn Umar, who said: if I found Umar's killer in the sanctuary I would not stir him (7463). Abu Kurayb and Abu al-Saib relay from Ibn Idris, from Layth, from Ata that al-Walid ibn Utba wished to apply a limit in the sanctuary and Ubayd ibn Umayr told him not to apply it there unless the offence was committed there (7464). From Mutarrif, from Amir, that one who incurred a limit and fled to the sanctuary is secure, and if he incurred it in the sanctuary it is applied there (7465); Ibn Bashshar relays from Muammal, from Sufyan, from Firas, from al-Shabi that one who incurred a limit in the sanctuary has it applied there, and one who incurred it outside and then entered is not spoken to or pledged with until he leaves, and then it is applied (7466). Said ibn Yahya al-Umawi relays from Abd al-Salam ibn Harb, from Ata ibn al-Saib, from Said ibn Jubayr, and from Abd al-Malik, from Ata ibn Abi Rabah, on a man who kills and enters the sanctuary, that the people of Makka do not sell to him or buy from him, give him to drink or eat or shelter him, and he listed many things, until he leaves the sanctuary and is seized for his offence (7467). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 32}

**The rest, and the view of security from the Fire (7468-7472, p. 33).** Ammar relays from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from Ata ibn al-Saib, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas that a man who incurred a limit and entered the sanctuary is not fed, given drink, sheltered, spoken to, married off or pledged with, and when he leaves it the limit is applied (7468). Al-Muthanna relays from Hajjaj, from Hammad, from Amr ibn Dinar, from Ibn Abbas the same: not sheltered, not sat with, not pledged with, not fed and not given drink until he leaves (7469), and from Hammad, from Ata ibn al-Saib, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas the like (7470). Muhammad ibn al-Husayn relays from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that if a man killed a man and then came to the Ka'ba and took refuge in it and the victim's brother met him, it would never be lawful for him to kill him (7471). Others said the sense is safety from the Fire; Ali ibn Muslim relays from Abu Asim, from Ruzayq ibn Muslim al-Makhzumi, from Ziyad ibn Abi Ayyash, from Yahya ibn Jada that it means secure from the Fire (7472). The editor says this report is not in itself a proof; it is a view on the meaning of the verse held by a successor, an opinion, and that Yahya ibn Jada ibn Hubayra al-Qurashi al-Makhzumi is a reliable successor but relates nothing here from anyone else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 33}

**The editor's note on the chain of 7472 (pp. 33-34).** The editor says the men of this chain are problematic. Ruzayq ibn Muslim al-Makhzumi stands so in the printed text and the manuscript, but the manuscript does not dot the letter, so it could be Ruzayq or Zurayq; Ibn Kathir, quoting the chain from the commentary of Ibn Abi Hatim, has "Zurayq ibn Muslim the blind, client of Banu Makhzum"; after long search he found no biography of him; he notes that al-Dhahabi in al-Mushtabih and Ibn Hajar in Tahrir al-Mushtabih distinguish the two forms, al-Dhahabi listing "Ruzayq ibn Hisham from Ziyad ibn Abi Ayyash" and "Ruzayq the blind from Abu Hurayra", whom he calls weak, and he could not be sure that either of these is the man here. His teacher Ziyad ibn Abi Ayyash also had no biography beyond mention in those two works; the printed text has "Ayyad", a corruption, and the manuscript leaves the letter undotted, and its being dotted in the two works favours the reading adopted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 34}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 34-35).** Al-Tabari says the soundest of the views for him is that of Ibn al-Zubayr, Mujahid, al-Hasan and those who said the sense is: the one who comes into it from elsewhere, taking refuge in it, is secure while in it, but is brought out and the limit applied if he committed outside what requires it and then took refuge, and if he committed it inside it is applied inside; so the meaning of the verse is: in it are evident marks, and the person who enters it seeking protection is secure from what he sought protection from while he is in it, until he leaves. If someone asks what prevents applying the limit in it, he says: the agreement of all the predecessors that one whose offence was elsewhere and who took refuge is not seized for it inside; they differed only on the manner of bringing him out so that he may be seized. Some said the manner is withholding the means that force him to leave; others said there is no manner but bringing him out by whatever means lead to applying God's limit. For that reason he said it is not permissible to apply the limit in it except after he is brought out, while for one who incurred the limit inside there is no disagreement that it is applied inside; so both questions are founded on agreed rulings as he described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 35}

**The objection and al-Tabari's answer (pp. 35-36).** Al-Tabari then raises a question: what proves that bringing out the one who takes refuge in the House, having come for protection from an offence or a limit, is permitted so that the limit be applied, when you accept that God made the one who enters secure, and "secure" differs from "fearful"? He answers that he said so because of the agreement of the earlier and later scholars of the community that bringing out one who took refuge from an offence or a grave act that makes a penalty due, by some means of bringing out, to seize him for what is due, is obligatory on the Muslims' imam and the people of Islam with him; they differed only on the means. Some said the means is that all Muslims leave off pledging with him, feeding him, giving him drink, sheltering him and speaking to him and the like, things with which the refugee cannot stay even if only some of them were done, let alone all; others of them said bringing him out to apply the penalty due is obligatory by every means. Since there is agreement that God's ruling on one who takes refuge in the House from a limit or an offence is to bring him out to carry out what God made obligatory on the believers, and they differed only on the means, it was binding on them and their imam to bring him out by whatever means possible until they apply the limit outside, if he had sought refuge from outside. The editor notes the syntax of a sentence in the manuscript and the printed text and that the earlier phrase is read as a question about how the senses of secure and fearful differ in the sanctuary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 36}

**End of al-Tabari's argument on the security of the one who enters (p. 37).** Al-Tabari concludes that, as he has described, the sense is that whoever enters it is secure while he remains in it. One who takes refuge in it from a penalty that has fallen on him is therefore secure for as long as he stays, until he leaves; he comes into fear only after leaving or being made to leave, and then he is neither entering it nor within it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 37}

**The obligation of pilgrimage and the dispute over "the way" (p. 37).** Al-Tabari explains that the sense is a binding obligation owed to God on every accountable person who is able to find the way to making pilgrimage to His sacred house. He reminds the reader that he explained the meaning of pilgrimage earlier and does not repeat it. He then reports that the interpreters differed over what "the way" is, the ability to find which makes pilgrimage obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 37}

**First view: provisions and a mount (7474-7483, pp. 37-39).** Some said the way is provisions and a riding animal. Al-Tabari reports this from Umar ibn al-Khattab (7474) and Amr ibn Dinar (7475), both through Ibn Jurayj, and from Ibn Abbas in the wording provisions and a camel (7476, through al-Dahhak; the chain includes Abu Jinab al-Kalbi, Yahya ibn Abi Hayya, of whom the editor notes that he was criticised because he practised concealment in transmission and related what he had not heard). Another report from Ibn Abbas (7477) says the way is that the person's body is sound and he has the price of provisions and a mount without being overburdened. Said ibn Jubayr relates from Ibn Abbas that one who owns three hundred dirhams has the way (7478). The same view of provisions and a mount is given from Ata (7479), from al-Suddi on Ibn Abbas (7480), from Said ibn Jubayr (7481) and from al-Hasan al-Basri (7482). Al-Hasan also relates (7483) that the Prophet recited the verse, a man asked what the way was, and he answered provisions and a mount. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 38} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 39}

**Their reliance on reports from the Prophet (7484-7487, pp. 39-41).** Al-Tabari says holders of this view relied on reports transmitted from the Prophet to the same effect. One is the report of Ibn Umar that a man asked the Prophet what the way was and he answered provisions and a mount (7484, then 7485 through Sufyan al-Thawri). The editor Ahmad Shakir sets out the chain at length: Ibrahim ibn Yazid al-Makki al-Khuzi is, in his words, very weak, as Ahmad, Ibn Main, Abu Zura and Abu Hatim judged, while his informant Muhammad ibn Abbad ibn Jafar al-Makhzumi is a trustworthy follower. He traces the report in al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, al-Shafii's al-Umm and al-Bayhaqi; al-Tirmidhi said he knew it only from al-Khuzi's report and that some scholars spoke against al-Khuzi over his memory. He quotes Ibn Kathir that its men are trustworthy except al-Khuzi and that another had corroborated him, but holds that the second chain Ibn Kathir gives is also weak, since Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Ubayd ibn Umayr al-Laythi is very weak (al-Bukhari said he is not a solid authority, al-Nasai that his reports are abandoned). The editor points ahead to al-Tabari's remark on p. 45 that these are reports whose chains are open to question. Then comes al-Hasan al-Basri's mursal report (7486, by two chains) that the Prophet recited the verse, was asked about the way and answered provisions and a mount; the editor calls it mursal, quotes Ibn al-Mundhir that no connected chain is firmly established for it and that al-Hasan's mursal is the soundest form, and explains that he does not mean a mursal report is sound, since such reports are weak. Last is the report of Ali (7487, repeated in 7489) that the Prophet said of one who owns provisions and a mount to reach God's house and does not make pilgrimage that it makes no difference to him whether he dies a Jew or a Christian, as transmitted. In its chain is Hilal ibn Abd Allah Abu Hashim, whom the editor calls very weak (al-Bukhari: his reports are disowned; al-Tirmidhi: unknown), and al-Harith al-Awar al-Hamdani, also very weak in the editor's judgement; al-Tirmidhi called the report strange with something to be said about its chain, and Ibn Adi is quoted that it is not preserved. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 39} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 40} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 41}

**Repeated reports (7488-7491, pp. 41-43).** Al-Tabari relates from Qatada, from al-Hasan, that it reached them that the Prophet was asked about the way and said it is whoever finds provisions and a mount (7488), repeats Ali's report through Shadh ibn Fayyad from Hilal Abu Hashim (7489; the editor says Ahmad ibn al-Hasan al-Tirmidhi was trustworthy, a companion of Ahmad ibn Hanbal, that Shadh ibn Fayyad is trustworthy, and that the report repeats 7487 with the same flaw in Hilal), and then two reports from al-Hasan of a man asking the Prophet and being answered provisions and a mount (7490 through Hammad ibn Salama from Qatada and Humayd, and 7491 in the same sense). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 41} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 42} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 43}

**Second view: the power to arrive (7492-7496, pp. 43-44).** Others said the way is the capacity to reach the house. They argued that this may be on foot or riding, and that a person can have provisions and a mount yet be unable to arrive because an enemy blocks the road, or water is scarce and the like; the clearest statement is therefore what God made plain, being able to reach it with nothing preventing, which may be on foot alone even if a mount is lacking. Al-Tabari relates from Ibn al-Zubayr that it is according to strength (7492, through an unnamed man). From al-Dahhak (7493) comes provisions and a mount, and that a healthy young man with no money must hire himself out for his food and bare subsistence until he completes his pilgrimage; when someone asked whether God burdened people to walk to the House, he replied that if one of them had an inheritance in Mecca he would go to it even crawling, and so with pilgrimage. The editor's note discusses the variant readings of the word for bare subsistence and gives his choice. From Ata (7494) comes that whoever finds something that carries him has found the way; from Amir al-Shabi (7495) that it is what God has made easy, where the editor repaired a confused chain in the manuscripts and refers to 7516; and from al-Hasan (7496) that whoever finds what carries him has been able. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 43} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 44}

**Third view: health (7497-7498, p. 44).** Others said the way is health, reported from Ikrima, the freedman of Ibn Abbas (7497, through Haywa ibn Shurayh and Ibn Lahia from Shurahbil ibn Sharik al-Maafiri). Al-Tabari then begins Ibn Zayd's report (7498): that it means finding strength in expenses, in body and in the means of carriage; the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 44}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 44 in the commentary on verse 97 and continue from p. 45. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 44}
