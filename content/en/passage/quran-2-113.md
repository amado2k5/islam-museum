## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari relates that this verse, "the Jews said the Christians have nothing to stand on...", came down about a group of the two peoples of the Book who disputed before the Messenger of God; he reports from Ibn Abbas (in Ibn Ishaq's report) that when the Christians of Najran came to the Messenger of God, Jewish rabbis came to them and they disputed before him; Rafi' ibn Huraymila said "you have nothing", denying Jesus and the Gospel, and a man of Najran said "you have nothing", rejecting the prophethood of Moses and the Torah, and the verse was revealed (the editor refers to the Sira of Ibn Hisham); and from al-Rabi' that they were the People of the Book in the Prophet's time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 513} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 514}

He says the verse means that the Jews said the Christians are not right in their religion, and the Christians said the Jews are not right in theirs; God reports their saying this to the believers to inform them that each party neglects the ruling of the Book whose truth it professes, while denying what God sent down in it, because the Gospel confirms the Torah's prophethood of Moses and the obligations on the Children of Israel, and the Torah confirms the prophethood of Jesus and what he brought. Each said what it said while reciting its own book, which witnesses against its falsehood, knowing it was in error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 514}

Asked whether the Jews and Christians were on anything after the Prophet was sent, he answers that each party's denial was a denial of the prophethood of the prophet whom the other party claims to believe in, not a denial that the other was on any of its religion at the time the Prophet was sent, since both denied the prophethood of Muhammad; the sense is that the Jews said the Christians have nothing of their religion since they took it up, and the Christians said the same of the Jews, and God called both parties false. He reports from Qatada: yes, the first Christians were on something, but they innovated and split, and so the Jews; and from Mujahid: the first of the Jews and Christians were on something. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 515}

On "while they recite the Book" he says it is the Book of God, the Torah and Gospel, which are witnesses against both parties of unbelief and of their going against God's command; from Ibn Abbas (in Ibn Ishaq's report): each recites in his book the confirmation of what he disbelieves, the Jews disbelieving in Jesus though they have the Torah with the covenant to believe in him, and the Gospel confirming Moses, each disbelieving what the other holds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 515} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 516}

On "thus said those who do not know, the like of their saying" he records that the interpreters differed over whom God means. Some said, from al-Rabi' and Qatada, that the Christians said the like of what the Jews said before them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 516}
