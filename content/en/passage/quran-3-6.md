## Summary
Verse 6 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 6 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-3-6|3:6]]), pp. 166 to 169. Al-Tabari says the meaning is: God is the one who gives you shapes inside your mothers as He pleased and loved, making one male and another female, one black and another red; by this He teaches His servants that everyone the wombs of women contain was formed and created by Him, that Jesus son of Mary is among those He formed in his mother's womb and created in it, and that had he been a god he would not have been contained by his mother's womb, since the creator of what is in wombs is not contained by wombs, which contain only creatures. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 166} The editor restores the letter fa at the start of a phrase that the print dropped, on the manuscript's authority.

In 6567, Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq): Jesus was among those formed in wombs, which they do not deny or reject, as others of Adam's sons were formed; how then could he be a god when he had that station? The editor says the word 'that is' is missing from the manuscript and the print and that he restored it from the Sira of Ibn Hisham, this being Ibn Ishaq's way in the earlier reports, and that it is the remainder of the reports ending at 6566. In 6568, al-Rabi' (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Jafar and his father): He formed Jesus in the womb as He pleased. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 167}

Others said what is in 6569, from al-Suddi, from Abu Malik and Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas, from Murra al-Hamdani from Ibn Mas'ud, and from some of the Prophet's Companions (through Musa ibn Harun, Amr ibn Hammad and Asbat): when the sperm falls in the womb it moves through the body for forty days, then becomes a clot for forty days, then a morsel for forty days; when it is time for it to be created, God sends an angel to shape it; the angel brings dust between his fingers, mixes it into the morsel, kneads it with it and shapes it as he is commanded, and says: male or female, wretched or fortunate, and what is his provision, lifespan, trace and afflictions? God speaks and the angel writes; when that body dies it is buried where the dust was taken from. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 168} The editor says the chain was discussed at report 168, and that the account of how a human is created in the mother's womb appears with other wording and another chain in Muslim (the book of destiny) and in al-Bukhari (the book of the beginning of creation, the chapter on the mention of angels, and the book of menstruation, the chapter on the formed and the unformed). In 6570, Qatada (through Bishr, Yazid and Said): God, our Lord, is able to form His servants in wombs as He pleases, male or female, black or red, complete in creation or not.

Al-Tabari then explains the close of the verse: this statement is God declaring Himself above having a peer or equal in His lordship, or that divinity could be permitted to any other, and a denial of those who said what they said about Jesus among the Najran delegation who came to the Messenger, and of all who held the like of their saying, and of everyone who claimed another object of worship with God or acknowledged another's lordship. Then God told His creation of His attribute as a warning to whoever worships another or associates anyone in His worship: He is al-Aziz, whom no one helps against one He wishes to punish, and no refuge or shelter saves that one from Him, because of His might before which every creature is lowly and every existing thing humble; then He told them He is al-Hakim in His management, His excusing Himself to His creatures and His following up His proofs against them, so that whoever perishes among them perishes after clear evidence and whoever lives lives after clear evidence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 168} The editor explains in a footnote that the two words for refuge both mean the shelter a frightened person flees to and takes protection in, and refers to the language indexes under the roots for might and for wisdom. The Christian sayings mentioned here are attributed to those who held them as al-Tabari relates, with no museum verdict.

In 6571, Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq): then the Lord said, declaring Himself above and affirming His oneness against what they set beside Him: al-Aziz in taking vengeance on whoever disbelieved in Him when He wills, and al-Hakim in His excuse and argument to His servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 169} The editor holds that the phrase 'in His help' in the print and the manuscript is wrong in meaning, since the word for help is the noun from 'to help' and has no place there, while the word for taking vengeance is the one needed; that the Sira of Ibn Hisham has 'in His argument and excuse to His servants', which is better because of the 'to'; and that to excuse oneself to someone is to go the whole way in guiding him until no room is left for apology. In 6572, al-Rabi' (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Jafar and his father): al-Aziz in His vengeance, al-Hakim in His command.
