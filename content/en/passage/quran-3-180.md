## Summary
Verse 180 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 180 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 180 (pp. 428-430): the readings of the first verb.** Al-Tabari says the reciters differ: a group of the people of the Hijaz and Iraq read it with ta', and another group with ya' (the note: the printed text and the manuscript wrote the first reading with ya' and the second with ta', plainly a mistake, since al-Tabari then says the grammarians differed over the reading with ya' and not ta', so the editor corrected the places of the two readings). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 428}

**The grammarians' difference (pp. 429-430).** One of the grammarians of Kufa (al-Farra') said the meaning is "let not the misers think that miserliness is better for them", the verb of the participle standing in for the noun, as one says "so-and-so came and I was glad of him", meaning of his coming; and "huwa" is a "'imad", which the Basrans call the pronoun of separation, also named "da'ama" and "sifa" (the note: reference to al-Farra', 1: 248-249 and 1: 104, and to earlier passages). A Basran grammarian said he meant "let them not think miserliness is better for them", dropping the noun on which the thinking falls, namely miserliness, since the thinking had been mentioned and what God had given of His bounty had been mentioned, so he implied both. The note says this sentence is extremely disordered; the editor prints the manuscript as it is, suspecting a dropped line, and found no reference to this Basran statement in the books of exegesis and of Qur'anic grammar; he conjectures that the Basran meant that the verse resembles its sister at [[passage/quran-3-188|3:188]], where the prohibition is repeated for emphasis because the speech grew long, and that here the thinking is likewise repeated and then the second dropped, the first that forced the repetition remaining; and he excuses himself, saying he found no other way to make the sentence run, and that if right it is by God's grace, and if wrong he asks forgiveness. The same speaker adds that omission has occurred in stronger form than this, as in [[passage/quran-57-10|57:10]], where one who spent after the conquest is not mentioned since what follows shows that he meant them. One who rejected the Basran view said that "man" there has the sense of a plural, the meaning being "among you those who spent before the conquest are not equal in their ranks and states, so how of those who spent after it?" so the first suffices. Of the verse under discussion he said that something is omitted, but it is omitted only where something in the speech stands in for it: "huwa" returns to miserliness and "better for them" returns to the nouns, so the two pronouns show there are two nouns before them, and the verb suffices for the noun of action. When read with ta', miserliness precedes the relative; when read with ya', miserliness follows it; and the verb has served for the noun, as the poet said (the extracted text breaks off before the line; the note says its author is not known). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 429} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 430}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 430 in the commentary on verse 180 and continue from p. 431. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 430}
