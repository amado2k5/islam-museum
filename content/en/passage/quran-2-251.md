## Summary
Verse 251 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 251 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-251|2:251]]) begins on p. 354. Al-Tabari says Talut and his troops routed Goliath's army and David killed Goliath, and that the passage leaves out something understood, namely that God answered their prayer, sent patience on them, steadied their feet and helped them, the rout being by God's decree and measure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 354} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 355} He says this David is David son of Jesse, a prophet of God, and the cause of his killing him is what he relates from Wahb b. Munabbih (5740). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 355} In the margin the editor refers to the name Jesse in the People of the Book's scriptures and corrects the word for the rout to the sense it requires; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 355}

In Wahb's report (5740), Goliath called for a duel and promised his kingdom to whoever killed him; David was brought to Talut and stipulated that if he killed him Talut would marry him to his daughter and give him authority over his property. Talut put arms on him, David disliked fighting in them and went out with a sling and a pouch of stones; Goliath said, you fight me, and he said yes; he hurled a stone that struck between his eyes, passed through his brain and felled him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 355} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 356} Then people claimed the killing and David hid the head; Talut said whoever brought the head had killed him, and David brought it and asked for what was promised. Talut regretted his terms and set his daughter's bride-price at three hundred foreskins of the enemy, hoping David would be killed; he raided and brought them, and Talut had no choice but to marry him to her; then regret overtook him and he wanted to kill David, who fled to the mountain, where Talut besieged him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 356} One night Talut and his guards slept and David took Talut's ewer, cut some hairs from his beard and a piece of the fringe of his clothes, and called to him that he could have killed him; Talut knew he could have, so this softened him toward David and he made a covenant with him, though at the end he was plotting to kill him, and he never fought an enemy but was beaten, until he died. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 356} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 357} Wahb said no revelation came to Talut, but a prophet was with him, Ashmwil, who had made him king. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 357} In the margins the editor explains words of the report and compares the manuscript, printed and al-Durr al-Manthur texts; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 355} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 356} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 357} These are reports from Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-251|2:251]]) from p. 357: from Ibn Ishaq from Wahb (5741): David was with four brothers of his and their old father; their father stayed behind and David stayed with him tending his flock, being one of the youngest, a short man, blue-eyed, with little hair on his head and a pure heart. His father sent him with provisions to his brothers who had gone out with Talut, with his pouch and sling, and he passed three stones that spoke to him, each asking him to take it to kill Goliath with, one the stone of Jacob, one of Isaac and one of Abraham, and he took them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 357} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 358} In the camp he heard how people magnified Goliath and said had he seen him he would kill him; he was brought to Talut, who asked about his strength, and he said he used to catch a lion that went for a sheep of his flock, take it by the head and prise its jaws off it; Talut called for a coat of mail for him. When the armies met they showed him Goliath, and the three stones leapt from his pouch; he took one, put it in his sling and struck him between the eyes, breaking his skull and killing him, and his army fled. The people deposed Talut and turned to David, though the People of the Book claim that Talut meant to murder David, God turned him from it, and he knew his fault and sought repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 359} In the margins the editor notes differences between the manuscript and the printed text and explains words; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 357} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 359}

In another report from Wahb (5742), unlike the previous two: God inspired the prophet of the Children of Israel to tell Talut to raid the people of Midian and leave none alive, and he killed all but their king, whom he took captive, driving off their livestock; God told Ashmwil to tell him He would take the kingship from his house and it would not return, and to go to Jesse and anoint with the oil of holiness the one God showed; Jesse showed six sons, each time not the one, and then David came, a ruddy boy tending sheep, whom he anointed, telling his father to keep it secret. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 359} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 360} Then Goliath marched, Talut marched and they prepared for battle; Goliath called for a duel, Talut had it cried that whoever came out against him and killed him, the king would marry him to his daughter; Jesse sent David to his brothers, he heard the cry and asked them whether any would come out, and they called him a foolish boy; he went to the crier and then the king and told him that a lion had attacked his sheep and he had taken it by the jaws; they dressed him in arms and he mounted a horse, returned and said, if God does not kill him for me, neither the horse nor the arms will; he took his pouch and sling and went and struck Goliath, hitting the nose-piece of his helmet till it reached his brain, and he fell, and David cut off his head. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 360} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 361} Talut grew resentful hearing the people speak of David; the son warned his sister, David hid and his wife made a shape in his bed like a sleeper; David was a fugitive in the mountains until Talut was killed and David reigned after him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 361} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 362} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 363} In the margins the editor explains the foreskin, the Jaramiqa and the manuscript's gaps; From Mujahid (5743): Talut was commander of the army, David's father sent something with David to his brothers, and David asked Talut what he would get if he killed Goliath; he said a third of his kingdom and his daughter. He took three stones and named them Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and said, in the name of my God and the God of my fathers; the one named Abraham came out and he hurled it, and it pierced thirty-three helmets from his head and killed thirty thousand behind him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 363} These are reports from Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-251|2:251]]) from p. 363: from al-Suddi (5744): David's father crossed the river with Talut among those who crossed, with thirteen sons, David being the youngest; he came to him one day and said that nothing he threw at with his sling failed to fall, and his father gave him good news; he said he had entered among the mountains and found a lion crouching and took it by the ears and it did not stir, and that he walked among the mountains glorifying and every mountain glorified with him; his father used to send him with food to his brothers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 363} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 364} The prophet brought a horn with oil and an iron mail-shirt and said that their man who would kill Goliath would have the horn put on his head, so that it boiled until he was anointed without it running down his face, and he would fill the mail-shirt; Talut tried the Children of Israel with it and none fitted, then asked David's father for a son who had not been present and he named David. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 364} When he came he passed three stones that spoke to him; Talut had said whoever killed Goliath he would marry to his daughter and share his seal in his kingdom; the horn was set on David's head and boiled until he was anointed, and he put on the mail-shirt and filled it, a thin, sallow man in whom no one else had put it on without rattling, so that the garment tightened on him until it creaked. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 364} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 365} He walked to Goliath, who was among the biggest and strongest of men, and God cast fear of him into Goliath's heart;
