**Commentary on verse 32 (pp. 460-461)**

On "messengers before you were mocked, and I gave respite to those who disbelieved, then I seized them; so how was My punishment", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise, to His Prophet Muhammad: Muhammad, if these polytheists of your people mock and ask you for signs, denying what you brought them, be patient with their harm to you and go on with your Lord's command in warning them and leaving them without excuse; for nations before you that have passed away mocked My messengers, so I prolonged their respite and extended their term, then brought down on them My punishment and vengeance when they persisted in their error and straying; so look how My punishment of them was when I punished them, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 460}

did I not make them taste painful punishment and make them a lesson for those of understanding? "Imla'" in Arab speech is prolonging; one says "amlaytu li-fulan" when you prolonged his respite; from it "al-mulawa of time", and their saying "tamallaytu habiban"; and for that reason night and day are called "al-malawan" for their length, as Ibn Muqbil said in a verse about the dwellings of the clan, worn away by night and day. A wide stretch of land is called "mala", as al-Tirimmah said in a verse describing water and water-carrying camels in the far-reaching expanse, for the length between its two ends and its extent. In a note the editor says al-Tirimmah's poem is disordered and much of it lost, that he could not determine where the verse belongs in it, and that for him "mutabatin" means far and wide, as in al-Zamakhshari's Asas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 461}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
