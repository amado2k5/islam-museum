## Summary
Verse 36 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 36 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "Then Satan caused them to slip from it" al-Tabari notes that most readers read "azallahuma" with a doubled lam, meaning he made them slip in their religion, which is why their leaving the Garden is attributed to Iblis, while others read "azalahuma", meaning he moved them away. He reports from Ibn Abbas that it means he led them astray. His own choice is "azallahuma", since God says in the next phrase that Iblis took them out, so removing and expelling cannot be one and the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 524} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 525}
He then asks how Iblis made them slip and says the scholars gave several accounts. He reports a long account from Wahb ibn Munabbih: Iblis entered the belly of the serpent, which then had four legs, took from the tree and brought it to Hawwa, who ate and then Adam, and their private parts appeared; God cursed the serpent, laid on Hawwa pregnancy and childbirth with hardship, and made the earth's fruit thorns. He reports a similar account from al-Suddi from Ibn Abbas and others: the keepers stopped Iblis entering the Garden, so he came to the serpent, which took him in its mouth; he spoke from there, asked "shall I show you the tree of immortality", swore by God that he was their adviser, and Hawwa ate first, then Adam. The editor lists manuscript and printed variants in the wording. Al-Tabari gives these as the reports of their narrators and adds no view of his own. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 525} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 526} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 527}

Al-Tabari continues with reports of how Iblis made them slip. From al-Rabi' he gives that Satan entered the Garden in the form of a beast with legs and, being cursed, lost them and became a serpent. From Abu al-Aliya he gives that Iblis came to Hawwa first and told them their Lord had forbidden the tree only so they would not become angels or immortals, and Hawwa ate and told Adam to eat. From Ibn Ishaq, on the authority of some people of knowledge, he gives that Adam, seeing the Garden's honour, said "if only there were immortality", and Satan seized on the word and approached him through immortality. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 528}
From Ibn Ishaq, as he was told, he gives that Satan began by wailing over them until they were grieved, saying they would die and lose their blessing, and then whispered to them. From Ibn Zayd he gives that Adam told his Lord he had been approached through Hawwa, and God decreed that she would menstruate each month and bear and give birth with hardship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 529}
He reports from Said ibn al-Musayyib, by a chain through Ibn Ishaq, that he used to swear that Adam did not eat from the tree knowingly, because Hawwa gave him wine until he was drunk and led him to it. From Ibn Abbas by way of Tawus he reports that Iblis offered himself to the beasts of the earth and all refused except the serpent, which took him between two fangs; it then walked on four legs, which God removed so that it crawls on its belly, and Ibn Abbas said to kill it wherever found. Ibn Ishaq adds that the People of the Torah say only that the serpent spoke to Adam, without Ibn Abbas's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 530}
He gives a report from Muhammad ibn Qays in which God asks Adam, Hawwa, the serpent and Iblis in turn who told them, and the curse falls on all, matched with the verse in surah al-A'raf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 530} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 531}
Al-Tabari then says these reports were handed down from those he names about how Iblis made them slip, and that the one nearest the truth in his view is what agrees with God's Book. The Book says Iblis whispered to them and swore he was their adviser, which is clear evidence that he addressed them himself, either visibly or hidden in something else, since swearing cannot be done by indirect means. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 531}

Al-Tabari goes on, and this is his own view, that Iblis's addressing them directly with an oath shows his dealing with them was not the same as with Adam's children today. The accounts from Ibn Abbas and Wahb ibn Munabbih of how he reached the Garden after being expelled contain nothing that reason or a binding report refutes and are within what is possible, and he holds them to be so because the commentators agree in confirming them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 531} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 532}
He notes that Ibn Ishaq said, and God knows best, that Iblis reached Adam and his wife through the power God gave him to test mankind, coming to a person asleep or awake and putting desire in the heart unseen, citing verses from surah al-A'raf and al-Nas; the editor adds that the report "Satan runs in the human being like blood" is very sound. Al-Tabari replies that Ibn Ishaq's uncertainty, even if firm, gives no ground to object to what is widely reported by the scholars and supported by the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 532} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 533}
On "and took them out of what they were in" he says it means from the ease of life in the Garden, and that the expulsion is attributed to Satan though God expelled them because Satan was its cause, as one says to someone who harmed him until he moved away, "none moved me but you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 534}
On "go down, some of you enemies to others" he says the verse shows that Adam, his wife and their enemy Iblis went down at one time. The interpreters agree that Adam and his wife are addressed and differ over the rest: from Abu Salih, Adam, Hawwa, Iblis and the serpent; from al-Suddi the same, with the serpent cursed and its legs cut off; from Mujahid, Adam, Iblis and the serpent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 534} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 535}

Al-Tabari continues the views on who is addressed by "go down": from Mujahid, Adam, Iblis and the serpent, and in another report Adam with his offspring and Iblis with his; from Abu al-Aliya, Iblis and Adam; from Ibn Abbas through al-Suddi, Adam, Hawwa, Iblis and the serpent; from Ibn Zayd, the two and their descendants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 535} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 536}
He asks what the enmity is, and answers that Iblis's enmity to Adam and his children is envy and arrogance towards prostrating, while the enmity of Adam and the believers among his children towards Iblis is the believers' enmity towards one who disbelieved in God and disobeyed Him, which is itself faith. For the enmity between mankind and the serpent he recalls the reports from Ibn Abbas and Wahb ibn Munabbih, and gives from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said "We have not been at peace with them since we fought them, and whoever leaves one of them from fear is not of us"; the editor calls the chain good and notes other sound chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 537}
Al-Tabari says he supposes the origin of this war is what the scholars he cited reported about the serpent letting Iblis into the Garden. He gives from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet was asked about killing snakes and said they and humans were created each an enemy to the other, so kill it where you find it; the editor records both the trust and the criticism of the narrator Jabir al-Ju'fi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 538}
On "and you will have on earth a place of settlement" he gives from Abu al-Aliya that this is the verse "who made the earth a bed", from al-Rabi' "a resting place", from al-Suddi and Ibn Abbas that it means the graves, and from Ibn Zayd their staying in it. Al-Tabari's own choice is that "mustaqarr" in Arabic is a place of settlement, so wherever they are on earth is their place of settlement, and the provision on earth stands in place of their provision in the Garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 538} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 539}

On "and provision for a time" al-Tabari reports from al-Suddi that it is a means to reach death, from Ibn Abbas that it is life, from Mujahid until the Day of Resurrection and the end of the world, and from al-Rabi' until a set term. His own choice is that "mata'" in Arabic is whatever one enjoys, and since there is no indication that a part was meant, the verse is general: they have on earth dwellings in which they settle and enjoy it and what it brings forth during their lives, and afterwards it holds their bodies in graves, until God replaces it with another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 539} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 540} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 541}
