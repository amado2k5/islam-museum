## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "Then Satan caused them to slip from it" al-Tabari notes that most readers read "azallahuma" with a doubled lam, meaning he made them slip in their religion, which is why their leaving the Garden is attributed to Iblis, while others read "azalahuma", meaning he moved them away. He reports from Ibn Abbas that it means he led them astray. His own choice is "azallahuma", since God says in the next phrase that Iblis took them out, so removing and expelling cannot be one and the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 524} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 525}
He then asks how Iblis made them slip and says the scholars gave several accounts. He reports a long account from Wahb ibn Munabbih: Iblis entered the belly of the serpent, which then had four legs, took from the tree and brought it to Hawwa, who ate and then Adam, and their private parts appeared; God cursed the serpent, laid on Hawwa pregnancy and childbirth with hardship, and made the earth's fruit thorns. He reports a similar account from al-Suddi from Ibn Abbas and others: the keepers stopped Iblis entering the Garden, so he came to the serpent, which took him in its mouth; he spoke from there, asked "shall I show you the tree of immortality", swore by God that he was their adviser, and Hawwa ate first, then Adam. The editor lists manuscript and printed variants in the wording. Al-Tabari gives these as the reports of their narrators and adds no view of his own. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 525} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 526} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 527}

Al-Tabari continues with reports of how Iblis made them slip. From al-Rabi' he gives that Satan entered the Garden in the form of a beast with legs and, being cursed, lost them and became a serpent. From Abu al-Aliya he gives that Iblis came to Hawwa first and told them their Lord had forbidden the tree only so they would not become angels or immortals, and Hawwa ate and told Adam to eat. From Ibn Ishaq, on the authority of some people of knowledge, he gives that Adam, seeing the Garden's honour, said "if only there were immortality", and Satan seized on the word and approached him through immortality. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 528}
From Ibn Ishaq, as he was told, he gives that Satan began by wailing over them until they were grieved, saying they would die and lose their blessing, and then whispered to them. From Ibn Zayd he gives that Adam told his Lord he had been approached through Hawwa, and God decreed that she would menstruate each month and bear and give birth with hardship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 529}
He reports from Said ibn al-Musayyib, by a chain through Ibn Ishaq, that he used to swear that Adam did not eat from the tree knowingly, because Hawwa gave him wine until he was drunk and led him to it. From Ibn Abbas by way of Tawus he reports that Iblis offered himself to the beasts of the earth and all refused except the serpent, which took him between two fangs; it then walked on four legs, which God removed so that it crawls on its belly, and Ibn Abbas said to kill it wherever found. Ibn Ishaq adds that the People of the Torah say only that the serpent spoke to Adam, without Ibn Abbas's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 530}
He gives a report from Muhammad ibn Qays in which God asks Adam, Hawwa, the serpent and Iblis in turn who told them, and the curse falls on all, matched with the verse in surah al-A'raf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 530} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 531}
Al-Tabari then says these reports were handed down from those he names about how Iblis made them slip, and that the one nearest the truth in his view is what agrees with God's Book. The Book says Iblis whispered to them and swore he was their adviser, which is clear evidence that he addressed them himself, either visibly or hidden in something else, since swearing cannot be done by indirect means. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 531}
