## Summary
Verse 21 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 21 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## Verse 21 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, p. 283)

Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-21|3:21]]) says the sense of those who disbelieve in God's signs is that they deny God's proofs and marks and call them false, among the two peoples of the Books, the Torah and the Gospel. In 6776, Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr (Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq): then He gathered the two peoples of the Books together and mentioned what they innovated and invented. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 283}

Report 6776 from Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr (Ibn Hisham, from Ibn Ishaq) closes: then He gathered the two peoples of the Books, the Jews and Christians, and addressed them in the verses from this one ([[passage/quran-3-21|3:21]]) to the twenty-sixth ([[passage/quran-3-26|3:26]]). The editor says it belongs to the remaining reports that ended at 6774. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 284}

On the description of their killing of the prophets, al-Tabari says they killed God's messengers who were sent to them to forbid what they did of disobedience and the practices God had earlier warned them against in their books, such as Zakariyya and his son Yahya and their like among God's prophets. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on this expression, in al-Baqara. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 284}

He then records the difference among the readers over the verb for the killing of those who command justice. Most readers of Medina, the Hijaz, Basra, Kufa and the other cities read it as 'kill'; one later reader of Kufa read it with the sense of 'fight', interpreting a reading of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud and claiming that Abdullah's codex has another form. Al-Tabari says the right reading in his view is 'kill', by the agreement of the authoritative readers, and because the interpreters interpreted it so. The editor says he does not approve the wording in the manuscript and print and thinks the right one is 'by the agreement of the authoritative readers on reading it so', and refers to al-Farra (1: 202) for this reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 284}

On who those commanders of justice were. In 6777, Ma'qil ibn Abi Miskin (Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih): revelation used to come to the prophets of the Banu Israel and they reminded their people, no Book having come to them, and were killed; then men who followed them and believed them stood and reminded their people and were killed; these are the commanders of justice among people. In 6778, Qatada (al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, his father): these are the people of the Book; the followers of the prophets used to forbid them and remind them, and they killed them. In 6779, Ibn Jurayj (al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj): there were people of the Banu Israel who had not read the Book, revelation came to them and they reminded their people and were killed for it; they are the commanders of justice. The editor notes that 6777 stands so in al-Tabari and in al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 13), from which he added the words in brackets; that its sense is that revelation came to the prophets of the Banu Israel, as other versions in al-Baghawi (2: 117-118) and al-Qurtubi (4: 46) show; and that dropping the predicate of the first 'was' in 6779 is a fluent and exact turn of Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 285}

In 6780, Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah (Abu Ubayd al-Wusabi Muhammad ibn Hafs, Ibn Himyar, Abu al-Hasan the client of the Banu Asad, Makhul, Qabisa ibn Dhu'ayb al-Khuza'i): I said, Messenger of God, which people will have the severest punishment on the Day of Resurrection? He said: a man who killed a prophet, or a man who commanded evil and forbade good. Then the Messenger of God recited this verse until he reached the end of the next verse, on the punished, and said: Abu Ubayda, the Banu Israel killed forty-three prophets at the start of the day in one hour; then a hundred and twelve men of the devout of the Banu Israel stood, commanded those who had killed them to do good and forbade them evil, and were all killed at the end of that day; they are those God mentioned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 285-286}

In his notes on this report the editor says that the wording of the verse in the manuscript, the print and al-Durr differs from other sources, and he kept the wording of Ibn Abi Hatim's version in Ibn Kathir (2: 118), which is the recited text; that Abu Ubayd al-Wusabi is Muhammad ibn Hafs al-Himsi, whose biography was given earlier, and that his brother al-Sayyid Ahmad had judged the words 'he said, he narrated to us' in the chain an error, rightly, so that it is better dropped; that the print and manuscript give a wrong name here, corrected from Ibn Kathir; that Ibn Himyar is Muhammad ibn Himyar ibn Unays al-Quda'i, who narrated from Ibrahim ibn Abi Abla, Muhammad ibn Ziyad al-Alhani, Mu'awiya ibn Sallam and others, of whom Ahmad said 'I know only good', Ibn Ma'in 'trustworthy' and Ibn Abi Hatim 'his hadith is written but not argued from', and who is in al-Tahdhib; that Ibn Abi Hatim in al-Jarh wa-l-ta'dil mentions this Abu Ubayd al-Wusabi, saying he met him and sought to hear from him, but some people of Homs told him he was not truthful and had not met Muhammad ibn Himyar, so he left him; that as for Abu al-Hasan the client of the Banu Asad, al-Hafiz in Lisan al-Mizan (6: 364) calls him unknown, Abu Kurayb having narrated from him, though the editor says that Abu Kurayb was not alone, since Muhammad ibn Himyar also narrated from him, and Abu Hatim lists him among those whose name is not known; and that al-Baghawi gave it through Muhammad ibn Amr ibn Hannan al-Kalbi from Muhammad ibn Himyar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 286}

Al-Tabari says that the sense of the verse is therefore: those who deny God's proofs, kill the prophets unjustly and kill those who command them to justice in God's command and prohibition, who forbade them to kill God's prophets and to commit His disobediences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 286}
