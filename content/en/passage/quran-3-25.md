## Verse 25 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 294-295)

Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-25|3:25]]) says the opening question means: what will be the state of these people who said what they said and did what they did, turning from God's Book, being deceived about their Lord and fabricating lies? It is a severe warning and a heavy threat. How great, he says, is the punishment and exemplary treatment they will meet when God gathers them for a day on which every worker is repaid for his deed according to his due, without wrong; none is punished except for what he committed or taken to account except for what he did, the doer of good is repaid with good and the evildoer with evil, and on that day none of his creatures fears injustice or diminution. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 294}

Al-Tabari raises a linguistic question: why the preposition lam before the day and not fi? He answers that the sense of lam differs from that of fi. With fi the meaning would be: how will it be when we gather them in the Day of Resurrection, what torment will they have? With lam the meaning is: when we gather them for what will happen on that day and for God's decisive judgement between his creatures, what punishment awaits them then? With lam an intended verb and a required predicate are understood though not stated, the lam itself pointing to it, which is not so with fi; hence lam was chosen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 294-295}

He then says the denial of doubt means there is no doubt about the coming of that day, adding that he has shown this with sufficient proofs earlier. The paying in full of every soul's earning means God pays it what it did of good and evil, and the denial of wrong means he does not reduce the reward of the good-doer nor punish the wrongdoer beyond his crime. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 295}

In his notes the editor says al-Tabari's sentence in the manuscript and print was devoid of sense; he judged that the copyist joined the letter of one verb to the next word and misread it, so he restored the sentence in the order the sense requires, noting that al-Tabari habitually delays such a word. He quotes al-Farra' (Ma'ani al-Qur'an 1: 202-203): in 'they were gathered for Thursday' the lam seems to belong to an implied verb, whereas with 'on Thursday' no verb is implied; and the gathering here means reckoning and requital. He refers to the earlier glosses of doubt and earning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 294-295}
