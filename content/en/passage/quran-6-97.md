## Summary
Verse 97 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 97 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "it is he who made the stars for you that you be guided by them in the darknesses of land and sea; we have detailed the signs for a people who know" al-Tabari says: God who made for you, people, the stars as guides on land and sea when you lose the road or are bewildered and not guided in them at night, by which you find the way and are guided to the road and the path, follow it and are delivered by them from those darknesses, as God said, [[passage/quran-16-16|16:16]], "and signs, and by the star they are guided", that is, from straying the road on land and sea. By the darknesses he meant the darkness of night, the darkness of mistake and error, and the darkness of earth or water. And "we have detailed the signs for a people who know" says: we have distinguished the proofs and separated the arguments among you and made them plain, people, that those of you with knowledge of God may reflect on them and those of understanding grasp them, and turn back from the ignorance they persist in and be held back from the error of their deed they stand firm in, and not carry on in obstinacy against God, knowing that what they persist in is error, in their going astray. A group of interpreters said the like of this, and 13612, from Ibn Abbas: a man strays from the road while he is in the darkness and bewilderment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 562}

The editor refers for "detailing" to an earlier passage, and says the printed text has "and not carry on in obstinacy of God", adding "in", which spoiled the speech utterly, the construction being "and not carry on in obstinacy against God... in their going astray"; and that he set off the parenthetical clause with dashes.

Verse 97 closes here on p. 562, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-98|6:98]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
