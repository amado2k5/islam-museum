**Commentary on verse 22: the people of Moses say there are giants in it (pp. 171-173)**

On 'they said: Moses, there is a people of giants in it', al-Tabari says this reports the answer of Moses' people when he commanded them to enter the Holy Land: they refused and excused themselves by saying that in it were mighty people they had no strength to fight, and they called them giants because, as reported, their strength and great build had overcome the other peoples. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 171}

The root sense of 'jabbar' is one who mends his own affair and that of others, then it came to be used of anyone who draws benefit to himself rightly or wrongly, until one who overreaches what is not his, oppressing people, overpowering them and rebelling against his Lord, is called a jabbar; the form is from the saying that someone mended a fracture, and he cites a rajaz line of al-Ajjaj. 'The Mighty' is also one of God's names, as He mends His servants' affairs and overpowers them by His power. In a note the editor explains the line and the meaning of its word for defect, noting it is from a long poem praising Umar ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Ma'mar al-Taymi; the museum describes it and does not reproduce it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 172}

Report 11656, from al-Suddi, in a story about Moses and the Children of Israel, says he commanded them to go to Jericho, the land of Bayt al-Maqdis, and when they drew near he sent twelve chiefs from the tribes to bring news of the giants. A giant named Aj met them, took the twelve and put them in the fold of his garment with a bundle of firewood on his head, and went to his wife, saying: look at these people who say they want to fight us. He put them down before her and asked whether he should crush them under his foot; she said: no, let them go so they tell their people what they saw, and he did so. The editor notes this report came earlier at length as 11572 and is in al-Tabari's History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 173}

Report 11657, from Ibn Abbas, says Moses was ordered to enter the city of the giants and camped near it, Jericho, and sent twelve scouts, one from each tribe. They entered and saw something great in their forms and bodies, and went into a garden belonging to one of them; its owner came to pick fruit and followed their traces, and every time he found one he put him in his sleeve with the fruit and took them to the king, who spilled them out before him. The king said: you have seen our state, go and tell your master; they returned to Moses and told him what they saw. Report 11658, from Qatada, says it was told that they had bodies and build no others had. Report 11659, from al-Rabi', says Moses told his people he would send men to bring news, took a man from each tribe, twelve chiefs, and said: go to them, tell me their story and do not fear, God is with you as long as you keep up prayer, give alms, believe in His messengers, support them and lend God a good loan; the men went until they came upon the people and saw giants of wondrous, enormous bodies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 173}

The rest of report 11659, from al-Rabi', says one of the giants caught sight of the chiefs as they tried to hide on seeing the wonder, took some of them to his chief and set them before him; they marvelled and laughed, and one said these people claimed they meant to fight you, and that but for God's protection they would have been killed; then they returned to Moses and told him the wonder.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 174}

Report 11660, from Mujahid on "twelve chiefs", says Moses sent a man from each tribe, and they found two of them could fit in one giant's sleeve, which he would toss out, that five men carried one cluster of their grapes on a pole, and that five or four could fit inside half a pomegranate once its seeds were removed. Report 11661, from Mujahid, is similar. Report 11662, from al-Dahhak, takes "a people of giants" to mean base people without character. The editor notes that 11660 occurs earlier as 11574 and 11573 and gives biographical notes on two narrators in 11662.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 174}

**Commentary on verse 22, continued: "we shall never enter it until they leave it", p. 175**

Al-Tabari says this is God reporting the reply of Moses' people to his order to enter the Holy Land: they would not enter until the giants there left, out of cowardice and dread of fighting; if they left, the people would enter, otherwise they had no strength to enter while the giants were in it, having neither power nor hand against them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 175}

Report 11663, from Ibn Ishaq, says Caleb son of Jephunneh quietened the people before Moses and said they would rise and inherit the land and had strength against them; those with him said they could not reach that people, who were bolder than they. The scouts then told the Children of Israel that the land they crossed devoured its inhabitants, its men were huge, they saw the giants, sons of giants, and in their eyes they were like locusts. The assembly trembled and wept loudly all night, murmured against Moses and Aaron, wished they had died in Egypt or in this wilderness, feared that their wives and children would become spoil, and one man began telling another to appoint a head and go back to Egypt. On the sense of "murmured", the editor refers to his note on report 11697.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 175}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
