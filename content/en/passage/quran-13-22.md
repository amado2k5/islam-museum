**Commentary on verse 22 (pp. 421-422)**

On "those who were patient, seeking the face of their Lord, and established prayer and spent of what We provided for them, secretly and openly, and repel evil with good, for those is the final abode", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: those who were patient in fulfilling God's covenant, in leaving the breaking of the pledge and in joining kinship, "seeking the face of their Lord", meaning by "seeking the face of their Lord" seeking to magnify God and to hold Him above being disobeyed in His command or having something done that He disliked, "and established prayer", that is, performed the obligatory prayer with its limits at its times, "and spent of what We provided for them, secretly and openly", that is, paid from their wealth its obligatory zakat and spent from it in the ways God commanded them to spend, "secretly" in concealment and "openly" in the open. Report 20335, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "and established prayer" means the five prayers, "and spent of what We provided for them, secretly and openly" he says, the zakat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 421}

Report 20336, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "patience" is staying; he said patience in these two: patience for God in what He loves though it is heavy on souls and bodies, and patience from what He dislikes though desires strive toward it; whoever is so is among the patient; and he recited "peace be upon you for your patience; excellent is the final abode" [[passage/quran-13-24|13:24]]. On "and repel evil with good" he says: they repel the wrong done them by people by doing good to them. Report 20337, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: they repel evil with good, not repaying evil with evil but repelling it with good. On "for those is the final abode" he says, mighty be His praise: those whose description we have given are those who have "the final abode", that is, those for whom God has made the abode of the Gardens follow from their abode, which would have been in the Fire had they not been believers, so God gave this in exchange for that. It has also been said that the meaning is: those who have, following their obedience to their Lord in the world, the abode of the Gardens. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 422}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
