## Summary
Verse 178 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 178 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 178 (p. 421): the meaning of "imla'".** Al-Tabari says: let not those who disbelieved in God and His Messenger and what he brought from Him think that our granting them respite is good for their own selves (the note refers to the earlier comment on "to reckon", shortly before, p. 384). By "imla'" he means lengthening life and postponing the term; the same word is in [[passage/quran-19-46|19:46]], meaning a long while, and from it comes the prayer "may you live long and enjoy a beloved"; "mala" is time itself and "malawan" are night and day, and he cites a verse by Tamim ibn Muqbil addressing the abodes of the clan which night and day have worn away (the verse is not reproduced). The notes say that the printed text has "and you enjoyed a while" in the prayer, an error, and the manuscript "and you enjoyed longing", a corruption, the editor adopting "beloved"; that this is a saying used in prayer, like the wish to someone wearing a new garment, "may you wear out the new and enjoy a beloved", meaning live with it a span of your time and take pleasure in it; that the line is also attributed to Ibn Ahmar and to a Bedouin of Banu 'Uqayl; that its sources are Sibawayh (2: 322), Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 109), the Amali (1: 233), al-Simt (p. 533), the Khizana (3: 275) and the Lisan under "m-l-l", and that it comes again in the tafsir (13: 106 of the Bulaq edition); that the author of the Khizana sorts out the attribution of these verses, mentions the disputed poetry and lists Ibn Muqbil's following verses (partly garbled in the extracted text), which speak of night and day wearing on ceaselessly, of the abodes of the clan and of the shocks of fate; and that Abu 'Ubayd al-Bakri glosses "amalla 'alayha" as persisted and clung, Abu 'Ubayda as returned upon them until it wore them out, that is, time lengthened over them, while the editor thinks it comes from "malal", weariness, so that it means reaching the limit of tedium and weariness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 421}

**The close of the explanation and the readings (p. 422).** Al-Tabari ends his explanation of "malawan" as night and day. He then reports that the reciters differ over the first verb: a group read it with ya' and fatha of the hamza of "anna", in the sense he has described, while others read it with ta', also with fatha, meaning "do not think, Muhammad, that those who disbelieved are better off for our respite". He raises an objection: why is the hamza opened in the ta' reading, when the verb has already acted on "those who disbelieved" and so cannot act on "anna", which can be governed only by an agent taking two accusatives? He answers that the correct Arabic and the familiar form of speech is to give the hamza kasra if the verb is read with ta', but supposes that whoever read ta' with fatha meant to repeat the verb over "anna", as though the sense were "do not think, Muhammad, of those who disbelieved, do not think that our respite is good for them", as in [[passage/quran-47-18|47:18]], which is taken as "are they awaiting anything but the Hour, are they awaiting anything but that it come upon them suddenly"; though permissible in Arabic, the usual form of Arab speech is what he described. The note refers to al-Farra' (1: 248) and Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 108-109). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 422}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 423).** The correct reading in his view is the one with ya' and fatha of "anna", so that the thinking belongs to those who disbelieved and no others, and the verb then acts on "anna" as an accusative, since it is not otherwise occupied and needs two objects. He chose it because the reciters agree on fatha for the first hamza, which indicates that the sound reading of the verb is with ya'. The second "inna" takes kasra as a new clause, by the reciters' agreement. The second sentence means to him that their terms are put off and lengthened so that they acquire sins and their wrongdoing grows, and that in the Hereafter they will have a humbling, abasing punishment (the note refers to the earlier explanation of the word for "humiliating"). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 423}

**Report 8267 (pp. 423-424).** 'Abdallah (8267), through Muhammad ibn Bashshar from 'Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from al-A'mash from Khaythama from al-Aswad: there is no soul, righteous or wicked, for which death is not better; and he recited the verse of this passage, [[passage/quran-3-178|3:178]], and [[passage/quran-3-198|3:198]]. The note says 'Abd al-Rahman is Ibn Mahdi, Sufyan is al-Thawri, Khaythama is Ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Sabra al-Ju'fi, a trustworthy Successor whom the authors of the six books used, and al-Aswad is Ibn Yazid al-Nakha'i; the editor holds that, though the report is stopped at the Companion in wording, it is raised to the Prophet in ruling since it concerns what cannot be known by opinion; it comes again as 8374 through 'Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri by this chain; al-Hakim relates it in the Mustadrak (2: 298) through Jarir from al-A'mash, calling it sound on the conditions of the two shaykhs, who did not extract it, and al-Dhahabi agreed; Ibn Kathir (2: 328) cites it through Ibn Abi Hatim from Abu Mu'awiya from al-A'mash to like effect and adds that 'Abd al-Razzaq related it from al-Thawri; al-Suyuti (2: 104) mentions it and adds attribution to Ibn Abi Shayba, 'Abd ibn Humayd, Abu Bakr al-Marwazi in the Janaiz, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Tabarani; and a similar sense comes from Abu al-Darda' as 8375. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 423} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 424}
