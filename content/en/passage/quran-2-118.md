## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and those who do not know said: why does God not speak to us, or a sign come to us" the interpreters differed over whom God means. Some said the Christians, from Mujahid. Others said the Jews of the Messenger's time, from Ibn Abbas (in Ibn Ishaq's report: Rafi' ibn Huraymila said to the Messenger of God, if you are a messenger from God as you say, tell God to speak to us so that we may hear His speech, and the verse came down). Others said the Arab polytheists, from Qatada and al-Rabi' (they are the unbelievers of the Arabs) and al-Suddi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 550} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 551}

Al-Tabari holds that those meant by "those who do not know" are the Christians and no one else, because it comes in the course of God's report on them and their invention against Him and their claim that He has a son, so they wished falsehoods upon God and said, in their ignorance of God and their standing with Him, "why does God not speak to us", as He speaks to His prophets, "or a sign come to us"; and it is not for God to speak except to His friends, nor to give a miraculous sign for anyone's claim except to one who is right in his claim and calls to God's oneness, while for one false in his claim it is not fitting that God speak to him or give him a sign that would support his falsehood. He rejects the view that they are the Arabs as a statement for which there is no sound report and no proof in the plain sense of the Book. "Lawla" means "why not", as in a verse of Jarir (the editor comments in a note on its attribution and explains its words); from Qatada, "so why does God not speak to us"; and "aya" is a sign. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 551} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 552} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 553}

On "likewise said those before them the like of their saying" the interpreters differed over whom God means. Some said the Jews, from Mujahid; others said the Jews and Christians, since "those who do not know" are the Arabs, from Qatada, al-Suddi and al-Rabi'. Al-Tabari says that those God meant by "those who do not know" are the Christians, and those who said the like are the Jews, who asked Moses to show them their Lord openly and let them hear their Lord's speech, and asked for signs they had no right to ask, in presumption against their Lord; so too the Christians wished upon their Lord that He let them hear His speech and show them the signs they wanted, and their saying resembles the Jews' for the likeness of their hearts in error and disbelief; though their ways differ, their hearts are alike, from Mujahid (the hearts of the Christians and Jews are alike). Another view, from Qatada and al-Rabi', is that "their hearts are alike" means the Arabs, Jews, Christians and others. Al-Tabari adds that "tashabahat" may not take the doubled form since the ta is the extra of the reciprocal form (the editor refers to al-Farra). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 553} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 554} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 555} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 556}

The verse means, in his reading: the Christians, ignorant of God and His greatness, said, why does God our Lord not speak to us as He spoke to His prophets and messengers, or why does a sign not come to us by which we know the truth of what we hold, as we ask and want; and God said: as these ignorant Christians said and wished upon their Lord, so did those before them of the Jews, who asked their Lord to show Himself openly and give them a sign, and presumed against Him and His messengers and wished wishes; so the hearts of the Jews and Christians are alike in rebelling against God, in little knowledge of His greatness and boldness toward His prophets, as their sayings are alike. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 556}

On "We have made the signs clear for a people who are certain" al-Tabari says: We have made clear the signs for which God became angry with the Jews, made apes and swine of some of them, and prepared a humiliating punishment for them in the Hereafter; those for which He abased the Christians in this world and prepared disgrace and a painful punishment for them in the Hereafter; and those for which He makes dwellers of the gardens those who submitted their faces to God doing good, in this sura and others; so He made known the causes for which each party deserved what was done to it, and singled out the people who are certain because they are people of verification in affairs and seekers of the truth of things with certainty and soundness, God's report being one whose hearer is not excused for doubting it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 557}
