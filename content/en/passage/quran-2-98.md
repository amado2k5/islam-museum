## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "whoever is an enemy to God, His angels, His messengers, Gabriel and Michael, then God is an enemy to the disbelievers", al-Tabari says that whoever is hostile to Gabriel is hostile to Michael and to all His angels and messengers, because those God names are His friends and people of obedience, and whoever opposes a friend of God opposes God. He reports from a man of Quraysh that the Prophet asked the Jews, by their book, whether they found that Jesus announced a messenger named Ahmad, and they said they had found him in their book but disliked him because he made lawful property and shed blood (the editor weakens the chain as broken); and from Ibn Abi Layla that a Jew met 'Umar and said Gabriel was their enemy, and 'Umar answered with the words of the verse, which then came down on his tongue. Al-Tabari holds the verse was revealed as a rebuke for their disbelief in Muhammad and to say that Muhammad's enemy is among the disbelievers. To the question why Gabriel and Michael are named again though angels, he answers that when the Jews said Gabriel was their enemy and Michael their friend, God named them so none could say "we are not enemies of God, His angels and messengers", since "angels" is a general word that could be read as particular. On repeating the name "God" in "then God is an enemy" rather than a pronoun, he says it avoids confusion over whom the pronoun refers to, and he rejects those who call it an instance of stating a name that should be a pronoun, like the verse of Jarir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 394} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 395} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 396}
