## Summary
Verse 27 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 27 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of repentance, and the will of the followers of desires (p. 212).** Abu Jafar says the meaning is that God wills to bring them back to obedience to Him and turning to Him, forgiving what is past of their sins and passing over what they did in their pre-Islamic life, treating as lawful what was forbidden to them, such as marrying the wives of their fathers and sons and other disobedience; and that those who seek the pleasures of this world and the desires of their selves wish them to turn away from God's command, going astray by doing what He forbade and committing sins, the great swerving meaning wrongdoing and a severe turning aside. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 212}

**Who the followers of desires are: the view of the fornicators (p. 213, reports 9129-9133).** The people of interpretation differed over those God so described. Some said they are the fornicators. Muhammad b. Amr (9129, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it means fornication, and that they wish you to fornicate; al-Muthanna (9130, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that the sense is that you be like them, fornicating as they do; al-Qasim (9131, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Mujahid that it is fornication, that the people of Islam would fornicate as they do, and that it is of the pattern of a phrase in sura 68 ([[passage/quran-68-9|68:9]]); Abu Kurayb (9132, from Yahya b. Abi Za'ida from Waraqa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports the same from Mujahid. Others said they are the Jews and the Christians, and Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9133, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal from Asbat) reports this from al-Suddi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 213}

**The view of the Jews in particular, and of any follower of desire in his religion (p. 214, report 9134).** Others said they are the Jews in particular, and their wish for the Muslims was that they follow their desires in marrying paternal half-sisters, since the Jews treated it as lawful; the meaning is that they wish the Muslims to turn from the truth and treat it as lawful as they did. Al-Tabari names no transmitter for this view. Others said the meaning is anyone who follows a desire in his religion for what was not permitted to him. Yunus b. Abd al-A'la (9134, from Ibn Wahb, who heard Ibn Zayd) reports that they are the people of falsehood and of desires in their religion, who wish you to turn aside in your religion, following the order of their religion and leaving God's command and the command of your religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 214}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 214-215).** Abu Jafar holds that the sounder of these views is that the meaning is those who follow the desires of their selves among the people of falsehood and those who seek fornication and marriage to paternal sisters and the rest of what God forbade; they wish you to turn from the truth and from what God permitted you, going astray from obeying Him to disobeying Him and becoming like them. His reason is that God used a general description and did not restrict it to some blameworthy desires, so the best sense is what the apparent wording indicates, not a hidden sense with no witness in a text or in analogy; thus the Jews, the Christians, the fornicators and every follower of falsehood are included, because everyone who follows what God forbade follows his own desire, and so the soundness of his choice is established. The editor notes that the manuscript and printed text have the phrase for the great swerving before the words 'from the truth', that he took it for a slip of the copyist, whose tongue ran on to the end of the verse, and left it out, since if it were right the later occurrence at the end of the paragraph would have no place.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 215}
