**Commentary on verse 4 (pp. 554-557)**

On [[passage/quran-12-4|12:4]] al-Tabari says: God says to His Prophet Muhammad, though you, Muhammad, were among the heedless of the story of Joseph son of Jacob son of Isaac son of Abraham, 'when Joseph said to his father', Jacob, 'my father, I saw eleven stars', that is, I saw in my dream eleven stars. It was said that the prophets' dreams were revelation; reports 18778 and 18779, from Ibn Abbas: the prophets' dream was revelation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 554}

It is reported what the eleven stars he saw in his dream prostrating with the sun and moon were, in report 18780, from Jabir: a man of the Jews called Bustana came to the Prophet and said, Muhammad, tell me of the stars Joseph saw prostrating to him, what are their names? The Prophet was silent and did not answer, and Gabriel came down and told him their names; he sent for him and said, will you believe if I tell you their names? He said, yes; he gave him thirteen names, and the Jew said, by God, those are their names. The editor severely weakens this report, since its narrator al-Hakam b. Zuhayr is abandoned and it is mursal, quotes al-Juzajani and al-'Uqayli, notes that the sources differ on the stars' names, and restores a reading of one name from the manuscript, saying he could not fix the pointing of the rest. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 555}

As for 'the sun and the moon, I saw them prostrating to me', it means I saw the sun and moon in my dream prostrating. He said 'sajidin' though stars, sun and moon are normally referred to in the feminine or inanimate form and not with the masculine plural ending, since that ending marks the plural of male humans, jinn or angels; it is said that way because prostration is an act of those whose male names are pluralised that way, so the plural of their names was put in the form of the plural of those who do it, as in [[passage/quran-27-18|27:18]] 'ants, enter your dwellings'. He said 'I saw them' though he had said 'I saw eleven stars', repeating the verb in the speech of those who say 'I spoke to your brother, I spoke to him', as emphasis by repetition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 556}

It was said that the eleven stars were his brothers and the sun and moon his parents; report 18781, from Qatada: the stars are his eleven brothers and the sun and moon his parents; 18782, from al-Suddi: he saw his parents and brothers prostrating to him, and when asked on whose authority, he said, if it is true, Ibn Abbas explained it; 18783, from Qatada, 18784, from Ibn Jurayj, and 18786, from al-Dahhak: the stars are Joseph's brothers and the sun and moon his parents; 18787, from Ibn Zayd: his parents and brothers; his brothers, who were prophets, reproached him, saying, he was not content that his brothers prostrate to him until his parents did too, when it reached them. It is reported of Ibn Abbas that he said the stars were his brothers and the sun and moon his father and his aunt, by a route not praiseworthy, so al-Tabari disliked to mention it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 557}

We stop here at the foot of p. 557, in the middle of the commentary on verse 4; the next batch begins at p. 558. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 557}
