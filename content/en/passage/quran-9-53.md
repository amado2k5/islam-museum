**Commentary on verse 53 (pp. 293-294)**

Al-Tabari says God commands His Prophet to say to these hypocrites: spend your wealth as you wish on this journey and others, in whatever state you wish, willing or unwilling, for if you spend it God will not accept your spending while you are in doubt about your religion, ignorant of your prophet's prophethood and ill-informed about God's reward and punishment; "you were a transgressing people", that is, departing from faith in your Lord. The editor refers to earlier explanations of willingness, unwillingness and transgression. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 293}

Al-Tabari explains that "spend willingly or unwillingly" is in the form of a command but means a condition and its result; the Arabs do this wherever the conditional particle "if" would fit, as in the words "ask forgiveness for them or do not ask forgiveness for them", and he adduces a line of Kuthayyir Azza addressed to a woman, telling her to treat him ill or well, since she is neither blamed by him nor hated if she turns away. So the sense is: if you spend willingly or unwillingly, it will not be accepted from you. The editor notes that the printed text wrongly has "report" where the manuscript, correctly, has "result", and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 293}

It is said that this verse came down about al-Jadd ibn Qays, when the Prophet proposed that he go out with him to fight the Romans and he said "this is my wealth with which I help you". Report 16803, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: al-Jadd ibn Qays said "when I see women I cannot restrain myself until I am tempted, but I will help you with my wealth"; he said, about him came down "spend willingly or unwillingly, it will not be accepted from you", because of his words "I will help you with my wealth". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 294}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
