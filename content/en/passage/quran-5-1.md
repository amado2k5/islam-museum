**Commentary on sura al-Ma'ida, verse 1: fulfilling covenants (pp. 449-451)**

Al-Tabari says that 'O you who believe, fulfil' means: O you who acknowledge God's oneness, submit to Him in servanthood and affirm His Messenger Muhammad in his prophethood and in what he brought of the laws of God's religion: fulfil the covenants, that is, the pledges your Lord made and those you made, which you bound yourselves to as rights and bound yourselves to God as obligations, complete them by keeping them, and do not break them after confirming them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 449}

The commentators differ on which covenants are meant, though all agree that covenants means pledges. Some say they are the covenants that the people of the pre-Islamic age made with one another for support, aid and backing against whoever sought to wrong them, which is the meaning of the alliance they made among themselves. Others say they are the pledges. Report 10893, from Ibn Abbas through Mu'awiya ibn Salih and Ali ibn Abi Talha: 'fulfil the covenants' means the pledges. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 449}

Report 10894, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih and Shibl: the pledges; report 10896, from Mujahid through Sufyan: the same. Report 10897, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas: we sat with Mutarrif ibn al-Shikhkhir while a man was narrating; he said: they are the pledges. Report 10899, from al-Dahhak through Juwaybir: the pledges. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 450}

Report 10900, from al-Dahhak, the same sense; report 10901, from Qatada through Ma'mar: the pledges; report 10902, from al-Suddi: the pledges; report 10903, from al-Thawri: the pledges; report 10904, from Mujahid through Ibn Jurayj: the pledges. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 451}

Al-Tabari explains that 'covenants' is the plural of 'aqd'; the root sense of 'aqd' is joining one thing to another, as a rope is knotted to a rope when tied; and he cites a line of al-Hutay'a about people who, when they made a covenant for their neighbour, fastened the knot on themselves so that he would feel secure in their support. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 451}

Report 10905, from Qatada: the covenants are the pacts of the pre-Islamic age; it is reported that the Prophet said: fulfil the pact of the age of ignorance, but do not make a new pact in Islam; and that Furat ibn Hayyan al-Ijli asked the Prophet about the alliance of the age of ignorance, and he said: perhaps you ask about the alliance of Lahm and Taym of God? He said yes; he said Islam increases it only in firmness. Report 10906, from Qatada through Ma'mar: the pacts of ignorance, the alliance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 452}

Report 10907, from Ibn Abbas through Mu'awiya ibn Salih and Ali ibn Abi Talha: the covenants are what God made lawful and unlawful and prescribed and limited in the whole Qur'an; do not betray and do not break them; he strengthens it with the verse about those who break God's covenant. Report 10908, from Mujahid through Shibl: what God bound on His servants of what He made lawful and unlawful for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 453}

Others say the covenants are the contracts people make among themselves and what a person binds on himself. Report 10909, from Abd Allah ibn Ubayda: the covenants are five: the oath, marriage, the pledge, sale, and alliance; report 10910 gives the same from Musa ibn Ubayda; report 10911, from Ibn Zayd, the same list (pledge, oath, alliance, partnership, marriage); report 10912, from Aslam's family, the same five. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 453}

Others say this verse is God's command to the People of the Book to fulfil the covenant taken from them, to act on what has come to them. Report 10913, from Ibn Jurayj: the covenants God took from the People of the Book to act on what came to them. Report 10914, from Muhammad ibn Muslim al-Zuhri: he read the Prophet's letter to Amr ibn Hazm when he sent him to Najran, which said: this is a declaration from God and His Messenger, and it included this verse among its verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 454}

Al-Tabari prefers the view of Ibn Abbas: the sense is fulfil the covenants of God that He made binding on you and bound in what He made lawful and unlawful for you, since God follows that with the statement of what He made lawful and unlawful and what He prescribed; so the command covers every covenant He permitted, and nothing may be excepted without proof. He notes that the Arabs have two forms of the verb: I fulfilled his covenant and I kept faith with him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 455}

On 'the beast of cattle', al-Tabari sets out the views: some say it means all cattle: Report 10915, from al-Hasan: camels, cows and sheep; report 10916, from Qatada through Ma'mar: all cattle; report 10917, from al-Suddi: all cattle; report 10918, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas: all cattle; report 10920, from al-Dahhak: the cattle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 455}

Others say it means the fetuses of cattle found in their mothers' bellies when the mother is slaughtered: report 10921, from Ibn Umar: what is in their bellies; if it is born dead, may I eat it? He said yes; report 10922 is the same and adds that it is like the mother's lungs and liver; report 10923, from Ibn Abbas: the fetus is of the beast of cattle, so eat it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 456}

Reports 10924 to 10926, from Ibn Abbas through Qabus and his father: a cow was slaughtered and a fetus found in its belly; Ibn Abbas took the fetus by its tail and said: this is of the beast of cattle made lawful for you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 456}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that the sense is all cattle: their fetuses, young and grown, since the Arabs do not refuse to call all of it beast and beasts; God did not exclude any of it, so it stays general until a proof of restriction comes. He notes that in Arabic 'cattle' names camels, cows and sheep, and 'beast' covers the grown as the young, since the young of cattle remain young after they grow. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 457}

On 'except what is recited to you', some say it means carrion and what is named with it: Report 10927, from Mujahid: except carrion and what is named with it; report 10928, from Qatada: the carrion God forbade and mentioned before; report 10929, from Qatada through Ma'mar: carrion and what was not named God's name on; report 10930, from al-Suddi: carrion, blood and pig's flesh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 458}

Reports 10931 and 10932, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha: carrion and pig's flesh, and what was dedicated to other than God. Others say the exception is pig's flesh: report 10933, from Ibn Abbas; report 10934, from al-Dahhak: pig's flesh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 458}

We stop here at the foot of p. 458, in the middle of the commentary on verse 1 of sura al-Ma'ida, and will continue from p. 458. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 458}
