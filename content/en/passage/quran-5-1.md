**Commentary on sura al-Ma'ida, verse 1: fulfilling covenants (pp. 449-451)**

Al-Tabari says that 'O you who believe, fulfil' means: O you who acknowledge God's oneness, submit to Him in servanthood and affirm His Messenger Muhammad in his prophethood and in what he brought of the laws of God's religion: fulfil the covenants, that is, the pledges your Lord made and those you made, which you bound yourselves to as rights and bound yourselves to God as obligations, complete them by keeping them, and do not break them after confirming them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 449}

The commentators differ on which covenants are meant, though all agree that covenants means pledges. Some say they are the covenants that the people of the pre-Islamic age made with one another for support, aid and backing against whoever sought to wrong them, which is the meaning of the alliance they made among themselves. Others say they are the pledges. Report 10893, from Ibn Abbas through Mu'awiya ibn Salih and Ali ibn Abi Talha: 'fulfil the covenants' means the pledges. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 449}

Report 10894, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih and Shibl: the pledges; report 10896, from Mujahid through Sufyan: the same. Report 10897, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas: we sat with Mutarrif ibn al-Shikhkhir while a man was narrating; he said: they are the pledges. Report 10899, from al-Dahhak through Juwaybir: the pledges. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 450}

Report 10900, from al-Dahhak, the same sense; report 10901, from Qatada through Ma'mar: the pledges; report 10902, from al-Suddi: the pledges; report 10903, from al-Thawri: the pledges; report 10904, from Mujahid through Ibn Jurayj: the pledges. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 451}

Al-Tabari explains that 'covenants' is the plural of 'aqd'; the root sense of 'aqd' is joining one thing to another, as a rope is knotted to a rope when tied; and he cites a line of al-Hutay'a about people who, when they made a covenant for their neighbour, fastened the knot on themselves so that he would feel secure in their support. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 451}

We stop here at the foot of p. 451, in the middle of the commentary on verse 1 of sura al-Ma'ida, and will continue from p. 451. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 451}
