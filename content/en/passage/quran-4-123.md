## Summary
Verse 123 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 123 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 123, opening clause: whom it addresses (pp. 228-235, note 1 on p. 229, notes 1-2 on p. 230, notes 1-3 on p. 231, note 1 on p. 232, notes 1-4 on p. 233, note 1 on p. 234 and note 1 on p. 235).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over those meant by the opening clause, which turns on the wishes of the addressees and of the People of the Book. He gives three views, as transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 235}

The first is that the Muslims are meant (reports 10490 to 10499). Report 10490 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Mansur, from Abu al-Duha, from Masruq) says the Christians and the people of Islam boasted, each side saying it was better, and God revealed the clause. In 10491 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from al-A'mash, from Abu al-Duha, from Masruq) the People of the Book answered the clause by saying that they and the Muslims were equal, and the next verse ([[passage/quran-4-124|4:124]], on those who do righteous deeds while believing) came down. In 10492 (Abu al-Sa'ib and Ibn Waki', from Abu Mu'awiya, from al-A'mash, from Muslim, from Masruq) the Muslims and the People of the Book argued over which was better guided, and the Muslims prevailed with that verse. Report 10493 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says the People of the Book boasted that their prophet and book came first and that they were closer to God, while the Muslims answered that their prophet was the seal of the prophets and their book judges over the earlier books, and God revealed the clause and then the verse on the best of religions ([[passage/quran-4-125|4:125]]), giving the Muslims' argument the victory. In 10494 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) people of the Jews and Christians met and said they were better, with an older religion, book and prophet, being on the religion of Abraham, and that only a Jew would enter Paradise, the Christians saying likewise; the Muslims said their book and prophet came after theirs, that the earlier peoples had been commanded to follow them, and that only those on their religion would enter Paradise; God answered with the clause and then favoured the believers. Reports 10495 (a report "I was told", from al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd ibn Sulayman, from al-Dahhak) and 10496 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, through his father, uncle and grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) give a similar dispute of the people of the Torah, the Gospel and Islam, in which the Muslims say no religion but Islam stands, that their book abrogated every book and that they were commanded to believe in the others' books and act on their own; God judged between them with the clause and then chose between the religions by naming Abraham's friendship. Reports 10497 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ya'la ibn Ubayd and Abu Zuhayr, from Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, from Abu Salih) and 10498 (Ibn Waki', from Abu Usama, from Isma'il, from Abu Salih) have the people of the Torah, Gospel and (in 10498) Psalms, and the believers, sitting and boasting, and God revealing the clause and then singling out the believers. In 10499 (Yahya ibn Abi Talib, from Yazid, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) the Jews boast of Moses whom God addressed face to face, the Christians of Jesus as the last of the messengers who, they say, Moses would have followed, the Magians and unbelieving Arabs of the age of their religion, and the Muslims of Muhammad as master of the prophets and the Furqan as the last and trustworthy book; God decided between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 235}

The second view is that the idol-worshipping polytheists are meant (10500 to 10505). Reports 10500 and 10501 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa; and al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, both from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) say it was Quraysh, who held they would be neither raised nor punished. In 10502 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) the Arabs said this, while the Jews and Christians said only Jews or Christians would enter Paradise ([[passage/quran-2-111|2:111]]) or that the fire would touch them for only a few days ([[passage/quran-2-80|2:80]]), the narrator Abu Bishr being unsure which. Report 10503 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Mujahid) names Quraysh and Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf. Report 10504 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says that Huyayy ibn Akhtab went to the polytheists, who asked him, as one of the people of books, whether they or Muhammad and his companions were better; he said that they and he were better than Muhammad. That is the matter of [[passage/quran-4-51|4:51]] and [[passage/quran-4-52|4:52]]; then God addressed the polytheists with the clause, and then named the Messenger and his companions among the believers who will enter Paradise, and Ibn Zayd adds that God promised the believers that He would expiate their evil deeds, citing [[passage/quran-29-7|29:7]], and made no such promise to the others. Report 10505 (Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam, from Anbasa, from Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza, from Mujahid) again says Quraysh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 235}

The third view is that the People of the Book alone are meant. Report 10506 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from al-Dahhak) says it came down about the People of the Book when they opposed the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 235}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: the more correct of the two views he weighs is Mujahid's, that the polytheists of Quraysh are meant. His reason is that no wishes of the Muslims have been mentioned before the clause, whereas the wishes of the devil's determined portion were mentioned, in the verses on arousing desires ([[passage/quran-4-119|4:119]]) and on promising and arousing desires ([[passage/quran-4-120|4:120]]); so joining the clause to what has already been mentioned is better than claiming a meaning for which there is no sign in the outward text, no report from the Messenger and no agreement of the interpreters. The sense is then that the matter is not by the wishes of the devil's allies and party, the rescue and victory their enemy of God leads them to hope for, nor by the wishes of the People of the Book who, deluded about God and His forbearance, said that the fire would touch them only for a few days or that only Jews or Christians would enter Paradise; God requites every doer, whoever among them or others does evil is requited and finds no one but God to protect or help him, and whoever does righteous deeds, man or woman, believing, enters Paradise. A further indication, he says, is that God described the devil's promise and its outcome and then followed with His own true promise, and that in speaking of the devil's promise He had also mentioned his arousing of desires; so it fits that the description of the desires should follow in the same way as that of the promise. Thus the clause is God's report of the wishes of the devil's allies, where they lead with their evil deeds, and where the deeds of God's allies lead; and God joined the People of the Book to the polytheists because the wishes of both groups come from the devil's arousing of desires which he had promised. The editor's notes give the manuscript and print variants on these pages (the added word "and" before the noun for arousing desires, the correct name of the narrator in 10505, the addition of Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf in 10503 whose sense he could not find, the third "we are better" in 10498, and the chain in 10506, where the Sufyan meant is al-Thawri), identifies Abu Bishr in 10502 as Ibn Ulayya, Isma'il ibn Ibrahim al-Asadi, a leading and reliable hadith scholar, notes that 10504 appeared in shortened form at 9794 and that the manuscript reading "we are better" is right there, explains falaj (p. 229) as victory, tahakum (p. 230) as disputing, and the word for face to face (p. 231) as meeting directly and not from behind a veil, with a reference to earlier occurrences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 235}

**Commentary on verse 123, second clause: the senses of the word for evil and the reports on requital in this world (pp. 235-246, notes on pp. 236-246).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the clause about whoever does evil being requited for it, and he gives three views, as transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

The first is that the word covers every act of disobedience: whoever commits a minor or major sin, believer or disbeliever, is requited by God for it. Report 10507 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) has al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad ask Ubayy ibn Ka'b about the verse, and Ubayy answer that he had thought him more discerning: it is the stumble, the scratch and the like. In 10508 (Ibn Waki', from Ghundar, from Hisham al-Dastuwa'i, from Qatada, from al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad) al-Rabi' says that if they were requited for everything they did they would perish; Ubayy replies that no man is touched by a scratch or a stumble except for a sin, and what God pardons is more, down to a sting and a kick. In 10509 (al-Qasim ibn Bishr ibn Ma'ruf, from Sulayman ibn Harb, from Hammad ibn Zayd, from Hajjaj al-Sawwaf, from Ayyub, from Abu Qilaba, from Abu al-Muhallab) Abu al-Muhallab visits Aisha to ask about the verse, and she says it is what befalls them in this world. In 10510 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Khalid) Mujahid says the requital is in this world, and, asked how far the afflictions reach, says anything one dislikes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

The second view is that the clause is about the disbelievers. Reports 10511 and 10512 (Ibn Waki', from Yazid ibn Harun, from Hammad ibn Salama, from Humayd; and from Sahl, from Humayd) have al-Hasan say that it means the disbeliever, and he recites the verse on requiting only the ungrateful ([[passage/quran-34-17|34:17]]). Report 10513 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abu Hammam al-Ahwazi, from Yunus ibn Ubayd) has al-Hasan say that it means the disbelievers and not the people of prayer. In 10514 (al-Harith, from Abd al-Aziz, from Mubarak) al-Hasan says God does not requite His believing servant for his bad deeds in a way that ends in punishment, cites the verse on requiting the evildoers for what they did and the good-doers with the best ([[passage/quran-53-31|53:31]]), and says the believers had sins which God forgave and did not requite. Report 10515 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says God promised the believers to expiate their evil deeds and made no such promise to the polytheists. Report 10516 (Ibn Waki', from Abu Mu'awiya, from Asim, from al-Hasan) says the clause concerns those whom God wills to humble, while the one He wills to honour is of the people of Paradise ([[passage/quran-46-16|46:16]]). Report 10517 (Yahya ibn Abi Talib, from Yazid, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) names the Jews, Christians, Magians and unbelieving Arabs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

The third view is that the word means shirk. Report 10518 (al-Muthanna, from Abdallah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas) says whoever associates partners is requited, and that is the evil, unless he repents before his death and God accepts his repentance. Report 10519 (Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam, from Anbasa, from Ibn Abi Layla, from al-Minhal ibn Amr, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) says shirk. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: the most correct is the view he reported from Ubayy ibn Ka'b and Aisha, that everyone who does evil, small or large, believer or disbeliever, is requited, because the verse is general for every evildoer with no restriction or exception, so it stands as general, there being no sign of restriction in it and no report from the Messenger establishing one. He then takes up an objection: how does this fit the verse that promises to expiate the evil deeds of those who avoid the major sins ([[passage/quran-4-31|4:31]]), and how can anyone be requited for what has been promised expiation? He answers that the promise of expiation does not mean leaving requital; it means that God spares its people exposure on the day of return, as He exposes the polytheists and the hypocrites. If He requites them in this world with misfortunes in order to expiate their sins, so that they meet Him with no sin left that deserves requital, then He has kept what He promised them and fulfilled what He guaranteed in [[passage/quran-4-122|4:122]]. The reports from the Messenger, he says, are numerous and agree with this view, and he gives them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

Report 10520 (Abu Kurayb, Sufyan ibn Waki', Nasr ibn Ali and Abdallah ibn Abi Ziyad al-Qatawani, from Sufyan ibn Uyayna, from Ibn Muhaysin, from Muhammad ibn Qays ibn Makhrama, from Abu Hurayra) says that when this verse came down it weighed heavily on the Muslims, they complained to the Messenger of God, and he told them to keep close to what is right and aim at it, for everything that befalls a Muslim is an expiation, even a stumble or the prick of a thorn. In 10521 (Abdallah ibn Abi Ziyad and Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Ramadi, from Zayd ibn Hubab, from Abd al-Malik ibn al-Hasan al-Harithi, from Muhammad ibn Zayd ibn Qunfudh, from Aisha, from Abu Bakr) Abu Bakr asks whether they are held to account for all they do, and the Messenger answers that what befalls him is his expiation. In 10522 (Ibrahim ibn Sa'id al-Jawhari, from Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata, from Ziyad al-Jassas, from Ali ibn Zayd, from Mujahid, from Abdallah ibn Umar) Ibn Umar says he heard Abu Bakr report that the Prophet said the requital is in the world. Reports 10523 to 10528 are six chains for one report. Abu Bakr asks how things can be well after the verse, and the Prophet asks which verse; Abu Bakr states it and asks whether they are requited for everything they do; the Prophet says God forgive you, Abu Bakr, do you not fall ill, grieve, meet hardship, grow weary, and he adds in some chains the stumble that wounds, and says that this is what they are requited with. The chains are 10523 (Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam, from Isma'il, from Abu Bakr ibn Abi Zuhayr, from Abu Bakr), 10524 (Yunus, from Sufyan, from Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, probably through Abu Bakr al-Thaqafi), 10525 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym), 10526 (Muhammad ibn Ubayd al-Muharibi, from Abu Malik al-Janbi), 10527 (Ibn Waki', from his father) and 10528 (Ibn Waki', from Yahya ibn Sa'id). Report 10529 (Abu al-Sa'ib and Sufyan ibn Waki', from Abu Mu'awiya, from al-A'mash, from Muslim) has Abu Bakr say how severe the verse is, and the Messenger say that the misfortune in the world is the requital. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

Report 10530 (Ibn Waki', from Rawh ibn Ubada, from Abu Amir al-Khazzaz, from Ibn Abi Mulayka, from Aisha) has Aisha say she knows the most severe verse of the book, and when the Prophet asks which, she names this one. He says that the believer is requited in the world for his worst deed, listing illness, weariness and finally the stumble, and that no one is reckoned with on the Day of Resurrection without being punished. She asks whether God does not speak of an easy reckoning ([[passage/quran-84-8|84:8]]), and he answers that this is at the presentation, while whoever is closely examined in the reckoning is punished; he gestured with his hand on his finger as if tapping it. In 10531 (al-Qasim ibn Bishr ibn Ma'ruf, from Sulayman ibn Harb, from Hammad ibn Salama, from Ali ibn Zayd, from Umayya) Umayya asks Aisha about the verse on revealing or concealing what is in one's souls ([[passage/quran-2-284|2:284]]) and about this verse, and she says no one had asked her since she asked the Messenger; he said it is God's turning to the servant through the fever and age that come to him and the parcel he puts in his sleeve and loses, fearing for it, then finds, until the believer comes out of his sins as red gold comes out of the furnace. Report 10532 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Abu Amir al-Khazzaz, from Ibn Abi Mulayka, from Aisha) is a short form: it is what befalls the believing servant, even the stumble. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

The editor Shakir adds notes on these pages. He gives biographical notes on al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad (who narrated from Ubayy and Ka'b al-Ahbar, was a governor of Khurasan for Mu'awiya, and whose hearing from Ubayy is mentioned by al-Bukhari in his history; the name is wrongly "Ziyad ibn al-Rabi'" in the print, the manuscript and al-Suyuti's Durr), on al-Qasim ibn Bishr ibn Ma'ruf (a teacher of al-Tabari found in the Baghdad history, where he fears the same man is meant), on Sulayman ibn Harb (judge of Mecca) and Abu al-Muhallab, and says that 10509 is in al-Hakim's Mustadrak, where al-Dhahabi marks it as meeting the conditions of the two sheikhs. He defines the stumble, the scratch, the kick, the hardship and the weariness. On pp. 240 to 244 he traces the chains: that 10520 is in the Musnad of Ahmad, the narrator Ibn Muhaysin is Umar ibn Abd al-Rahman, and Muhammad ibn Qays ibn Makhrama is a reliable successor; that Muhammad ibn Zayd ibn Qunfudh in 10521 could not have seen Aisha, so the report from her is disconnected, and that only Ibn Jarir is credited with it by Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti; that Ziyad al-Jassas in 10522 is very weak and Ali ibn Zayd ibn Jud'an reliable but of poor memory, and Ahmad Shakir calls that chain weak; that 10523 to 10528 are one report, that Abu Bakr ibn Abi Zuhayr al-Thaqafi is a minor successor of unknown standing, that the chain is disconnected, and that al-Hakim called it sound and al-Dhahabi agreed, which he finds strange, with its places in Ahmad, al-Bayhaqi, al-Hakim, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti; that 10529 lacks Masruq, whom the version of Ibn Mardawayh has, and he fears the copyists dropped him; and that 10524 is missing from the manuscript. For 10530 he states that all its narrators are reliable (Rawh ibn Ubada, Abu Amir al-Khazzaz, whose name is Salih ibn Rustum al-Muzani, and Ibn Abi Mulayka), defines the verb for being closely questioned in the reckoning as drawing out a thorn hidden under the skin until nothing is left, and gives a long tracing of the report in al-Bukhari (Fath al-Bari), Muslim, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, who calls it good and sound, in Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti, and later in al-Tabari's own commentary on sura 84. On p. 246 he discusses the word for God's turning to the servant in 10531, preferring the manuscript's reading as a sound one (the return of God to the servant with forgiveness, the sense of reward), and the places where the parcel is put, in the sleeve, the lap-fold or the pocket, and records manuscript variants of the narrators' names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 246 after report 10532 in the commentary on the second clause of verse 123 of sura 4, and continue with report 10533, which begins at the foot of that page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 246}
