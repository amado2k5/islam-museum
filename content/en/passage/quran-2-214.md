## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "or did you think that you would enter Paradise while there has not yet come to you the like of those who passed before you? Hardship and adversity touched them and they were shaken until the Messenger and those who believed with him said, when will God's help come? Surely God's help is near": al-Tabari says "am" is a question opening a sentence with no preceding interrogative particle, because it follows speech connected with it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 287} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 288} The meaning is: or did you think, believers in God and His messengers, that you would enter Paradise without suffering what befell the followers of the prophets and messengers before you of hardships and trials, so that you be tried with "ba'sa'", severe need and poverty, and "darra'", illnesses and pains, and shaken as they were, meaning that fear and terror from their enemies seized them so hard that the people felt God's help slow and said, when will God help us? Then God told them His help was near and that He would raise them over their enemy, and He fulfilled what He promised. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 288} This verse, say the interpreters, was revealed on the day of the Trench, when the believers met the hardship of fear of the confederates, the pain of cold and the narrowness of living, and he cites the verses of al-Ahzab (33:9-11). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 288} From al-Suddi: this came down on the day of the Confederates when one of them said "God and His Messenger promised us nothing but delusion" (33:12) (4064); from Qatada: it came down on the day of the Confederates, when the Messenger of God and his companions suffered trial and siege, and were as God said, "hearts reached the throats" (4065). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 289}

the clause of [[passage/quran-2-214|2:214]]: most grammarians take it as "and has not come to you", saying "ma" is a filler; "the like of those who passed before you" means their likeness, "mathal" being likeness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 289} From Ibn Jurayj on "until the Messenger and those who believed said": it is their shaking and their being made aware of God (4067), with the editor noting report 4066 from al-Rabi' is incomplete and he could not find its remainder. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 290} There are two readings of "until the Messenger said", raised and subjunctive; those who raise it do so because the verb before it is not prolonged and what follows has already happened and finished, as in "I stood to so-and-so until I struck him", meaning I struck him; where what precedes is prolonged and what follows is not finished, the subjunctive is right, as in Imru' al-Qays's "I travelled with them until their mounts wearied". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 290} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 291} Al-Tabari chooses "they were shaken until the Messenger says" with the subjunctive, because the shaking here is fear of the enemy, not an earthquake, and so is prolonged, making the subjunctive more eloquent and sound; the editor notes that al-Farra' covered "hatta" fully and al-Tabari relied on him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 291}
