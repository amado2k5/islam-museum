## Summary
Verse 53 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 53 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the question (p. 472, reports 9796-9797, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is whether these people hold any portion of rule; that is, they hold none. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9796, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that if they had a portion of it they would not give Muhammad even a naqir; al-Qasim (9797, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj) reports from Ibn Jurayj that they have no portion, and had they one they would not give people a naqir, out of stinginess. In his notes the editor refers to earlier explanations of cursing, helper and portion; and says the print dropped a clause in report 9797 which he thinks a shaky sentence, so he gave it as the manuscript has it, in brackets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 472}

**The view that the naqir is the dot on the back of the date-stone (pp. 473-474, reports 9798-9805, notes 1-2).** The interpreters differed over the sense of naqir. Some said it is the dot on the back of the date-stone. Al-Muthanna (9798, from Abdullah, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha) reports this from Ibn Abbas; Sulayman b. Abd al-Jabbar (9799, from Muhammad b. al-Salt, from Abu Kudayna, from Qabus, from his father) from Ibn Abbas; Ja'far b. Muhammad (9800, from Ubaydallah, from Israel, from Khusayf, from Ikrima) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is the middle of the stone; Muhammad b. Sa'd (9801, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) from Ibn Abbas that it is the naqir of the stone, its middle; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9802, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi that it is the spot in the middle of the stone; Yunus (9803, from Ibn Wahb, from Talha b. Amr) from Ata b. Abi Rabah that it is the one on the stone's back; Yahya b. Abi Talib (9804, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) from al-Dahhak that it is the hollow on its back; Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9805, from Hushaym, from Husayn) from Abu Malik likewise. The note on report 9799 refers to the editor's comment on report 9745; the note on 9800 says he did not identify Ja'far b. Muhammad al-Kufi al-Marwazi, saw Abu Ja'far citing a Ja'far b. Muhammad in the History without "al-Marwazi", and did not identify Ubaydallah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 474}

**The view that it is the grain in the middle of the stone (p. 474, reports 9806-9810).** Others said it is the grain in the stone's middle. Muhammad b. Amr (9806, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports this from Mujahid; al-Muthanna (9807, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) likewise; Muhammad b. Bashshar (9808, from Yahya b. Sa'id, from Sufyan b. Sa'id, from Mansur) from Mujahid that it is in date-stones; al-Qasim (9809, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah b. Kathir) from Mujahid that it is the naqir of the stone in its middle; and in 9810 al-Husayn b. al-Faraj (from Abu Mu'awiya, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) reports the same from al-Dahhak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 474}

**The view that it is a flick of the fingertips (p. 475, report 9811, note 1).** Others said it is a man's flicking something with his fingertips. Ibn Waki' (9811, from his father, from Yazid b. Dirham Abu al-Ala) heard Abu al-Aliyah say that Ibn Abbas put the tip of his thumb on the back of his forefinger, lifted both and said: this is the naqir. The note says Yazid b. Dirham occurred in report 9747 in a like chain, that the print here also has "Zayd b. Dirham", and that the manuscript's writing is poor and unpointed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 475}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the naqir (p. 475).** Abu Ja'far says the soundest view is that God described this group of the People of the Book as stingy with a trifle of no weight even had they been kings with power over great things; so the best sense of naqir is the smallest of such hollows, the hollow on the back of the stone being among the smallest, and anything like it falls under it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 475}

**The grammar of the verb after idhan (p. 475, note 2).** Al-Tabari explains that the verb is in the indicative and not put in the subjunctive by idhan, although idhan by rule puts future verbs in the subjunctive when a sentence opens with it, because a particle precedes it; when a conjunction enters, it is taken sometimes as starting the clause and sometimes as shifting away from idhan to what follows, and here the particle is meant to shift, so the sense is: have they a portion, then they would give people no naqir in that case. The editor refers to al-Farra's full treatment of idhan in Ma'ani al-Qur'an (vol. 1, pp. 273-274). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 475}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 475}
