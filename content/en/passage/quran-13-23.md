**Commentary on verses 23-24 (pp. 423-427)**

On "gardens of Eden which they will enter, together with those who were righteous among their fathers, their spouses and their offspring; and the angels will enter upon them from every gate: peace be upon you for your patience; excellent is the final abode", al-Tabari says: "gardens of Eden" is a "tarjama", an explanatory restatement, of "the final abode", as one says "excellent the man, Abd Allah", where Abd Allah is the man of whom "excellent the man" is said. The meaning of the speech is: those have, following their obedience to their Lord, the abode that is the gardens of Eden. We have explained the meaning of "Eden", that it means a stay with no departure in it. On "together with those who were righteous among their fathers, their spouses and their offspring" he says, mighty be His praise: the gardens of Eden are entered by these whose description He gave, who are those who fulfil God's covenant, those who join what God commanded to be joined and fear their Lord, and those who were patient seeking the face of their Lord, established prayer and did the deeds He, mighty be His praise, mentioned in these three verses, "and those who were righteous among their fathers, their spouses", their women and families, "and their offspring"; their righteousness is their faith in God and their following His command and the command of His messenger. The editor notes that the "tarjama" of the Kufans is the "explanatory apposition" and, for the Basrans, the "substitution". Then report 20338 begins, from al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, through Shababa and Waraqa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 423}

Report 20338, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "those who were righteous among their fathers", he said, whoever believed in the world. Reports 20339 and 20340, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, and Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa: the same. Report 20341, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "those who were righteous among their fathers", he said, whoever of their fathers, spouses and offspring believed. On "and the angels will enter upon them from every gate: peace be upon you for your patience" he says, mighty be His praise: the angels enter upon these whose description He gave in these three verses, in the gardens of Eden, from every gate of it, saying to them "peace be upon you for your patience" in obedience to your Lord in the world, "excellent is the final abode". It is mentioned that the gardens of Eden have five thousand gates. Report 20342, from Abd Allah ibn Amr, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ali ibn Jarir, Hammad ibn Salama, Ya'la ibn Ata' and Nafi' ibn Asim: in Paradise there is a palace called "Adn", around which are towers and meadows, with five thousand gates, at each gate five thousand striped cloaks, and none enters it but a prophet, a truthful one or a martyr; the editor notes that the "hibra" is a kind of Yemeni striped cloth, that the chain is sound to Abd Allah ibn Amr but for the unknown status of this Ali ibn Jarir, and that it is a statement of the Companion which he found no one to have raised to the Prophet. Report 20343, from al-Dahhak, through Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra' and Juwaybir: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 424}

on "gardens of Eden", he said, the city of Paradise, in which are the messengers, the prophets, the martyrs and the imams of guidance, with the people around them according to the number of gardens around it. The word "saying" is omitted from "and the angels will enter upon them from every gate: peace be upon you", the speech sufficing to indicate it, as it is omitted from "if you could see the guilty hanging their heads before their Lord: our Lord, we have seen" [[passage/quran-32-12|32:12]]. Report 20344, from Abu Umama, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Baqiyya ibn al-Walid and Artat ibn al-Mundhir, who heard a man from the elders of al-Jund called Abu al-Hajjaj: I sat with Abu Umama and he said, the believer, when he enters Paradise, will be reclining on his couch, with two rows of servants by him and at the end of the rows a barred door; an angel comes asking leave to enter, and the farthest servant says to the one next to him, an angel is asking leave, and that one says to the next, an angel is asking leave, until it reaches the believer, who says, let him in; the one nearest the believer says, let him in, and the next says to the next, let him in, and so on until {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 425}

it reaches the farthest one, who is at the door; it is opened for him, he enters, greets and leaves. In a note the editor goes into this chain, saying that Baqiyya ibn al-Walid is reliable but his reports have objectionable matter, mostly from unknown people; that the shaykh of Artat is named by some narrators "Abu al-Hajjaj Yusuf al-Alhani" and by al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim "Abu al-Dahhak", a Follower whose condition is not stated; that Isma'il ibn Ayyash also narrated it from Artat; and that the chain is open to question. Report 20345, from Muhammad ibn Ibrahim, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibrahim ibn Muhammad and Suhayl ibn Abi Salih: the Prophet used to come to the graves of the martyrs at the head of each year and say, peace be upon you for your patience, excellent is the final abode, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 426}

and so did Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman. As for "peace be upon you for your patience", the interpreters said much as we said. Report 20346, from Abu Imran al-Jawni, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Razzaq and Ja'far ibn Sulayman: he recited this verse "peace be upon you for your patience" and said, for your religion. Report 20347, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "peace be upon you for your patience", he said, when they were patient in what God loves and put it first; and he recited "and rewarded them for their patience with a garden and silk" up to "and your effort has been appreciated" [[passage/quran-76-12|76:12-22]]; they were patient from what God disliked and forbade them, and patient with what was heavy on them yet loved by God, so He greeted them for that; and he recited "and the angels will enter upon them from every gate: peace be upon you for your patience; excellent is the final abode". As for "excellent is the final abode", its meaning, God willing, is as in report 20348, from Abu Imran al-Jawni, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Razzaq and Ja'far: "excellent is the final abode", he said, Paradise in place of the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 427}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
