## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 116 to 117 in a single passage. He explains His saying: "And do not say, 'This is lawful and this is forbidden,' so that you may invent lies against Allah. Indeed, those who invent lies against Allah will not succeed": reciters differed in their interpretation, and the majority of the reciters of Hijaz and Iraq read it as 'and do not say what your tongues describe is falsehood,' meaning 'and do not say what your tongues describe is falsehood,' meaning: 'And do not say what your tongues describe is lying,' so 'Ma' is the verbal noun. It was mentioned from Al-Hasan al-Basri that he recited: "And do not say what your tongues describe is lying," which is by diminutive of lying, meaning: do not say to the lies described by your tongues, "This is lawful and this is forbidden," making lying a translation of the "ma" in "lama," thus reducing it by lowering "ma." It has been narrated from some: "When your tongues describe lying," they raise "lying," making lying an adjective of tongues, and deriving a verb as a combination of lying and lying, such as "shakur" and "shukr." In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid, Al-Hasan, and Ibn Jurayj.
