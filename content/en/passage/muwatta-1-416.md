## Overview
The hadith of [[person/umm-hani-bint-abi-talib|Umm Hani bint Abi Talib]] on the forenoon prayer on the day of the [[event/conquest-of-mecca|Conquest of Mecca]], and on her granting protection to a man. [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] transmits it from [[person/abu-al-nadr-salim-ibn-abi-umayya|Abu al-Nadr]], from [[person/abu-murra-mawla-aqil|Abu Murra, freedman of Aqil]], from her, in the chapter "on the forenoon prayer" {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.217}.

## What the hadith reports
Umm Hani went to the Prophet in the year of the Conquest and found him washing, while [[person/fatima|Fatima]], his daughter, screened him with a cloth. She greeted him, and he said: "Who is this?" She said: "Umm Hani bint Abi Talib." He said: "Welcome, Umm Hani." When he had finished washing, he stood and prayed eight rak'as, wrapped in one garment, and then turned away. She said to him: "O Messenger of God, the son of my mother, [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], claims that he will kill a man I have given protection to, so-and-so ibn Hubayra." He said: "We have given protection to the one you have protected, Umm Hani." Umm Hani said: "That was at forenoon" {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.217}.

## Transmission, as the editor sets it out
The editor notes the transmitters from Malik: Abu Mus'ab al-Zuhri, and from him Ibn Hibban (1188); Ishaq ibn 'Isa in Ahmad (6/425); Isma'il ibn Abi Uways in al-Bukhari (357); Suwayd ibn Sa'id; al-Qa'nabi, in al-Bukhari (280, 6158) and al-Bayhaqi; Abdallah ibn Yusuf in al-Bukhari (3171) and in al-Adab al-Mufrad; Ibn al-Qasim; Ibn Mahdi in Ahmad and al-Nasa'i (1/26); Ubayd Allah ibn Abd al-Majid in al-Darimi; Muhammad ibn al-Hasan; and Yahya ibn Yahya al-Naysaburi in Muslim (1/182). See also al-Tamhid (21/186). {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.217}

## Its place in the museum
The hadith is a foundation among Sunnis for the forenoon prayer and for the validity of a woman granting protection. It adds, to the picture of the day of the [[place/mecca|Conquest of Mecca]], a scene from the Prophet's household. See [[practice/salah|Prayer]]. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.217}
