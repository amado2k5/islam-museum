## Summary
Verse 243 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 243 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On ([[passage/quran-2-243|2:243]]) al-Tabari explains the opening question as meaning, do you not know, Muhammad, a seeing of the heart and not the eye, since the Prophet did not live to see those whom God reports on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 266} He says the interpreters differed over the sense of their being thousands. Some took it as a number: from Ibn 'Abbas, that they were four thousand who went out fleeing the plague, saying they would come to a land with no death, whereupon God caused them to die, then a prophet passed by them, prayed to his Lord to bring them to life, and He did (5596, 5597). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 266} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 267} In the margin the editor notes that al-Hakim transmitted both reports and graded them sound by the standard of the two Sheikhs, and that al-Dhahabi remarked on a transmitter; these are their judgements, neither al-Tabari's nor the museum's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 267} The editor also refers to Ibn Kathir and al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 267}

The rest of the reports on ([[passage/quran-2-243|2:243]]), on why they left, how many they were, and their return to life. From Wahb b. Munabbih (5598): people of the Children of Israel were struck with hardship and wished for death, so God told Ezekiel (Hizqil) to go to a graveyard with four thousand; he called the bones to gather, then to be clothed in flesh, then the spirits to return to their bodies, and they stood and said the takbir once. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 267} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 268} In the margin the editor gives an addition from al-Durr al-Manthur, corrections to the manuscript and notes on the two transmitters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 268} From Ibn 'Abbas by another path (5599): they were a great number who left in flight from fighting in God's way; He caused them to die, then revived them and told them to fight their enemy, and the next verse ([[passage/quran-2-244|2:244]]) follows on this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 268}

From Ash'ath b. Aslam al-Basri (5600): 'Umar was praying with two Jews behind him; one said to the other, is it he? 'Umar asked, and they said they find in their book an iron horn given what was given to Ezekiel who revived the dead by God's leave; 'Umar said he found in God's book neither Ezekiel nor reviving the dead except 'Isa; they said, do you not find messengers whose story He did not tell you ([[passage/quran-4-164|4:164]]); he said yes; then they told him that plague fell on the Children of Israel and a group who left were caused to die, and God sent Ezekiel who revived them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 268} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 269} In the margin are the pointing of the manuscript's words, the report's sources in al-Tabari's History and al-Durr al-Manthur, and the difference over the transmitter's name; this is the editor's judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 268} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 269} From al-Hajjaj b. Artat, they were four thousand (5601). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 269}

From al-Suddi (5602, 5603): a village before Wasit called Dawardan was struck by plague and most of its people fled, those who stayed perished and the rest were spared; the next year some thirty-odd thousand fled to a wide valley, two angels called to them from below and above, and they died. A prophet called Ezekiel passed, reflected and marvelled at God's power, and it was revealed to him, do you want me to show you how I revive them; he called the bones to gather, then to be clothed in flesh, then the bodies to rise, and they rose. From Mujahid, they said praise and glorification then, and returned to their people with the mark of death on their faces, never wearing a garment but it turned to a shroud, until they died at their appointed terms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 270} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 271} The editor explains in the margin the village's name, the wording and the manuscript's variants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 270} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 271} From 'Ata' al-Khurasani, they were three thousand or more (5604); from Ibn 'Abbas, thirty-eight thousand in al-Tabari's text (the editor thinks the number in the source is faulty and prefers al-Durr al-Manthur), and the smell is still found in that tribe of the Jews (5605). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 271}

From Wahb b. Munabbih (5606): when God took Caleb son of Jephunneh after Joshua, Ezekiel son of Buzi, called "son of the old woman" because his mother, old and barren, asked God for a child and He gave her one, succeeded among the Children of Israel, and he prayed for the people God mentions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 271} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 272} From Muhammad b. Ishaq (5607): they left in flight from some pestilence, until they camped on open ground and all died; the people of the land fenced them off from the beasts; ages passed until they were rotted bones; Ezekiel passed, marvelled and felt pity, and called each bone to return to its owner, then flesh, sinew and skin, then prayed for life; a distress covered him until he fainted, then he woke and the people sat glorifying God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 272} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 273} The editor notes differences between the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 272} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 273}

Others said the sense is that they were joined together: from Ibn Zayd, plague struck a village and one group left while another stayed, the plague fell hard on those who stayed; the next year more left, in the third they all left. It was not division that sent them out as for war, their hearts were joined, they left in flight; where they sought life God caused them to die, then revived them; and a man passed the village as bones gleamed and stood looking, and God caused him to die for a hundred years (5608). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 273} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 274} The editor notes Ibn Sida's view that the plural is of a form meaning one who joins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 273}

Al-Tabari then gives those who said they left in flight from the plague: al-Hasan al-Basri, that they fled the plague, God caused them to die before their terms and revived them to their terms (5609, 5610). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 274} These reports come from Companions, Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.
