**Commentary on verse 98 (pp. 430-432)**

Al-Tabari says: among the Bedouin there is one who counts what he spends in striving against a polytheist, helping a Muslim or something God called His servants to as a "fine", meaning a debt laid on him, hoping for no reward for it and warding off no punishment by it; "and he waits for turns of fortune to befall you" means they wait for the turnings, when days and nights turn to something disliked, the coming of something loved and the overcoming of an enemy of yours; God, mighty is His mention, says "upon them be the evil turn", meaning God made the evil turn and the descent of the disliked fall on them, not on you, believers, nor with you; "and God is hearing" of the prayers of those who pray, "knowing" of their scheming and of the punishment from God that is coming down on them and the painful punishment to which they are going. The editor refers to earlier explanations of waiting, turns, hearing and knowing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 430}

What the interpreters said agrees with what we said. Report 17094, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: these are the hypocrites of the Bedouin who spend only to be seen, in order to avoid being raided, fought or attacked, and who regard their spending as a fine; do you not see that He says "and he waits for turns of fortune to befall you; upon them be the evil turn"? The readers differed in reading it. Most of the readers of Medina and Kufa read "the evil turn" with a fatha on the sin, as an attribute of the turn even though the turn is in construct with it, like their saying "a man of evil" and "a man of truth", as though, when it is vowelled with fatha, it is a verbal noun from "I harmed him, I harm him, harm and distress". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 431}

Some of the people of the Hijaz and some of the Basrans read "the turn of evil" with a damma on the sin, as though they made it a noun, as one says "upon him be the turn of affliction and torment". Whoever says "the turn of evil" with the damma does not say "this is a man of evil" with the damma, and the poet al-Farazdaq said in a line in which he likens himself to the wolf of evil that, when it saw blood on its companion one day, turned on the blood, that is, came toward it. The editor refers to al-Farra and to the Diwan of al-Farazdaq, the Tabaqat Fuhul al-Shu'ara' and al-Hayawan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 431}

Al-Tabari holds the correct reading to be with the fatha on the sin, meaning upon them the turn that will harm them with harm, as one says "a man of truthfulness" as an attribute. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 432}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
