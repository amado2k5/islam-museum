## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 83 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 83: "When they hear what was sent down to the Messenger, you see their eyes overflow with tears", pp. 506-508**

Al-Tabari says: when those who said "we are Christians", whom God described as nearest in affection to the believers, hear what was sent down to the Prophet of the Book recited, you see their eyes overflow with tears. Overflowing of the eye is its filling and then flowing, like the overflow of a river or vessel, and he cites a line of al-A'sha. "From what they recognised of the truth" means their tears overflow from knowing that what is recited to them of God's book, sent down to His messenger, is true.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 507}

Report 12325, from al-Suddi, says the Negus sent twelve men to the Prophet to question him and bring back word of him; he recited the Qur'an to them and they wept, seven monks and five priests or the reverse, and the verse came down about them. Reports 12326 to 12328, from Urwa and Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, say it came down about the Negus and his companions; the editor notes that one narrator in the chain was reliable but practised concealed omission. Report 12329, from al-Zuhri, says he kept hearing the scholars say that these verses and the verse in the surah of the criterion on the ignorant were revealed about the Negus and his companions.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 508}

**Commentary on verse 83, continued: "They say: our Lord, we believe, so write us with the witnesses", pp. 509-511**

Al-Tabari says "they say" would be a state if it were in the form of a noun; the sense is that they say: our Lord, we believed what You sent down to Your prophet of Your book, we acknowledge that it is from You and is the truth, with no doubt in it. On "write us with the witnesses", reports 12330 to 12334, from Ibn Abbas and Ibn Jurayj, say that the witnesses are Muhammad and his community, because they witnessed that he had conveyed the message and witnessed for the messengers that they had conveyed. The editor traces these chains to al-Hakim and al-Haythami and calls them sound.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 509}

Al-Tabari thinks the one who gave this interpretation had in mind the verse in the surah of the cow, "that you be witnesses over the people", so Ibn Abbas took the witnesses to be those of Muhammad's community; the sense would then be: write us with those who will witness for Your prophets on the day of resurrection that they conveyed Your messages to their peoples. He adds that it would also be right to say the witnesses are those who bear witness that what You sent down to Your messenger is true, since that closes the description of their belief in what they heard; then their request is that God count them among those whose witness to this is sound with Him and join them in reward to their ranks. "Write" here means make and establish us among them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 511}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
