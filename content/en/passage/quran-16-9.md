## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "And upon Allah is the intention of the way, and among it is a wrongdoer, and if He willed, He would surely guide you all." And it is incumbent upon Allah, O people, to show you the path of truth. Whoever is guided, it is for his own benefit; and whoever goes astray, he only goes astray against it. And 'sabil' is the path, and the intended meaning of the path is the straight one without any crookedness, as the commentator said: 'He deviated from the path of the intended one.' And His saying: 'and among it is unjust,' meaning He, the Exalted: 'And from the way is one that goes beyond straightness and is crooked.' The one who intends from the paths is Islam, and the unjust among them: Judaism, Christianity, and other disbelief sects, all of which are unjust to the straight path and its purpose, except for the Muslim Hanifism. It was said: And some of it is unjust, because the path is feminine and masculine, so it was feminized in this context. Some have said: It was said 'and from it' because the way is the same, even if its wording is the same, it means plural. In the Maqta', statements are transmitted from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, al-Dahhak, Ibn Zayd, and Ibn Jurayj.
