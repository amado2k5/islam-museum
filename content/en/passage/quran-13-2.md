**Commentary on verse 2 (pp. 322-327)**

On "God is He who raised the heavens without pillars you can see, then established Himself on the Throne, and subjected the sun and the moon, each running for a specified term; He arranges the matter; He details the signs, that you may be certain of the meeting with your Lord", al-Tabari says: God, Muhammad, is the One who raised the seven heavens without pillars you can see, making them a lofty roof over the earth. "'Amad" is the plural of "'amud", the pillars and what a building rests upon, as al-Nabigha said in a verse recalling that Solomon was told to subdue the jinn so that they build Tadmur with slabs and pillars. The editor says the verse is from his famous poem in which he apologised to al-Nu'man, that Tadmur is a city in Syria which Nashwan al-Himyari describes in Shams al-'Ulum as built of great rocks, and that "sifah" are thin, broad, hard stones. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 322}

The plural of "'amud" is "'amad", as "adim" is pluralised "adam"; if it were pluralised with a damma, "'umud", it would be allowed, as "rasul" is pluralised "rusul" and "shakur" "shukur". The interpreters differed over "who raised the heavens without pillars you can see". Some said the sense is: God who raised the heavens with pillars that you cannot see. Report 20051, from Ikrima, through Ahmad ibn Hisham, Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh and Imran ibn Hudayr: I said to Ibn Abbas, so-and-so says that it rests on pillars, meaning the sky; he said, read it "without pillars you can see", that is, you do not see them. Report 20052, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, the same, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn al-Sabbah, Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh and Imran ibn Hudayr. Report 20053, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Affan, Hammad, Humayd and al-Hasan ibn Muslim: with pillars you do not see. Report 20054, from Mujahid: it is that you do not see them. The editor gives notes on al-Hasan ibn Muslim ibn Yanaq al-Makki, who is reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 323}

Report 20055, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "without pillars", he says, pillars you do not see; the editor notes that the bracketed words are an addition that cannot be avoided and are not in the manuscript. Report 20056, from Mujahid, the same, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. Report 20057, from al-Hasan and Qatada, through Ishaq, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: "God who raised the heavens without pillars you can see"; Qatada said, Ibn Abbas said: with pillars, but you do not see them. Report 20058, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sharik, Simak and Ikrima: how do you know! perhaps with pillars you do not see. Whoever interprets it so is aiming at the Arab practice of moving the negation from the end of a sentence to its beginning, as in Ibn Harma's verse saying, "I do not see her ceasing to be unjust, bringing misfortune on me", meaning "I see her not ceasing", the negation moved from its place. The editor says the verse is one of nine in Sharh Shawahid al-Mughni, Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and Ibn al-Anbari's al-Addad, and that Ibn Harma, told that Quraysh do not pronounce the hamza, said he would compose a poem that pronounced it throughout in the Quraysh tongue; and as another said {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 324}

in a verse whose poet the editor does not know, saying: if time pleases you with a man's state, leave him and entrust his state to the nights; they will come upon whatever good he has, even if in what people do not see he is not remiss, meaning, even if in people's view he is not slack. The editor says it is in Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and Ibn al-Anbari's al-Addad and that "ali" means one who falls short. Others said it is raised without pillars: report 20059, from Iyas ibn Mu'awiya, through Muhammad ibn Khalaf al-Asqalani, Adam and Hammad ibn Salama: "who raised the heavens without pillars you can see", he said, the sky is domed over the earth like a dome. The editor gives notes on Iyas ibn Mu'awiya al-Muzani, Abu Wathila, the qadi of Basra, reliable, a jurist and chaste, known for intelligence and shrewdness. Report 20060, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "without pillars you can see", he said, He raised it without pillars. Al-Tabari says: the most correct of the sayings on this is to say as God said, "God is He who raised the heavens without pillars you can see", so they are raised without pillars as our Lord, mighty be His praise, said, and there is no report otherwise and no proof binding us to submit to any saying besides. The editor draws attention to al-Tabari's caution, understanding and scrupulousness in his commentary. As for "then He established Himself on the Throne", it means He rose above it; we have already explained the meaning of "istiwa'", the differences of those who differed about it and the sound view, with evidence, earlier, which makes repeating it here unnecessary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 325}

On "and subjected the sun and the moon" he says: He set the sun and the moon running in the sky and subjected them in it for the benefit of His creatures and made them subservient for their use, so that they know by their course in it the number of years and reckoning and distinguish by it night from day. On "each running for a specified term" he says, mighty be His praise: all of that runs in the sky "for a specified term", that is, a known time, which is until the end of the world and the rising of the Resurrection at which the sun is rolled up, the moon eclipsed and the stars scattered. This was dropped from the speech because the hearers among the speakers of the language in which the Qur'an came down understand its meaning, and because "kull" must be annexed to what it encompasses. The interpreters said the same: report 20061, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: "and subjected the sun and the moon, each running for a specified term", he said, the world; the editor notes that he means the passing away of the world. On "He arranges the matter" he says: God who raised the heavens without pillars you can see decides the affairs of this world and the Hereafter, all of them, and manages them all alone, with no partner, supporter or helper, glory be to Him. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "arsh", "tadbir", "ajal musamma" and "kull". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 326}

The interpreters said the same: reports 20062 to 20064, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih; Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "He arranges the matter", He decides it alone. On "He details the signs" he says: your Lord details for you the verses of His book and makes them clear to you as proof against you, O people, "that you may be certain of the meeting with your Lord", that is, that you be certain of meeting God and the return to Him, and so confirm His promise and threat, desist from worshipping gods and idols and devote your worship to Him when you are certain of that. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "tafsil al-ayat" and "iqan". Report 20065, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "that you may be certain of the meeting with your Lord", for God, blessed and exalted, sent down His book and sent His messengers only so that we believe His promise and be certain of meeting Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 327}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
