**Commentary on verse 131 (pp. 47-48)**

Al-Tabari says: when well-being, plenty, ease and abundant fruit came to Pharaoh's people and they saw what they loved in their world, they said "this is ours", that is, we deserve it; "but if a misfortune strikes them", meaning drought, dearth and affliction, "they attribute it to the ill omen of Moses and those with him", saying our shares of ease, plenty and well-being are gone since Moses came to us. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "good" and "evil". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 47}

Report 14983, from Mujahid through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the good is well-being and ease; "ours" means we have more right to it; the misfortune is affliction and punishment; "ill omen" is to take Moses as an evil sign. Report 14984, from Mujahid, is similar. Report 14985, from Ibn Zayd through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: they said, this has befallen us only through you, Moses, and those with you; we saw no evil and none touched us until we saw you; the good is what they like, and when something they dislike came they said it came only through the ill luck of these wrongdoers; Salih's people said "we augur ill of you and those with you", and God said "your omen is with God; rather you are a people being tried" ([[passage/quran-27-47|27:47]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 47}

On "Indeed their omen is with God, but most of them do not know" he says: what befalls Pharaoh's people and others of the shares of good and evil is with God, but most of them do not know that it is so, and out of their ignorance they took Moses and those with him as an ill omen. The editor notes that the printed text and manuscript add a word that belongs to another verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 48}

Report 14986, from Ibn Abbas through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "their omen is with God" means their misfortunes are with God. Report 14987, from Ibn Abbas through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the matter comes from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 48}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
