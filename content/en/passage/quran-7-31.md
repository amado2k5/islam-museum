## Summary
Verse 31 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/hums|the Hums]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 31 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Children of Adam, take your adornment at every place of prayer, and eat and drink, but do not be excessive; He does not love the excessive", God says to those idolaters of the Arabs who strip when circling His Sacred House and expose their private parts there, and who forbid themselves to eat what God had not forbidden them of His lawful provision, as a supposed act of piety toward their Lord: children of Adam, take your adornment of clothing and covering at every place of prayer, eat of the good things I provided and made lawful to you, drink of the lawful drinks, and forbid only what I forbade you in My Book or through my messenger Muhammad. The commentators said the like of what he said: he gives (14503) from Ibn Abbas that women used to circle the House naked, and in another place he said without clothes, except that a woman would put a rag on her private part, saying in a line whose sense is: today part of it shows or all of it, and what shows of it I do not make lawful; he said that this verse was then revealed, "take your adornment at every place of prayer". The note says Abu Ja'far gave the report by three routes from Shu'ba from Salama ibn Kuhayl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 389}

He gives (14504) from Ibn Abbas that they used to circle naked, the men by day and the women by night, and a woman would say the same line, and God said "take your adornment"; (14505) from him that "adornment" is clothes; and (14506) from him that a woman used to circle the House naked, with, as Wahb said, her chest exposed, and would say, who will lend me a tiwaf, which she would put over her private part, and say the line; and God sent down "Children of Adam, take your adornment at every place of prayer". The note says Muslim gave the report and al-Hakim called it sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing, that a "tiwaf" is a garment they made for circumambulation, and it quotes al-Nawawi that the people of the Jahiliyya circled naked, throwing their clothes on the ground and never taking them up again, until Islam came and God commanded the covering of nakedness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 390}

He gives (14507) from Ibn Abbas that they used to circle the House naked and God commanded them to wear their clothes and not strip; (14508) from him that men used to circle naked and God commanded adornment, and "adornment" is clothing, which covers the nakedness, and beyond that fine cloth and goods, so they were commanded to take their adornment at every place of prayer; (14509), (14510) and (14511) from Ata that they used to circle naked and were commanded to wear their clothes; and (14512) and (14513) from Ibrahim that some people used to circle the House naked and were forbidden it and commanded to wear clothes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 391}

He gives (14514) and (14515) from Mujahid that "your adornment" is what covers the nakedness, even a cloak; (14516) and (14517) from him that it concerns Quraysh, for their leaving clothes in the circumambulation; (14518) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that it is clothes; (14519) from Tawus that the wrap is of adornment, and the note says a "shamla" is a cloak less than a heavy one, so called because it envelops the body; (14520) from Tawus that it is clothes; and (14521) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that they used to circle the House naked, and a woman circled naked and said the line, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 392}

and (14522) from Qatada that there was a tribe of the people of Yemen, one of whom, when he came for hajj or umra, would say, it is not fitting that I circle in a garment I have defiled myself in, and would say, who will lend me a waistcloth? If he could get one, well; otherwise he circled naked, and God sent down about them this verse; (14523) from al-Suddi that the meaning is what covers the nakedness at every place of prayer; (14524) from al-Zuhri that the Arabs used to circle the House naked except the Hums, Quraysh and their allies; whoever came from others put off his clothes and circled in the clothes of one of the Hums, as it was not lawful for him to wear his own; if he found none of the Hums to lend him clothes, he threw down his clothes and circled naked; and if he circled in his own clothes, he threw them down when he finished and made them forbidden to himself, and so God said "take your adornment at every place of prayer"; and (14525) from Ibn Tawus, from his father, that the wrap is of adornment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 393}
