## Summary
Verse 75 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 75 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 75: the faithful and the faithless among the People of the Book.** Al-Tabari says this is God's report that among the People of the Book, who are the Jews of the Children of Israel, there are people of trust who discharge it and do not betray it, and among them the one who betrays his trust, perjures himself and treats as lawful what is not his. The editor suggests that the manuscript has a gap and that the sentence should read "who treats as lawful the property of the unlettered Arabs" or "of the believers", as the rest of the commentary on the verse shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 519}

**Why God reported it.** Al-Tabari puts a question: what is the point of God informing His prophet of this, when people have always been so, some discharging a trust and some betraying it? He answers that God meant, by informing the believers of their state as He set it out in these verses, to warn them against entrusting their property to them and to frighten them off being deceived by them, since many of them treat the property of believers as lawful. The sense is then: among the People of the Book is one who, if you, Muhammad, entrust him with a great deal of money, gives it back to you without betrayal, and one who, if you entrust him with a single dinar, betrays you in it and does not return it unless you press him with demand. The editor says an unpointed word in the manuscript was read well by the first publisher, and his reading is the right one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 519}

**The preposition.** Al-Tabari says the two prepositions "with" and "upon" alternate in this place, as one says "I passed him" in either form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 520}

**First view: persistent demand.** He says the interpreters differed on the meaning of standing over the debtor. Some said it means as long as you keep pressing him for payment. In report 7261 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) it is as long as you seek him and pursue him. In report 7262 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada) it is that you collect from him. In report 7263 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) it means persisting; report 7264 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 520}

**Second view: standing over his head.** Others said it means as long as you stand over his head. In report 7265 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) he acknowledges his trust while you stay at him, and when you leave and come back to ask for it, he denies you; so there is the one who pays and the one who denies. The editor says the printed text added a word which spoiled the sentence, and the right reading is from the manuscript; that the phrase on the two kinds explains the two named in the verse; and that denying his right means refusing him it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 520} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 521}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** He says the sounder of the two views is that of those who said the meaning is for as long as you hold to demand and collection, from the saying "so-and-so stood for my right against so-and-so until he extracted it for me", that is, worked to free it and strove to extract it. He reasons that God described them as treating the property of the unlettered as lawful and said that among them is one who pays what he owes only under hard collection and demand; that standing over the debtor's head does not by itself move him from his holding it lawful; but that, with his holding it lawful to go off with what he owes the owner, there can be a way to extract it by collecting, going to court and dispute; and that collecting is the owner's standing to extract his right from the debtor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 521}

**The second half of verse 75: the claim of no blame regarding the unlettered.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is that the Jew who treats betrayal as lawful and denies the rights of the Arab owed to him, and does not pay back what the Arab entrusted to him except while the Arab presses him, does so because he says there is no blame or sin on them in what they take of the Arabs' property, since the Arabs are not on the truth and are polytheists. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the word for the unlettered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 521} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 522}

**Reports 7266-7268.** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed, and some spoke like him. In report 7266 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) the Jews said there is no blame on them in what they took from the Arabs' property. In report 7267 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada) they meant no blame regarding the polytheists, that is, those not of the People of the Book. In report 7268 (Muhammad, from Ahmad, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) one of them is asked why he does not return his trust and says there is no blame on them in the Arabs' property, since God made it lawful for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 522}

**Reports 7269 and 7270 (Said ibn Jubayr).** Al-Tabari gives (Ibn Humayd, from Yaqub al-Qummi, from Jafar, from Said ibn Jubayr) that when this verse was sent down the Prophet said that those who said this had lied, and that every matter of the Jahiliyya lies under his feet except the trust, which is to be returned to the pious and the wicked alike. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 522} In report 7270 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Hisham ibn Ubaydallah, from Yaqub al-Qummi, from Jafar, from Said ibn Jubayr) when the Jews said this, meaning taking their property, the Prophet said much the same, except that he said "under these two feet of mine", except the trust, which is to be returned, and added nothing more. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 523}

**The editor on report 7269.** He says Yaqub is the son of Abdallah al-Ashari al-Qummi and Jafar the son of Abi al-Mughira al-Khuzai al-Qummi, both of whom came in report 617. He says his brother Ahmad said of a chain like this earlier that the report is raised but disconnected, because Said ibn Jubayr is a Follower, and that its chain to him is good. He traces it to Ibn Kathir's commentary (2: 169-170), from the commentary of Ibn Abi Hatim, and to al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 44), where it is also attributed to Abd ibn Humayd and Ibn al-Mundhir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 522}

**Reports 7271-7273.** In report 7271 (Muhammad ibn Sad, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) the People of the Book used to say there is no blame on them in what they took from these people, because they are unlettered. In report 7272 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) some Muslim men had dealt with the Jews in the Jahiliyya, and when they became Muslims and asked them for the price of their sales, the Jews said you have no trust from us and no payment due, since you abandoned your religion, and claimed they found this in their book; and God stated that they speak lies about God knowingly. The editor says the printed text dropped the word "he said" and he restored it from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 523} In report 7273 (Ibn Waki, from his father, from Sufyan, from Abu Ishaq, from Sasaa) he said to Ibn Abbas that they raid the People of the Book and take some of their fruit; Ibn Abbas replied: do you say what the People of the Book said, that there is no blame on them regarding the unlettered? The editor identifies Abu Ishaq as al-Hamdani and Sasaa as the son of Yazid, also said to be the son of Zayd, notes that al-Bukhari recorded the disagreement over his name and pointed to the transmission of this report in his al-Tarikh al-Kabir, and that Ibn Abi Hatim records him too. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 523}

**Report 7274 (Ibn Abbas).** Al-Tabari gives (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Abu Ishaq al-Hamdani, from Sasaa) that a man asked Ibn Abbas: in the raid we take, or in another word about which al-Hasan was unsure, a chicken or a sheep from the property of the protected non-Muslims. Ibn Abbas asked what they say about it. He said we say there is no harm on us in that. He replied: this is what the People of the Book said, that they had no blame in the property of the unlettered; when they pay the poll-tax their property is not lawful to you except with their own good will. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 524}

**The editor on report 7274.** He says it is another route for the previous report with different wording, and lists who relates it: Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam in Kitab al-Amwal (p. 149, no. 415) through Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Abu Ishaq from Sasaa in other words; al-Bayhaqi in the Sunan (9: 198) through Shuba from Abu Ishaq from Sasaa, in words different from all those given; Ibn Kathir (2: 169) from the commentary of Abd al-Razzaq, where it reads "from Abu Sasaa ibn Yazid", an error for Sasaa, and he adds that al-Thawri also related it from Abu Ishaq in similar form; al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 44), who ascribes it to Ibn Jarir, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim; and al-Zamakhshari in his commentary on the verse, with al-Tabari's text, al-Qurtubi (4: 118-119) and Abu Hayyan (2: 501) from Abd al-Razzaq as well. He says all have "we take in the raid" except al-Qurtubi, who has other words; al-Bayhaqi has "we come to a village in the Sawad", and the Amwal "we travel in the land of the protected people and take from them". He says the base of the manuscript and printed al-Tabari has a word he could find nowhere and which means nothing, that everyone who copied from Abd al-Razzaq's commentary by this chain agrees on the same wording, so he adopted it and put what al-Hasan ibn Yahya doubted between brackets, and he thinks it most likely "the expedition" or "the village". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 524}

**The close of verse 75: the claim that God made it lawful.** Al-Tabari says those of them who said there was no blame on them in the property of the unlettered Arabs, in betraying them in it and not repaying it, are saying by their words that God made that lawful to them; they are thus lying against God, committing the sin deliberately, and this is the meaning of their knowing it. The editor notes that the word for the lie is the object of the verb with an intervening phrase between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 525}

**Reports 7275 and 7276.** In report 7275 (Muhammad, from Ahmad, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) it is the one among them who, when asked why he does not return his trust, says there is no blame on them in the property of the Arabs, since God has made it lawful for them. In report 7276 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) it is their claim to have found in their book that they had no blame in the property of the unlettered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 525}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
