## Summary
Verse 102 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 102 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Piety and a sincere submission (pp. 64-65).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: you who have believed in God and His messenger, fear God and be mindful of Him by obeying Him and avoiding what He forbids as He is due to be feared, which is that He is obeyed and not disobeyed, thanked and not denied, remembered and not forgotten, and do not let death find you other than as submitters to your Lord, yielding to Him in obedience and sincere to Him in divinity and worship. He says the interpreters spoke along these lines. The editor refers to al-Tabari's earlier treatment of the word for fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 64} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 65}

**Reports from Ibn Mas'ud (7536-7543, pp. 65-66).** That due piety is that He is obeyed and not disobeyed, remembered and not forgotten, thanked and not denied is related from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud by many chains turning on Zubayd from Murra (al-Hamdani, that is Murra ibn Sharahil al-Bakili): from Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan and from al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri (7536); from Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Shu'ba (7537); from Ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba (7538); from Abu Kurayb and Abu al-Sa'ib, Ibn Idris, Layth (7539); from al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, Jarir (7540); from al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym, Mis'ar (7541); from al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym, al-Mas'udi, Zubayd al-Ayami (7542); from Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Mansur (7543). The editor notes that al-Hakim records it in the Mustadrak by way of Abu Nu'aym from Mis'ar (7541) without the clause on thanking, calling it sound by the conditions of the two shaykhs though they did not record it, and al-Dhahabi agreed; and that the manuscript and printed text differ on Murra's attribution, both being correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 65} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 66}

**Reports from the Successors (7544-7551, pp. 66-67).** The same is related from Amr ibn Maymun (7544 via Muhammad ibn Sinan, Yahya, al-Thawri, Abu Ishaq; 7545 via Ibn Waki', his father, Isra'il), from al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym (7546 via Ibn al-Muthanna, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Shu'ba, Amr ibn Murra; 7547 via al-Muthanna, Abu Dawud, Shu'ba), from Tawus (7548 via al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Qays ibn Sa'd), from al-Hasan (7549 via Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad), from al-Suddi (7550, via Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, in which the address is to the believing Ansar), and from Qatada (7551 via al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, Hammam): that He is obeyed and not disobeyed. In chain 7544 the editor corrects the name to Yahya ibn Abi Bukayr al-Aslami, says Sufyan is al-Thawri and Abu Ishaq is al-Sabi'i, and corrects the name al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 66} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 67}

**Second view: striving in God (7552, p. 67).** Others gave what al-Muthanna relates from Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya, Ali, Ibn Abbas, that due piety is to strive in God as He is due, with no fear of any blamer in God's cause, and to stand for God in justice even against themselves, their fathers and their sons. The editor says al-Nahhas relates it in al-Nasikh wa-l-Mansukh with some difference of wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 67}

**Is the verse abrogated? (pp. 67-68).** Al-Tabari notes the interpreters differed on whether this verse is abrogated. Some said it is firm and not abrogated, relating from Ibn Abbas by the same chain that it was not abrogated and that the sense is striving in God as He is due (7553), and from Tawus that if they do not do it or cannot, they should not die except as submitters (7554 via al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ibn Abi Najih; 7555 via al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 67} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 68}

Others said it is abrogated by [[passage/quran-64-16|64:16]], which enjoins piety according to capacity. Al-Tabari relates from Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7556) that God then sent down ease and facility and returned with His mercy to what He knows of the weakness of His creatures, so that verse came with relief, well-being and ease. Report 7557, from al-Muthanna via al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, opens at the foot of the page and is completed on the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 68}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 68}

**The rest of the abrogation reports (7557-7560, pp. 68-69).** Report 7557, from al-Muthanna via al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal al-Anmati, Hammam, Qatada, says this verse was abrogated by the verse in Surat al-Taghabun ([[passage/quran-64-16|64:16]]), on which the Prophet took the pledge of hearing and obeying as far as people were able. From al-Rabi ibn Anas (7558, via al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, his father): when this verse came down, the verse of capacity came after it and abrogated it. From al-Suddi (7559, via Muhammad, Ahmad, Asbat): people could not bear it, so God lifted it from them with that verse. From Ibn Zayd (7560, via Yunus, Ibn Wahb): a hard command came and they asked who could know its measure or reach it; when He knew it weighed on them He abrogated it for them and brought the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 68} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 69}

**The editor's note on the lack of a preference (p. 69).** The editor says al-Tabari left aside preferring one of the two views, though he ought to have made it plain, and quotes what Abu Ja'far al-Nahhas wrote in al-Nasikh wa-l-Mansukh (pp. 88-89) after relating Qatada's statement: it is impossible to call this abrogating and abrogated except by artifice, since abrogation is removing a thing and bringing its opposite, and that cannot be said of the command to be pious, especially given the hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal from the Prophet that God's right over His servants is that they worship Him and associate nothing with Him, in which no abrogation can occur. All that the verse mentions is therefore obligatory on Muslims, as Ibn Mas'ud said about obeying, remembering, thanking and striving. As for Qatada's statement, for all his standing in knowledge, it may mean that the second verse was sent down as like the first, since God charges no one beyond capacity. This is al-Nahhas as the editor quotes him, with no museum verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 69}

**Dying as a Muslim (7561, p. 70).** Al-Tabari gives the interpretation of the prohibition on dying except in submission as what al-Muthanna relates from Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Qays ibn Sa'd, Tawus: in Islam and in the sanctity of Islam. The editor refers to al-Tabari's comment on a parallel clause earlier in the work. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 70}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 70}
