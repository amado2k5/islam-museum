## Summary
Verse 130 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 130 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 130, "O company of jinn and mankind, did messengers not come to you from among you, relating My signs to you and warning you of the meeting of this day of yours?", al-Tabari says this is a report from God of what He will say on the Day of Resurrection to those idolaters of mankind and jinn who set others equal to Him: did there not come to you messengers from among you relating My signs, informing you of what was revealed to them of My drawing your attention to the places of My proofs, showing you My evidence for My oneness, the truth of My prophets, doing My command and keeping within My limits; "and warning you of the meeting of this day" means cautioning you about meeting My punishment on this day and My penalty for your disobedience, so that you would desist. This is reproach and rebuke from God to these unbelievers for the wickedness and disobedience they committed in the world; its sense is that messengers came to you who pointed out the error of what you persisted in, with conclusive proofs, and warned of God's threat for staying as you were, and you did not accept it, did not reflect and did not take warning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 121}

The commentators differed on whether messengers were sent to the jinn from among themselves. Some said messengers were sent to them as they were to mankind; he relates (13896) that al-Dahhak was asked whether there was a prophet among the jinn before the Prophet was sent, and said, have you not heard God's words "did messengers not come to you from among you", meaning messengers from mankind and messengers from the jinn? Others said no messenger was ever sent from among them, the messengers being from mankind alone, and from the jinn only warners; God said "messengers from among you" though the messengers are from one of the two groups, as He said "He let the two seas flow, meeting" and then "pearls and coral come out of both", whereas pearls and coral come only from the salt sea and not the fresh. The note says this is al-Farra's statement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 121}

The argument continues: the other side said that milk is drunk and not eaten, and likewise the messengers come from one of the two groups; he gives (13897) Ibn Jurayj, that God joined them as He joined them in "from each you eat fresh flesh and take out ornaments you wear" (35:12), though ornaments do not come from rivers; and Ibn Abbas, that they are the jinn who met their people, and they are messengers to their people. On Ibn Abbas's view some of the jinn are messengers of mankind's messengers to their people, and the verse means: did there not come to you, jinn and mankind, messengers from among you, the messengers of mankind being messengers from God to them, and the messengers of the jinn being messengers of God's messengers from the children of Adam, those who on hearing the Qur'an turned back to their people as warners (the editor refers to 46:29-32). Those who held al-Dahhak's view said God reported that there were messengers from the jinn sent to them as He reported messengers from mankind; had it been allowable to take His report of the jinn's messengers to mean they are mankind's messengers, it would be allowable to take His report of mankind's messengers to mean they are the jinn's, and the falsity of this shows both reports mean that they are God's messengers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 122}

Then "they said: we bear witness against ourselves; the life of this world deceived them, and they bore witness against themselves that they were unbelievers". Al-Tabari says this is God's report of what the idolaters of the jinn and mankind will say when He rebukes them with "did not messengers come to you from among you": they will say "we bear witness against ourselves" that Your messengers came to us with Your signs and warned us of the meeting of this day, and we denied them, rejected their message, did not follow Your signs and did not believe in them. God then says, as a fresh statement, that the life of this world deceived these who set idols and statues, and their allies among the jinn, equal to God, meaning its adornment, the seeking of leadership in it and rivalry over it, instead of submitting to God's command and obeying His messengers, so they grew proud. The mention of "the life of this world" suffices for the things that deceived them. "They bore witness against themselves" means that they were in the world unbelievers in Him and His messengers, so that God's proof is complete against them through their own admission of what makes them deserve His punishment. The editor notes a gap in the manuscript sentence, which he marked with dots. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 123}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
