**Commentary on verse 25: "my Lord, I control only myself and my brother", pp. 187-188**

Al-Tabari says this reports what Moses said when his people spoke as they did; angered by their words, he prayed: my Lord, I have power only over myself and my brother, that is, I cannot make anyone follow what I love and want of obedience to You and of following Your commands and prohibitions except myself and my brother; the idiom means "I have no power over anything but this". The editor refers to p. 105 for the sense of owning.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 187}

"Separate us from the wicked people" he explains as: divide us from them by a judgement of Yours on us and them, so that You keep them far from us; from the saying "I separated these two", and he cites a line of rajaz verse. The editor says the line is in Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, that he did not find it in this wording, thinks it is from the rajaz of Hubayna ibn Turayf al-'Akali, gives the story of his exchange with Layla al-Akhyaliyya and lists the sources. The interpreters spoke along the same lines.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 188}

Reports 11686 and 11687, from Ibn 'Abbas, give "separate us" the sense of judge between me and them. Report 11688, from al-Suddi, says Moses was angry when the people told him to go with his Lord and fight, and prayed against them, and it was a haste on Moses' part. Report 11689, from al-Dahhak, takes it as judge and open between us, and says God's judgement between him and them was to name them wicked. Al-Tabari means by the wicked those who left belief in God for disbelief in Him, and refers back to his earlier explanation that the root sense of wickedness is going out from one thing to another.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 189}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
