**Commentary on verse 81 (pp. 209-212)**

On "go back to your father and say, O our father, your son has stolen; we testify only to what we know, and we were not guardians of the unseen", reporting Rubil's words to his brothers when Joseph took his brother because of the cup that was brought out of his bag, al-Tabari says: go back, my brothers, to your father Ya'qub and say to him, O our father, your son Benjamin has stolen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 209}

The readers read this word with a fatha on the sin and ra' and no doubling, "saraqa"; from Ibn Abbas is reported "surriqa", with a damma on the sin and a doubled ra', in the passive, meaning he was charged with theft. On "we testify only to what we know" the interpreters differed. Some said it means: we only said he stole by the outward knowledge we had that it was so, since the king's cup was found in his bag and not in other bags. Report 19632, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "go back to your father", for I will not return until his command comes to me, "and say, O our father, your son has stolen, and we testify only to what we know", that is, the theft was found in his baggage while we looked on, and we have no knowledge of the unseen, "and we were not guardians of the unseen". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 210}

Others said it means rather: we testified before Joseph that a thief is taken for his theft only according to what we knew. Report 19633, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: Ya'qub said to them, how would this man know that a thief is taken for his theft except by your word! They said "we testify only to what we know": we did not testify that a thief is taken for his theft except that which we knew. He said that the rule among the prophets, Ya'qub and his sons, was that the thief is taken for his theft as an enslaved servant. On "and we were not guardians of the unseen" he says: we did not think your son would steal and our matter come to this, and we only said "we shall guard our brother" against what we had the means to guard him from. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 210}

The interpreters said the same: report 19634, from Ikrima, through al-Husayn ibn al-Harith Abu Ammar al-Marwazi, al-Fadl ibn Musa, al-Husayn ibn Waqid and Yazid: "we were not guardians of the unseen", he said, we did not know your son would steal. The editor gives notes on the men of the chain and corrects "al-Hasan ibn Waqid" to "al-Husayn ibn Waqid". Reports 19635 to 19637, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; and al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, all from Ibn Abi Najih: we did not realise he would steal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 211}

Report 19638, from Mujahid and Qatada, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Abu Sufyan and Ma'mar: we neither thought nor realised that he would steal. Reports 19639 and 19640, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, and Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: we did not think he would steal; we did not think your son would steal. Al-Tabari says: the better of the two interpretations, in our view, of "we testify only to what we know" is that of those who said: we testified that your son stole only according to what we knew from seeing the cup in his bag, because it follows right after "your son has stolen", so it is more fitting that it be a report of their testimony about that than a report about something detached. It is also reported that "ghayb" in the dialect of Himyar is the night itself; the editor says this is a rare meaning that he did not find in the dictionaries at his disposal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 212}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
