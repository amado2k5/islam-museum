**Commentary on verse 137 (pp. 76-79)**

Al-Tabari says: We gave as inheritance to the people whom Pharaoh and his people had oppressed, slaughtering their sons, sparing their women and forcing them to serve and be enslaved, namely the Children of Israel, "the eastern parts of the land", which is Syria, the side of it toward the east, "and its western parts, which We blessed", meaning where We placed good, lasting for its people. He says "We gave as inheritance" because He made the Children of Israel inherit it through the destruction of the Amalekites who were there. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "oppression" and "blessing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 76}

Report 15043, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', Yahya ibn Yaman, Isra'il and Furat al-Qazzaz: Syria; report 15044, from al-Hasan, similar, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya and Abd al-Razzaq; report 15045, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', Qabisa, Sufyan and Furat: the land that We blessed is Syria. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 76}

Report 15046, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid ibn Zuray' and Sa'id: it is the land of Syria; report 15047, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the one He blessed is Syria. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 77}

Some grammarians claimed that "the eastern and western parts of the land" is accusative as a locative, giving the sense: We gave as inheritance to the people who were oppressed in the east and west of the land, with "We gave as inheritance" falling upon "which We blessed". Al-Tabari rejects this as meaningless: the Israelites were oppressed in Pharaoh's days by no one but Pharaoh and his people, who had authority only in Egypt, so it cannot be said they were oppressed in the east and west of the earth; and if it were said to mean the east and west of Egypt, that would be far from what the speech conveys, besides departing from the sayings of the interpreters and the scholars of commentary. The editor explains that "falling" means that the verb passes over to the object. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 77}

On "and the fair word of your Lord was fulfilled for the Children of Israel because they were patient" he says: God's promise to the Children of Israel was fulfilled in full as He promised them, giving them power in the land and victory over their enemy Pharaoh; His "fair word" is His saying "We wished to show favour to those who were oppressed in the land" through "what they feared" ([[passage/quran-28-5|28:5]], [[passage/quran-28-6|28:6]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 77}

Report 15048, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: Moses's people prevailed over Pharaoh, and God's giving them power in the land was what He made them inherit of it; report 15049, from Mujahid, is similar, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. The editor notes that the printed text has a different noun and an added conjunction, and he kept the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 78}

On "and We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people had made" he says: We destroyed what Pharaoh and his people had made of buildings and farms; "and what they were erecting" means what they built of buildings and palaces, and We drove them from all of it and ruined all of it. He has already explained "trellising". Report 15050, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha: they built. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 78}

Report 15051, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "they erect" means they build houses and dwellings as far as they reached, while their vines were not trellised; report 15052, from Mujahid, is the same. The editor notes that the printed text reads "not trellised" with another form and he kept the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 79}

The readers differed in reading "they erect": most readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read the ra' with kasra, except Asim ibn Abi al-Najud, who read it with damma. Al-Tabari says they are two well-known dialects among the Arabs, "arasha ya'rishu" and "ya'rushu", so whichever a reader uses he is right, since the meaning is the same and both are known from Arab speech; the Arabs do so with the verb pattern "fa'ala" when they turn it to the imperfect, sometimes with damma on the middle letter and sometimes with kasra. Still the reading he prefers is the kasra, for its fame among the common people, the many readers who use it, and because it is the sounder of the two dialects. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 79}
