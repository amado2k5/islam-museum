## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when Abraham raised the foundations of the House, and Ishmael" al-Tabari says: remember when Abraham raised the foundations of the House, the "qawa'id" being the plural of "qa'ida", the base of the House. He then sets out the interpreters' disagreement on whether the two of them made the foundations or they existed before them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 57}

Some said they are the foundations of a house Adam built at God's command, which was effaced until God settled Abraham there and he built it, from 'Ata' and Ibn Abbas; in 'Ata''s report people say it was built from five mountains (Hira', Tur Zayta, Tur Sina', Lebanon and al-Judi). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 57} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 58}

Others said they are the foundations of a house God sent down to Adam from heaven, which he circled as the Throne is circled in heaven, and which was raised up in the days of the flood, so Abraham raised the foundations of that house, from 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, 'Ata', Qatada and Aban. In the editor's footnotes, al-Suyuti and al-Haythami mention the first report and the editor thinks it may be among what Abdullah ibn 'Amr heard of the reports of the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 58} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 59} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 60}

Others said the place of the House was a red mound like a dome, because when God wished to create the earth foam rose on the water at the place of the Sacred House and He then spread the earth out from beneath it, from Mujahid, 'Ata', 'Amr ibn Dinar and Ibn Abbas. From 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, people found in Mecca a stone with writing on it that attributes words to God mentioning the House, and the editor compares it in a footnote with the versions of al-Azraqi and Ibn Ishaq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 60} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 61}

From Mujahid and others among the people of knowledge, through Ibn Ishaq: when God settled Abraham at the place of the House he set out for it from Syria with Ishmael and his mother Hajar, with Gabriel showing the place, at which time the House was a red mound; Abraham left them at the place of the Hijr, told Hajar to make a hut, and said "our Lord, I have settled some of my offspring". Ibn Ishaq adds that an angel came to Hajar before the foundations were raised, pointed to the House and said it is the first house set on earth and that Abraham and Ishmael will raise it for the people, and he says this is what they claim and God knows best. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 61} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 62}

Al-Tabari goes on with the reports about the place of the House: from Mujahid that God created the place of the House two thousand years before creating anything of the earth, with its corners in the seventh earth; from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab from Ka'b that the House was a flotsam on the water forty years before God created the earth and the earth was spread out from it; and from 'Ali ibn Abi Talib that Abraham came from Armenia with the "sakina" showing him where to settle the House. In a footnote the editor says the first report, from Ka'b, "has no value" and that the second appears to be among what the Companions told of the reports of the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 62} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 63}

Al-Tabari then gives his choice. God reported that Abraham and his son Ishmael raised the foundations of the Sacred House, and it may be that these are foundations of a house sent down with Adam, or the dome that 'Ata' mentioned, of the foam of the water, or a ruby or pearl sent down from heaven, or that Adam built it and it fell down; he has no knowledge of which it was, because its truth is reached only by a report from God and His Messenger through wide transmission, and there is no report to settle it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 64}

On "our Lord, accept from us" he says: when Abraham raised the foundations of the House, and Ishmael, saying "our Lord, accept from us"; this is said to be so in Ibn Mas'ud's reading, from al-Suddi, and from Ibn Abbas, Ishmael carried the stones on his neck while the old man built. He mentions that some said the one who said "our Lord, accept from us" was Ishmael alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 64} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 65}

He then reports the disagreement on who raised the foundations, after agreement that Abraham was among those who did. Some said Abraham and Ishmael together; in al-Suddi's report Abraham went to Mecca, and he and Ishmael took pickaxes not knowing where the House was, and God sent a wind called the wind of al-Hujuj that swept clear around the Ka'ba the foundation of the first House, and they dug until they laid the foundation. When they reached the place of the corner Abraham asked Ishmael for a good stone, and Gabriel brought him the Black Stone from India, which was white, a white ruby, which Adam had brought down from the garden and which turned black from people's sins. From 'Ubayd ibn 'Umayr it reached him that Abraham and Ishmael raised the foundations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 65} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 66}

Others said Abraham raised the foundations and Ishmael handed him stones, from Ibn Abbas in a long report: Abraham came while Ishmael was trimming arrows near Zamzam and told him God had commanded him to build a house here; Ishmael said do what your Lord commanded, and Abraham asked, will you help me, and he said I will help you; Ishmael brought stones and Abraham built, and when the building rose he brought this stone and stood on it as he built, while they said "our Lord, accept from us, You are the Hearing, the Knowing". The editor notes the report is sound, that al-Bukhari relates it at length and that this is a part of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 67}

In a second report from Ibn Abbas, part of a long report which the editor says al-Bukhari relates, Abraham told Ishmael God had commanded him to build a house for Him and Ishmael said obey your Lord, and when the building rose and the old man was too weak to lift the stones he stood on a stone, which is the station of Abraham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 68}

Others said Abraham alone raised the foundations while Ishmael was then a small child, from 'Ali ibn Abi Talib: when Abraham was commanded to build the House, Ishmael and Hajar went out with him, and when he reached Mecca he saw above the place of the House something like a cloud with something like a head in it, which spoke to him and said build to my shade or my measure, neither adding nor taking away. When he had built he left, leaving Ishmael and Hajar; the report tells of Ishmael's thirst, Hajar's running seven times between al-Safa and al-Marwa, the springing of Zamzam, and Gabriel calling to her. In a footnote the editor quotes Ibn Kathir's comment that this account has Abraham building the House before leaving them, which he says could mean he first set a surrounding wall. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 68} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 69}

From 'Ali by another route, a man asked him whether it was the first house set on earth, and he said no, but it is the first house in which blessing was placed, the station of Abraham, and whoever enters it is safe; then a report that God revealed to Abraham to build Him a house, and sent the "sakina" (a violent wind with two heads) that came to Mecca and coiled over the place of the House, Abraham being told to build where the sakina settled; and that Gabriel brought the Black Stone from heaven. The editor notes these are three chains for one report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 69} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 70} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 71}

Al-Tabari then gives his choice. For those who said Abraham and Ishmael raised it, or Abraham raised it and Ishmael handed him the stones, the correct view is that the unstated speaker of "our Lord, accept from us" is both of them; on what is reported from 'Ali, it could only be Ishmael. He holds that the foundations were raised by both together, because between them one built and the other carried the stones and helped place them, and the Arabs do not hesitate to ascribe building to whoever caused or helped it, and because the interpreters agree that Ishmael is meant by the words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 71} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 72}

The sense of the verse for him is: when Abraham raised the foundations of the House, and Ishmael, saying "our Lord, accept from us our work, our obedience and our worship in reaching Your command to build Your House". He sees in their saying "accept from us" a sign that they did not build it as a dwelling for themselves but for whoever wished to worship God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 72}

At the close of the verse al-Tabari says "the Hearing, the Knowing" is the One who hears our supplication and knows what is in our hearts of submission to Him in obedience and what we show and hide of our works, and from Ibn Abbas: accept from us, You hear the supplication. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 72} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 73}
