## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 13 to 14 in a single passage. He explains His saying, "So that you may stand firmly upon His back, then remember the favor of your Lord when you are established upon Him and say, 'Glory be to Him Who subjected us to this, and we were not to associate with Him': so that you may stand on the backs of what you ride." The Arabs differed regarding the unification of the 'ha' in the phrase: 'on its back' and its masculine. Some grammarians from Basra said: Its masculine refers to what you ride, and what is masculine, as it is said: Among my women are those who agree with you and show you, and they may be mentioned as cattle and feminine. He said elsewhere: 'from what is in his bellies,' and elsewhere: 'their bellies.' Some grammarians of Kufa said: 'Zahir' was added to the singular because that singular means plural, like soldiers and army. He said: If it is said: Why not say: to stand on his back, and make the back singular if you add it to one. I said: The singular has the meaning of plural, so I redirected the apparent to the meaning, not saying 'back,' so it would be like 'al-wahid' whose meaning and wording are the same.
