## Summary
Verse 60 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 60 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "it is he who takes you in the night and knows what you wrought by day" al-Tabari, addressing his prophet Muhammad, says: say to them, Muhammad, God knows best the wrongdoers, and God is he who takes your souls in the night, seizing them from your bodies, "and knows what you wrought by day", that is, what you earned of deeds by day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 404}

He explains that "tawaffi" in Arab speech means completing a number, and cites a line of rajaz in which the poet says Quraysh do not count certain people in their number, that is, do not complete their count with them. "Ijtirah" among the Arabs is a man's work with his hand, foot or mouth, these being the "jawarih", the limbs of the body, and anyone who earns a deed is called "jarih" from the Arabs' use of it for these limbs, and the usage spread until anyone who earns any gain by any part of his body is called "mujtarih". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 405}

The editor says the poet is Mansur al-Wabari, that the line is in Lisan al-Arab and comes again later in the commentary, that the printed text and Lisan garble the name of the people and the right form is the one given later; they are Banu al-Adram of Taym ibn Ghalib ibn Fihr, of the Quraysh of the outskirts and not the Quraysh of the valley, so the poet mocks them by saying the Quraysh of the valley do not make up their number with them when they count; and refers for "tawaffi", "jawarih" and "ijtirah" to earlier passages.

Al-Tabari then reports from the interpreters in line with what he said. 13309, from al-Suddi: "takes you in the night" is sleep, and "knows what you wrought by day" is the sin you earned; 13310, from Ibn Abbas: that is, what you earned of sin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 405}

13311 and 13312, from Qatada: "what you wrought by day" is what you did by day; 13313, also from Qatada: he means their sleep, and he knows what sin you did, nothing of it hidden from him; 13314, from Mujahid: his taking them in the night is their sleep, and "what you wrought by day" is what you earned by day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 406}

Al-Tabari says that although these words report God's power and knowledge, they also argue against the polytheists who denied his power to revive them after their death and raise them after their perishing. He says: he who seizes your souls by night and raises you by day to reach an appointed term, while you see it and know it is true, is not to be denied the power to seize your souls and make you perish, return them to your bodies and bring you into being after death; for that is like what you witness, and one who can do what you witness is not to be denied the power to do what you have not witnessed, since what you have not seen resembles what you have. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 406}

On "then he raises you in it, that an appointed term be fulfilled; then to him is your return; then he will tell you what you used to do" al-Tabari says: "then he raises you" means he stirs you and wakes you from your sleep, "in it" meaning in the day, the pronoun referring to the day; "that an appointed term be fulfilled" says: that God fulfil the term he set for your lives, which is death, so that it reaches its span and end; "then to him is your return" is to God your resort and destination; "then he will tell you what you used to do" means he will inform you of what you did in your worldly life and then repay you, good for good and evil for evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 407}

Report 13315, from Mujahid: "raises you in it", in the day; 13316 and 13317, from Qatada: in the day, and the raising is waking; 13318, from al-Suddi: by day; 13319, from Abdullah ibn Kathir: he raises you in sleep. On "that an appointed term be fulfilled", which is death: 13320, from Mujahid: it is death; 13321, from al-Suddi: it is the term of life until death; 13322, from Abdullah ibn Kathir: their span. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 408}

The editor says the printed text and manuscript omitted "then he raises you in it", the text of the recitation, so he restored it; that "in" fell out of "the pronoun in it" and he restored it; that in 13318 the printed text has a different wording and he kept the manuscript's; and refers for "the raising", "an appointed term", "the return" and "the tiding" to earlier passages.

Verse 60 closes here at p. 408, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-61|6:61]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
