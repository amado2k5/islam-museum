## In al-Tabari's commentary: "God mocks them"
Al-Tabari records that the exegetes differed over the nature of God's mockery of the hypocrites. Some said it is what He reports He will do to them on the Day of Resurrection, and to the disbelievers by granting them respite, as in the verses of al-Hadid and Al Imran. Others said it is His rebuke and blame of them for their disobedience, or their destruction, as in a line of Ubayd ibn al-Abras. Others said it is by way of reply, as a man says "I am the one who deceived you" though he used no deceit, the sense being that the plot and mockery have overtaken them. Others said it is a report that He repays them with the recompense of mockery and punishes them with the punishment of deceit, the report of the repayment given in the wording of the act that earned it though the two meanings differ, as in "the recompense of an evil is an evil like it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 301} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 302}

Al-Tabari also gives a further view: that God's mockery of them is that He shows them rulings in this world contrary to their standing with Him in the next, as they showed the Prophet and the believers what differed from their inner thoughts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 302} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 303}
His own choice is that "istihza" in Arabic means showing the one mocked, in word and deed, something that outwardly pleases him while leaving him inwardly harmed, and likewise deceit, ridicule and plotting. On that basis he holds that God applied the rulings of Islam to the hypocrites in this world while knowing their falsehood, and prepared a painful punishment for them until He separates them from His friends and places them in the lowest depth, so that He is mocking, ridiculing, deceiving and plotting against them in the sense he described, without any wrong in it; he gives Ibn Abbas that God "ridicules them in order to take vengeance on them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 303} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 304} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 305}
He objects to those who say it is only by way of reply and that there was no mockery or plotting from God, holding that this denies what God affirmed of Himself, as if one denied that God swallowed up or drowned those He said He did. To those who say mockery is jest and play, which must be denied of God, he answers that play and jest have a sense distinct from mockery, ridicule, plotting and deceit, and says he omits a longer treatment to avoid lengthening the book. This is his reading and is attributed to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 305} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 306}
