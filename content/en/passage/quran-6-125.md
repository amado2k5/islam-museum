## Summary
Verse 125 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 125 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 125, "whoever God wills to guide, He opens his breast to Islam", al-Tabari says: whoever God wills to guide to faith in Him, in His Messenger and in what he brought from his Lord, and enables him to it, "He opens his breast to Islam", meaning He widens his breast for that, eases it and makes it light for him by His kindness and help, until Islam lights up in his heart and shines for him and his breast widens to accept it, as the report from the Messenger of God has it. Report 13852, from Abu Ja'far, says that when the verse came down they asked how the breast is opened, and he said when the light comes down into the heart the breast opens and widens for it. They asked if there was a sign by which it is known, and he said yes: turning to the abode of permanence, drawing away from the abode of delusion, and preparing for death before death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 98}

The editor's long notes say that the name "Abd Allah ibn Murra" in the chain does not stand in his view and that the right reading is probably "Abu Abd Allah" or "Amr ibn Murra", and that the Abu Ja'far who transmits this is Abd Allah ibn al-Miswar al-Hashimi al-Mada'ini, whom he describes as weak and a liar, citing the criticism of the hadith critics, among them Ahmad ibn Hanbal and Ibn Abi Hatim; he concludes that reports 13852 to 13854 and 13856 are feeble. He notes that Ibn Kathir mentioned them and said they support one another, which the editor judges a mistake, because the reports of a liar support nothing. The notes are the editor's own judgement, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 99}

Al-Tabari then gives the other reports to the same effect. Report 13853, from Abu Ja'far, says the Prophet was asked which believers are the shrewdest and said those who most remember death and prepare best for what is after it; asked about the verse, he said a light thrown into him so that he opens and widens, and its sign is turning to the abode of permanence, drawing away from the abode of delusion and preparing for death before death. Report 13854, from a man called Abu Ja'far who lived in al-Mada'in, is the same, and 13855, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, is the same through a chain passing Amr ibn Murra and Abu Ubayda; the editor calls it weak because the chain has Sa'id ibn Abd al-Malik al-Harrani, who was criticised, and because Abu Ubayda did not hear from his father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 100}

Report 13856, from Abd Allah ibn al-Miswar, says the Prophet recited the verse and then said that when the light enters the heart it widens and opens, and the sign is turning to the abode of permanence, drawing away from the abode of delusion, and preparing for death before it comes; the editor refers in the note to the entry on Khalid ibn Abi Karima and to Abd Allah ibn al-Miswar being the Abu Ja'far of al-Mada'in, described as a liar. Report 13857, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud by another chain (passing Mahbub ibn al-Hasan, Yunus and Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Utba), gives the same sense; the editor calls it weak, suggests the right reading of the chain is Abd Allah ibn Utba ibn Mas'ud, and says Ibn Kathir's view that the chains, broken and connected, strengthen one another is refuted by an examination of the chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 101}

The editor notes in passing that the numbering of the edition skipped number 13858 by mistake. Al-Tabari then says the commentators spoke along these lines: he gives (13859) al-Suddi, that "He opens his breast to Islam" means He widens his breast for it; (13860) Ibn Jurayj, that it is by the words "there is no god but God"; and (13861) Ibn Jurayj again, that by the words "there is no god but God" He makes in his breast room for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 103}

Then "and whoever He wills to lead astray, He makes his breast tight and constricted". Al-Tabari says: whoever God wills to lead astray from the path of guidance, He occupies him with his unbelief and with barring His path, and makes his breast, through abandoning him and the dominance of unbelief over him, constricted. "Haraj" is the most severe tightness, that nothing passes through because of its narrowness; here it is the breast to which no admonition reaches and into which the light of faith does not enter, because the rust of idolatry covers it. Its root is "haraja", a thicket of tangled trees. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 103}

On the sense of "haraj", al-Tabari gives report 13862, from Abu al-Salt al-Thaqafi, that Umar ibn al-Khattab read "haraj" with a fatha on the ra', while a companion of the Prophet with him read it with a kasra; Umar said, find me a man from Kinana, and let him be a shepherd, and let him be of the Mudlij. They brought him and Umar asked what the "haraja" is. He said it is the tree among other trees which no grazing animal, wild beast or anything reaches. Umar said, so is the heart of the hypocrite: nothing of good reaches it. The editor regards this report as rare for showing how the language was transmitted and explained by asking bedouin and shepherds, notes that Mudlij was a Kinana clan famed for tracking, and gives notes on the transmitters. Then (13863) Ibn Abbas: God narrows the breast of the one He wills to lead astray, so that Islam becomes narrow for him although Islam is wide, as in "He has not placed any hardship on you in religion" (22:78). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 104}

The commentators differed on the meaning of "narrow and constricted". Some said doubtful, which is given (13864) from Mujahid and (13865) from al-Suddi; others said confused, from Qatada (13866 and 13867); others said that from the intensity of narrowness faith cannot reach him: from Sa'id ibn Jubayr (13868), he finds no way except upward; from Ata' al-Khurasani (13869 and 13870), good has no passage in it; and from Ibn Jurayj (13871 and 13872), with "there is no god but God" he finds no room for it in his breast, so that it cannot enter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 105}

He then gives the differing readings: most Meccans and Iraqis read "haraj" with fatha on ha' and ra', as the plural of "haraja"; most readers of Medina read "harij" with kasra on the ra'. Those readers then differed on its meaning: some said it is the same as "haraj" and the two are well-known dialect forms, like "danaf" and "danif", "wahad" and "wahid", "farad" and "farid"; others said rather it means sin, from the phrase "so-and-so is sinful and constricted", and an Arab usage is reported, "my wronging is constricting and sinful to you"; the editor refers to al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 106}

Al-Tabari's view is that they are two well-known readings and two current dialect forms with one meaning, and whichever a reader uses is correct, since the meanings agree. As for "ayyiq", most readers read it with fatha on the dad and a doubled ya', except some Meccans who read "dayq" with a quiescent ya'. That has two possible explanations: either he made it quiescent while intending the doubled form, as one says "hayn layn" for "hayyin layyin", or he made it quiescent intending the verbal noun of "the matter narrowed, it narrows, narrowness". He cites a line of Ru'ba and "do not be in distress over their plotting" (16:127) and another line of his; the note explains "ma'zul" and other words of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 107}

It is reported of al-Kisa'i that he said "diyq" with kasra is for livelihood and place, and "dayq" for a matter. Al-Tabari then says that in this verse is the clearest statement, for whoever is enabled to understand it, that the cause by which one reaches faith and obedience differs from the cause by which one reaches unbelief and disobedience, and that both causes are from God; for God reported of Himself that He opens the breast of whoever He wills to guide to Islam and makes the breast of whoever He wills to lead astray tight and constricted, as if ascending into the sky. It is known that opening the breast to faith is the opposite of narrowing it; were faith reached through narrowing the breast, there would be no difference between narrowing and opening, and God would have opened Abu Jahl's breast to faith and narrowed His messenger's, which is among the gravest unbelief; so the falsity of that is the clear proof that the cause by which believers believed differs from the cause by which unbelievers disbelieved, and that both are from God and in His hand. The note says this answers the Mu'tazila and the editor counts it among the best answers to their claim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 108}

Then "as if he were ascending into the sky". Al-Tabari says this is a likeness God drew for the heart of this unbeliever in the intensity of its narrowing against faith reaching it, like his inability to ascend into the sky, since that is not within his power. He gives (13873 and 13874) Ata' al-Khurasani: his likeness is that of one who cannot ascend into the sky; (13875 and 13876) Ibn Jurayj: with "there is no god but God" it cannot enter, as though he ascended into the sky from the hardship of it; and (13877) al-Suddi: from the narrowness of his breast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 109}

Al-Tabari gives the differing readings of "ka-anna-ma yassa''adu". Most readers of Medina and Iraq read "yassa''adu", meaning "yatasa''adu", assimilating the ta' into the sad; some Kufans read "yassa'adu", meaning "yatasa'adu"; some Meccans read "yas'adu", from "sa'ida, yas'adu". All are close in meaning and whichever a reader uses is correct, but he chooses "yassa''adu" with a doubled sad and no alif, because of how many readers use it and because of Umar ibn al-Khattab's saying that nothing was ever harder for him than a marriage sermon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 110}

Then "thus God places defilement on those who do not believe". Al-Tabari says: as God makes the breast of the one He wills to lead astray tight and constricted, as if ascending into the sky from its narrowness against faith, and repays him with that, so God gives Satan power over him and those like him who refuse belief in God and His Messenger, so that he misguides him and bars him from the path of truth. They differed on "rijs": some said it is whatever has no good, from Mujahid (13878 and 13879); others said punishment, from Ibn Zayd (13880); others said Satan, from Ibn Abbas (13881). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 111}

A Kufan grammarian is cited as saying "rijs" and "najs" are two forms of the same word, and a Basran grammarian that "rijs" and "rijz" are the same and mean punishment; the editor refers to Abu Ubayda. Al-Tabari's choice: what Ibn Abbas said, and those who said "rijs" and "najs" are one, because of the report (13882) that the Prophet used to say on entering the privy, "O God, I seek refuge in You from the defiled, the foul, the corrupt and corrupting, Satan the accursed". That report shows that "rijs" is "najs", the filth with no good, and is a description of Satan. The note gives Abu Ubayd's explanation of "khabith" and "mukhbith"; the editor takes the chain through Anas as sound but did not find the report in Anas's hadith, and found it with this wording in the hadith of Abu Umama by a weak chain, reported by Ibn Majah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 112}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
