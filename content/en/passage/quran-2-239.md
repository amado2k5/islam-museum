## Summary
Verse 239 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 239 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-239|2:239]]) begins on p. 237: al-Tabari says the sense is, stand to God in your prayer obedient, and if you fear an enemy you dread for yourselves when you meet them, from praying standing on the ground, then pray walking on your feet while you fight, or riding on your mounts, and that suffices you then in place of standing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 237} He explains the accusative form of the word for those on foot as resting on an omitted sense, the way the Arabs speak in conditions, explains the plurals of the foot-soldier and the rider and the dialect forms, cites a line of a man of the Banu 'Uqayl for which the editor gives the sources in Lisan al-'Arab and Ibn Hisham, and relates two readings, one with a doubled letter and one with a pointed vowel on the first letter and no doubling, which he does not allow, as they depart from the reading widespread in the Muslim cities. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 237} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 238} Interpreters said the same: Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, that at the time of pursuit he prays wherever his face is, riding or walking, making prostration lower than bowing, and prays two units, indicating by gesture (5535, 5536, 5537); Sa'id b. Jubayr, when the horses are driven on, he gestures (5538, 5539); al-Hasan (5540, 5545), that he prays riding or walking wherever his face is, gesturing, and in one report one unit; Mujahid, that the Companions in battle on horseback, when fear occurs, each man should pray in any direction, standing or riding, or as he can, gesturing with his head or speaking with his tongue (5541, 5542); al-Dahhak, that at battle, if he is pursued or a beast of prey pursues him, he prays wherever his face is, and in one report two takbirs by gesture (5543, 5544); al-Suddi, that the walker gestures with his head wherever he turns and the rider on his mount likewise (5546). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 238} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 239} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 240} The editor says part of the old division of the copy ends here and that the colophon of the copy records an audition dated Sha'ban in the year 408 AH; this is from his note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 240}

More reports on ([[passage/quran-2-239|2:239]]) on the prayer in fear: Qatada, that God allowed one afraid in battle to pray riding and running, gesturing with his head wherever his face is, two units if he can and otherwise one (5547, 5552); Tawus, that this is at close sword fighting (5548); al-Zuhri, that when the enemy pursues them they may pray in any direction on foot or mounted, gesturing, two units, and Qatada said one unit suffices (5549); al-Rabi', that when they feared the enemy they prayed two units mounted or on foot (5550); Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, that he prays the obligatory prayer on his mount wherever his face is, gesturing at each bowing and prostration with the prostration lower (5551); al-Hasan (5553, 5554); al-Hakam, Hammad and Qatada, one unit (5555, 5556, 5557); Ibn Sirin, of the one in flight, that he prays however he can (5558); that Harim b. Hayyan, in command of an army when the prayer time came, said that each man should make one prostration under his shield wherever his face is (5559, 5560, 5561); 'Ata', that he prays wherever his mount turns, gesturing, for the obligatory prayer (5562, 5564); Jabir b. 'Abd Allah, that the prayer in fear is one unit (5563); Malik, that it applies to the mounted and the walking, citing a verse in Surat al-Hajj (22:27) to show that those on foot and those mounted are meant (5565). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 241} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 242} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 243} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 244} The editor says what has been said of some narrators and corrects some names and wording in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 242} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 243} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 244} These are the editor's notes.

Al-Tabari says the fear for which a person prays the obligatory prayer walking or riding is fear for his life at the drawing of swords and close fighting with an enemy of the Muslims or a combatant, or when a beast of prey pursues him, a charging camel, or a torrent in which he fears drowning, and anything that would most probably destroy a man if he prayed the prayer of safety: he may pray the prayer of severe fear wherever his face is, gesturing, because the verse is general and does not restrict fear to one kind. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 244} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 245} He cites Ibn 'Umar's report from the Prophet on the prayer in fear, that the leader stands with a party, they make one prostration, then they go to face the enemy and the other party comes and prays one prostration with the leader, then each party makes up one prostration alone, and that if the fear is worse than that they pray on foot or mounted (5566); from Ibn 'Umar, that when they are mixed in the fighting it is only remembrance and a sign with the head, and from the Prophet, that if they are more than that, they pray standing and mounted (5567); and from Ibn 'Umar on the two parties with one unit each, with the same provision for worse fear (5568). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 245} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 246} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 247} The editor says 'Abd Allah b. Nafi' in chain 5566 is very weak, but Ibn Majah relates the report with a sound chain and Malik has it in the Muwatta', and that al-Bukhari relates 5567; these are his gradings, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 245} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 246}

On the number of units in that state, al-Tabari says he prefers that it not be less than in safety, but if one shortens and prays one unit he regards it as sufficient, because of Ibn 'Abbas's report that God prescribed prayer through your Prophet as four when resident, two on a journey and one in fear (5569). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 247} This is al-Tabari's jurisprudential view and argument; the other views are given under their names, the museum gives no verdict, and none of this is a ruling.

Al-Tabari explains the close of ([[passage/quran-2-239|2:239]]): when you are safe, believers, from your enemy that he can kill you while you pray, and from whatever else you feared, and are at ease, remember God in your prayer and outside it with thanks, praise and glorification for the favour of guidance to the truth from which your unbelieving enemies strayed, as He reminded you by teaching you His rulings, the lawful and forbidden, the news of earlier nations and of what would come after you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 248} Mujahid said that safety means leaving the abode of travel for the abode of residence (5570); and Ibn Zayd said as al-Tabari does, that when they are safe they pray the prayer as God made it obligatory, and that in fear there was an allowance (5571). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 248} The editor thinks the order of the two reports and some wording in the manuscript were disturbed by a copyist; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 248} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 249}

Al-Tabari then chooses: another view is sounder than Mujahid's, because all agree that once fear is gone the person praying an obligatory prayer, even on a journey, must do it with its bowing, prostration and limits, standing on the ground, not walking or riding, as when resident, except for the shortening allowed on a journey; and no travel is mentioned in the verse, only prayer in safety and in severe fear, so the meaning is, when the fear is gone, keep up your prayer and the remembrance of God in it and outside it as I made it obligatory before fear arose. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 249} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 250} He adds that had travel been mentioned, He would have said when you are resident, not when you are safe, and the wording itself shows his view sound and Mujahid's otherwise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 250} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument.

Al-Tabari says the men who die are meant, leaving wives who were their wives in life by marriage, not by ownership, and then the report turns to their wives as in ([[passage/quran-2-234|2:234]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 250} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 251} Readers differ over the word for the bequest: some read it in the accusative, meaning let them make a bequest for their wives, others in the nominative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 251} The grammarians differ over the nominative: some said it is raised in the sense that the bequest was prescribed for them, arguing that this is how it is in the reading of 'Abd Allah b. Mas'ud; others said it is raised by the phrase "for their wives", meaning for their wives is a bequest. Al-Tabari prefers the first, because the Arabs omit the indefinite's raising word before it, giving examples and citing two verses, from Surat al-Nur and Surat al-Tawba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 251} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 252} The editor gives the reading of Ibn Mas'ud from works on irregular readings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 251}
