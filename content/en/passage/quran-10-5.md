**Commentary on verse 5 (pp. 23-24)**

He then explains "He it is who made the sun a radiance and the moon a light and determined stages for it, that you may know the number of the years and the reckoning; God did not create that except in truth; He details the signs for people who know". Your Lord, God, who created the heavens and the earth, is the One who made the sun a radiance by day and the moon a light by night, that is, He lit the sun and illumined the moon, "and determined stages for it" meaning He decreed it and fixed it in stages that it neither passes nor falls short of, always in one state. The editor refers to the gloss on "determination" in the eleventh volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 23}

Al-Tabari says the singular "determined it" after the mention of sun and moon has two explanations: one, that the pronoun refers to the moon alone, since the end of months and years is known by the crescents and not by the sun; the other, that mention of one stands for the other, as in "God and His messenger have more right that they should please him" in Surat al-Tawba. He cites a line by Ibn Ahmar, or by al-Azraq ibn Tarafa al-Firasi, in which the poet says he was accused of something of which he and his father were innocent (described, not quoted). The editor adds that the man had a quarrel with an opponent over a well, the opponent called him a thief son of a thief, and he spoke the line; he also corrects the wording and explains "al-tawa" as the well and "al-jawl" as a side of the well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 23}

On "that you may know the number of the years and the reckoning": He fixed it in stages so that you, people, may know the number of the years, that is, the entering of those that enter and the passing of those to come, and their reckoning, meaning the reckoning of their times and the count of their days and hours; "God did not create that except in truth" means He created the sun, the moon and their stages wholly in truth, alone, with no help or partner; "He details the signs" means He makes the proofs clear "for people who know", those who on reflection grasp the truth of God's oneness and the soundness of what Muhammad calls them to, putting away rivals and renouncing idols. The editor refers to the glosses on "detailing" and "sign". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 24}
