## Summary
Verse 79 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 79 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 79 (pp. 381-394)**

Al-Tabari says: those who find fault with the volunteers in the alms given to the people of need and want, beyond what God made obligatory on their wealth, and slander them over it by saying "they gave it only for show and to be heard, and did not seek God's face", and who find fault with those who find nothing to give in alms but their effort, which is their capacity, belittling them and saying "God was surely independent of the alms of these", mocking them; "they mock them, God mocks them". We have explained the nature of God's mockery of those of His creatures He mocks elsewhere, which spares repeating it here. "And theirs is a painful punishment" means that from God on the Day of Resurrection they will have an agonizing, painful punishment. The editor refers to earlier explanations of faulting and volunteering, and to a later one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 382}

It is mentioned that those meant by "the volunteers among the believers" are Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Asim ibn Adi al-Ansari, and that those meant by "those who find nothing but their effort" is Abu Aqil al-Arashi, brother of Banu Unayf. Report 17003, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdallah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf brought forty ounces of gold to the Prophet, and a man of the Helpers brought a measure of food, and some of the hypocrites said "by God, Abd al-Rahman brought what he brought only for show", and they said "God and His Messenger were surely independent of this measure". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 382}

Report 17004, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: the Messenger of God went out to the people one day and called out among them "gather your alms", and the people gathered their alms; then a man from the last of them came with a measure of dates and said "Messenger of God, this is a sa' of dates; I spent my night drawing water with the rope until I earned two sa' of dates, and kept one and brought you the other", and the Messenger of God ordered him to scatter it among the alms; and some men mocked him and said "by God, God and His Messenger are surely independent of this, and what will they do with your sa'?". Then Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, a man of Quraysh of Banu Zuhra, said to the Messenger of God, "is there anyone left of the recipients of these alms?", and he said "no", and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf said "I have a hundred ounces of gold for the alms", and Umar ibn al-Khattab said to him "are you mad?", and he said "I am not mad", and he said "then teach us what you said", and he said "yes: my wealth is eight thousand; four thousand of it I lend to my Lord, and four thousand are mine", and the Messenger of God said to him "may God bless you in what you kept and in what you gave". The hypocrites disliked it and said "by God, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf gave his gift only for show", and they lied, for he was a volunteer; and God sent down his excuse and the excuse of his poor companion who brought the sa' of dates, saying in His book "those who find fault with the volunteers among the believers over the alms". The editor explains the measure as a unit of capacity and the rope as the cord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 383}

Reports 17005, 17006 and 17007, from Mujahid, by the routes of Ibn Waki', Abu Usama, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih, and of Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, al-Muthanna and Abu Hudhayfa: Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf brought the alms of his wealth, four thousand, and the hypocrites found fault with him and said "he showed off"; "and those who find nothing but their effort", he said a man of the Helpers hired himself out for a sa' of dates, having none other, and brought it, and they found fault with him and said "God was independent of this one's sa'". Report 17008, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf came with half his wealth and drew near to God with it, and the hypocrites found fault with him and said "he gave that only for show and to be heard"; then a man of the poor Muslims called Habhab Abu Aqil came and said "Prophet of God, I spent the night pulling the rope for two sa' of dates; one sa' I kept for my family and here is the other", and the hypocrites said "by God, God and His Messenger are surely independent of this", and God sent down about that in the Qur'an "those who find fault". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 384}

Report 17009, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf gave half his wealth, his wealth being eight thousand dinars, and gave four thousand dinars, and some hypocrites said "Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf is greatly given to showing off", and a man who had two sa' of dates brought one of them, and some hypocrites said "God was surely independent of this one's sa'", so the hypocrites kept slandering and mocking them, and God said "and those who find nothing but their effort, they mock them, God mocks them, and theirs is a painful punishment". The editor notes a sharp dispute over the name of the man with the sa', and that Ibn Hajar in the Fath lists seven views: Habhab Abu Aqil; Sahl ibn Rafi'; Rifa'a ibn Sahl ibn Rafi'; Abu Aqil ibn Abdallah ibn Tha'laba al-Baliyy; Abd al-Rahman ibn Samhan; Abu Khaythama Abdallah ibn Khaythama; and Ulba ibn Zayd al-Muharibi; Ibn Hajar thought this shows that more than one man brought a sa', and the editor says the matter needs more investigation and that he noted it as a reminder for whoever wishes to follow it up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 385}

Report 17010, from Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, Abu Awana and Umar ibn Abi Salama: the Messenger of God said "give alms, for I wish to send out an expedition", and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf said "Messenger of God, I have four thousand, two thousand of which I lend to God and two thousand for my family", and the Messenger of God said "may God bless you in what you gave and bless you in what you kept", and a man of the Helpers said "I have two sa' of dates, one sa' for my Lord and one for my family"; the hypocrites found fault and said "Ibn Awf gave this only for show", and said "was God not independent of this one's sa'?", and God sent down "those who find fault with the volunteers among the believers", to the end of the verse. The editor notes that Umar ibn Abi Salama is held weak, that al-Haythami records the report and says al-Bazzar related it by two routes, one connected through Abu Hurayra and the other from Abu Salama in a mursal form, and that Ibn Kathir also relates it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 386}

Report 17011, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Sa'd and Abu Ja'far: the people were struck by severe hardship and the Messenger of God ordered them to give alms, and Abd al-Rahman came with four hundred ounces, and the Messenger of God said "O God, bless him in what he kept", and the hypocrites said "Abd al-Rahman did this only for show and to be heard"; he said, and a man came with a sa' of dates and said "Messenger of God, I hired myself out for two sa', so I took one of them to my family and brought one sa' of dates", and the hypocrites said "God is independent of this one's sa'", and God sent down this verse "and those who find nothing but their effort, they mock them, God mocks them, and theirs is a painful punishment". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 387}

Report 17012, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the volunteers among the believers in the alms were Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, who gave four thousand dinars, and Asim ibn Adi, brother of Banu al-Ajlan, for the Messenger of God had encouraged and urged alms, and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf stood and gave four thousand dirhams, and Asim ibn Adi stood and gave a hundred loads of dates, and they found fault with them and said "this is only showing off"; and the one who gave his effort was Abu Aqil, brother of Banu Unayf, of Arash, ally of Banu Amr ibn Awf, who came with a sa' of dates and emptied it into the alms, and they laughed at him and said "God is surely independent of Abu Aqil's sa'". The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira and to report 16990. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 387}

Report 17013, from Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari al-Badri, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Abu al-Nu'man al-Hakam ibn Abdallah, Shu'ba, Sulayman and Abu Wa'il: when the verse of alms came down we used to carry loads for hire; Abu al-Nu'man said "we used to work"; he said a man came and gave a great deal in alms, and another man gave a sa' of dates, and they said "God is surely independent of this one's sa'", and there came down "those who find fault with the volunteers among the believers in the alms and those who find nothing but their effort". The editor traces the report to the Sahih of al-Bukhari and of Muslim and explains the carrying for hire as bearing loads on the back for a wage to earn what one would give in alms. Report 17014, from the son of Abu Aqil, from his father, through Ibn Waki', Zayd ibn Hubab, Musa ibn Ubayda and Khalid ibn Yasar: I spent the night pulling the rope on my back for two sa' of dates, and turned back with one of them to my family, on which they might make do, and brought the other to draw near with it to the Messenger of God; I came to the Messenger of God and told him, and he said "scatter it in the alms", and the hypocrites mocked him and said "God was surely independent of this poor man's alms", and God sent down "those who find fault with the volunteers among the believers over the alms" and the following verse. The editor notes that Musa ibn Ubayda al-Rabadhi is very weak, that he found no entry for Khalid ibn Yasar, that al-Haythami cites the report from al-Tabarani and says its men are reliable except Khalid ibn Yasar, and that Ibn Kathir took it from al-Tabari and said the name of Abu Aqil was Hubab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 389}

Report 17015, from Abu al-Salil, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and al-Jurayri: a man stood before the clan and said, my father or uncle told me, "I was present when the Messenger of God was saying 'who will give alms today, for which I shall testify before God on the Day of Resurrection?'; I had a turban on, and I took off a twist or two to give in alms, then what befalls the son of Adam came upon me and I wound them back round my head. Then a man came, and I never saw in al-Baqi a man shorter in stature, blacker or uglier in the eye, leading a she-camel, and I never saw in al-Baqi one finer or more beautiful, and he said 'is it alms, Messenger of God?', and he said 'yes', and he said 'then take her', and threw down her halter; a man sitting there found fault with him and said 'by God, he gives alms with her and she is better than he', and the Messenger of God looked at him and said 'rather he is better than you and than her', saying so three times". The editor notes that Abu al-Salil is reliable, that the report is in Ahmad's Musnad and in Ibn Kathir, and explains the words for stature and for ugliness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 390}

Report 17016, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abdallah ibn Ka'b ibn Malik, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb and Ibn Shihab: the one who gave alms of a measure of dates and was found fault with by the hypocrites was Abu Khaythama al-Ansari. Report 17017, from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir, through al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Rajaa and Amir ibn Yasaf: Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf brought four thousand dirhams to the Messenger of God and said "Messenger of God, my wealth is eight thousand; I have brought you four thousand to put in God's way and kept four thousand for my family", and he said "may God bless what you gave and what you kept"; and another man came and said "Messenger of God, I spent the night drawing water for two sa'; one of them I left for my family and the other I have brought you to put in God's way", and he said "may God bless you in what you gave and what you kept"; and some of the hypocrites said "by God, Abd al-Rahman gave only for show and to be heard, and God and His Messenger were surely independent of so-and-so's sa'", and God sent down "those who find fault with the volunteers among the believers over the alms", meaning Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, "and those who find nothing but their effort", meaning the man with the measure, "they mock them, God mocks them, and theirs is a painful punishment". The editor says he found no entry for Muhammad ibn Rajaa and that Amir ibn Yasaf is held weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 391}

Report 17018, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and Mujahid: the Prophet ordered the Muslims to gather their alms, and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf came with four thousand and said "this is my wealth, which I lend to God, and I have the like of it left", and he said to him "may you be blessed in what you gave and what you kept", and the hypocrites said "he gave only for show, and the man with the measure gave only for show; God and His Messenger are surely independent of this, and what will God do with a sa' of anything?". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 391}

Report 17019, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the Prophet ordered the Muslims to give alms, and Umar ibn al-Khattab said "that found my wealth plentiful, so I shall take half of it"; he said, I came carrying a great deal of wealth, and a man of the hypocrites said to him "are you showing off, Umar?", and he said "yes, I show off to God and His Messenger, and not to anyone else"; he said, and a man of the Helpers who had nothing hired himself out to pull the rope on his neck for two sa' through the night, left one sa' for his family and came carrying one, and some of the hypocrites said to him "God and His Messenger are surely independent of your sa'"; and that is God's saying "those who find fault with the volunteers among the believers over the alms and those who find nothing but their effort", this Helper, "they mock them, God mocks them, and theirs is a painful punishment". The editor notes where the manuscript and the printed text differ in the wording of this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 392}

We have explained the meaning of faulting in the speech of the Arabs earlier, with evidence. As for "the volunteers", it means those who volunteer, the "t" assimilated into the "t" with a doubling, as is said in the verse of al-Baqara "whoever volunteers good", [[passage/quran-2-158|2:158]], meaning he volunteers. As for the word for effort, the Arabs have two forms of it: one says "he gave me of his effort" with a damma on the first letter, which is mentioned to be the dialect of the Hijaz, and with a fatha, which is the dialect of Najd. The reading of the great cities is with the damma, and that is our choice because of the agreement of the authority of the reciters on it. The scholars of the Arabs' speech, the reciters of poetry and the grammarians, claim the vowelling with fatha and with damma has one meaning and that the difference is only of dialect, as their dialects differ over "wajd" and "wujd". The editor refers to al-Farra and Abu Ubayda. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 393}

It is reported from al-Sha'bi on this, report 17020, through Abu Kurayb, Jabir ibn Nuh and Isa ibn al-Mughira: the one form is effort in work and the other is effort in sustenance; report 17021, from al-Sha'bi, is the same, through Ibn Waki', Hafs and Isa; report 17022, from him, through Ibn Idris: the one in work, the other in sustenance. The editor explains the word for sustenance as the food by which a person's body is kept up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 394}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
