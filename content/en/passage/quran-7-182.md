**Commentary on verse 182 (pp. 286-287)**

Al-Tabari says: those who denied Our proofs and marks and rejected them and did not take heed by them, We shall give respite in his delusion and make the evil of his deed seem fair to him until he thinks that in his denial of God's signs he does good to himself, and until he reaches the end that was written for him of. The editor notes that al-Tabari slipped here from the plural pronoun to the singular, which is very strange, and that he left it as in the manuscript and printed edition, thinking that at times what the author wanted to write carried him along so that his thought turned from one side of the sentence to another, which helps in knowing the habits of authors; he refers to what will come at p. 338. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 286}

…the term, then He seizes him for his evil deeds and repays him for them with the punishment He has prepared for him; that is God's drawing him on by degrees. The root of "istidraj" is the one drawn on being deluded by the kindness of the one who draws him, seeing that the one he is drawn toward does him good, until he entangles him in something he dislikes. He says he has already explained the reason God does this to those who disbelieve in Him, at a length that makes repetition unnecessary; the editor says he could not find the place, and that the words in brackets are missing from the manuscript and printed edition though the sense requires them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 287}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
