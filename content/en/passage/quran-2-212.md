## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "the life of this world is made attractive to those who disbelieve, and they mock those who believe; but those who are mindful of God will be above them on the Day of Resurrection": al-Tabari says love of this world's quick pleasures is made attractive to those who disbelieve, so they seek in it rivalry in numbers and boasting, positions of leadership and pride, and are too proud to follow you, Muhammad, and to acknowledge what you brought, and they mock the believers who follow you for leaving rivalry and boasting in this world and its ornament. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 273}

Al-Tabari completes the sense: those who worked for God, turned to obeying Him, and gave up the pleasures and desires of this world, following the Prophet, seeking what is with God, and guarding themselves by performing the obligations and avoiding disobedience, will be above those who disbelieved on the Day of Resurrection, by the entry of the God-fearing into Paradise and the disbelievers into the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 274} From Ibn Jurayj: the disbelievers seek this world and mock the believers for seeking the next; Ibn Jurayj said he thought it was from 'Ikrima: they said, if Muhammad were a prophet as he claims, our nobles and chiefs would have followed him; by God only needy people followed him, like Ibn Mas'ud (4046). From Qatada: "above them" means in Paradise (4047). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 274}

On "and God provides for whom He wills without reckoning": it means God gives those who were mindful of Him on the Day of Resurrection of His favours, honours and abundant gifts without taking account of them. Al-Tabari asks what praise lies in this, and answers that it informs that He does not fear His treasuries running out and so need to count what leaves them, since a giver's reckoning is only to know the measure of what leaves his property so as not to give beyond what would ruin him, whereas God fears neither the exhaustion of His treasuries nor any diminution of His dominion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 274} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 275}
