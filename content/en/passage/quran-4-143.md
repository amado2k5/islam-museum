**Commentary on verse 143: wavering between belief and unbelief (pp. 332-335)**

Al-Tabari says the word for wavering means going back and forth, and the root sense is movement and agitation. God means that the hypocrites are confused in their religion, returning to no belief sound and firm; they are neither with the believers with insight nor with the polytheists in ignorance, but bewildered between the two. The Prophet gave them a parable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 332}

Report 10728, from Ibn Umar through Ubayd Allah and Nafi', from the Prophet: the likeness of the hypocrite is a goat wandering between two flocks, going this way once and that way once, not knowing which to follow. Report 10729 gives the same from Ibn Umar as a halted report, and report 10730 gives the Prophet's word by another route. The editor notes that Muslim and Ahmad transmit it by several routes, and that the wandering goat is one moving between two flocks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 333}

Report 10731, from al-Suddi: they are not polytheists who show shirk, nor believers. Report 10732, from Qatada: they are neither sincere believers nor open polytheists. Qatada reports that the Prophet gave a likeness of believer, hypocrite and unbeliever as three men who came to a river: the believer crossed and was carried on, the hypocrite hesitated as he neared him, the unbeliever called him to come to him and the believer called him to come to him; the hypocrite kept going between them until a flood carried him away and drowned him, and he had been in doubt until he died. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 334}

Report 10733, from Mujahid: the hypocrites. Report 10734, from Mujahid through Shibl: not with the companions of Muhammad, nor with those Jews. Report 10735, from Ibn Jurayj: they have not made their belief sincere so as to be with the believers, and they are not with the people of shirk. Report 10736, from Ibn Zayd: between Islam and unbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 335}

As for the closing words, al-Tabari says it means whoever God forsakes from the right path, which is the Islam to which He called His servants; there is for him no other way to the truth, and God has said that whoever seeks a religion other than Islam, it will not be accepted from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 335}

The reports on verse 143 end at p. 335; verse 144 begins on p. 336. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 335}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
