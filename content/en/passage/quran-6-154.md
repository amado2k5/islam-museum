## Summary
Verse 154 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 154 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Then We gave Moses the Book, completing it for the one who did well, and detailing everything", al-Tabari says it means: then say after that, Muhammad, your Lord gave Moses the Book. The word "say" is left out because earlier in the account there is something showing it is meant, namely "say, come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden you"; He related what was forbidden and made lawful to them and then said: then say, We gave Moses; "say" is dropped because what came before shows it. The editor notes that the copies have "that is His saying" without a waw, and that the sense requires it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 232}

He says it is meant in the speech because Muhammad was undoubtedly sent long after Moses and was commanded to recite these verses to those he was commanded to recite them to only after his mission, while it is known that Moses was given the Book before God commanded Muhammad to recite these verses; and "thumma" in Arab speech is a particle showing that what follows it, in speech and report, comes after what precedes it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 233}

The commentators then differ on "completing it for the one who did well". Some said it means completion for the doers of good: he gives (14171) from Mujahid that it is for the believers, and (14172) from him that it is for the believers and the doers of good; as if Mujahid took the sense to be that God reports having given Moses the Book as a distinction over what He gave the doers of good among His servants. If one asks how "alladhi" can be singular when the sense is plural, the answer is that the Arabs do this especially with "alladhi" and with alif-lam when they mean the whole and all, as in [[passage/quran-103-2|103:2]], where the human kind is meant, and as they say "the dirham became plentiful in people's hands". The note says the printed text has a meaningless reading here, that the manuscript has "the dirham", and that a similar discussion appeared earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 233}

It is also reported that Abdullah ibn Mas'ud read "upon those who did well", which supports Mujahid. On that sense "ahsana" is a past verb, hence its fatha. It is also possible that "ahsana" is in the genitive but takes fatha because the pattern "af'ala" is not declined; and if asked by what is it put in the genitive, the answer is by following "alladhi", since nothing appears to make it nominative; the sense is then "upon the one who is best", with "huwa" dropped and "ahsana" placed next to "alladhi" and given its case, as it is like a definite noun because alif-lam does not enter it and "alladhi" is the same, as the Arabs say "I passed by the one better than you and worse than you", and as a rajaz poet said in a line cited by al-Farra' in the Ma'ani al-Qur'an, where "mithl" follows "alladhi" in case. The notes record the variant transmission of that line, explain some of its words and point out an error in the printed Ma'ani where "huwa" was added. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 234}

One who says this would not say "I passed by the one a scholar", because "scholar" is indefinite and "alladhi" definite, and an indefinite does not follow a definite. Others said the sense of "upon the one who did well" is upon what Moses did well in what God tested him with of command and prohibition in this world: (14173) from al-Rabi' that it is in what God gave him; (14174) from Qatada that whoever does good in this world, God completes it for him in the Hereafter; and (14175) from him that God's honour is completed for him in the Hereafter. On this interpretation "ahsana" is a past verb in the accusative and "alladhi" means "what", so the sense is: then We gave Moses the Book to complete My honour for him in the Hereafter, completing his good in this world in worshipping God and doing what He charged him of obedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 235}

Others said it means: then We gave Moses the Book as completion of God's kindness to His prophets and His favours with them: (14176) from Ibn Zayd that it is completion from God, from His kindness in which He was kind to them and guided them to Islam and gave them that Book as completion of His favour on them; on this too "ahsana" is a past verb in the accusative and "alladhi" means "what". It is reported of Yahya ibn Ya'mar that he read "ahsanu" in the nominative, meaning "upon the one who is best", which (14177) is given through Abu Amr ibn al-Ala. Al-Tabari says he does not allow reading it so, though it has a sound Arabic aspect, because it is against what the proof of the unanimous readers of the lands holds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 236}

Al-Tabari chooses as the soundest view that of those who said it means: then We gave Moses the Book completing Our favours to him, over the good that Moses did in observing Our commands and prohibitions, because that is its most apparent sense, and because giving Moses his Book is a favour and a great gift from God; so God reports that He favoured him with it without any prior good deed or obedience on his part. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 236}

Had the meaning been Ibn Zayd's, the speech would have been "upon the good We did", or "then God gave Moses the Book"; and that God describes Himself as giving the Book and then turns the report, with "ahsana", to someone other than the one speaking of himself, with the two reports so close, is clear proof that the meaning is not Ibn Zayd's. As for Mujahid's taking "alladhi" to mean all of them, nothing in the speech shows he is right; the apparent sense is nearer to al-Tabari's choice, and when the interpretation of a speech is disputed, the best sense is the one that most prevails on the apparent meaning, unless reason or report clearly shows another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 237}

"And detailing everything" means making clear every matter of the religion they were commanded. The sense is therefore: then We gave Moses the Torah completing Our favours and gifts to him, completing Our honour to him for his good, his obedience to his Lord and his carrying out the laws of religion he was charged with, and making clear everything his people and followers needed of their religion; (14178) from Qatada that "detailing everything" means that in it is its lawful and unlawful. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "detailing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 237}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
