**Commentary on verse 83 (pp. 403-405)**

Al-Tabari says to His Prophet Muhammad: if God brings you back, Muhammad, to a party of these hypocrites from this campaign of yours and they ask your leave to go out with you on another, say to them "you shall never go out with me, nor fight an enemy with me; you were content with sitting the first time", that is, when the Prophet went out to Tabuk, "so sit with those who stay behind", meaning sit with those of the hypocrites who sat opposing the Messenger of God, for you are of them, so follow their guidance and do as they did of disobedience to God, for God is angry with you. The editor refers to earlier explanations of party and sitting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 403}

Report 17047, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: a man said "Messenger of God, the heat is severe and we cannot go out, so do not go out in the heat", that was on the Tabuk campaign, and God said "say, the Fire of Hell is hotter if only they understood", and He ordered him to go out; some men stayed behind, then their own souls caught up with them and they said "by God, we have done nothing", and three of them set out and joined the Messenger of God, and when they came to him they repented, then returned to Medina, and God sent down "if God brings you back to a party of them" to the words "nor stand at his grave", and the Messenger of God said "those who stayed behind are lost", and God sent down their excuse when they repented, saying "God has turned to the Prophet, the emigrants and the Helpers", to the words "God is the Accepter of repentance, the Merciful", [[passage/quran-9-117|9:117-118]]. Report 17048, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "sit with those who stay behind", that is, with the women; it was mentioned to us that they were twelve men of the hypocrites, of whom was said what was said. Report 17049, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdallah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "those who stay behind" are the men. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 404}

Al-Tabari holds that the correct interpretation of "those who stay behind" is what Ibn Abbas said. As for what Qatada said, that it means the women, it has no sense, because the Arabs do not form the plural of women alone, with no men among them, with the "y" and "n" or the "w" and "n" ending; had women been meant it would have said "with the stay-behind women" in the feminine plural, but its sense is what we said, that it means sit with the sick men, those with disabilities, the weak among them and the women, and when men and women are joined in a statement the Arabs let the masculine prevail over the feminine, hence "with those who stay behind". Had the meaning been turned to "sit with the corrupt", from the saying that a man "stayed behind from his family" in the sense of becoming corrupt, and "he is a bad successor", it would have been a possible reading; and its root, if that sense is meant, is from the saying that milk "stayed behind" when it spoiled from long keeping in the skin until it went bad, and that the mouth of a fasting person "stayed behind" when its smell changed. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the root. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 405}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
