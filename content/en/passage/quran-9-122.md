**Commentary on verse 122 (pp. 565-568)**

Al-Tabari says: the believers were not to go out all together; the editor refers to earlier discussion of "all together" and of going out. Then the interpreters differed over what God meant by this verse and what going out He disliked for all the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 565}

Some said it refers to a group among people in the desert whom the Messenger had sent to teach people Islam; when the verse "it was not for the people of Medina" came down, they left the desert for the Prophet, fearing that they were among those who stayed behind from him and whom the verse meant, so God revealed their excuse with "the believers should not all go out together" and disliked all of them leaving the desert for Medina. Report 17466 is from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: some of the Prophet's companions went out into the desert, found kindness from people and benefit from fertile land, and called those they found to guidance; people said, "We see that you left your companions and came to us," and this troubled them so they all came from the desert to the Prophet, and God said that from every group a party should go out seeking good, to gain understanding and hear what was among the people and what God revealed after them, and to warn their people, all the people. Reports 17467 to 17469 give the same, one noting that some went out and some stayed seeking good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 566}

Others said the meaning is that the believers should not all go out against their enemy and leave the Prophet alone. Report 17470 is from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: not all of them go; rather from every clan and tribe a party goes out and a party stays, so those who stay with the Prophet gain understanding of the religion and warn those who went out when they return. Report 17471 is from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah, Mu'awiya and Ali: the believers should not all go out and leave the Prophet alone; the party means the raiding detachments, which go only with his permission, and when they return after revelation came in their absence, those who stayed have learned it from the Prophet and tell them of the Qur'an revealed after they left; the detachments stay to learn what was revealed and other detachments are sent, and that is "to gain understanding in the religion". The editor notes that the bracketed words are missing from the manuscript and the printed edition's publisher added them, probably from the Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 567}

Report 17472 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: this applies when the Prophet sends armies, telling them not to leave him alone, so a party stays with the Messenger to gain understanding in religion, and a party goes to call its people and warn them of God's blows on those before them. Report 17473 is from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: when the Prophet himself campaigned, no Muslim could stay behind except the excused; when he stayed and sent detachments, they could not go without his leave; when Qur'an came after a detachment left, he recited it to his companions who stayed, and when the detachment returned, those who stayed told them God had revealed Qur'an after they left, and taught them and gave them understanding of the religion; so the Muslims should not all go out while the Prophet is sitting, but when he sits, detachments go out and most people stay with him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 568}

Others said the meaning is that those who went out were not believers; had they been believers, not all of them would have gone out, but they were hypocrites; had they been truthful about their belief, some would have gone to gain understanding in the religion and warn their people on return; the accounts continue on the next page. The editor notes differences between the manuscript, printed edition and Durr al-Manthur in the wording "and they teach". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 568}

We stop here at the foot of p. 568, in the commentary on verse 122 (at the view that those who went out were hypocrites); the next batch begins at p. 569. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 568}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
