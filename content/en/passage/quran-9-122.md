## Summary
Verse 122 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 122 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 122 (pp. 565-574)**

Al-Tabari says: the believers were not to go out all together; the editor refers to earlier discussion of "all together" and of going out. Then the interpreters differed over what God meant by this verse and what going out He disliked for all the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 565}

Some said it refers to a group among people in the desert whom the Messenger had sent to teach people Islam; when the verse "it was not for the people of Medina" came down, they left the desert for the Prophet, fearing that they were among those who stayed behind from him and whom the verse meant, so God revealed their excuse with "the believers should not all go out together" and disliked all of them leaving the desert for Medina. Report 17466 is from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: some of the Prophet's companions went out into the desert, found kindness from people and benefit from fertile land, and called those they found to guidance; people said, "We see that you left your companions and came to us," and this troubled them so they all came from the desert to the Prophet, and God said that from every group a party should go out seeking good, to gain understanding and hear what was among the people and what God revealed after them, and to warn their people, all the people. Reports 17467 to 17469 give the same, one noting that some went out and some stayed seeking good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 566}

Others said the meaning is that the believers should not all go out against their enemy and leave the Prophet alone. Report 17470 is from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: not all of them go; rather from every clan and tribe a party goes out and a party stays, so those who stay with the Prophet gain understanding of the religion and warn those who went out when they return. Report 17471 is from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah, Mu'awiya and Ali: the believers should not all go out and leave the Prophet alone; the party means the raiding detachments, which go only with his permission, and when they return after revelation came in their absence, those who stayed have learned it from the Prophet and tell them of the Qur'an revealed after they left; the detachments stay to learn what was revealed and other detachments are sent, and that is "to gain understanding in the religion". The editor notes that the bracketed words are missing from the manuscript and the printed edition's publisher added them, probably from the Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 567}

Report 17472 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: this applies when the Prophet sends armies, telling them not to leave him alone, so a party stays with the Messenger to gain understanding in religion, and a party goes to call its people and warn them of God's blows on those before them. Report 17473 is from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: when the Prophet himself campaigned, no Muslim could stay behind except the excused; when he stayed and sent detachments, they could not go without his leave; when Qur'an came after a detachment left, he recited it to his companions who stayed, and when the detachment returned, those who stayed told them God had revealed Qur'an after they left, and taught them and gave them understanding of the religion; so the Muslims should not all go out while the Prophet is sitting, but when he sits, detachments go out and most people stay with him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 568}

Others said the meaning is that those who went out were not believers; had they been believers, not all of them would have gone out, but they were hypocrites; had they been truthful about their belief, some would have gone to gain understanding in the religion and warn their people on return; the accounts continue on the next page. The editor notes differences between the manuscript, printed edition and Durr al-Manthur in the wording "and they teach". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 568}

Report 17474 is from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: this verse is not about jihad; when the Messenger of God prayed against Mudar for years of drought, their land dried up and whole tribes came together to Medina in hardship, pretending to Islam while lying, burdening the Prophet's companions; God revealed to His Messenger that they were not believers, and he sent them back to their clans and warned their people not to do as they did; hence "to warn their people". Report 17475 is a third view from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father and uncle: a band from each Arab clan used to come to the Prophet to ask about their religion and gain understanding, asking what he would have them do and say to their clans; he told them to obey God and His Messenger and sent them to their people with prayer and almsgiving; when they reached their people they called that whoever embraced Islam was one of them, warned them, invited them to Islam and gave them good news of Paradise. The editor notes that a sentence in the manuscript and printed text is oddly ordered and he fears something has dropped from it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 569}

Others said this is God's refutation of hypocrites who disparaged Muslim Bedouin and others for staying behind from the Messenger, who were people God had excused. Report 17476 is from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and Sulayman al-Ahwal: when "it was not for the people of Medina" was revealed, some hypocrites said that those who stayed behind were ruined, and "the believers should not all go out together" was revealed, as was verse 16 of Surat al-Shura. Report 17477 is from him through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, Ibn Uyayna and Sulayman al-Ahwal: when "if you do not go out He will punish you with a painful punishment" in verse 39 came down, then "it was not for the people of Medina", the hypocrites said the desert people who stayed behind from Muhammad and did not go out with him were ruined, though some of his companions had gone out to the desert to their people to teach them; so God revealed "the believers should not all go out together". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 570}

Those who said the verse forbids everyone going out in a detachment and leaving the Prophet alone differed on who is meant by "to gain understanding in religion and warn their people when they return". Some said it means the group left behind with the Messenger: "why does not a party from every group go out for jihad, so those who stay gain understanding and warn their people, the ones who went out in the detachment, when they return from their campaign", which is Qatada's view. Report 17478 is from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: those who sat with the Prophet gain understanding, and warn those who went out when they return; report 17479 is from al-Hasan and Qatada: not all, leaving the Prophet. Others said the meaning is that the party which goes out gains the understanding, and warns the stay-behinds; report 17480 is from al-Hasan: those who went out gain understanding through what God shows them of victory and help against the polytheists, and warn their people on return. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 571}

Al-Tabari says the soundest view is that the meaning is that the believers should not all go out and leave the Messenger alone: God forbids by this verse the believers going out for campaign, jihad or other business and leaving the Messenger alone, and they must, when he sends a detachment, send with it from every Arab tribe a party, which can be from one person to any number; this is so far one of the views reported from Ibn Abbas and is the view of al-Dahhak and Qatada. It is preferable because God had forbidden staying behind from the Messenger without excuse when he went out to fight, then followed with "the believers should not all go out", so it is clear that after informing them of what is obligatory and permitted on his campaign, He informs them of their duty when he stays in Medina and others go out. The editor refers to earlier discussion of the group, the party and "why not". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 572}

As for "to gain understanding in religion and warn their people when they return", the soundest view is that the party who go out gain understanding by seeing God's help to people of His religion and the Messenger's companions over His enemies, so that one who lacked it learns by sight the truth of Islam and its victory over the religions, and warn their people of God's might coming on them like what they saw fall on the polytheists the Muslims defeated, that they may take heed and believe. This is the view of al-Hasan al-Basri, since "going out", used without a qualifier, mostly means jihad and campaigning in Arab usage, and "to gain understanding" is a condition of going out, since it follows it. If asked why it cannot mean that those who stayed behind gain understanding, he answers it is impossible, for if the going out of the one party caused the understanding of the other, their staying would have caused ignorance, though staying would not have prevented their understanding. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 573}

Besides, "and to warn their people when they return" is joined to "to gain understanding"; the party which went out had already received God's warning, so what would the staying party's warning of the going party mean when both knew God's warning equally? If one could be described as warning the other, it would be the going party, which saw God's power and the believers' help that the staying party did not. Rather it warns its own kin and tribe who have not believed, when it returns, that what befell their like among the polytheists whom God let the believers defeat may befall them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 574}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
