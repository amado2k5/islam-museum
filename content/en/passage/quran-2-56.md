## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "then We raised you up after your death, that you might give thanks" al-Tabari says "ba'athnakum" means We brought you back to life; "ba'th" originally means stirring a thing from its place, hence the Day of Resurrection when people are stirred from their graves, and he cites a line of verse. The sense is that He did it so that they might thank Him for restoring them and sparing them, to return to repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 84} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 85}
He records al-Suddi's view that it means "We raised you up as prophets", by treating the clause as moved from its place, and rejects it because the apparent wording indicates otherwise and the commentators agree in finding it mistaken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 85}

On why they said "we will not believe you until we see God openly", al-Tabari gives Ibn Ishaq: Moses chose seventy of their best to repent to God for what had been done; when his Lord spoke to him and they heard His voice commanding and forbidding, they said they would not believe until they saw God openly, and the quake took them and they died, until Moses pleaded and God returned their souls. From al-Suddi: they said it and the thunderbolt took them; God revived them and they asked Moses to pray for them, and God made them prophets, al-Suddi holding that the verse puts one thing before another. From Ibn Zayd: they refused to take the tablets until they saw God openly, a thunderbolt struck them after their repentance and then God revived them, and when they still refused He raised the mountain over them. Qatada and al-Rabi' say they were raised to complete the rest of their appointed terms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 86} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 87} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 88} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 89}
Al-Tabari comments that he has no report of sound standing on the reason for which a proof can be based, though some of it may be true; God reports their saying only to rebuke the people addressed for denying Muhammad, and that proof is established without knowing the reason. The editor notes that al-Tabari uses such reports only to clarify some meanings, though they are not proof in commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 89} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 90}
