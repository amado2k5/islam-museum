## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "then We raised you up after your death, that you might give thanks" al-Tabari says "ba'athnakum" means We brought you back to life; "ba'th" originally means stirring a thing from its place, hence the Day of Resurrection when people are stirred from their graves, and he cites a line of verse. The sense is that He did it so that they might thank Him for restoring them and sparing them, to return to repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 84} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 85}
He records al-Suddi's view that it means "We raised you up as prophets", by treating the clause as moved from its place, and rejects it because the apparent wording indicates otherwise and the commentators agree in finding it mistaken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 85}
