## In al-Tabari's commentary: "they seek to deceive God and those who believe"
Al-Tabari explains the hypocrite's deception of his Lord and the believers as showing by his tongue affirmation contrary to the doubt and denial in his heart, to ward off God's ruling of death and captivity. To the objection that one who hides his belief only out of caution is not really deceiving, he replies that the Arabs call a person who gives with his tongue what differs from his inner thought, to escape what he fears, a deceiver of the one he escapes from. He adds that the hypocrite also deceives himself, thinking he does it good while leading it to punishment, which he takes to be the sense of "they deceive only themselves, though they do not perceive". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 272} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 273}

He cites Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd that the hypocrites deceive God, His messenger and the believers by showing that they believe. Al-Tabari sees in the verse among the clearest proofs against those who claim God punishes only one who disbelieves out of obstinacy after knowing, since God reports that they do not perceive their error and yet a painful punishment awaits them for their denial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 273} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 274}
He takes up the "mufa'ala" form: one language authority (he means Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an) said "yukhadi'u" means "yakhda'u", like "qatalaka Allah", but al-Tabari disagrees and holds it is a reciprocal form between two parties: the hypocrite deceives God by the falsehood of his tongue and God deceives him by abandoning him from the right insight to what saves him in the next life, citing verses of Al Imran and al-Hadid. He also mentions a view of some Basran grammarians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 274} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 275}
He says it is wrong to say they deceived the believers, since that would grant them a real deception; one says rather that they sought to deceive and deceived only themselves, because the believers took no property or family from them that their deceit then recovered. So he prefers the reading "wa-ma yakhda'una illa anfusahum" to "yukhadi'una", since God affirmed their deceiving at the start of the verse and does not then deny it. He glosses "and they do not perceive" as "they do not know". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 275} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 276} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 277}
