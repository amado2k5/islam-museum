## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 37 to 38 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "And whoever turns away from the remembrance of the Most Merciful, he will have a companion for him": Whoever turns away from the remembrance of Allah without fearing His authority or fear His punishment, "He will have a devil opposite to him, so he will have a companion." He means: We will make for him a devil to mislead him, so he will have a companion: he says: He is a companion for Satan, meaning he becomes like that. The origin of 'al-'ishw' is looking at something other than a confirmed cause in the eye. It is said: 'So-and-so will nest, they will live, and they will live.' When his eyesight weakens and his eyes darken, it is as if he is covered with a veil, as the poet said: 'When you come to him to sit by the light of his fire, you find abundant wood and a blazing fire,' meaning: when you are in need, you will come to him to help you. As for if the sight is gone and he cannot see, it is said about him: 'So-and-so 'asha' 'isha 'asha minus', and from this is the saying of al-A'sha: 'I saw a man absent among the newcomers, of different characters, 'asha' and blind, from which it is said: 'a man who is 'a'sha' and a woman is 'ashwa'. In the maqta', he quotes from Qatadah, al-Suddi, and Ibn Zayd.
