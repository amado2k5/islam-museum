## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "Wealth and children are the adornment of worldly life, and the lasting good deeds are better in reward and better hope with your Lord": wealth and children, O people, which are the pride of their eyes and the bald, and they boast of them before Salman, Khabbab, and Suhaib, for what adorns themselves in this worldly life, and they are not counted among the Hereafter. "And the lasting good deeds are better with your Lord as a reward." He says: What Salman, Khabbab, and Suhaib do in obedience to Allah, and their supplication to their Lord morning and evening, seeking His Face, is what remains for them of righteous deeds after the end of worldly life. What is best, O Muhammad, Your Lord is a reward of wealth and children that these polytheists boast about, which perish, leaving no hope left for their people. "And the best hope" means: What is hoped for from that is Salman, Suhayb, and Khabbab, better than what Uyaynah and Al-Qara hope for from their wealth and children. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Al-Hasan, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Al-A'mash, Ibrahim, and Sufyan.
