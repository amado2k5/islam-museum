## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "O Children of Israel" al-Tabari says those meant are the descendants of Jacob son of Isaac son of Abraham. Jacob was called Israel, meaning God's servant and chosen one, "il" being God and "isra" the servant, as in Jibril. He reports from Ibn Abbas that Israel is like saying "servant of God", and from Abd Allah ibn al-Harith that "il" is God in Hebrew; the editor vouches for their narrators. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 553}
He holds that the address is to the Jewish rabbis who lived around the Prophet's place of emigration, named after Jacob as Adam's descendants are named after Adam. He says they are singled out in these verses because the proofs in them concern the reports and stories of their forebears, matters of which they in particular have knowledge, so that Muhammad's being informed of them, though his people were far from knowing them and he had little dealing with the books containing them, shows he reached them only by revelation. He reports from Ibn Abbas that it means "People of the Book", the rabbis of the Jews. The editor adds a long note on the phrase "little dealing" and stresses that al-Tabari does not mean the Prophet studied the People of the Book's writings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 553} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 554} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 555}
On "remember My favour which I bestowed upon you" he says the favour was His choosing messengers from among them, sending them books, rescuing them from Pharaoh and his people, establishing them in the land, making springs gush from the rock and feeding them manna and quails; and that He told their descendants to keep in mind what He did for their forefathers, lest the punishments fall on them that fell on those who forgot and denied His favour. He reports from Ibn Abbas that it means His blessings on them and their forefathers when He saved them from Pharaoh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 555}

Al-Tabari continues the reports on "remember My favour": from Abu al-Aliya, that He made prophets and messengers from among them and sent down books; from Mujahid, making the rock gush, sending manna and quails and saving them from bondage to Pharaoh's people; and from Ibn Zayd, that His favours are general and none is greater than Islam. He sets their reminding by Muhammad beside Moses's reminding of his people in surah al-Ma'ida. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 555} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 556}
On "fulfil My covenant and I shall fulfil your covenant" he says the covenant here is God's covenant and charge which He took from the Children of Israel in the Torah: to make known to people Muhammad's mission, finding it written with them, and to believe in him and in what he brought; and His covenant to them was to admit them to Paradise if they did so, citing verses of surah al-Ma'ida and surah al-A'raf. He reports from Ibn Abbas that it is the covenant laid on their necks concerning the Prophet when he came, and that His fulfilling is carrying out what He promised for believing in him, the lifting of the burdens and fetters. From Abu al-Aliya: His covenant is the religion of Islam, and "your covenant" is Paradise; al-Suddi says the same; from Ibn Jurayj it is the pledge taken from them in al-Ma'ida; from al-Dahhak through Ibn Abbas the sense is to keep what He commanded and forbade, regarding the Prophet and otherwise, and He will be pleased with them and admit them to Paradise; and from Ibn Zayd it is the covenant of surah al-Tawba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 557} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 558} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 559}
On "and Me alone, be in awe of Me" he says the sense is to fear and be mindful of Him, addressed to those among the Children of Israel who neglected His covenant and denied His messenger, lest He bring on them what He brought on their forebears who opposed His command, unless they repent by following him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 559}
