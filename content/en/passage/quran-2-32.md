## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "they said, Glory to You, we have no knowledge except what You taught us" al-Tabari holds that once the angels saw where they had erred they returned to God in repentance and admitted their limits, as Noah did in surah Hud after being reproved, which he calls the way of everyone guided to the truth. He reports from Ibn Abbas, through al-Dahhak, that "Glory to You" declares God above anyone else knowing the unseen, and "except what You taught us" disclaims knowledge of the unseen apart from what Adam was taught. He says "subhan" is a verbal noun that is not conjugated, meaning "we glorify You". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 491} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 492} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 495}
On "You are the Knowing, the Wise" he says that, having denied any knowledge of their own beyond what they were taught, they affirmed for their Lord knowledge of all that has been and will be, without being taught. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 495}
Al-Tabari regards these three verses as an argument for the Prophet against the Jews of Medina, since he was told of matters of the unseen that can be known only by report, and as a sign that anyone who claims knowledge of the unseen without proof, such as soothsayers, augurs and astrologers, speaks falsely. He also says the verses remind the People of the Book of God's favours to their forefathers and warn them against persisting, as happened to Iblis. The editor explains these terms in a note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 494} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 495}
