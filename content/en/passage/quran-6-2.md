## Summary
Verse 2 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 2 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

By "he it is who created you from clay" al-Tabari means that God who created the heavens and the earth, darkened their night and lit their day, and was then disbelieved by the unbelievers despite his favour to them, who held equal to him what neither helps nor harms them, is the one who created you, people, from clay; he means that people are the children of him whom he created from clay, so he put it in the form of an address to them since they are his offspring. The interpreters said the like: report 13049, from Qatada: the beginning of creation, God created Adam from clay. 13050, from Mujahid: it is Adam; 13051, from al-Suddi: Adam. 13052, from al-Dahhak: Adam was created from clay and people from a drop of despised water. 13053, from Ibn Zayd: Adam was created from clay, then we were created from Adam when we were taken from his back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 255}

The editor says the printed text has "so he disbelieved in him" and that in the manuscript "then" is joined to the word "disbelieved" as some copyists do, so he gave the wording the sense requires.

Then "then he decreed a term, and a fixed term with him": the interpreters differed on this. Some said "then he decreed a term" means he decreed a term for you, people, from your creation until you die, "and a fixed term with him" from your death until you are raised. Report 13054, from al-Hasan, says so; 13055, from Qatada: the term of your life until you die and the term of your death until you are raised, so you are between two terms from God. Report 13056, from al-Dahhak: he decreed the term of death, and every soul's term is death; he said, God will not put back a soul... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 256}

The editor identifies Waki' ibn al-Jarrah al-Ru'asi and his father, and Abu Bakr al-Hudhali, whose name is disputed and who is weak.

The rest of 13056: God will not put back a soul when its term comes, "and a fixed term with him" meaning the term of the Hour, the passing of the world and arrival with God. Others said it means: then he decreed this world, and with him is the next. Report 13057, from Ibn Abbas: "a term" is this world, "a fixed term with him" the next. Reports 13058, 13059 and 13060, from Mujahid: "decreed a term" is the next world with him, "a fixed term" this world. Reports 13061 and 13064, from Qatada and al-Hasan: he decreed the term of this world, from your creation until you die, "and a fixed term with him" the day of resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 257}

Report 13062, from Mujahid and Ikrima: he decreed the term of this world and the fixed term with him is the term of the raising. Report 13063, from them: "decreed a term" is death, "a fixed term with him" the next world. 13065, from Mujahid: the term of this world, and the raising. 13066, from Ibn Abbas: "decreed a term" is the term of death, "the fixed term" the term of the Hour and standing before God. 13067, from al-Suddi: "decreed a term" the term of death, "a fixed term with him" the day of resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 258}

Others said otherwise: report 13068, from Ibn Abbas: "decreed a term" is sleep, in which the soul is taken and then returns to its owner on waking, and "a fixed term with him" the term of a person's death. Report 13069, from Ibn Zayd: Adam was created from clay, then we were created from Adam, taken from his back, and then the term and the covenant were taken together in one fixed term in this present life. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 259}

Al-Tabari holds the soundest to be the saying: then he decreed the term of life in this world, "and a fixed term with him" is the term of the raising with him. For God pointed his creatures to where his argument against them lies in themselves, saying to them: people, the one to whom your unbelievers make gods and rivals equal is he who created you, began you and made you from clay, forming you as living bodies after you were lifeless clay, then decreed the terms of your life ending in your passing and death, to return you to dust and clay as you were before he made you, "and a fixed term with him" to return you as living bodies as you were before your death; that is like [[passage/quran-2-28|2:28]] on death, giving life, and returning to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 259}

The editor refers for "term" and "fixed" to earlier volumes 8, 6 and 5.

Then "yet you doubt": yet you doubt the power of him who was able to create the heavens and the earth, darken the night and light the day, and create you from clay until he made you as you are, to make you anew after your death and passing and to bring you into being after you were nothing. "Miriya" in Arab speech is doubt, as he showed earlier with evidence. Report 13070, from Ibn Zayd: "tamtarun" is doubt, and he recited [[passage/quran-11-17|11:17]], "in doubt about it"; 13071, from al-Suddi, the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 260}

The editor says the printed text added a "wa" in "and on his creating you", which is a plain error, since "on his creating you" attaches to "you doubt the power of him who was able", and refers for "doubting" to volumes 6 and 3.

Verse 2 closes here at p. 260, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-3|6:3]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
