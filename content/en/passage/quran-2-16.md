## In al-Tabari's commentary: "they bought error at the price of guidance"
Al-Tabari raises the difficulty: how did the hypocrites buy error with guidance when they had never held guidance before? He then sets out the views: Ibn Abbas, disbelief for belief; al-Suddi, they took error and left guidance; Qatada, they preferred error to guidance; Mujahid, they believed and then disbelieved. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 311} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 312}
He notes that those who gloss "ishtaraw" as "preferred" cite the verse of Fussilat about Thamud, the interchange of "ba" and "ala", and the Arab use "I bought this over that" meaning "I chose it", with lines by al-A'sha and Dhu al-Rumma. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 313} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 314}
Al-Tabari does not choose this reading, though he allows it is a possible one, because God says "so their trade profited them not", which points to buying in its ordinary sense of exchanging one thing for another. As for those who say they had believed and then disbelieved, he holds that the opening verses describing them show they never took light from belief nor entered the religion of Islam, since God describes them as showing falsehood with their tongues. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 314}
