## In al-Tabari's commentary: "they bought error at the price of guidance"
Al-Tabari raises the difficulty: how did the hypocrites buy error with guidance when they had never held guidance before? He then sets out the views: Ibn Abbas, disbelief for belief; al-Suddi, they took error and left guidance; Qatada, they preferred error to guidance; Mujahid, they believed and then disbelieved. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 311} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 312}
He notes that those who gloss "ishtaraw" as "preferred" cite the verse of Fussilat about Thamud, the interchange of "ba" and "ala", and the Arab use "I bought this over that" meaning "I chose it", with lines by al-A'sha and Dhu al-Rumma. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 313} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 314}
Al-Tabari does not choose this reading, though he allows it is a possible one, because God says "so their trade profited them not", which points to buying in its ordinary sense of exchanging one thing for another. As for those who say they had believed and then disbelieved, he holds that the opening verses describing them show they never took light from belief nor entered the religion of Islam, since God describes them as showing falsehood with their tongues. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 314}

Al-Tabari adds that it cannot be conceded to those who take the word "ishtaraw" as proof that they had believed and then disbelieved, since a word that allows several senses is not turned to one of them without proof. He prefers what he reports from Ibn Abbas and Ibn Masud, that they took error and left guidance, because every disbeliever exchanges belief for disbelief, citing the verse of al-Baqara "whoever exchanges belief for disbelief has strayed from the right way". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 314} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 315}
He explains "so their trade profited them not" as meaning they lost and did not gain, having exchanged bewilderment for right guidance, error for guidance, and fear for safety, along with the painful punishment prepared for them; he gives Qatada that they went out from guidance to error, from the community to division, from security to fear, and from the sunna to innovation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 315} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 316}
To the question of how profit is attributed to trade, he replies that the Arabs speak in such terms as "your effort has failed" and "your night slept", so profit is ascribed to trade while meaning they gained nothing in their trading, and he cites lines of al-Hutay'a, Ru'ba and Jarir. He glosses "and they were not guided" as that they were not right-minded in choosing error over guidance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 316} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 317}
