## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "On the Day every soul will come to argue about itself, and every soul will be compensated for what it did, and they will not be wronged": Your Lord after that will be forgiving and merciful. "On the Day every soul will come," it will dispute for itself, and it will argue against it for what it previously did in this world, whether good or evil, faith, or disbelief. "And every soul will be compensated for what it did" in this world, whether in obedience or disobedience. "And they are not wronged": He means: they are only treated with what they deserve and deserve for what they have done, whether good or bad. The doer of good is only rewarded with kindness, and the wrongdoer is only rewarded for what was previously done wrong. A doer of good is not punished, nor is the reward of his kindness diminished, and the wrongdoer is rewarded only with the reward of his deed. The Arabs differed regarding the reason for which it was said "to argue" and feminized all. Some grammarians from Basra said: This is said because the meaning of every soul is every person, and feminine because the soul is masculine and feminine. It is said: I have never received one soul and one soul.
