## Summary
Verse 76 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 76 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "when the night covered him he saw a star and said: this is my Lord; and when it set he said: I do not love those that set" al-Tabari says: when night covered him and hid him. One says "janna alayhi al-layl", "jannahu al-layl", "ajannahu" and "ajanna alayhi"; when you drop "alā" the speech with the alif is more eloquent than without it: "ajannahu al-layl" is more eloquent than "ajanna alayhi", and "janna alayhi al-layl" more eloquent than "jannahu"; all are accepted and heard from the Arabs, "jannahu al-layl" among Asad and "ajannahu" and "jannahu" among Tamim; the verbal noun of "janna alayhi" is "jannan", "junun" and "janan" and of "ajanna" is "ijnan"; one says "so-and-so came in the jinn of the night". The jinn are so called because they are hidden from the eyes of the sons of Adam and are not seen, and anything that hides from people's sight the Arabs say "has been jinn-ed". He cites a line by a Hudhali poet describing a water he came to just before sleep, covered by the dark of the end of the night, and a line of Ubayd describing a vast fearful desert in which owls cry with the echo when the night covers it; from it also "I covered the dead" when you hide him in the niche, like "janantu" the night in the sense of covering it; and the shield is called "mijann" because it covers whoever takes cover in it and hides him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 478}

The editor says the printed text has a different wording, the manuscript being right, that this explanation is not found in the dictionaries, and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an; that the first form is better known in Asad's speech and the second in Tamim's; that the Hudhali poet is al-Buraiq al-Hudhali, Iyad ibn Khuwaylid, though al-Asma'i transmitted the line for Amir ibn Sudus al-Khuna'i, the line being from verses in which he glorifies himself; that Ubayd's line is from his diwan, that a "kharq" is a wide desert, that the diwan's reading is "the owl cries" but Abu Jafar's is better since "al-sada" is also a male owl; and ends by noting that the poem says a man longs for life and its length while in length of life is the hardest torment.

"He saw a star" says he saw a star as it rose, "said: this is my Lord"; 13462, from Ibn Abbas: by "we show Abraham the dominion of the heavens and the earth" he means the sun, moon and stars, "when the night covered him he saw a star and said this is my Lord", so he worshipped it until it set, and when it set he said I do not love those that set; "when he saw the moon rising he said this is my Lord", so he worshipped it until it set, and when it set he said if my Lord does not guide me I shall be among the people who go astray; "when he saw the sun rising he said this is my Lord, this is greater", so he worshipped it until it set, and when it set he said: my people, I am free of what you associate. 13463, from Qatada: he knew that his Lord is lasting and does not pass away; then he read till he reached "this is my Lord, this is greater", he saw a creation greater than the first two and brighter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 480}

The reason for Abraham's saying that is what Muhammad ibn Ishaq related (13464), as we were told, God knows best: that Azar was a man of Kutha, a village in the Sawad of Kufa, and the king of the east at that time was Nimrod. When God wished to send Abraham as proof against his people and messenger to his servants, and there had been no prophet between Noah and Abraham but Hud and Salih, then as Abraham's time came near the people of the stars came to Nimrod and said: know that we find in our knowledge that a boy will be born in this village of yours called Abraham, who will part from your religion and break your idols, in such a month of such a year. When the year the star people described began, Nimrod sent for every pregnant woman in his village and held her with him, except Abraham's mother, the wife of Azar, whose pregnancy he did not know of, since she was a young woman who, as it is said, had not known the pregnancy in her womb, and since God willed what he willed for her child; he wanted to kill every boy born in that month of that year for fear for his kingdom, and so no woman bore a boy in that month of that year without his order that he be slaughtered. When Abraham's mother felt labour she went out at night to a cave near her and bore Abraham in it, did for him what is done for a newborn, then closed the cave on him and went back home; then she would look in on him in the cave to see what he did and find him alive sucking his thumb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 481}

The editor says the printed text has "Nimrod ibn Canaan" which is not in the manuscript or in al-Tabari's history, where it is "Nimrod the sinner", called the Hasir, whose kingdom, they claim, encompassed the east and west of the earth; that Abu Jafar abridged the report here as usual and split it in his history; that the bracketed addition in the manuscript is from his history; that "ta'allam" is an imperative meaning "know" and common in Ibn Ishaq's Sira; that "hadatha" means young and small, "hadba" in print being a plain error; and that the sentence "and for what God willed" needs its dividing phrase set off between dashes.

They claim, God knows best, that God put Abraham's provision in it and what came to him from sucking. Azar, they claim, asked Abraham's mother about her pregnancy, and she said she bore a boy and he died; he believed her and was silent. A day on Abraham in his youth, they say, was like a month and a month like a year. He stayed in the cave only fifteen months before he said to his mother: take me out to look. She took him out at evening and he looked and thought about the creation of the heavens and the earth and said: the one who created me, provided for me, fed me and gave me drink is my Lord, I have no god but him; then he looked at the sky and saw a star and said this is my Lord, then followed it with his eyes until it set, and when it set he said I do not love those that set; then the moon rose and he saw it rising and said this is my Lord, followed it with his sight until it set and when it set said if my Lord does not guide me I shall be among the people who go astray. When day came on him and the sun rose he thought the sun great and saw something of greater light than anything he had seen before, and said this is my Lord, this is greater; when it set he said: my people, I am free of what you associate; I have turned my face to him who originated the heavens and the earth, as one upright, and I am not of the polytheists, the rest of which is in [[passage/quran-6-77|6:77]], [[passage/quran-6-78|6:78]] and [[passage/quran-6-79|6:79]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 482}

Then Abraham went back to his father Azar, with his direction straight, knowing his Lord and free of his people's religion, though he did not declare it to them. He told him he was his son, Abraham's mother told him he was his son and told him what she had done for him, and Azar was pleased and rejoiced greatly. Azar used to make the idols his people worshipped and give them to Abraham to sell, and Abraham, they say, went with them and said: who will buy what neither harms nor benefits him? and no one bought from him. When they did not sell he took them to a river, put their heads down in it and said: drink, mocking his people and the error they were in, until his faulting them and mocking them spread among his people and the people of his village, without it reaching King Nimrod. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 483}

The editor says that earlier the printed text has "what is done with the newborn", the publisher trying to mend Abu Jafar's speech to the weakness of his own Arabic, the right form being from the manuscript and the history; that "a'zama al-shams" means he thought it great and found it vast, which is right, the history having "he saw the vastness of the sun", also right; that "barat" is as in the history, the manuscript having an unpointed word, and that "sawwaba ru'usaha" means turned them down; and that Abu Jafar relates it at length in his history.

Abu Jafar said: a group not of the people of transmission denied this statement reported from Ibn Abbas and those who related from him, that Abraham said to the star or the moon "this is my Lord", and said it is not allowed that God have a prophet sent with the message of whom a time came, once adult, in which he was not a monotheist, knowing God and free of all that is worshipped beside him. They said that were it allowed that a time came when he disbelieved in God, it would not be allowed that God single him out for the message, since there is no meaning in him that is not in other unbelievers like him, and there is no kinship between God and any of his creatures that he should favour him with honour; they said God honoured whom he honoured among them for his excellence in himself and rewarded him as he deserved the reward. They claimed that God's report of Abraham's saying, on seeing the star, moon or sun, "this is my Lord", was not out of ignorance that this could not be his Lord, but was said in the way of denial that it be his Lord and of faulting his people for worshipping idols, since the star, moon and sun are brighter, finer and more brilliant than idols and yet were not worshipped, and set and passed away without lasting, so idols, which are less in beauty and smaller in body, are more fitting not to be worshipped {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 483}

We stop here at the foot of p. 483, in the middle of al-Tabari's presentation of the argument of those who denied that Abraham said "this is my Lord" in earnest, and he continues on p. 484.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
