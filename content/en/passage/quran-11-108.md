**Commentary on verse 108 (pp. 486-488)**

On [[passage/quran-11-108|11:108]] the readers differ over 'su'idu': most readers of Medina, the Hijaz and Basra and some Kufans read it with fatha on the sin, 'sa'idu', and a group of Kufans with damma, meaning they were granted happiness. Al-Tabari says: both are known readings and whichever the reader takes is right. If someone asks, how is 'su'idu' said in the passive when one does not say of the active 'God made him happy' but 'asadahu Allah'? The answer is that it resembles 'majnun' and 'mahbub' in the passive, and when the agent is named one says 'ajannahu Allah' and 'ahabbahu'; the Arabs do this often. The meaning is: those made happy by God's mercy are in Paradise, abiding in it as long as the heavens and the earth endure, that is, for ever, 'except as your Lord wills'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 486}

The interpreters differ on that: some said 'except as your Lord wills' is the time they stayed in the Fire before entering Paradise, concerning believers brought out of the Fire and entered into Paradise, and report 18583, from al-Dahhak, says so. Others said it means except for what your Lord wills in addition to the length of the heavens and earth, which is abiding in it for ever; report 18584, from Abu Sinan: His will is their abiding in it, and then He followed it with 'a gift never cut off'. The grammarians differ on the exception here: one group said there are two senses, one that He makes an exception He does not carry out, like saying by God I will strike you unless I see otherwise, while you are resolved to strike him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 487}

The other is that when the Arabs except much together with the like of it or more, 'illa' has the same sense as 'and', so the meaning is abiding in it as long as the heavens and the earth endure, besides what He adds to them of abiding and eternity, like saying you owe me a thousand except two thousand, meaning besides the two thousand; he said this is the likelier of the two to him, since God does not break His promise, and the exception is joined to 'a gift never cut off'. Another of them said much the same and allowed a third sense, that the exception is of their abiding in Paradise by their being held back from it between death and resurrection, which is the barzakh, after which comes abiding for ever. Another said it may be that the endurance of the heavens and earth means eternity as the Arabs use it, and the will is excepted from their endurance. The rest of the grammarians' discussion is on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 488}
