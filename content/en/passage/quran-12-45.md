**Commentary on verse 45 (pp. 119-122)**

On "and the one of the two who was saved said, remembering after a time" al-Tabari says: the one saved from being killed, of the prison's two companions who asked Joseph to interpret, "remembered", that is recalled what he had forgotten of Joseph's affair and request to the king, which Joseph asked him to mention with the words "mention me to your lord", "after a time" meaning after a while. Reports 19339 to 19353, from Ibn Abbas, Abu Razin, al-Hasan, Mujahid, Ibn Kathir, al-Suddi and Ikrima: after a while, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Asim, Abu Kurayb, Waki', Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash, Abu Nu'aym, Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Affan, Ibn Jurayj and others; one report from Ibn Abbas has after years, and one from Ikrima after a span of time. Al-Tabari says this interpretation goes with the reading with a damma on the alif and a doubled mim, the reading of the readers of the Muslim cities. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 119}

It is reported of a number of early scholars that they read it with a fat-ha on the alif and the mim lightened, meaning after forgetting, and some said the Arabs say of a man that he "forgot" with that verb, and those who read so interpret it so. Reports 19354 to 19361, from Ibn Abbas, Ikrima and Qatada, that he read it so and explained it as after forgetting, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Affan, Hammam, Ibn Humayd, Bahz ibn Asad, Abu Ghassan Malik ibn al-Khalil al-Yahmadi, Ibn Abi Adi, Abu Harun al-Ghanawi, Ya'qub, Ibn Waki', Ibn Ulayya, Abd al-Wahhab, Sa'id, Bishr, Yazid, Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar. The editor notes that Abu Ghassan Malik ibn al-Khalil al-Yahmadi al-Azdi was a teacher of al-Tabari and that al-Nasa'i and others narrated from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 122}

Report 19362, from Mujahid, that he read "after amah"; reports 19363 and 19364, from al-Dahhak: after forgetting, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad, Abu Marzuq, Juwaybir, Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman. A third reading is reported, report 19365 from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, Sufyan and Humayd: he read it with the mim unvowelled and light, and the one who read so seems to have meant the verbal noun of the verb "to forget", the meaning being like that of those who read it with fat-ha on the alif and the mim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 123}

On "I will tell you its interpretation" he says: I will inform you of its interpretation, "so send me" means release me that I may go and bring you its interpretation from the one who knows it. Something is omitted from the words, left out because what is shown suffices: "so they sent him, and he came to Joseph and said to him: Joseph, O truthful one". Report 19366, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the king said to the chiefs around him what he said and they replied as they did, and Nabu heard it and was asked about its interpretation, so he recalled Joseph and what he had interpreted for him and his companion and how it had come out as he said, and said, I will tell you its interpretation, so send me; he came to him and said, Joseph, the king has seen such and such, and told him the dream, and Joseph said what God has told us in the Book, and its interpretation came to them like the splitting of dawn; Nabu left Joseph with the ruling he gave them and told the king what he had said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 124}

It is also said that the one saved said "send me" because the prison was not in the city; report 19367, from al-Suddi: Ibn Abbas said the prison was not in the city, so the cupbearer went to Joseph and said, give us a ruling on seven fat cows, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 124}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
