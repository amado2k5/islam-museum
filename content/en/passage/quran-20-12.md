## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 12 to 13 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "So when Moses came to it, it was called": When Moses came to the fire, his Lord called out to him: "O Moses, indeed I am your Lord, so take off your sandals." As narrated from Ibn Ishaq, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, who said: Moses went toward it, meaning toward the fire, and found it in a tree of mulberries. Some of the People of the Book say it was in a bush. When he approached, I delayed it, and when he saw its Istikhar, he turned away from it, fearing it in his soul. When he wanted to return, it approached him, then he spoke from the tree. When he heard the sound, he became familiar and said Allah, blessed be He. And the Exalted said: "O Moses, take off your sandals; indeed, you are in the sacred valley of Tuwa." So he took them off and threw them away. Scholars differ on the reason why God commanded Moses to remove his sandals. Some said: He ordered them because they were made from the skin of a dead donkey. He thought of stepping into the sacred valley with them, and wanted to touch it from the valley's pool. In the passage, he quotes from Al-Hasan, Ikrimah, Ibn Jurayj, and Ibn Ishaq.
