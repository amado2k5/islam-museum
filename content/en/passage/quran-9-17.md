**Commentary on verse 17 (pp. 165-166)**

Al-Tabari says: it is not fitting for the polytheists to tend God's mosques while bearing witness against themselves to unbelief; mosques are tended for the worship of God in them, not for unbelief in Him, so one who disbelieves in God has no business tending His mosques. As for their witnessing against themselves to unbelief, report 16552, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat, explains: it is not fitting that they tend them; and the Christian, asked what he is, says Christian, the Jew says Jew, the Sabian says Sabian, and the polytheist says polytheist, which only the Arabs would say. Reports 16553 and 16554, from al-Suddi, say much the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 166}

"Their deeds have come to nothing" means their deeds were voided and their rewards gone, since they were not for God but for Satan, and "in the Fire they will abide" means remaining in it forever, neither alive nor dead. The reciters differed on "God's mosques": most of the reciters of Medina and Kufa read the plural, while some of Mecca and Basra read the singular, meaning the Sacred Mosque; all agree on the plural in the next verse, since read so it can mean one or many, the Arabs sometimes using the singular for the plural and the plural for the singular, as in the saying "he has on him a worn-out garment" in the plural. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 167}
