## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari explains "believe as the people believed" as: affirm Muhammad and what he brought as the believers affirmed it. He gives Ibn Abbas through the al-Dahhak chain that it means affirm as the Companions of Muhammad affirmed, say that he is a prophet and messenger, and affirm the Hereafter and resurrection. He says the article on "al-nas" is the definite article of a known group, since they were people known to those addressed, as in the verse of Al Imran. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 292} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 293}
He defines the safih as the ignorant, weak in judgement and poor at knowing where benefit and harm lie, which is why God calls women and children "safih" in Surat al-Nisa, and most exegetes say these are women and children for the weakness of their judgement. He takes the hypocrites to mean by "the foolish" the Companions of the Prophet, and cites al-Suddi, al-Rabi' ibn Anas, Ibn Zayd and al-Dahhak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 293} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 294}
He reads "truly it is they who are the foolish" as God's statement that the hypocrites are the ignorant in their religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 294}

Al-Tabari sees their being called foolish as folly itself, since a foolish person ruins things where he thinks he is mending them, and so the hypocrite disobeys his Lord where he thinks he is obeying; he gives Ibn Abbas, through the al-Dahhak chain, that the "safih" are the ignorant who do not use their reason. He finds in this verse a further sign against those who say punishment is deserved only by one who is obstinate after knowing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 294} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 295}
