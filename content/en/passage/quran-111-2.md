## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 2 to 6 in a single passage. He interprets the phrase "May the hand of Abu Lahab be forgiven," meaning: I lost the hand of Abu Lahab, and he lost. What he meant by his saying: "May the hand of Abu Lahab be forgiven," is that his deeds were repented. Some Arabic scholars used to say: His saying: "May the hand of Abu Lahab be forgiven," is a prayer from God upon him. As for his saying: "And repent," it is a report. It is mentioned that this is in Abdullah's recitation: "May the hand of Abu Lahab be forgiven, and he has repented." The entry of 'Qad' indicates that it is a predicate, and this is illustrated by the saying to another: 'God destroyed you,' 'He destroyed you,' 'made you righteous,' and 'He made you righteous.' In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd, and Al-A'mash.
