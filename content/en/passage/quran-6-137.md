## Summary
Verse 137 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 137 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 137, "thus their partners made the killing of their children attractive to many idolaters, to ruin them and to confuse their religion for them; had God willed they would not have done it, so leave them with what they invent", al-Tabari says: as the partners of these who set idols and statues equal to their Lord made attractive what they made attractive of their assigning their Lord a share of their property by their claim, and their leaving what reached the gods' share from the share they assigned to God, so their partners from among the devils made attractive to many idolaters the killing of their children, adorning the burying of girls alive for them; "to ruin them" means to destroy them, "to confuse their religion" means to mix up their religion so that it is muddled, and they go astray and are destroyed by doing what God forbade them. Had God willed that they not do what they did of killing, they would not have done it, by His guiding them to the truth and enabling them to be upright; but God abandoned them away from right guidance, so they killed their children and obeyed the devils who misled them. God tells His Prophet, threatening them for their great forgery, "leave them", Muhammad, "and what they invent", meaning the lies and falsehood they ascribe to Me, for I am watching them, and punishment lies ahead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 136}

He relates (13908) from Ibn Abbas: they made the killing of their children attractive to them; (13909) and (13910) from Mujahid: their devils commanding them to bury their children alive for fear of want (the note says "'ayla" means poverty and great need); (13911) from Qatada: their partners made it attractive to them; (13912) from Ibn Zayd: their devils whom they worshipped; and (13913) from al-Suddi: the devils commanded them to kill girls, "to ruin them" means to destroy them and "to confuse their religion" means to muddle it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 137}

The readers differed. The readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read "zayyana ... qatla awladihim shuraka'uhum", with fatha on the za', "qatla" in the accusative and "shuraka'uhum" in the nominative as the one who made it attractive; some readers of Syria read "zuyyina" in the passive, "qatlu" in the nominative, "awladahum" in the accusative and "shuraka'ihim" in the genitive, meaning the killing by their partners of their children was made attractive, separating the possessed noun from its possessor with the object, which in Arabic is ugly and not fluent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 137}

He mentions that a line of poetry was reported from some people of the Hijaz that supports the Syrians' reading, which the poetry transmitters and Arabic scholars of Iraq rejected, the saying "so I thrust it, firmly seated, as the thrust of the young she-camel of Abu Mazada"; the editor's note explains the line: "zajja" is to push with the iron spike at the lower end of a lance and "qalus" is a young she-camel, and says Kufans cite it for the allowing of separation between the possessed noun and possessor by something other than an adverb or preposition, out of poetic necessity, while al-Farra' rejected it in Ma'ani al-Qur'an. Al-Tabari: the reading I do not allow any other than is "zayyana" with fatha, "qatla" in the accusative because "zayyana" falls on it, "awladihim" in the genitive by the annexing of "qatl" to them, and "shuraka'uhum" in the nominative as their act; I said I do not allow another because the authoritative readers agree on it and because the commentators' interpretation came in this form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 138}

Were it not that the interpretation of all the commentators came in that form, and a reader read "zuyyina" in the passive with "al-qatl" in the nominative and "al-awlad" and "al-shuraka'" in the genitive, taking "al-shuraka'" as genitive by apposition to "al-awlad", since the children are the partners of their fathers in lineage and inheritance, that would be allowable (the editor refers to al-Farra'). If a reader read it so but put "al-shuraka'" in the nominative and "al-awlad" in the genitive, as one says "was struck Abd Allah, your brother", with the doer shown after the report has gone by what has no named doer, that too would be sound and allowable in Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 139}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
