## Summary
Verse 137 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 137 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (pp. 228-229).** Abu Ja'far says "patterns" means what passed from God among those before the Companions of Muhammad and the believers in him: the peoples of 'Ad, Thamud, Hud and Lut and other earlier nations. They are exemplary punishments carried out against them and those who denied their prophets, through His giving the deniers respite and drawing them on step by step until the term He had set for the prophets and believers to prevail over them was reached; then He brought His punishment and sent His vengeance on their ground and left them as examples and lessons for those after. The command to travel is addressed to those who thought that giving the polytheists the upper hand over Muhammad and his Companions at Uhud was not a gradual drawing on: they should go through the lands of the earlier nations, see the outcome of their denial and the end of their opposition, and so learn that His giving the polytheists the turn was only a drawing on and a respite until the term is reached; then either their state ends as the earlier nations' did, with hastened punishment, or they turn back to obedience and to following His messenger. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "khala" (4: 289 and 3: 100, 128), explains "sulaf" as a plural of "salaf" (also "aslaf"), the forebears who passed on, notes the printed text read "niqmati" where he kept the manuscript, and says the printed text read "an khilafihim" where the manuscript has an undotted word the first publisher could not read; he glosses "ghibb al-amr" as the outcome and end of a matter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 228}

**The interpreters (pp. 229-231).**
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr, Abbad, al-Hasan (7867): have you not travelled the land and seen how God punished the people of Noah, Lot and Salih and the nations He punished? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 229}
- From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7868): the patterns concern unbelievers and believers, good and evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 229}
- From al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7869): among believers and unbelievers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 229}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7870): the passage takes up again the calamity that struck the Muslims at Uhud, the trial, the sifting and God's taking martyrs from them, and says by way of consolation and instruction about what He had done and would do to them: acts of vengeance have passed from Me against those who denied My messengers and associated others with Me, 'Ad, Thamud, the people of Lut and the people of Midian; travel and see the exemplary punishments that passed on them and on any who are as they were, even if I gave them respite, so that you do not think My vengeance has left your enemy and Mine because of the turn I gave them over you, which I did to test you and to know what is in you. The editor says the copies read "and associating in 'Ad and Thamud", very wrong, and that the right wording is from Ibn Hisham; that they also had wrong wording in three other places which he corrected from Ibn Hisham; that "imla'" is respite and drawing on, "idala" is prevailing, and "dawla" is the passing from one state to another in war and elsewhere, with a reference to a later passage (p. 239); and he traces it to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 116), the continuation of the reports ending at 7866. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 229}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7871): He let them enjoy a little in this world, then sent them to the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 230}
- From Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Ibn Zayd (7872): the patterns are examples. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 231}

**The meaning of "patterns" (pp. 230-231).** Abu Ja'far says "sunan" is the plural of "sunna", the model that is followed and the leader who is taken as imam; one says "so-and-so set a good sunna among us" or "a bad one" when he did something that others followed, whether good or bad. He cites a line from the mu'allaqa of Labid in which his people are described as a group whose forefathers set a custom for them, and every people has a custom and an imam (the verse itself is not reproduced here). The editor explains that the line connects to earlier lines in which Labid recalls his people and their merit, and that it means this habit is a way they inherited, that every custom has a leader who went before and was followed, and that they are people of an old merit their forebears founded. Abu Ja'far then gives a line of Sulayman ibn Qatta about those of the house of Hashim killed at al-Taff, who consoled one another and so set for the noble a way to follow. The editor says the printed text read "Qanna" and that "Qatta" is his mother's name; he was a freedman of Taym Quraysh and a Successor who narrated from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas, Amr ibn al-As and Muawiya, and has entries in al-Bukhari's Kabir and Ibn Abi Hatim; whoever said he was Sulayman ibn Habib al-Muharibi was wrong, as they are two men; he was one of the poet-horsemen and one of the first to set the pattern of elegies for the family of the house, with much verse on it. He traces the line to al-Tabari's History (7: 184), Ansab al-Ashraf (5: 339), al-Shajari's Amali (1: 131) and the Lisan, adds that Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr recited it before he was killed so people knew he would not leave until slain, that al-Taff is a land on the edge of Kufa on the desert road where al-Husayn ibn Ali was killed, and that "ta'assaw" means they became an example to one another in patience facing death without fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 230}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 231}
