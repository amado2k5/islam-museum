**Commentary on verse 89: "God does not hold you to account for idle oaths", p. 523**

Al-Tabari says God tells those of the Prophet's Companions who forbade themselves good things under oaths they had sworn, after forbidding them to forbid, that their Lord does not hold them to account for idle talk in their oaths. Report 12356, from Ibn Abbas, says that when the first verse came down about the people who had forbidden themselves women and meat, they asked what to do about the oaths they had sworn, and God sent down "God does not hold you to account for idle oaths".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 523}

**Commentary on verse 89, continued: "But He holds you to account for oaths you have made binding", and the reading, pp. 524-525**

Al-Tabari says report 12356 indicates that the people had forbidden themselves what they forbade under oaths they swore, and the verse came down on their account. The readers differed over the word for "made binding": most readers of the Hijaz and some of Basra read it with a doubled letter, meaning you confirmed the oaths and repeated them, and the readers of Kufa read it without doubling, meaning you made them binding on yourselves and your hearts were set on them. Al-Tabari holds the reading without doubling to be better, because the Arabs scarcely use that doubled form except for what is repeated again and again, and all agree that an oath whose breach requires expiation binds from a single swearing even if the swearer does not repeat it, so the doubling has no intelligible point.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 524}

The sense for him is therefore: God does not hold you to account, believers, for oaths in which you spoke idly, but holds you to account for what you made binding on yourselves and your hearts were set on; he has already set out, earlier in the work, which oath is idle, which one a person is held to, which one is breached and which is not. Reports 12357 and 12358, from Mujahid, say that "made binding" means what you did on purpose, and report 12359, from al-Hasan, that where you meant to commit a sin, expiation is on you.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 525}

**Commentary on verse 89, continued: "The expiation is feeding ten poor people", pp. 525-530**

Al-Tabari notes the interpreters differed on what the pronoun in "its expiation" returns to. Some said it returns to "what" in "what you have made binding"; report 12360, from al-Hasan, says an idle oath is swearing to a thing while you imagine it is as you swore and it is not, for which there is neither accountability nor expiation, whereas accountability and expiation apply to what you swore knowingly. Report 12361, from al-Sha'bi, says an idle oath has no expiation, and where the oath was made binding expiation is due.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 526}

Report 12362, from Abu Malik, says oaths are of three kinds: one that is expiated, swearing not to do a thing and then doing it; one that is not expiated, swearing to a matter on which one deliberately lies; and one for which the swearer is not held, swearing to a matter he thinks is as he swore and it is not, which is the idle oath. Report 12363, from Aisha, says an idle oath is one on which the swearer did not set his heart, and 12364, from Ibrahim, that idle oaths have no expiation. Report 12365, from Urwa on Aisha, says the oaths that require expiation are every oath a man swears seriously, in anger or otherwise, to do or to leave something.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 526}

Reports 12366 to 12369, from Yahya ibn Sa'id, Ali ibn Abi Talha, Qatada on al-Hasan, and al-Suddi, say there is no expiation for an idle oath. Al-Tabari says the sense on this view is: God does not hold you to account for idle oaths but holds you to account for oaths you made binding, and the expiation of those is feeding ten poor people.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 527}

Others said the pronoun returns to the idle oath, as a reference to it, so the sense is that God does not hold you to account for idle oaths if you expiate them, but holds you to account if you made oaths binding and then persisted in carrying them out without breaking them and expiating; persisting is not permitted to you, so the expiation of the idle oath, once broken, is feeding ten poor people. Report 12370, from Ibn Abbas, says the idle oath is a man swearing not to do something good, then seeing something better, so God commanded him to expiate and do the better thing; and that one who persists in forbidding what God made lawful and does not expiate is the one held to account.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 528}

Reports 12371 to 12374, from Said ibn Jubayr, say an idle oath is swearing to commit a sin, and God does not hold him if he leaves it, but he expiates the oath and does the better thing, and the one held is the man who swears to a sin and persists in it, whose expiation is feeding ten poor people. Report 12375, from Ibrahim, says an idle oath is one that the swearer is not held to but carries expiation, and 12376, from al-Dahhak, that it is the oath that is expiated.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 529}

Al-Tabari holds that the pronoun in "its expiation" returns to "what" in "what you made binding", because it cannot be said to one on whom expiation falls in his oath and who is held to account for it that God does not hold him to account for idle oaths, and in "God does not hold you to account for idle oaths" is clear proof that he whom God reports He does not hold is not held in any way. If someone thinks the sense is only that there is no punishment in the hereafter if one breaks and expiates, while still being held in this world through expiation, al-Tabari answers that God's reports, commands and prohibitions in His book go by the apparent general sense, not by a hidden sense with no proof, and neither reason nor report shows that only some senses of accountability were meant; one on whom expiation falls is held to account by a punishment in his property in this world, so he is not the one God reported He does not hold. The sense is then: God does not hold you to account, people, for idle words and oaths when you did not mean disobedience or going against His command by them, but holds you to account for what you meant as sin, made binding on yourselves and set your hearts on.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 530}

We stop here at the foot of p. 530, in al-Tabari's choice on what the pronoun in "its expiation" refers to and on the meaning of accountability; the rest of verse 89 follows in the next batch. The pages of commentary end at p. 585, before the indexes beginning at p. 587.
