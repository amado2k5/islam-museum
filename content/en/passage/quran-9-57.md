**Commentary on verse 57 (pp. 298-300)**

Al-Tabari says: if these hypocrites could find a refuge, that is, a stronghold of a fortress in which to take shelter from you, "or caves", the hollows in the mountains, singular maghara, a noun of place from a verb meaning to enter into something, from which comes the saying that an eye "sank" when it entered its socket, "or a place to enter", that is, a burrow in the earth into which they could go, from a verb meaning to go in. The editor notes that the printed text added the word for the verse into the sentence, probably a printing error. "They would turn to it" means they would flee to it away from you, "rushing" meaning hurrying in their walk. It is said that rushing is a gait between two gaits, and he cites for it a line ascribed to Muhalhil describing his rush into the blood of the enemy until men of rank among them fell still. The editor says this is a rare statement not found in the dictionaries and that it should be recorded there with its witness, that he did not find the line in Muhalhil's poetry, and that "fell still" means became quiet, as redness fades. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 298}

God described them so, al-Tabari says, because they lived among the Companions of the Messenger in their disbelief, hypocrisy and enmity toward them and toward the faith in God and His Messenger which they held, since they lived among their own people and kin, in their houses and with their wealth, and could not leave all that; so they flattered the community with hypocrisy and defended themselves, their property and their children by disbelief and a claim of faith, while in themselves was hatred of the Messenger and the believers and enmity toward them; and God described them as their hidden thoughts were. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 299}

Report 16808, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the refuge is a shelter in the mountains, the caves are the hollows in the mountains, and the place to enter is a tunnel. Report 16809, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: "refuge" means a shelter, "caves" the hollows, and "a place to enter" going into the earth, a passage in the earth, which is a tunnel. Report 16810, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "if they could find a refuge" means a shelter to which they would flee from you. Report 16811, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: a shelter for them to which they would flee from you; and from Ibn Abbas: "refuge" means a shelter, "caves" the hollows, and "a place to enter" a tunnel in the earth. Report 16812, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "refuge" means fortresses, "caves" hollows, "a place to enter" burrows, "they would turn to it, rushing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 300}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
