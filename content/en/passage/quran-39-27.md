## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 27 to 28 in a single passage. He explains His saying, "And We have certainly set forth for mankind in this Qur'an every parable that they may remember": And indeed, We have set forth for these polytheists of Allah every parable from the centuries of the empty nations, as a warning and warning from Us to them, "that perhaps they will remember." He means: that they may remember and turn away from what they are settled upon in disbelief in Allah. And His saying: "An Arabic Quran," the Exalted says: We have set forth for the people in this Qur'an an Arabic Qur'an from every parable: "without confusion," meaning: with confusion, as reported by Ibn Abi Nujayh, from Mujahid: "An Arabic Qur'an, not with any confusion." His phrase "an Arabic Quran" is in the accusative case of the phrase: this Qur'an, because the Qur'an is definite, and His saying "an Arabic Qur'an" is indefinite. In the passage, he quotes sayings from Mujahid.
