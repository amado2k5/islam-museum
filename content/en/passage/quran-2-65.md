## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "you know those among you who transgressed on the Sabbath" al-Tabari says "'alimtum" means you knew, and "i'tadaw" means they overstepped God's limit and did what they were forbidden on the Sabbath. He says this verse and those after it recount to the Israelites living among the Ansar what their forebears did, and warn them that if they persist in disbelief the like of what befell the earlier ones, transformation, the quake and the thunderbolt, may befall them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 166} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 167}
He reports from Ibn Abbas, through al-Dahhak, that God sent no prophet without commanding Friday and telling of its merit, and whoever followed kept to it; that the Jews told Moses the Sabbath was best because God finished the creation then, and the Christians told Jesus the first day was best so they were given Sunday; that God told Moses to leave them to the Sabbath and not fish on it, and the fish would show on the water on the Sabbath; and that when some took them and no punishment came, they thought Moses's warning false, and God turned them into apes. On this account a transformed creature lives no more than three days and neither eats nor breeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 167} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 168} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 169}
In another report from Ibn Abbas through Ikrima, God had prescribed Friday for them, they went against it to the Sabbath, and He tried them on it by forbidding fish, in a town between Ayla and Tur called Madyan; a man contrived to take a fish secretly, tied it with a cord and ate it the next day, and others did likewise until they fished openly and sold the fish in the markets; the people divided into those who forbade it and those who said "why preach to people God will destroy", and the persisters woke as apes in their locked houses, recognisable by their persons. Ibn Abbas says that had God not said He saved those who forbade evil, we would have said He destroyed them all, and that this is the town in surah al-A'raf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 169} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 170}

Al-Tabari completes the reports: Qatada says the fish were made lawful on other days and forbidden on the Sabbath as a trial, so the people became three kinds, one holding back and forbidding, one holding back without forbidding, and one breaking the prohibition; God then said "be apes, despised", and they became apes with tails. Al-Suddi says they were the people of Ayla; the fish came out on the Sabbath and stayed deep the rest of the week, so they contrived to dig a pit linked to the sea, open it on the Sabbath and take the fish on Sunday; their scholars forbade it but they persisted, so the town was divided by a wall, and the transgressors were found as apes leaping upon one another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 171} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 172}
From Mujahid, in two reports, comes the view that they were not turned into apes, but their hearts were transformed, the account being a parable God gave like that of the donkey carrying books. Al-Tabari rejects it as contrary to what the Book of God plainly says, that He made some of them apes and swine, and because Mujahid departs from the agreement of the commentators, whose agreement in calling it mistaken is enough to show its error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 173}
On "We said to them: be apes, despised" he says "sabt" originally means stillness and rest, whence a sleeper is called "masbut", and some say it is so called because God finished creation on the day before, Friday. "Kunu" means become, and "khasi'" is one driven away and rebuffed like a dog, which he reports from Mujahid, Qatada, al-Rabi' and al-Dahhak as lowly and abased. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 174} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 175}
