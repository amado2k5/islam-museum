## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "And believe in what I have sent down, confirming what is with you" al-Tabari says "believe" means "affirm", what was sent down is the Qur'an, and the Qur'an confirms what the Jews have of the Torah; their affirming it affirms what they hold, because the Qur'an's command to acknowledge Muhammad's prophethood and follow him matches what is in the Torah and Gospel, and denying it is denying what they hold. He parses "confirming" as a circumstantial accusative. He reports from Mujahid that the Qur'an was sent confirming the Torah and Gospel they have, and from Abu al-Aliya that it means People of the Book, believe in what was sent down to Muhammad, since they find him written with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 560} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 561}
On "and be not the first to disbelieve in it" he takes up the question why "disbeliever" is singular when the address is to a group, and answers that a derived comparative may be singular when added to a group because it stands for "whoever", as in "the army is routed", citing a line of verse and the view of al-Farra'. He says the sense is: People of the Book scholars, affirm what I sent to My messenger, which confirms your scripture, and do not be the first of your community to deny it and reject that it is from Me, when you have knowledge of it that others lack. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 561} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 562} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 563}
He reports from Ibn Jurayj that "it" refers to the Qur'an, and from Abu al-Aliya that it means do not be the first to disbelieve in Muhammad. He mentions another view that "it" refers to their own scripture, since denying Muhammad is denying their book. Al-Tabari holds that these two views are far from the apparent sense of the wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 563}
