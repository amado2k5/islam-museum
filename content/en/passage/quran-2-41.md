## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "And believe in what I have sent down, confirming what is with you" al-Tabari says "believe" means "affirm", what was sent down is the Qur'an, and the Qur'an confirms what the Jews have of the Torah; their affirming it affirms what they hold, because the Qur'an's command to acknowledge Muhammad's prophethood and follow him matches what is in the Torah and Gospel, and denying it is denying what they hold. He parses "confirming" as a circumstantial accusative. He reports from Mujahid that the Qur'an was sent confirming the Torah and Gospel they have, and from Abu al-Aliya that it means People of the Book, believe in what was sent down to Muhammad, since they find him written with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 560} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 561}
On "and be not the first to disbelieve in it" he takes up the question why "disbeliever" is singular when the address is to a group, and answers that a derived comparative may be singular when added to a group because it stands for "whoever", as in "the army is routed", citing a line of verse and the view of al-Farra'. He says the sense is: People of the Book scholars, affirm what I sent to My messenger, which confirms your scripture, and do not be the first of your community to deny it and reject that it is from Me, when you have knowledge of it that others lack. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 561} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 562} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 563}
He reports from Ibn Jurayj that "it" refers to the Qur'an, and from Abu al-Aliya that it means do not be the first to disbelieve in Muhammad. He mentions another view that "it" refers to their own scripture, since denying Muhammad is denying their book. Al-Tabari holds that these two views are far from the apparent sense of the wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 563}

Al-Tabari explains his preference, which is his own, that "it" refers to the Qur'an: what is commanded to be believed at the start of the verse is what was sent down, that is, the Book, not Muhammad, so what is forbidden to be disbelieved at its end must be the same, and for the one commanded and the one forbidden to differ within one verse is less usual and less apparent. He reports from Ibn Abbas that the sense is "and you have knowledge of it that others lack". The editor notes that Ibn Kathir took both readings as compatible and correct, while al-Tabari is fixing what the pronouns point to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 563} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 564} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 565}
On "and do not sell My signs for a small price" he reports from Abu al-Aliya that it means do not take payment for it, adding that it is written with them in the earlier Book to teach freely as you were taught freely; and from al-Suddi that it means do not take a little gain and conceal God's name. Al-Tabari's view is that the sense is not to sell the knowledge given them in God's book for a small worldly gain; their selling it was their leaving unexplained what their book says of Muhammad for a small price, namely the satisfaction of their leadership over their followers and the fees they took from those they explained it to; and Abu al-Aliya's reading shows that the prohibition of taking fees for explaining is the prohibition of buying a small price with His signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 565} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 566}
On "and Me alone, be mindful of Me" he says: be mindful of Me in selling My signs for a paltry price and in disbelieving what I sent down, lest I bring on you what I brought on your forebears of exemplary punishments and retributions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 566}
