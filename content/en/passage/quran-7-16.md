## Summary
Verse 16 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 16 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "He said: because You led me astray, I will sit in wait for them on Your straight path", God says that Iblis said to his Lord, "because You led me astray", that is, because You made me go astray; and he gives (14361) from Ibn Abbas that "You led me astray" means You made me go astray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 332}

He gives (14362) from Ibn Zayd that "because You led me astray" means in that You made me go astray. Some interpreted "because You led me astray" as "by what You destroyed me", from the saying "the weanling went astray", when it lacks milk and dies, as in a poet's line describing a bow (the editor ascribes it to Mudrij al-Rih al-Jarmi and traces it). The original sense of "igh-wa'" in Arab speech is a man's making something look fair to another until it seems good to him, deceiving him. It is reported of some Tayy tribes that they say "so-and-so became ghawi", meaning he fell sick, and the note says this is a text that ought to be recorded in the dictionaries. Some took it as an oath, as if the sense were "by Your leading me astray, I will sit in wait for them on Your straight path", as one says "by God I will do such and such". Some took it as requital, as if the sense were "because You led me astray" or "since You led me astray, I will sit in wait for them on Your straight path". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 333}

Al-Tabari says there is a clear refutation here of what those he calls the Qadariyya say, that everyone who disbelieves or believes does so because God delegated the causes of it to him, and that the cause by which the believer reaches faith is the cause by which the disbeliever reaches disbelief. For if it were as they say, the wicked one, in saying "because You led me astray", would have said "because You set me right", since the cause of leading astray would be the cause of setting right and his report of being led astray would report being set right; but since the two causes differ, and the cause by which he went astray and perished was from God, he attributed it to Him and said "because You led me astray". So Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi said, and (14363) is given from him: God fight the Qadariyya, Iblis knew God better than they! The note says that for al-Tabari the Qadariyya here are those who deny the decree and disbelieve in it, that those who believe in the decree are called the people of affirmation, and that "delegation" is the saying of the Mu'tazila and their likes. As for "I will sit in wait for them on Your straight path", he says: I will sit for the children of Adam on Your straight path, that is, Your upright way, which is God's true religion, Islam and its laws; the sense is, I will turn the children of Adam away from worshipping and obeying You, lead them astray as You led me astray and mislead them as You misled me. That is as reported from Sabra ibn Abi al-Fakih {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 334}

(14364) that he heard the Prophet say: Satan sat in wait for the son of Adam on his roads. He sat for him on the road of Islam and said, will you become a Muslim and leave your religion and your forefathers' religion? He disobeyed him and became a Muslim. Then he sat for him on the road of emigration and said, will you emigrate and leave your land and your sky, when the emigrant is like a horse on a tether? He disobeyed him and emigrated. Then he sat for him on the road of jihad, which is struggle with self and wealth, and said, will you fight and be killed, your wife be married and your wealth be divided? He disobeyed him and fought. The note says the report was given by Ahmad, al-Nasa'i and al-Bukhari in his Tarikh, that Ibn Hajar called the chain good though it has some disagreement, and that Ibn Hibban held it sound. It is reported of Awn ibn Abdullah on this (14365) that "I will sit in wait for them on Your straight path" is the road to Mecca, and the note says Abdullah ibn Bukayr in its chain is not strong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 335}

Al-Tabari says that what Awn said, though part of God's straight path, is not the whole path; the enemy of God reported that he would sit in wait on God's straight path without specifying a part of it over another, so what is reported from the Messenger of God on this is closer to the apparent text and better as interpretation, since the wicked one spares no effort to turn God's servants from everything that brings them near to God. The commentators said the like of what he said about "straight" here: he gives (14366) and (14367) from Mujahid that it is the truth, and (14368) from him that it is the way of the truth, "so I will mislead them, all but a few". The Arabists differ on this: some Basran grammarians said the meaning is "I will sit in wait for them on Your straight path", {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 336}

as one says "he headed Mecca" meaning to Mecca, and as a poet said in a line meaning "to catch a bird", dropping the preposition, and as in [[passage/quran-7-150|7:150]], "did you hasten your Lord's command?", meaning hasten away from His command. Some Kufan grammarians said the meaning, and God knows best, is "I will sit in wait for them on their way and in their way", saying that dropping the qualifier here is allowed, as you say "I sat for you the face of the road" and "on the face of the road", because "road" is in sense an adverb and bears what "day", "night" and "year" bear when one says "I will come to you tomorrow" and "in tomorrow", which is the wording of al-Farra', the note says. Al-Tabari says this is the sounder of the two views to him, because sitting requires a place in which to sit, so as one says "I sat in your place" one says "I sat on your path" and "in your path", as the poet Sa'ida ibn Ju'ayya al-Hudhali said in a line describing a spear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 337}
