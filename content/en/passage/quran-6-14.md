## Summary
Verse 14 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 14 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: shall I take as protector any but God, creator of the heavens and the earth, who feeds and is not fed?" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to these polytheists who hold idols and images equal to their Lord, who deny you pure oneness for your Lord and call to the worship of gods and idols: shall I take as protector anything but God, whom I ask for help and aid in misfortunes and events? Report 13110, from al-Suddi: the "wali" is the one they take as patron and acknowledge as lord. "Creator of the heavens and the earth" is an epithet and description of God, and for that reason is genitive; by it he means their originator, beginner and creator. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 282}

Report 13111, from Ibn Abbas: I did not know what "fatir al-samawat wa-l-ard" meant until two Bedouins came to me quarrelling over a well, and one said to the other "I made it" ("fatartuha"), meaning I began it. 13112, from al-Suddi, and 13113, from Qatada: creator of the heavens and the earth. One says "fatarahaha Allah yaftiruha wa-yaftiruha fatran wa-futuran", and from it comes [[passage/quran-67-3|67:3]], "do you see any rifts" (futur), meaning cracks and splits; one says "a sword is futar" when it has many cracks, which is a flaw in it, and from this comes Antara's line describing his sword. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 283}

The editor refers for "protector" to volume 10 and for "creator" to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, says that al-Tabari's wording on the meaning of "fatara" is very faulty and something has fallen out, which he left as it stood lest al-Tabari's text hold something the dictionaries do not record; and he notes that Antara's line is in his Diwan, in Ibn al-Shajari's Amali and in Lisan al-Arab, among verses he spoke in threatening Umara ibn Ziyad al-Absi, who envied his courage.

We stop here at the foot of p. 283, within the explanation of "creator of the heavens and the earth", after al-Tabari cites Antara's line, which continues on p. 284.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
