## Summary
Verse 14 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 14 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: shall I take as protector any but God, creator of the heavens and the earth, who feeds and is not fed?" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to these polytheists who hold idols and images equal to their Lord, who deny you pure oneness for your Lord and call to the worship of gods and idols: shall I take as protector anything but God, whom I ask for help and aid in misfortunes and events? Report 13110, from al-Suddi: the "wali" is the one they take as patron and acknowledge as lord. "Creator of the heavens and the earth" is an epithet and description of God, and for that reason is genitive; by it he means their originator, beginner and creator. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 282}

Report 13111, from Ibn Abbas: I did not know what "fatir al-samawat wa-l-ard" meant until two Bedouins came to me quarrelling over a well, and one said to the other "I made it" ("fatartuha"), meaning I began it. 13112, from al-Suddi, and 13113, from Qatada: creator of the heavens and the earth. One says "fatarahaha Allah yaftiruha wa-yaftiruha fatran wa-futuran", and from it comes [[passage/quran-67-3|67:3]], "do you see any rifts" (futur), meaning cracks and splits; one says "a sword is futar" when it has many cracks, which is a flaw in it, and from this comes Antara's line describing his sword. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 283}

The editor refers for "protector" to volume 10 and for "creator" to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, says that al-Tabari's wording on the meaning of "fatara" is very faulty and something has fallen out, which he left as it stood lest al-Tabari's text hold something the dictionaries do not record; and he notes that Antara's line is in his Diwan, in Ibn al-Shajari's Amali and in Lisan al-Arab, among verses he spoke in threatening Umara ibn Ziyad al-Absi, who envied his courage.

The rest: from it one says "the camel's tusk split" when the flesh cracked and came out, and from it [[passage/quran-42-5|42:5]], "the heavens almost split asunder from above them", meaning they crack and break. As for "he feeds and is not fed" it means he provides for his creatures and is not provided for. Report 13114, from al-Suddi: he provides and is not provided for. Some mention that it was read "yut'imu wa-la yat'amu", that he feeds his creatures and does not himself eat, which has no sense because so few readers have it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 284}

From Antara's line the editor explains "aqiqa" as a flash of lightning that splits, "kami'" as a bedfellow, and "afall" as one whose edge is notched; he corrects the wording "that he used to read this" in manuscript and print, and says this reading is recorded by Ibn Khalawayh in Shawadhdh al-Qira'at, who ascribes it to al-A'mash, and by Abu Hayyan, who also ascribes it to Mujahid, Ibn Jubayr, Abu Hayat, Amr ibn Ubayd and Abu Amr in one report.

Then "say: I am commanded to be the first of those who submit, and not to be among the polytheists": say, Muhammad, to those who call you to take gods as protectors besides God and urge you to worship them: shall I take as protector anything but God, creator of the heavens and the earth, who provides for me and others and is provided for by none, a protector who is his servant and owned, a creature? Say to them also: my Lord has commanded me to be the first to submit, that is, the first of the people of my age and time to bow in servanthood to him, humble myself to his command and prohibition and yield to him; "and do not be among the polytheists" means I was told not to be among those who join others with God, who make gods and rivals partners. He put "I was commanded" in place of "it was said to me", because its sense is that it was said to me, so mention of the command suffices for mention of saying, since a command is known to be a saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 285}

Verse 14 closes here at p. 285, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-15|6:15]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
