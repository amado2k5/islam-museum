## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and Abraham bequeathed it to his sons, and Jacob" al-Tabari says: he bequeathed this word, meaning "I have submitted to the Lord of the worlds", which is the Islam that God commanded His Prophet, namely sincerity in worship, the oneness of God and the humility of the heart and limbs before Him; "bequeathed" means he charged and commanded them with it. On "and Jacob" he says Jacob too bequeathed it to his sons; from Qatada, after Abraham, and from Ibn Abbas, he bequeathed them Islam and Jacob bequeathed the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 93} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 94}

He mentions the view of some that "Abraham bequeathed it to his sons" is a finished statement and "Jacob" begins a new one, that Jacob bequeathed to his sons "my sons, God has chosen the religion for you"; al-Tabari sees no sense in this, because what Jacob bequeathed matches what Abraham bequeathed, the urging to obedience and Islam. Asked why "an" is dropped, he says because bequeathing is speech and so is taken by its sense, as in "God charges you about your children", and he mentions the view of some grammarians that it is dropped because the address suffices. A group of readers read "awsa biha", meaning charged, and those who read "wassa" with the doubled sad mean he charged them charge after charge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 94} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 95} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 96}

On "my sons, God has chosen the religion for you" he says God has chosen for you this religion in which He charged you, and "the" in "the religion" is used because those addressed already knew it from their bequest. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 96}

On "so do not die except as Muslims" he asks whether life and death are in the hands of the children of Adam so that one could be forbidden to die except in a certain state, and says the sense is: do not leave this religion, which is Islam, throughout your lives, since no one knows when his end will come. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 96} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 97}
