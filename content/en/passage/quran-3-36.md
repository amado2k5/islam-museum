## Verse 36 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 333-339)

**The sense of giving birth, and the feminine pronoun.** Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-36|3:36]]) says the sense is: when Hanna gave birth to the dedicated child; the pronoun is feminine because the child was a girl, and had it referred back to the relative pronoun in the earlier verse it would have been masculine. The verb means 'bore', and one says 'the woman put down, putting down'; her words mean that she bore the dedicated one as a girl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 333}

**The two readings of the verb.** Al-Tabari says most reciters read the verb with the feminine ending, as God's report about Himself that He is the One who knows what she bore, not part of her speech; some of the early readers read it in the first person, making Mary's mother the speaker. He holds that the better reading is the one that the proof carried by widespread transmission has, the first, and that an irregular reading is not set against it. So the sense is that God is the best knower of all His creation of what she bore, and then he returns to her words, excusing herself to her Lord for what she had vowed and freed for His service: a male is not the same as a female, because a male is stronger for the service and better able to keep it, whereas a female in some conditions is not fit to enter the sanctuary or serve the church because of menstruation and childbirth, and she named her Mary. This is al-Tabari's choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 334}

**Reports on her words.** Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (6877) glosses it as: when she had made her freed for Him as a vow. The editor says Ibn Hisham's text in the European edition matches al-Tabari, that the Halabi edition has 'freed for You', and that one manuscript of Ibn Hisham has the feminine form, which is a good reading; the printed al-Tabari altered the manuscript for reasons he does not know, and the report is the remainder of those ending at 6859. Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq (6878) says a male is stronger for this than a female. Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada (6879) says a woman cannot be treated so, meaning freed for the church to serve and sweep it without leaving, because of the menstruation and harm that befall her, and from that she said what she said; the editor says the manuscript has the right form of the verb, and that he thinks the correct preposition is the causal one meaning 'on account of this', in the manner of the ancients. Al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada (6880) says they only freed boys, and al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi (6881) says she hoped God would give her a boy, because a woman cannot serve the church without leaving it, owing to the harm that befalls her. Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi (6882) says she thought what she carried was a boy and gave it to God; when she bore, it was a girl, so she excused herself to God that only boys are freed, God said He knew best what she bore, and she said she named her Mary. Al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza, from Ikrima and Abu Bakr (6883) says it refers to menstruation and that it is not fitting for a woman to be with men, her mother saying so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 336}

**Seeking refuge for her and her offspring.** Al-Tabari says she meant that she was making her refuge, and her offspring's refuge, with God from Satan the stoned; the root sense of the word for refuge is a place of retreat and shelter; God answered her, protected her and her offspring, and gave Satan no way over her. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the verb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 336}

**Reports that Satan touches the newborn.** Abu Kurayb, from Abda ibn Sulayman, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from Yazid ibn Abd Allah ibn Qusayt, from Abu Hurayra (6884), reports the Prophet as saying that every newborn is struck by Satan with that thrust, which is why the baby cries out, except Mary the daughter of Imran: when her mother bore her she said the words of seeking refuge, so a veil was drawn before her and he struck it. The editor says Yazid ibn Abd Allah ibn Qusayt al-Laythi al-Madani was a trustworthy jurist of the successors, one of Malik's teachers, cited by Malik in the Muwatta and by the six books; that the report comes again after this with two other chains to Ibn Ishaq; that Ibn Kathir refers to it in al-Tarikh (2:57); that al-Hakim gives it in al-Mustadrak (2:594) from Isma'il ibn Jafar, from Yazid, from Abu Hurayra, saying the chain is sound though the two did not include it, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; that Isma'il ibn Jafar was the reciter of Medina, trustworthy; and that the added words 'from his father' in al-Mustadrak and al-Dhahabi's abridgement are a plain error, probably by copyists. Al-Tabari then gives it with many chains up to no. 6899, all from Abu Hurayra except 6893, which is from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 336}

Abu Kurayb, from Yunus ibn Bukayr, from Ibn Ishaq, from Yazid, from Abu Hurayra (6885), has the Prophet say that every child of Adam has a thrust from Satan, with which the baby cries out, except Mary and her son, for when her mother bore her she spoke the words of refuge, so a veil was drawn before the two and he struck the veil; Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq (6886) is similar. Ibn Humayd, from Harun ibn al-Mughira, from Amr, from Shu'ayb ibn Khalid, from al-Zuhri, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, from Abu Hurayra (6887), who heard the Prophet say that no child of Adam is born without Satan having touched him at birth, so that he cries out at the touch, except Mary and her son; Abu Hurayra said: recite, if you wish, the words in this verse. In the editor's notes, Amr, the teacher of Harun, is Amr ibn Abi Qays al-Razi al-Azraq, trustworthy and praised by al-Thawri; Shu'ayb ibn Khalid al-Bajali, judge of al-Rayy, is trustworthy, praised by al-Thawri, and Ibn Uyayna said he memorized from al-Zuhri and Malik when young; the printed text has 'al-Zubayr' for 'al-Zuhri', an error corrected from the manuscript; al-Bukhari (6:338-339) and Muslim (2:224) give the report through Shu'ayb ibn Abi Hamza al-Himsi from al-Zuhri; of the six books only Abu Dawud narrates from Shu'ayb ibn Khalid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 337}

Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Abi Dhi'b, from Ajlan the client of al-Mushma'il, from Abu Hurayra (6888), has the Prophet say that every child born of Adam's descendants is touched by Satan with his finger, except Mary and her son; Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Wahb, from his uncle Abd Allah ibn Wahb, from Amr ibn al-Harith, from Abu Yunus Sulaym, client of Abu Hurayra (6889), says Satan touches every child of Adam on the day his mother bears him, except Mary and her son. The editor says Ajlan was a trustworthy successor, that Ahmad gave the report in the Musnad through three narrators from Ibn Abi Dhi'b, and that Ibn Kathir quotes it in al-Tarikh (2:57) and in the tafsir (2:130); that Sulaym ibn Jubayr was a trustworthy Egyptian successor, the printed text's 'Sulayman' being corrected from the manuscript; and that Muslim (2:224) gives it from Ibn Wahb through Amr ibn al-Harith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 338}

Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from 'Imran', that Abu Yunus related from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet the same (6890); the editor says 'Imran' stands so in the manuscript and print and he does not know who he is, thinking the correct form is 'Ibn Imran', that is Harmala ibn Imran al-Tujibi al-Misri, trustworthy, who narrates from Sulaym ibn Jubayr and from whom Ibn Wahb narrates. Al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Ibn al-Musayyab, from Abu Hurayra (6891), says no child is born without Satan touching him so that he cries out at the touch, except Mary and her son, and Abu Hurayra then says to recite the words in this verse; the editor notes it came by a similar chain at 6887, that Ahmad gave it through Abd al-Razzaq and Abd al-A'la from Ma'mar, that al-Bukhari (8:159) and Muslim (2:224) gave it through Abd al-Razzaq, and that Ibn Kathir quotes it. Al-Muthanna, from al-Himmani, from Qays, from al-A'mash, from Abu Salih, from Abu Hurayra (6892) has the Prophet say that no child is born without Satan having squeezed him once or twice, except Jesus son of Mary and Mary, and then he recited the words of this verse. The editor says al-Himmani is Yahya ibn Abd al-Hamid, a hafiz, much disputed, whom he judges trustworthy, Ibn Ma'in having found him so while others spoke harshly of him, and Qays ibn al-Rabi al-Asadi is trustworthy, as he judged elsewhere. These statements are given as transmitted, with no verdict from the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 339}
