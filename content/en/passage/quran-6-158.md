## Summary
Verse 158 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 158 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Do they await anything but that the angels come to them, or your Lord comes, or some of your Lord's signs come?", al-Tabari says God asks whether these idolaters who equate idols and images with their Lord await anything except that the angels come to them with death and take their souls, or that your Lord, Muhammad, comes among His creation at the place of standing on the Day of Resurrection, or that some of your Lord's signs come, which the commentators said is the rising of the sun from where it sets. He gives (14195) from Mujahid that "the angels come" means at death when they take them, "your Lord comes" is the Day of Resurrection, and "some of your Lord's signs" is the sun's rising from the west. He begins (14196) from Qatada that the angels come by death, and its remainder is in the next batch. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 245}

He completes (14196) from Qatada that "your Lord comes" is the Day of Resurrection and "some of your Lord's signs" is a decisive sign, the rising of the sun from the west or whatever God wills; (14197) the same from him; and (14198) from al-Suddi that the angels come at death and some of the signs is the sun's rising from the west. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 246}

He gives (14199) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that they will wake with the sun and moon coming from the direction of the west like two yoked camels, with Ibn Humayd adding in his version the verse that follows, on faith not benefiting then. The editor notes that none of the authors of the six books gave this report, that its chain is sound, that al-Haythami in Majma' al-Zawa'id says al-Tabarani gave it by two routes, one containing Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn Sa'id ibn Abi Maryam, who is weak, and the other brief with trustworthy men, and that al-Suyuti ascribes it to a group of hadith compilers; and (14200) from Ibn Jurayj that the angels take souls by death and the Lord comes on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 246}

Then, on "the day some of your Lord's signs come, no soul will benefit from its faith, if it had not believed before or earned some good through its faith", he says: on the day some of your Lord's signs come, it will not benefit one who was an idolater before to believe after that sign comes. It was said that this sign is the sun's rising from the west. He gives (14201) from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that the Prophet said it is the sun's rising from the west; the note says the chain includes Ibn Abi Layla, a truthful jurist with poor memory, and Atiyya al-Awfi, who is weak, that Ahmad gave it in his Musnad and that al-Tirmidhi called it gharib and said some narrated it without raising it to the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 247}

He gives (14202) the same, and (14203) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said the Hour will not come until the sun rises from the west; when people see it, all on earth will believe, and that is when no soul will benefit from its faith. The note lists the routes of Abu Hurayra's report that al-Tabari gives, says that this route was recorded by al-Bukhari, Muslim, Ahmad, Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah and that its chain is sound, and describes Umara ibn al-Qa'qa' and Abu Zur'a. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 248}

He gives (14204) from Abu Dharr that the Prophet asked one day, do you know where this sun goes? They said God and His Messenger know best. He said it goes to its resting place beneath the Throne and falls down in prostration, and stays so until it is told to rise from wherever it likes, and it rises from its place of rising; then it runs until it reaches its resting place beneath the Throne and prostrates, and this repeats while people notice nothing amiss, until it is told to rise from its west, and it rises from where it sets. Then he asked, do you know which day that is? They said God and His Messenger know best, and he said that is the day when no soul will benefit from its faith. The note says the chain is sound, that al-Bukhari, Muslim, al-Tayalisi and al-Tirmidhi gave it, and that the printed "al-Yashkuri" is an error for "al-Sukkari" in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 249}

He gives (14205) the same by another chain from Abu Dharr; (14206) from Safwan ibn Assal that the Prophet said there is an open door toward the west for repentance until the sun rises from that direction, and when it does, no soul will benefit from its faith; and (14207) from him that repentance has a door in the west a journey of seventy or forty years, which stays so until some of your Lord's signs come. The note says Safwan's report was given by Ahmad, al-Tayalisi, Ibn Majah, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i and that Ibn Kathir says al-Nasa'i held it sound, and discusses the narrators of 14207, among them Ash'ath al-Iyami, whom Ibn Hibban counts trustworthy, al-Nasa'i weakens and Ibn Adi defends. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 250}

He gives (14208) from Safwan that in the west is an open door for repentance of a seventy years' journey, and when the sun rises from the west no soul will benefit from its faith; the note says Sahl ibn Amir al-Bajali is very weak, so the chain is weak. And (14209) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said the Hour will not come until the sun rises from the west, and when it rises and people see it all on earth believe, which is when no soul benefits from its faith, a repeat of 14203. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 251}

He gives (14210) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said the Hour will not come until the sun rises from the west, and that day all people together will believe, which is when no soul benefits from its faith; the note says Muslim gave it and the chain is sound. (14211) from Abu Hurayra that repentance is accepted until the sun rises from the west. (14212) from Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As, from the Prophet, that repentance stays accepted until the sun rises from the west; when it rises, every heart is sealed with what is in it and people are spared further work. And (14213) the like by another route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 252}

He gives (14214) from Abu Zur'a that three Muslims sat with Marwan ibn al-Hakam in Medina and heard him say that the first of the signs to appear is the Dajjal; they went to Abdullah ibn Amr and told him, and he said Marwan said nothing: I memorised something from the Prophet on this that I have not forgotten; I heard him say that the first of the signs to appear is the sun's rising from the west or the coming out of the Beast on people in the forenoon, whichever comes before the other, the second follows soon after. Then Abdullah ibn Amr, who used to read books, said: I think the first to appear is the rising of the sun from the west, because each time it sets it comes beneath the Throne, prostrates and asks leave to return, and is given leave, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 253}

until God decides it should rise from its west; it does as it used to, comes beneath the Throne, prostrates and asks leave to return, and is given no answer; it does that three times with no answer, until as much of the night has passed as God wills, and it knows that if given leave it would not reach the east, and it says, how far the east is, my Lord, who will save me for the people! Until the horizon is like a ring it asks leave to return, and is told, rise from your place, and it rises from its west; then he recited the verse. The note says the chain is sound, that Ahmad gave it, that al-Hakim in the Mustadrak called it sound by the standard of the two Shaykhs with al-Dhahabi agreeing, and that Muslim, Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah gave the short form. He gives (14215) from al-Sha'bi that three men went in to Marwan and he mentioned the like from Abdullah ibn Amr; the chain is weak because Abu Rabi'a Fahd (Zayd ibn Awf) is abandoned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 254}

He gives (14216) from Safwan ibn Assal that the Prophet said there is an open door in the west for repentance of a seventy years' journey, not closed until the sun rises from its direction; (14217) from him that when the sun rises from the west, that day no soul benefits from its faith; and (14218) from Zirr ibn Hubaysh that he went early to Safwan, who said that the Prophet said the door of repentance is open from the west, its breadth a seventy years' journey, and stays open until the sun rises from it; then he recited the verse. The note says the chain is weak because of Abu Rabi'a Fahd. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 255}

He gives (14219) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said the Hour will not come until the sun rises from the west, and when it does all people believe, which is when no soul benefits from its faith (the note calls it the fifth route and says al-Bukhari gave it); (14220) from him that whoever repents before the sun rises from its west is accepted, which Ahmad and Muslim gave; and (14221) from Abu Dharr that when the sun sets it comes beneath the Throne and prostrates, and is told rise from where you set; then he recited the verse. The note points to a break in the chain, since Ibrahim al-Taymi did not meet Abu Dharr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 256}

He gives (14222) from Abu Dharr: I rode behind the Prophet one day on a donkey and he looked at the sun as it set and said, it sets in a hot spring; it goes until it falls prostrate to its Lord beneath the Throne until He gives it leave; and when He wants to make it rise from its west He holds it back, and it says, my Lord, my journey is long, and He says, rise from where you set; that is when no soul benefits from its faith. The note says the printed text has "muddy" for "hot" and that al-Tabari held in his commentary on [[passage/quran-18-86|18:86]] that both are widespread readings, each with a sound sense; and (14223) from Abu Dharr that the Prophet looked at the sun one day and said it is about to come and stand before God, who will say, go back to where you came from, and then no soul benefits from its faith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 257}

He gives (14224) from Ibn Abbas that the verse means faith does not benefit an idolater at the signs but benefits the believers if they had earned good before; and that the Prophet came out one evening and said, servants of God, repent to God, for you are about to see the sun from the west, and when that happens repentance is held back, deeds are folded up and faith sealed. People asked, is there a sign for it, Messenger of God? He said, the sign is that that night will be as long as three nights; those who fear their Lord wake and pray, finish their prayer with the night still there, go to their beds and sleep, and wake with the night still there, and fear a great matter lies ahead; in the morning the sun is slow to rise and as they wait it rises on them from the west, and then no soul benefits from its faith. The note says all the narrators in this chain are weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 258}

He gives (14225) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said the Hour will not come until the sun rises from the west, and when it does and people see it, all believe together, and that day no soul benefits from its faith; the note says Salih, freedman of al-Tawama, is trustworthy but became confused late in life and Ibn Jurayj is among the early narrators from him, so the report is acceptable; (14226) that Ibn Abi Atiq heard Ubayd ibn Umayr recite the verse and say, we used to be told, and God knows best, that it is the sun rising from the west, that Amr ibn Dinar heard him say it too, that Ibn Abi Mulayka heard Abdullah ibn Amr say the sign after which faith does not benefit is the sun's rising from the west, and that Mujahid said the same; and (14227) from Ibn Mas'ud that it is the sun's rising from the west. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 259}

He gives (14228) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that "the day some of your Lord's signs come" is the sun's rising from the west; and (14229) from Abu Ubayda ibn Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that Ibn Mas'ud used to say that the signs mentioned have passed except four: the rising of the sun from the west, the Beast of the earth, the Dajjal and the coming out of Gog and Magog; and that the sign with which deeds are sealed is the sun's rising from the west, citing the verse. The note says 14228 is weak through a break, since Zurara ibn Awfa did not hear from Ibn Mas'ud, lists the routes of Ibn Mas'ud's report, and says 14229 is the third route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 260}

He gives (14230) from Masruq from Abdullah that it is the sun's rising from the west with the moon, like two joined camels; (14231) from Qatada from Zurara from Ibn Mas'ud that it is the sun's rising from the west; and (14232) and (14233) from Masruq from Abdullah the same account of the two camels. The note says 14229 is broken because Abu Ubayda did not narrate from his father, that al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak and called it sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing, though its flaw is the break, and that Ibn Kathir mentions it and al-Suyuti cites it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 261}

He gives (14234) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that repentance is spread out as long as the sun has not risen from the west; (14235) from Qatada that it was mentioned to him that Ibn Umm Abd, who is Ibn Mas'ud, used to say the door of repentance stays open until the sun rises from the west, and when people see it they believe, which is when no soul benefits from its faith; (14236) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said the Hour will not come until the sun rises from the west and then all people believe; and (14237) from Ubayd ibn Umayr that it is the sun's rising from the west. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 262}

He gives (14238) the same from al-Dahhak; (14239) from Ibn Mas'ud that repentance stays spread out while the sun has not risen from the west; (14240) from Mujahid that it is the sun's rising from the west; (14241) from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi that the verse means that when the signs come faith does not benefit a soul, and that it is the rising of the sun from the west; and (14242) from Safwan ibn Assal that it is the sun's rising from the west. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 263}

And (14243) from Abdullah ibn Amr that it is the sun's rising from the west; the note says Wahb ibn Jabir al-Haywani is a trustworthy follower who met Abdullah ibn Amr in Jerusalem and that only Abu Ishaq narrated from him. Others said it is rather one of the three signs: the Beast, Gog and Magog, and the sun's rising from the west. He gives (14244) from Abdullah that repentance is offered to the son of Adam, if he accepts it, until one of three comes out: the rising of the sun from the west, the Beast, or the opening of Gog and Magog; and (14245) the like in a different order. The note says the chain is weak through a break. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 264}

He gives (14246) from Aisha that when the first of the signs appears, the pens are cast down, the recording angels are held back and the bodies testify to deeds, with a sound chain as the note says; (14247) from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said three things, when they appear, mean no soul benefits from its faith: the rising of the sun from the west, the Dajjal and the Beast of the earth (the seventh route, given by Muslim and al-Tirmidhi); and (14248) from al-Hasan that the Prophet said, hasten to good deeds before six: the sun's rising from the west, the Dajjal, the smoke, the Beast of the earth, the private matter of each of you and the general affair. The note says Mu'awiya ibn Abd al-Karim al-Thaqafi was held trustworthy by Ahmad and others yet criticised, and that Muslim gave the report raised to the Prophet by another route from Abu Hurayra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 265}

He gives (14249) from Qatada that it was mentioned that the Prophet used to say the like. Then al-Tabari says the soundest view is what the reports from the Messenger of God agree on: that this is when the sun rises from the west. As for "or earned some good through its faith", it means: or did good deeds in affirmation of its belief in God, righteous works confirming and realising its word before the sun's rising from the west. It does not benefit a disbeliever who had not believed in God before its rising to believe then in God and His messengers, because it is a state in which no soul can refrain from acknowledging God, for the great terror coming on them from God's command; so their faith has the ruling of their faith when the Hour comes, a state in which creation cannot refrain from acknowledging God's oneness, as they see such horrors of that day that their need for reflection, inference, inquiry and consideration is gone; and it does not benefit one who believed in God and His messengers but neglected God's obligations, not earning any obedience to God with his limbs, when {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 266}

it rises from its west, his deeds, if he does any, and his earnings, if he earns any, are of no avail, for his neglect before its rising. He gives (14250) from al-Suddi that "earned some good through its faith" means earned righteous work in its affirmation, so these are the people of the qibla; if it was a believing soul and did no good before, and did good after seeing the sign, it is not accepted from it; but if it did good before the sign and good after, it is accepted; and (14251) from al-Dahhak that whoever is overtaken by some of the signs while doing righteous work with his faith, God accepts his work after the sign as He accepted it before. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 267}

Then, on "say: wait, we too are waiting", God tells His Prophet to say to those who equate idols and images with their Lord: wait for the angels to come to you with death and take your souls, or for your Lord to come to decide between us and you at the place of standing on the Day of Resurrection, or for the sun's rising from the west to come upon you, when the scrolls of deeds are folded and your faith will not then benefit you if you believe, until you then know who among us is in the right and who in the wrong, who does ill and who does good, who is truthful and who a liar, and see on whom God's punishment and painful retribution falls and who is saved among us and you and who perishes; we await it so that God may multiply His reward for our obedience to Him, our sincerity in worship and our singling Him out for lordship, and decide between us and you with truth, for He is the best of those who decide. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 267}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
