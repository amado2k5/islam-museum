**Commentary on verse 15: the Messenger makes clear what the People of the Book hid (pp. 140-144)**

On 'and God will soon inform them of what they used to do', al-Tabari says God tells His Prophet to pardon and overlook those who planned to stretch out their hands to him and his companions, for God is behind the reckoning with them and will tell them, when they come to Him on the return, of what they did in the world: breaking His covenant, undoing His pact, altering His book and distorting His command and prohibition, and He will punish them as they deserve. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 140}

Then 'People of the Book, Our messenger has come to you making clear much of what you used to hide of the Scripture, and pardoning much': God addresses the whole body of the Jews and Christians of the Prophet's time, and the messenger is Muhammad, as report 11608 from Qatada says. 'Making clear much of what you used to hide' means he makes clear much that you concealed from people of what is in your book; among what they hid and the Prophet made plain to people was the stoning of two married adulterers. It is also said that the verse came down about his clarifying this after their concealing it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 141}

Report 11609, from Ibn Abbas, says whoever rejects stoning has rejected the Qur'an without knowing it, and that stoning was among what they hid; report 11610 gives the same. The editor identifies the transmitters of both chains, judges them sound, and notes that al-Hakim records the report in the Mustadrak and al-Dhahabi agreed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 141}

Report 11611, from Ikrima, says the Jews came to the Prophet to ask about stoning and gathered in a house; he asked which of them was most learned, they pointed to Ibn Suriya, and he adjured him by the One who sent down the Torah on Moses and by the covenants taken from them until he trembled. Ibn Suriya said their women were beautiful and killing increased among them, so they lightened the penalty to a hundred lashes, shaving the head and mounting riders in reverse. The Prophet ruled stoning for them, and God sent down this verse about them together with the verse in al-Baqara on their saying to one another 'will you tell them what God opened to you?'. The editor glosses the rare words, points to the later stoning reports, and notes that this cause suits as another reading of the al-Baqara verse which al-Tabari did not give there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 142}

On 'and pardoning much', al-Tabari says He leaves you unpursued over much of what you hid from your book, the Torah that God sent down to you, so you do not act on it until God commands His Prophet to hold you to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 143}

On 'there has come to you from God a light and a clear book': the light is Muhammad, through whom God lit up the truth, made Islam manifest and wiped out idolatry, a light for whoever seeks it that makes the truth plain; part of his making truth plain is clarifying to the Jews much of what they hid. The clear book is the Qur'an, sent down on His Prophet, containing the explanation of what they disputed, of God's oneness, the lawful and the forbidden and the rulings of the religion, making plain to people all they need of their religion so that they can tell its truth from falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 144}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
