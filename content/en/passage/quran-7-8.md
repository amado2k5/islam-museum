## Summary
Verse 8 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 8 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "the weighing that day is the truth; those whose scales are heavy, they are the successful", he says: "al-wazn" is a verbal noun from "I weighed such and such", like "I promised him" giving "promise", and it is nominative by "the truth" and "the truth" by it. The sense is: the weighing on the day We ask those to whom messengers were sent and the messengers is the truth, and by "the truth" he means justice. Mujahid used to say that "the weighing" here is the judgment, and (14328) is given from him. The note says the report al-Tabari held sound was not found in full, that Ibn Khuzayma gave its opening in similar words, that al-Haythami cites it and that in al-Bazzar's chain is Abd al-Aziz ibn Aban, who is abandoned; and that its sense has come with sound chains, with al-Tirmidhi giving it from Adi ibn Hatim as good and sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 309}

Mujahid also used to say that "the truth" here means justice, and (14329) is given from him. Others said the meaning of "the weighing that day is the truth" is the weighing of deeds: he gives (14330) from al-Suddi that deeds are weighed; and (14331) and (14332) from Ubayd ibn Umayr that a huge, tall man who eats and drinks much will be brought and will not weigh a gnat's wing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 310}

He gives (14333) from Hudhayfa that the keeper of the scales on the Day of Resurrection is Gabriel, who is told, weigh between them, and returns from some to others, there being neither gold nor silver there; if the wrongdoer has good deeds, some are taken from his good deeds and given to the wronged, and if he has none, some of his companion's evil deeds are loaded on him, so the man returns with mountains like them upon him; that is the words "the weighing that day is the truth". The note says Abd al-Aziz ibn Aban is in the chain and is a liar who forged hadith, and that the printed text dropped words without which the sense fails, which were restored from the manuscript. The commentators differ over "whose scales are heavy". Some said it means whoever's good deeds are many, and he gives (14334) from Mujahid that they are his good deeds. Others said it means whoever's scales by which his good and evil deeds are weighed are heavy, saying that this is the scale people know, with a tongue and two pans; he gives (14335) from Amr ibn Dinar that they see a scale and two pans, and that he heard Ubayd ibn Umayr say a huge tall man is put in the scale and does not stand up to a fly's wing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 311}

Al-Tabari chooses the view he reported from Amr ibn Dinar, that this is the known scale by which things are weighed and that God weighs the deeds of His creatures, good and bad, as He said "whose scales are heavy", that is, the scales of his righteous deeds, "they are the successful", those who attained success, won what they sought and everlasting stay in the gardens, because of the many reports from the Messenger of God that nothing placed in the scale is heavier than good character and the like, which establish that this is a scale by which deeds are weighed. The note says al-Tirmidhi gave a similar report from Abu al-Darda and called it good and sound. If an ignorant person who misunderstands the meaning of God's report of the scale and His Messenger's report denies it, saying, does God need to weigh things when He knows the measure of everything, or saying, how are deeds weighed when they are not bodies to be described as heavy or light, and things are weighed only to know their heaviness from their lightness and their many from their few? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 312}

The answer is that God's weighing of deeds is like His recording them in the Mother of the Book and having them copied in books, with no need of it nor fear of forgetting, but so that it be a proof against His creation, as He said in [[passage/quran-45-28|45:28]] that every community will be called to its record and that this is Our record speaking against you in truth. So His weighing of His creatures' deeds in the scale is a proof against them and for them, whether through shortfall and neglect in obedience or through completion and perfection. The note praises this proof and the subtlety of al-Tabari's reasoning. As for how it is possible, he gives (14336) from Abdullah ibn Amr that a man will be brought on the Day of Resurrection to the scale and put in a pan, and ninety-nine scrolls will come out for him containing his faults and sins; then a card the size of a fingertip comes out containing the testimony that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger; it is put in the pan and outweighs his faults and sins. The note says the chain is sound, that Ahmad and Ibn Majah gave it and that al-Hakim called it sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 313}

So God's weighing of His creatures' deeds is by putting the servant and the records of his good deeds in one pan of the scale and the records of his evil deeds in the other, and God brings about heaviness and lightness in the pan of the one more entitled, as a proof from God against His creation, like what He does with many of them in making their hands and feet speak, to bear witness against them. The one who denies it is asked: God has told us He will make some people's scales heavy on the Day of Resurrection and others' light, and reports from the Messenger confirm it, so what makes you deny that it is the scale whose description we gave and which people know? Is it a rational proof that makes its soundness remote? There is nothing in God's weighing His creatures and the records of their deeds, to show them which of the two is heavier by the scale, that departs from wisdom or enters injustice in judgment, so what makes it impossible for you, by reason or report? There is no way to the truth of declaring false what reason does not reject except through one of those two, and neither exists; in the absence of proof for his claim lies the clear falsity of his saying and the soundness of what the people of truth said. This is not the place to dwell on one who denies the scale, since the aim of the book is to explain the interpretation of the Qur'an, and what is given is enough for one granted understanding. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 314}
