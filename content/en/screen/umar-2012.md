## Summary
*Omar*, also known as *Farouk Omar*, is a historical series produced by MBC Group and Qatar TV that tells the life of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] from the age of eighteen to the moment of his death. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} It was directed by [[person/hatem-ali|Hatem Ali]], with Chadi Abo co-directing the battle scenes and effects, written by [[person/walid-saif|Walid Saif]], and broadcast in 31 episodes on MBC1 and Qatar TV in Ramadan 1433, from 20 July 2012. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} [[person/muhammad|The Prophet]], his children and his wives are never shown, but the four rightly guided caliphs and other Companions are played by actors, and scholars divided over it. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}

## What it is
The series opens with Umar as caliph addressing the pilgrims, then turns back to his youth in [[place/mecca|Mecca]] working for his father al-Khattab ibn Nufayl, his conversion and migration, Badr, Uhud, the Trench, Hudaybiyya and the conquest of Mecca, then the Prophet's death, the caliphate of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and the Ridda wars. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} Umar's own caliphate takes in [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|Yarmuk]], [[event/battle-of-al-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]], [[event/conquest-of-jerusalem|the taking of Jerusalem]], the year of famine and the plague, up to his killing by [[person/abu-lulu-firuz|Abu Lu'lu'a]] and the choice of [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]]. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}

Samer Ismail plays Umar, with the voice of Assad Khalifa; Ghassan Massoud plays Abu Bakr, Ghanem Zrelli [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] and Tamer Al-Arbeed Uthman. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} Hassan Al-Jundi and Muna Wassef, who had both acted in the Arabic version of [[screen/al-risala-1976|*The Message*]], are also in the cast. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}

## Making it
The project began with an agreement between MBC and Qatar TV signed on 30 September 2010, for broadcast in Ramadan 2011. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} It cost 200 million Saudi riyals and was filmed in Morocco, in Marrakesh, Tangier, El Jadida, Casablanca and Mohammedia. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} MBC described it as the largest Arabic production to date, with 30,000 actors and extras and technical crews from ten countries working for 300 days. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} A committee of scholars reviewed the script for historical accuracy: [[person/yusuf-al-qaradawi|Yusuf al-Qaradawi]], Akram Diya al-Umari, Salman al-Awda, Abd al-Wahhab al-Turayri, Ali al-Sallabi and Saad al-Otaibi. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} The Turkish composer Fahir Atakoglu wrote the score, and the last episode closes with a nasheed in Umar's memory sung by [[person/mishary-rashid-alafasy|Mishary Rashid Alafasy]]. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}

## The scholars' debate
:::perspectives
- **Sunni (those who forbade it):** the Saudi Grand Mufti Abd al-Aziz Al al-Shaykh called the production a grave mistake and a crime; [[person/saleh-al-fawzan|Salih al-Fawzan]] forbade watching it, recalling the scholars' consensus and the Muslim World League's resolution against representing the Companions; al-Azhar also opposed it. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} {cite:thr-omar-fatwa-2012}
- **Sunni (those who approved it):** Yusuf al-Qaradawi, Salman al-Awda, [[person/yasir-qadhi|Yasir Qadhi]], Ali al-Sallabi and Khaled al-Musleh approved it. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} {cite:alarabiya-omar-2012} {cite:national-omar-2012}
- **In Shia perspective:** some Shia scholars and commentators criticised its portrayal of Ali ibn Abi Talib and what they regarded as historical distortions. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}
:::

A campaign before the broadcast called for it to be stopped, while its defenders argued that dramatising the Companions had precedent. {cite:egyptind-omar-2012} {cite:alarabiya-omar-2012} [[person/zakir-naik|Zakir Naik]] took a middle line: he advised Muslims to avoid series and films, but said that those who could not should watch works such as *Omar* and *The Message*. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}

## Broadcast beyond the Arab world
From 20 July 2012 the series was also shown on Algerian television, Tunisia's Nessma, Turkey's atv and Indonesia's MNCTV; then on Lebanon's Future TV and Morocco's Medi1 TV in 2013, Uzbekistan's Milly TV in 2018, in Kosovo in 2020, on the United Kingdom's Islam Channel from 24 June 2020, and on the Maldives' Raajje TV, dubbed into Dhivehi, from 11 March 2021. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en} It was dubbed into Persian, Turkish, Urdu and Indonesian, and was banned in Iran, as Iran International reported in 2025. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}

## Today
The series has Wikipedia articles in twenty-one languages, Punjabi, Kazakh and Uzbek among them. {cite:wikipedia-omar-tv-series-en}
