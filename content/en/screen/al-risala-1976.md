## Summary
*The Message* (al-Risala) is an epic film on the birth of Islam, directed and produced by [[person/moustapha-akkad|Moustapha Akkad]] and shot in two separate versions, Arabic and English, with different casts. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} It was first shown in London on 29 July 1976 as *Mohammad, Messenger of God*, was retitled *The Message*, and opened in New York on 9 March 1977. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} [[person/muhammad|The Prophet]] is never seen or heard in the film, and Maurice Jarre's score was nominated for an [[award/academy-awards|Academy Award]]. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}

## What it is
The film follows the mission from the first revelation in [[place/mecca|Mecca]], the persecution of the first Muslims and the migration to Abyssinia, through [[event/hijra|the Hijra]] to [[place/medina|Medina]] and the building of the first mosque, to [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]] and the truce of Hudaybiyya, and on to [[event/conquest-of-mecca|the conquest of Mecca]] and [[event/farewell-sermon-at-arafat|the Farewell Sermon]]. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-ar} It takes [[person/hamza|Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib]] as the figure the story follows, and ends with present-day footage of the circling of the Kaaba and of the call to prayer in mosques around the world. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-ar} {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}

The English version runs 178 minutes and the Arabic 207. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} In the English version Anthony Quinn plays Hamza, Irene Papas [[person/hind-bint-utba|Hind bint Utba]], Michael Ansara [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]], Johnny Sekka [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal]] and Michael Forest [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]]. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} In the Arabic version Abdullah Gaith plays Hamza, Muna Wassef Hind, Hamdi Ghaith Abu Sufyan, Ali Ahmed Salem Bilal, Mahmoud Said Khalid and Sanaa Gamil Sumayya. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-ar} H. A. L. Craig wrote the English version; [[person/tawfiq-al-hakim|Tawfiq al-Hakim]], Abdel Hamid Gouda al-Sahhar, Abd al-Rahman al-Sharqawi and Mohammad Ali Maher worked on the Arabic. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}

## Filming without showing the Prophet
The opening intertitles state that scholars of al-Azhar and of the Higher Islamic Shia Council of Lebanon approved the film's accuracy, and that its makers honour the Islamic tradition holding that impersonating the Prophet offends against the spirituality of his message, so his person will not be shown. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} In scenes where he is present the camera takes his point of view, and a light marks his presence. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} Akkad said that he showed neither the faces nor the voices of the Prophet and the rightly guided caliphs, and so chose Hamza as the character the audience follows. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-ar} The scholar Khaled Abou El Fadl, a friend of Akkad, called the solution brilliant. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}

## Making the film
Akkad first thought of the film in 1967; ten million dollars were raised from Kuwait, Libya, Morocco, Saudi Arabia and the United States, and the final budget was 17 million. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} Filming began in Morocco in April 1974 with a crew of three hundred and more than five thousand extras, and a replica of Mecca was built near [[place/marrakesh|Marrakesh]]. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} On 5 August 1974 Moroccan police stopped the shoot, King [[person/hassan-ii-of-morocco|Hassan II]] having been pressed by King [[person/faisal-bin-abdulaziz|Faisal]]; the crew moved to Libya after Akkad showed his footage to [[person/muammar-gaddafi|Muammar Gaddafi]], and filmed there from October 1974 to May 1975. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} The two versions were shot at the same time. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}

## Release and controversy
Five days before the London opening, threatening calls to a cinema led Akkad to change the title to *The Message*, at a cost of £50,000. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} The film was banned in Egypt, Kuwait and Saudi Arabia, in Saudi Arabia until 2018. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} In Egypt the censorship office later asked al-Azhar to permit both versions after twenty-two years of prohibition. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-ar}

In March 1977, as the film was about to open in the United States, the Hanafi Movement besieged the Washington chapter of B'nai B'rith and demanded that the opening be cancelled, in the mistaken belief that Anthony Quinn played the Prophet; the film was pulled on its premiere day and resumed after the siege ended. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} {cite:atlantic-message-2012} Akkad offered to show the film to the besiegers and to destroy it if they found it offensive. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}

In December 2007 a Hindi-Urdu dub was released in India with a new soundtrack that included [[person/a-r-rahman|A. R. Rahman]]'s "Marhaba Mustapha"; its release was approved by Kalbe Sadiq, vice-president of the All India Muslim Personal Law Board, and other Indian scholars. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} In February 2008 its screenings in [[place/hyderabad-deccan|Hyderabad]] were stopped after objections from a local group and from the Majlis-e-Ittehadul Muslimeen. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} A 4K restoration was shown at the Dubai International Film Festival in December 2017 and released in Saudi cinemas in 2018. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-ar}

## Reception
The film needed 35 million dollars to break even but earned 5 million in its theatrical run, 2 million of it in the United States. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} Critics divided. Dilys Powell in the Sunday Times judged the avoidance of showing the Prophet right on artistic as well as religious grounds, Variety praised the photography and the battle scenes, and Bob Thomas of the Associated Press called it a reverent, plodding, ultimately rewarding epic. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} Richard Eder in the New York Times found the Prophet's absence awkward, John Pym in the Monthly Film Bulletin faulted its staging, and Derek Malcolm in the Guardian its length. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} {cite:nyt-message-review-1977} Forty years on, NPR described the once-controversial film as a classic. {cite:npr-message-2016} Muna Wassef's Hind in the Arabic version brought her international recognition. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}

## Honours
Maurice Jarre's score, performed by the London Symphony Orchestra, was nominated for Best Original Score at the 50th Academy Awards (1978); the award went to John Williams for *Star Wars*. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-ar}

## Afterwards
In October 2008 the producer Oscar Zoghbi announced a remake, *The Messenger of Peace*; it was not made. {cite:guardian-message-remake-2008} {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en} The film has Wikipedia articles in thirty-four languages. {cite:wikipedia-the-message-1976-en}
