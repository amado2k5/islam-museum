# Canary — islam.museum release notes

The museum's public release notes: every change that lands on `main`, recorded in
reader's language in the same commit that makes the change. Newest release first.
The site build reads the version and date from the top block below and shows them
in the footer of every page and on the changelog page (`/{lang}/changelog/`).

Rules for every agent and contributor (`docs/engineering/15-release-notes-and-versioning.md`):
append your entry under the current version block, grouped by kind — Added, Changed,
Fixed, Removed, Performance, Tests, Data, Breaking, Rolled back. Write what changed
and why it matters, never a bare commit message. If your change bumps the version,
open a new block on top with the new version and today's date.

---

## v1.2.0 — 2026-11-05

### Added

- The Inventions Browser opens at /ar/inventions/, /en/inventions/ and /ur/inventions/ with its first sourced record, Ibn al-Nafis's account of the pulmonary circulation: the domains, an A-to-Z list with a name filter, and an Explore menu entry and landing card. The invention page shows the Wellcome manuscript image with its credit, quick facts, the makers, the claim and its basis, what came before and after, and a diagram drawn at build time.
- A second invention joins the browser: Rufayda's medical tent in the Prophet's Mosque at Medina, where Sa'd ibn Mu'adh was cared for after the Battle of the Trench, told from Ibn Hajar's al-Isaba with the open question of what counts as the first hospital in Islam set out without a verdict, a 1908 photograph of the mosque and a diagram of the path from the Trench to the tent.
- A third invention joins the browser: the first minarets, which Maslama ibn Mukhallad, governor of Egypt, had built at the corners of the Mosque of Amr in Fustat in 53/673, told from Ibn Abd al-Hakam, al-Maqrizi and Ibn Hajar, with a 2011 photograph of the mosque and a diagram of what came before and after.
- A fourth invention joins the browser: the barid, the relay post of stations and fresh mounts that Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan set up for the caliphate on the Persian and Byzantine model and Abd al-Malik perfected, told from al-Qalqashandi's Subh al-A'sha and al-Suyuti's Tarikh al-Khulafa, with a modern map of the Yemeni barid stations and a diagram of what came before and after it, in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- A fifth invention joins the browser: the bimaristan that al-Maqrizi says the Umayyad caliph al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik built in 88/707, the first in Islam on his account, with physicians paid by the state and allowances for lepers and the blind, told from al-Maqrizi's Khitat, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti and set beside Rufayda's tent as a contested first without a verdict, in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- A sixth invention joins the browser: the tiraz, the band of writing woven into the caliphate's garments, which al-Baladhuri, al-Suyuti and Ibn Khaldun show Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan turning from Persian images and Greek writing to the remembrance of God, the change that led to the first Islamic dinars, with a CC0 tiraz fragment from the Metropolitan Museum, in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- A seventh invention joins the browser: algebra, al-jabr wal-muqabala, which al-Khwarizmi set out in Baghdad under al-Ma'mun as a science of three kinds of term and six types of equation, each with a rule and a drawn proof, read in his own book with Ibn Khaldun's account and TDV's survey of the priority question, with a public-domain Bodleian manuscript image, in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- An eighth invention joins the browser: the Tabula Rogeriana, the world al-Idrisi drew at Palermo for Roger II, engraved on a silver disc and laid out in his book *Nuzhat al-mushtaq* as seventy maps, each followed by its description, read in the book's own preface with TDV's survey, with Konrad Miller's public-domain copy of the maps, in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- A ninth invention joins the browser: the camera obscura, Ibn al-Haytham's dark room in *Kitab al-Manazir*, where light through a small hole is followed through dust, cut by a body and checked with a ruler, and candles before one aperture cast separate spots that do not mix, read in Sabra's edition of the Arabic text with TDV's survey, with a page of al-Farisi's *Tanqih al-Manazir*, in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- Guidance for every agent on borrow-only books: they are borrowed and read in the browser one request at a time, the founder signs in when asked, and pages are OCR'd from screenshots outside git.
- The machine-readable foundation for the Muslim Inventions Browser: a new invention record class and sourced links to related people, places, works, objects and concepts.
- Static API exports for invention records and their precursor and dependent chains.
- The research queue now contains 506 provisional leads (467 active and 39 cited exclusions), including three page-anchored claims from the *1001 Inventions* Home chapter; an early-engineering screen leaves al-Muradi's gearing, the saqiya improvement and Sistan windmill claims unresolved, while concept screens excluded twelve non-invention signals, generic Ibn Taymiyya, Kamal Khujandi and Ulubatlı Hasan census signals and the Alpujarras Rebellion event hit were closed as unrelated, and the generic chess-transmission, kohl, miswak and turban leads were closed with cited reasons. No invention records have been published.
- A registered 1908 Arabic scan now supports the Fakhri sextant's location at Rayy and preserves al-Biruni's account of the instrument; al-Khujandi's Sunni basis and the exact scale remain unresolved, so the lead has not been promoted.
- An early-institutions screen adds source links for minarets, tiraz, barid, hisba and waqf, and keeps three distinct bimaristan claims as provisional leads rather than a settled “first hospital” chronology; the registered TDV entry calls Rufayda's tent the first Islamic bimaristan, but its source chain and the meaning of “first” remain unresolved.
- Relevant sections of five independent scholarly references have been read and their scope and limitations recorded before invention writing.
- Seven contemporary figures join the People wing, opening the museum's Ethiopian coverage: the menzuma artist Muaz Habib, the Afaan Oromo teacher Anwar Yusuf and the comparative-religion preacher Wahid Omar Negash from Ethiopia; the Bangladeshi scholars Ahmadullah, Abu Taw Haa Muhammad Adnan and Enayetullah Abbasi; and the Senegalese preacher Abdoul Wahab Dièye — each with sourced pages in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- Five Islamic media groups join the collection: Minber TV and Nejashi TV from Ethiopia, Africa TV from Nigeria, and Taw Haa Tv and Holy Tune from Bangladesh — channels carrying sermons, lectures and nasheed to audiences from tens of thousands to over ten million subscribers, each with sourced pages in Arabic, English and Urdu.
- An index of the embeddable widgets at /ar/embed/, /en/embed/ and /ur/embed/, listing "Did you know?" and "Trail of the week" with the code to place each on another site.

### Changed

- The open-data guide now documents all 23 schema classes and the invention API endpoints.
- Entry pages no longer show a "Draft" label or a "Completeness n/5" score, which are internal workflow notes; the invitation to help complete an entry stays, next to "Report an error".
- The overview map in the Places wing is easier to read: names no longer pile on top of each other. The best-connected places are labelled first and a name that would cover another is left off; every place's dot is now a link with its name, so every place on the map can still be opened.

### Fixed

- The Connections Explorer loads again: the site's content security policy now allows pages to fetch from the museum's API host (api.islam.museum), where the explorer's graph-data requests are redirected — the policy previously blocked those loads, so every entry failed with a "could not load" message.
- The museum's API catalog at /.well-known/api-catalog, the machine-readable list of its data services that every page's Link header points to, is published again. The build kept the site in an archive that silently leaves out folders whose names begin with a dot, so the catalog never reached the live site and the address answered "not found"; the archive now keeps them, and the catalog is served with the other root data files.
- The Hijri calendar's "Today is" line, highlighted day and opening month now follow the reader's own date, worked out in the browser with the same conversion as the Time Machine. They had been fixed on the day the site was built. Without JavaScript the page still shows a complete month.
- A page that does not exist under /en/ or /ur/ now says so in English or Urdu instead of Arabic; the rest of the site keeps the Arabic page.
- Each exhibit now appears once on the exhibits list in every language. The two Alexandria trails were one trail recorded twice: they are now one, and the old address still leads to it. "Great Mosques of the Garrison Cities" and «دول المغرب الأدنى» (States of the Maghreb) are different trails that shared a title with another, and now have their own.
- The Urdu embed index (/ur/embed/) now reads in Urdu; it had been added with English placeholder text.
- On the Places wing overview map, no dot now covers a place name and no two names touch: names are drawn above the dots, and the space each name needs is measured as it actually renders, including Arabic script.

### Tests

- A connections diagram for every connected record: the tests and the API export now fail if any record with at least one typed connection to another record has no Connections Explorer file, if a diagram is not centred on its record or draws a link that is not a real connection, or if an entry page offers "explore connections" without a diagram behind it. Records linked only through shared themes have no diagram by design; their "see also" list is a recommendation, not a connection.

### Performance

- Search starts faster, above all on phones and slow connections: the list of variant spellings (Saladin, Salah al-Din…) is now split into small pieces and the search page fetches only the few it needs for the words typed, about 14 KB each, instead of the whole list on every visit (2 MB compressed, 10 MB in Arabic and Urdu). Each search still waits for its pieces, so results and counts are the same as before.
- The repository checks and the test suite repeat less work: the prose files, the compiled schemas and the frozen Tanzil files are now read once per run and reused, where each validation call read them again. Nothing the checks decide has changed, and a Tanzil file that is edited is still read again and still fails its recorded hash. This matters because the full test suite ran for about 14 minutes on the shared build machines and one run failed on a test-runner timeout, not on a rule.
