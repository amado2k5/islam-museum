# Canary — islam.museum release notes

The museum's public release notes: every change that lands on `main`, recorded in
reader's language in the same commit that makes the change. Newest release first.
The site build reads the version and date from the top block below and shows them
in the footer of every page and on the changelog page (`/{lang}/changelog/`).

Rules for every agent and contributor (`docs/engineering/15-release-notes-and-versioning.md`):
append your entry under the current version block, grouped by kind — Added, Changed,
Fixed, Removed, Performance, Tests, Data, Breaking, Rolled back. Write what changed
and why it matters, never a bare commit message. If your change bumps the version,
open a new block on top with the new version and today's date.

## v1.30.1 — 2026-11-14
### Changed
- **The museum's block on terrorist-organisation content now covers the last seven designated names in full.** On the founder's ruling, Ansarallah (the Houthis), Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, Ansar Dine, Hizb ut-Tahrir, Ansar al-Islam and the Sudanese Muslim Brotherhood are now caught by the build's extremism check, under a complete ban: no propaganda material about them, in support of them or from them anywhere in the museum, in existing records or in future ones. Every existing record was audited mention by mention before the check was widened, and none contains any such material; the neutral historical mentions that remain (a corps commander's biography, a Nobel laureate's exile, a film that condemns the group it depicts) are recorded as the founder's exceptions, and two unrelated bodies that share a name — a Malian Sufi movement and a Nigerian society — are protected as non-matches. The content-safety rule now also lists where each of the seven classifications comes from, with the official designation lists' addresses. One name, Al-Andalus Media, is designated and banned in full, but its automatic check awaits the founder's ruling on a single ambiguous company name in one existing record.
### Data
- **Ten more places have prose in Arabic, English and Urdu (Lane B1, packet 11).** Heraklion, Batočina, Sidon and Ptolemais Hermiou carry a Pleiades section (title, number, class, span of years, recorded names, description); Mallawi, Stara Zagora, Gebze and Cambridge carry an iDAI.gazetteer section (class, parent, names, linked identifiers); Erzurum carries both. Antioch is also told from Yaqut al-Hamawi's entry in the Mu'jam al-buldan (vol. 1, scan pp. 358, 361 and 362: its founding, its conquest, its loss to the Byzantines and the Franks and its recovery). All draft, every sentence cited.
- **Canon of Medicine, Fann 2 chapter 19 and the causes of bodily symptoms, chapters 1 to 18 (scan pp. 140 to 147): twenty concept records in Arabic, English and Urdu.** The bath, its natural action and three rooms, how it warms and cools, dry and wet use and timing, its harms, medicinal bath waters, basking in the sun, burial in sand and soaking in oil; then the causes of warming, cooling, moistening, drying, deformity, obstruction, widening, roughness and smoothness, dislocation, unnatural movements, enlargement and deficiency, solution of continuity, ulcer and swelling. Drafts with a provenance block, cited to printed pages of the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya edition. Garbled OCR lines on pp. 141 and 143 were left out.
- **Sirat al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, printed pages 129 to 136 (batch 17).** Eight new event records (the march to Najran and the troublemakers of Lubayban; the felling of a wrongdoer's palms at Rajla; reports of a boy's sight restored and a child's tongue loosed; the narrator's answer on why the imam did not call down harm on his opponents; the seventeen days at Najran and the prisoners jailed at al-Ghayl; the departure for Khiwan; Banu Ma'mar at al-Ahsa and the first days at Hawth; the array at the pond of al-Damriyya), seven person records, four places and one group, each in Arabic, English and Urdu, all draft with provenance and cited to the printed page; the work record gains one section and the ledger a new resume point. The account is Zaydi and its signs and rulings are attributed to its narrators.
- **Ten more places have prose in Arabic, English and Urdu (Lane B1, packet 12).** Chamchamal, Kütahya, Sheffield, Drama, Rostov-on-Don, Trabzon, Plovdiv and Patras are told from the iDAI.gazetteer of the German Archaeological Institute (preferred name, class, parent, recorded names, linked GeoNames number), and Aphrodisias from its Pleiades record. Biskra also carries Yaqut al-Hamawi's entry from the Mu'jam al-buldan (vol. 2, scan p.586: its palms and dates, its walls, markets and baths, its salt mountain, its distances from Qal'at Bani Hammad and Tubna). Each place reads fully in all three languages as draft, with the access date recorded in its data file.
