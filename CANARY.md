# Canary — islam.museum release notes

The museum's public release notes: every change that lands on `main`, recorded in
reader's language in the same commit that makes the change. Newest release first.
The site build reads the version and date from the top block below and shows them
in the footer of every page and on the changelog page (`/{lang}/changelog/`).

Rules for every agent and contributor (`docs/engineering/15-release-notes-and-versioning.md`):
append your entry under the current version block, grouped by kind — Added, Changed,
Fixed, Removed, Performance, Tests, Data, Breaking, Rolled back. Write what changed
and why it matters, never a bare commit message. If your change bumps the version,
open a new block on top with the new version and today's date.

## v1.30.1 — 2026-11-14
### Changed
- **The museum's block on terrorist-organisation content now covers the last seven designated names in full.** On the founder's ruling, Ansarallah (the Houthis), Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, Ansar Dine, Hizb ut-Tahrir, Ansar al-Islam and the Sudanese Muslim Brotherhood are now caught by the build's extremism check, under a complete ban: no propaganda material about them, in support of them or from them anywhere in the museum, in existing records or in future ones. Every existing record was audited mention by mention before the check was widened, and none contains any such material; the neutral historical mentions that remain (a corps commander's biography, a Nobel laureate's exile, a film that condemns the group it depicts) are recorded as the founder's exceptions, and two unrelated bodies that share a name — a Malian Sufi movement and a Nigerian society — are protected as non-matches. The content-safety rule now also lists where each of the seven classifications comes from, with the official designation lists' addresses. One name, Al-Andalus Media, is designated and banned in full, but its automatic check awaits the founder's ruling on a single ambiguous company name in one existing record.
### Data
- **Eleven more people read from al-Zirikli's al-A'lam (Lane B2, packet alm17).** Short cited biographies in Arabic, English and Urdu (draft, each cited to volume and page): Ali al-Sijilmasi (4:250, Maliki), Ibn Mawdud al-Mawsili (4:130, Hanafi, author of al-Ikhtiyar), Abu al-Abbas al-Sabti (1:84, his chroniclers' differing judgements each attributed), al-Shinshawri (4:115, inheritance scholar), al-Mahdi al-Husayn (2:219, Zaydi imam labelled first), Ibrahim al-Thaqafi (1:36, Zaydi then Imami), Abd al-Hamid al-Zahrawi (3:286, journalist and politician hanged at Damascus), al-Shaykh al-Mufid (6:291, Imami labelled first), Shaykhizade (3:286), al-Bazzazi (7:29) and Ibn Fahd al-Hilli (1:220). Each page says only what the entry says. Corrections to the cards: al-Sijilmasi is a Maliki jurist who died in 1647 (not an eighteenth-century writer), his unsourced birth year is removed; al-Shinshawri's region is Egypt; Shaykhizade is no longer called a translator; al-Mahdi's birth year follows the printed heading (994). Several cards keep their year where the entry's Hijri year spans it.
- **Sirat al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, pp. 152 to 164 (Lane A, batch 20): the march from Sa'da to Najran and the fighting at the fortress, Sawhan and al-Hajar.** Eleven event records, seven persons, five places and two groups, each in Arabic, English and Urdu (draft, each cited to the printed page): the exchange of verses, the evening fight at Sa'da between Banu Sa'd and the Akilis, the march to Najran on 2 Dhu al-Hijja 286 AH, the first fighting at the fortress, al-Hadi's stand alone at Sawhan, the night of Adha at Sa'da and the breach in the wall of al-Hajar. The account is Zaydi and attributed to its narrator; verse is summarised, not quoted. The ledger gives the resume point (printed p. 165).
- **Canon of Medicine, Fann 2 Ta'lim 3 chapters 5 to 11 and the opening of the pulse (scan pp. 164 to 171): twenty-three concept records in Arabic, English and Urdu.** Signs of a poorly formed constitution, the two kinds of fullness and their signs, the signs of excess of blood, phlegm, yellow bile and black bile, signs of obstructions, of winds (by pain, movement, sound and touch), of hot and cold swellings, the course and migration of inner swellings, signs of separation of continuity and its hidden forms in nervous organs and joints, then the pulse: definition, two motions and two rests, why the forearm is felt, the ten genera (size, strength, speed, texture, fullness, heat, rest, evenness, regularity) and the pulse as music and rhythm. Drafts with provenance, cited to printed pages of the Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya edition; a damaged OCR stretch on p. 165 (end of the chapter on obstructions) and garbled lines on pp. 166, 168 and 171 were left out. The pulse chapter 1 ends on p. 171; chapter 2 (the even and the uneven pulse) begins there.
- **Ten more people read from al-Zirikli's al-A'lam (Lane B2, packet alm14).** Short cited biographies in Arabic, English and Urdu (draft, each cited to volume and page): Ibn al-Zamlakani (6:179, Shafi'i jurist and judge of Aleppo), Ahmad al-Jawhari (1:111, short entry), Ibn al-Fakhkhar (6:291, Maliki of Cordoba who fled to Valencia), Ibn Arafa (7:29, with the entry's note on a tomb inscription giving 800 AH), al-Yafi'i (4:57), Hasan al-Damistani (2:219, Imami labelled first, short entry), al-Damanhuri (1:156, shaykh of al-Azhar), Abd al-Aziz al-Dabbagh (4:10), Abdallah al-Suwaydi (4:57) and Sultan al-Mazzahi (3:108). Each page says only what the entry says. Corrections to the cards: al-Jawhari is Egyptian, not Arabian; al-Damistani's region is Eastern Arabia, not Persia; al-Dabbagh is dated 1684 to 1720 (not 1679 to 1719); al-Suwaydi is recorded as a jurist. Several cards keep their year where the entry's Hijri year spans the card's year (Ibn Arafa, al-Damistani, al-Damanhuri, al-Mazzahi). One card in the packet was skipped: Ahmad ibn Musa al-Kazim (the entry found is for Ali al-Rida, the eighth Imami imam, a different person).
