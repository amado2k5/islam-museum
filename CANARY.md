# Canary — islam.museum release notes

The museum's public release notes: every change that lands on `main`, recorded in
reader's language in the same commit that makes the change. Newest release first.
The site build reads the version and date from the top block below and shows them
in the footer of every page and on the changelog page (`/{lang}/changelog/`).

Rules for every agent and contributor (`docs/engineering/15-release-notes-and-versioning.md`):
append your entry under the current version block, grouped by kind — Added, Changed,
Fixed, Removed, Performance, Tests, Data, Breaking, Rolled back. Write what changed
and why it matters, never a bare commit message. If your change bumps the version,
open a new block on top with the new version and today's date.

## v2.25.0 — 2026-11-20
### Added
- **The Juha and Islamic Folklore Browser (متصفح جحا والفلكلور الإسلامي عبر العصور والكوميديا والنكت والطرائف والنوادر), at `/{lang}/juha/` in all five languages.** A new front door for humour in the Islamic tradition, requested by the founder on 20 November 2026: Juha and Nasreddin Hoca, the named wits and fools, the anecdote genre (nawadir), jesting, laughter and smiling, and the ethics of the joke — in the Qur'an, the Sunnah, the seerah, the reports of the Companions and Successors, and the harvested books. The page is a storyteller's gathering unlike any other browser: Juha riding his donkey backwards, the gathering of the wits as medallion seats, a strip of Juha's anecdotes as speech bubbles, a "scale of laughter" showing which kinds of humour each section carries, and the shelf of the anecdote books; below them, six sections with kind chips and a search box. It has a card on the homepage, an entry in the Explore menu, and its own data file at `/api/v1/juha.json` (documented in `docs/data/README.md`, the OpenAPI file and `llms.txt`). Passages are found at build time by a guarded detector (`scripts/juha.ts`), so every book harvested later brings its jokes here with no further work. The founder's brief is kept word for word in `docs/engineering/64-juha-browser-requirements.md` with twelve acceptance tests for production; the plan is `docs/plan/72-juha-browser.md`.
- **A new browser: the Rulings Browser — «متصفح الفروض والواجبات والطاعات والمستحبات والمكروهات والمحرمات وصغائر الذنوب» (`/[lang]/ahkam/`, plan 72, requested by the founder on 20 November 2026).** One front door for the degrees of obligation: the obligations, the obligation on every person (fard 'ayn) and on the community (fard kifaya), the acts of obedience, the obligatory, the recommended, the forbidden, the disliked, the minor sins, and what remains of the scale, the permitted and the concession. Its opening view is its own, "the scale of the five rulings": five degrees side by side from the act that must be done to the act that must be left, as al-Ghazali divides them in al-Mustasfa, each with the themes that stand on it. Ten theme pages open with a definition card from the books of usul. The build holds 4,607 records (`npx tsx scripts/ahkam-audit.ts`): 130 Qur'an ayahs, 2,500 Sunnah passages, 208 seerah excerpts, 1,735 excerpts from other books and 34 curated records; by theme (a record can carry more than one) 913 obligations, 180 individual obligation, 81 communal obligation, 237 obedience, 929 obligatory, 791 recommended, 1,774 forbidden, 478 disliked, 85 minor sins, 955 permitted and concession. Qur'an verses come from a reviewed table; other passages are found at build time by a guarded word list, so a book harvested later appears by itself; a bare prohibition is not classed as forbidden or disliked, because that is a jurist's reading. Six new records carry prose in Arabic, English, Urdu, French and Spanish (`concept/al-ahkam-al-taklifiyya`, `fard`, `fard-ayn`, `fard-kifaya`, `taa`, `haram-hukm`), with al-Shafi'i's account of the communal obligation cited to al-Risala. The browser has a card on the landing page, an entry in the Explore menu, the dataset `/api/v1/ahkam.json` and its acceptance checklist in `docs/engineering/64-ahkam-browser-requirements.md`. The museum describes what the texts and the jurists say and issues no ruling. Limits said plainly: the translations are machine-made and the excerpts from scanned books machine-read, none yet read by a person; the full build was not run before the merge, on the founder's instruction; the Explore menu entry changes every page at the next deploy (`rewrites-all-pages`).

### Data
- **Juha's anecdotes, one record each.** Every anecdote of *Nawadir Juha al-Kubra* (ed. Darwish al-Juwaydi) that passed the text rules is now its own passage record (`passage/nawadir-juha-<n>`): the printed Arabic title, a retelling in the museum's own words in Arabic, English, Urdu, French and Spanish, the page and number, and an excerpt of at most forty words. The retellings come from a machine-read scan that was not checked against the page images, and the translations are machine translations no human has read; each record says so.
- **Humour in the hadith collections and the books.** 150 reports of jesting, laughter, smiling and the limits of the joke from the seven hadith collections, and 644 cited excerpts from 24 scanned books — al-Nuwayri's *Nihayat al-Arab* (volume 4), Ibn Qutayba's *'Uyun al-Akhbar*, *al-Mustatraf*, al-Jahiz's *al-Bayan wa-l-Tabyin* and *al-Hayawan*, the *Ihya'*, and the seerah, Companion, biography and history books. Excerpts are chosen by vocabulary, so a few carry the word without being funny; the label says what was detected.
- **New and enriched records.** Four concepts — jesting (`concept/al-muzah`), laughter and smiling (`concept/al-dahik`), the anecdote (`concept/al-nawadir`) and the wits and the fools (`concept/al-zurafa-wa-al-mughaffalun`) — and first full prose in five languages for Juha, Nasreddin Hoca and *Nawadir Juha al-Kubra*, written from the edition's introduction with the editor's claims attributed to him and his contradictory dates for Nasreddin Hoca both given. A source record for the Cairo 1996 scan of *'Uyun al-Akhbar*. Twenty-three existing records (persons, works, places, a concept) carry the browser's tag. The Urdu title of *Nawadir Juha al-Kubra*, which a machine translation had misspelt, is corrected.
- **886 hadith reports and 1,368 book excerpts for the Rulings Browser** (lane `ahkam`): reports from the seven Arabic hadith-api editions and excerpts of at most forty words from 81 books already OCR'd on the harvest drive, led by the books of usul al-fiqh (al-Risala, al-Burhan, al-Mustasfa, al-Muwafaqat, Irshad al-Fuhul), each with labels and summaries in the five languages and a `mentions` edge to its theme's record. No book was downloaded and no PDF, OCR text or page image entered git.

### Added
- **The museum opens in the visitor's own language.** Someone who arrives at the bare address `islam.museum/` is now taken to the museum in their browser's default language when that language is English, Urdu, French or Spanish; Arabic speakers, speakers of any other language, and browsers without JavaScript get the Arabic page the root has always served. Only the root does this: `/ar/`, `/en/` and every deeper address stay where they are, so choosing a language in the header is never undone. A language chosen in the header is remembered: the next visit to the root opens in that language, whatever the browser's own language, until the visitor chooses another. The only thing kept is that language code, in the visitor's own browser storage, never sent anywhere (the privacy page lists it); search-engine crawlers are left on the Arabic root. The change lives in one shared script file (`site/public/shell/shell.js`), so no page is rewritten.

It also records what two earlier rounds of research found about where the texts can lawfully come from. Nothing is built or published; the answers will be checked before any record is written.

- **A written proposal for the Dictionaries Browser (`docs/plan/72-dictionaries-browser.md`).** It sets out what the browser holds (the dictionaries, their authors, their sources, and entries readable in full), the first run of 40 works, what two rounds of research established about where texts can lawfully come from, and the one decision still open: where the readable text comes from. It also records that the museum's own scans of *Lisān al-ʿArab*, *al-Qāmūs* and *al-Ṣiḥāḥ* are too noisy to display. Nothing is built.
- **The Dictionaries Browser starts: «متصفح المعاجم» at `/{lang}/maajim/`.** A new page in all five languages lists the museum's records of the great dictionaries in sections (general dictionaries of Arabic, the vocabulary of the Qur'an, geographic dictionaries), with each work's author, and explains where the text of a dictionary will and will not be shown. Nine dictionary records are in it in this first batch, among them *Lisān al-ʿArab*, *Kitāb al-ʿAyn*, *al-Ṣiḥāḥ*, *al-Qāmūs al-Muḥīṭ* and *Tāj al-ʿArūs*; the five general ones now carry dates, named sources and their arrangement, each taken from the Turkish TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi and cited on the record (for example that Ibn Manẓūr finished *Lisān al-ʿArab* on 22 Dhū l-Ḥijja 689 AH, 26 December 1290, from five named earlier dictionaries). The whole list is also available as `/api/v1/maajim.json`, is documented in `docs/site/browsers.md`, `site/public/openapi/v1.json` and `llms.txt`, and is found by search.
- **Eight encyclopaedia source records and two scan records for the dictionaries.** Each TDV article that a record relies on is its own source record, with what it was used for and what it does not say (two of them are short cross-references and are used only for one fact each). The two scan records describe the Bulaq 1282 AH / 1865 printing of *al-Ṣiḥāḥ* held by the Internet Archive, which the archive itself marks as not in copyright, with the file sizes and checksums of the copies the museum downloaded.
- **A pilot for reading a dictionary in full.** The two volumes of the 1865 Bulaq *al-Ṣiḥāḥ* were downloaded and volume 1 is being read with Apple Vision; on a test page the result is far cleaner than the museum's older scans of the same book but still contains errors, so no entry is published yet. ### Changed
- **New content must come in all five languages, whatever its kind (founder rule, 20 November 2026; `AGENTS.md`, `docs/language.md`).** The standing rule asked for Arabic, English, Urdu, French and Spanish on prose and interface strings. It now also names the titles, summaries and every other language block inside data records, the site datasets, the vocabulary files and text written in pages: a new record, field or dataset that lacks one of the five is not finished. Older content stays valid and is being filled.
- **Every pull request now goes to `main`; the Spanish release branch is retired (founder's instruction, 19 November 2026).** The Spanish and French translation work was collected on `release/spanish`. It is now merged into `main`, and the lane document, the translation how-to and the shipping scripts of the lane (`scripts/local-mt/ship_gt.sh`, `ship.sh`) raise against `main` only.
- **How bulk translation is done is written down (`docs/engineering/63-translation-by-document-files.md`, pointer in `AGENTS.md` and the Spanish lane document).** One English Word file per round goes through Google Translate's document translation, once for Spanish and once for French; link labels travel as Word hyperlinks and citations as 10-digit numbers, which kept 96–100% of records intact where text markers kept 11–63%. The page records the limits found (about 1.2 million words per file; a usage limit after about fifteen files) and that Arabic as the source was tested and not adopted.
- **Translation moves from the local model to Google Translate documents (founder's instruction, 19 November 2026).** A pilot showed that a plain-text Word file of about one megabyte (1,644 concept records, about 494,000 English words) is translated by translate.google.com in under a minute, where the local model needs more than a day. `scripts/local-mt/import_gt.py` splits the downloaded file back into records, restores citations and link ids, and applies the same checks as the local translator; 1,588 Spanish and 1,282 French records from the pilot passed and are in, each naming Google Translate in its `translators` and `provenance`. Records whose citation or link markers Google altered (about 3% in Spanish, 22% in French) were not imported. The local model threads are stopped. Nobody has read these translations.
- **The site build now clears unused tools off the build machine before it starts.** GitHub's build machine is documented with 14 GB of disk, and a build of five languages writes more than that; the build of 18 November on the Spanish release branch most likely stopped for want of disk, though that run printed no reading that proves it. The machine ships with tools this build never uses (the Android SDK, .NET, Haskell, CodeQL, Swift, Docker images). A new first step removes them and prints the free disk before and after. How much it frees is not yet measured: the next build's log will show it. The site itself is unchanged.
- **The site build now reports the build machine's free disk and memory while it runs.** The build of 18 November on the Spanish release branch stopped after two hours because GitHub lost contact with the machine. Its log simply stops while pages are being written, about 462,000 pages in, with no error message, so nobody could tell whether memory or disk had run out. (An earlier wording of this note said the run left no log; that was wrong, and is corrected here.) The build step now prints both before it starts and every five minutes, and raises a warning on the run page when under 4 GB of disk or 1 GB of memory is left. It only reads; the build itself is unchanged.
- **Translation now runs on a local model on the founder's Mac (from wave 11).** Spanish and French prose are translated from English by TranslateGemma 27B, served locally with Ollama, under `scripts/local-mt/` (a translator that protects citations, link ids and markers and checks every record against its English source, and a supervisor that restarts it). Claude manages the queue, the checks and the merges and no longer writes the translations. The local drafts are freer than the earlier ones and are labelled with the model's name on every record; nobody has read them.
